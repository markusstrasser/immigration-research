# Audit of the all-age fiscal total, remittances and later generations

**Verdict, September 17, 2026: partial cosign.** Commit `24e75ae` exists and its all-age baseline arithmetic reproduces. This is a useful expansion of the population coverage, with informative child and senior decompositions. The roughly **$280bn “all-in” headline is not a verified fiscal cost**. The additions mix populations, comparison rules and fiscal versus nonfiscal quantities; the remittance ceiling is circular; the later-generation table joins incompatible definitions. Preserve the measured differences and withdraw the stronger interpretations. [RAW REPRODUCTION + SOURCE AUDIT]

Inputs audited: the user's pasted all-age summary; all 59 lines of the supplied follow-up text (population, remittances and G3/G4/G5 claims); the actual commit, source code, generated tables and held primary data. The historical CPS2024 39.0m composition in the follow-up was not freshly replicated; the CPS2025 population behind the new headline was. The published Duncan AFQT row retains its earlier source-audit status, not a claimed fresh full-text replication. [COVERAGE]

The same selected taxes, transfers and public-medical costs produce every column. Consequently, **−$212bn means lower balance than the benchmark, not a $212bn observed deficit**. Conversely, +$32bn does not establish a complete fiscal surplus: components remain omitted. The peer's original memo already called its figure a partial benchmark gap; this audit makes that qualification numerically visible. [SOURCE: [independent generator and input hashes](../infra/immigration-fiscal/aggregate_audit_2026_09_17/README.md), `derived/audit.json`; [original calculation](../infra/immigration-fiscal/aggregate_gap_2026_09_17/aggregate_gap.py).]

The baseline allocates each SPM unit's income/payroll taxes, selected cash benefits and noncash resource values equally among all members, then attaches public-medical donor costs by individual age and nativity. The three target categories do not overlap. Adding previously omitted ages therefore does **not** inherently double count children. But it changes the population and composition of the comparison; “the earlier number was too low” is warranted only if the intended target was this all-age account. [MEASUREMENT]

For each group, the gap is `sum over age bands [target population × (target mean balance − reference mean balance)]`. Eight broad bands control age composition only coarsely. This is a period snapshot, not a longitudinal lifetime NPV, the marginal fiscal effect of an additional migrant, or the effect of removing immigration. The all-native reference includes the target descendants; it is not the all-US-parent reference in the separate LATAM comparison. The white reference is a framing choice; this calculation does not establish that it is the highest-balance origin group in the country. [METHOD / FRAMING-SENSITIVE]

**Children:** 5.418m Mexican G2 children have an own modeled partial balance of **−$748 each**, versus **+$6,244** for reference children. Their **−$6,992 difference** contributes −$37.885bn. The tax-allocation difference is −$7,055; lower cash transfers offset $293, higher noncash resources subtract $247, and lower modeled health costs offset $17. Thus the result principally describes allocated household taxes, not $7,000 of extra Medicaid or school costs per child. Changing household allocation would move dollars across age/generation labels. [RAW REPRODUCTION]

**Seniors:** Mexico-born adults 65+ have negative own balances but less negative balances than the reference. Their favorable benchmark contribution is **+$9.224bn**, consistent with the pasted “about $10bn.” This directly contradicts the earlier follow-up's claim that adding these seniors necessarily worsens the gap. Separately, adding the modeled health component narrows the total white-reference gap by **$9.564bn**; that is a different decomposition, not another senior Social Security offset. Health donor cells distinguish US/foreign birth, not Mexican origin specifically. [RAW REPRODUCTION]

## 2. Why the additions do not certify $280bn

- **The adult extension is a hybrid.** Its stored component estimates reproduce −$53.118bn against whites and −$44.765bn against all natives. Added to the baseline, these give −$264.849bn/−$197.995bn. But the baseline matches eight age bands; the extension compares pooled 25–64 groups and excludes shares allocated to all other ages. A consistent all-age extension must apply one universe, allocation and comparison rule to every component. Its correction could move the result either way. [SOURCE: `gen_ledger_extension_2026_09_16/extend_ledger.py`, `extended_ledger_by_generation.csv`; independent audit output.]
- **There is a specific lunch overlap, not wholesale child double counting.** The base subtracts SPM school-lunch resources and the K–12 addition includes food service. The extension already contains a net-of-lunch sensitivity. Applying it narrows the hybrid gaps by $1.002bn/$0.856bn. This is small relative to the headline but should be resolved; resource valuation and actual lunch expenditure are not necessarily identical. [SOURCE: extension generator `composite` and `extended_balance_net_of_school_lunch`.]
- **Crime harm is not all a government bill.** The crime lane includes victim losses and valuations of pain, suffering and death as well as corrections expenditure. Such costs can matter to a welfare account, but adding them to fiscal balances does not produce a fiscal total. Its origin/offense transport, institutional-population proxy and comparison baseline also need harmonization; the audit does not independently certify the $13–19bn range. Adding institutional corrections again would risk overlap. [SOURCE: [crime lane](../infra/immigration-fiscal/crime_cost_2026_09_16/RESULT.md), §§1–4 and assumptions.]
- **The $3–4bn remittance addition is not reproduced by its cited lane.** The existing national mechanical sales-tax scenarios give $1.277–2.299bn with Mexico-born geographic weights, or $1.169–2.105bn with all-person weights. These assume taxable-spending displacement; they are not identified tax losses. No estimated multiplier in that lane establishes the larger combined number. [SOURCE: `remit_leak_2026_09_16/remit_leak.py` national calculation and `remit_result.txt`.]

The claim that all omissions make the benchmark gap worse is false as a general rule. Equal per-person public-goods charges cancel: `(B_group − c) − (B_reference − c) = B_group − B_reference`. Unequal allocations need explicit assumptions. Corporate-tax incidence has not been established here. The existing institutional-care sensitivity actually narrows the adult G2 gap by about $48 because the reference includes more nursing-home expense. Missing items are neither guaranteed adverse effects nor measured zeros. [ALGEBRA; SOURCE: crime lane §4 and confidence-ladder 76.]

No all-age sampling interval was computed by the original generator; it uses full weights and discards medical donor covariance. Reproduced arithmetic is stronger than an unsupported number, but is not precise estimation of a complete economic burden. [CODE AUDIT]

## 3. The remittance bound fails

CEMLA constructs **16.7%** as approximately **$62.529bn of US-to-Mexico remittances / $373.726bn of Mexico-born workers' earnings**. That numerator does not identify senders' generation. Applying the quotient to a different $352bn wage estimate yields roughly $58.8bn, leaving $3.7bn. This remainder reflects the imposed first-generation assumption and differing wage denominators; it is not independent evidence of a ceiling on US-born sending. [SOURCE: [CEMLA note, pages 1–2](https://www.cemla.org/foroderemesas/notas/2025-03-notas-de-remesas.pdf); INFERENCE from its displayed construction.]

Let `R = R_first + R_USborn + R_other`. Bounding `R_USborn` from above requires an independent lower bound on `R_first` (and compatible coverage). An upper bound on the immigrant sending rate supplies no such lower bound. For illustration, first-generation sending of $45bn, US-born sending of $17.529bn and zero other sending fit the same total, while $45bn/$352bn = 12.8% is below 16.7%. This is a counterexample to the claimed bound, not an estimate of actual sending. Withdraw both **“US-born at most $3.7bn”** and the derived **“G2 below 0.9% of earnings”**. Rates previously excluded by that argument are scenarios, not ruled out observations. [ALGEBRA / FALSIFICATION]

The original 2024 Mexico total was $64.745bn; $62.529bn is specifically its US corridor. A further useful distinction resolves part of the claimed BEA mismatch: the historical **$69.940bn** in BEA's March 20, 2025 release is preliminary 2024 personal transfers to all destinations **by foreign-born US residents**. US-born residents' transfers enter another category. The series therefore cannot cap all US senders, and a full numerical reconciliation remains undone. [SOURCE: [Banxico original release](https://www.banxico.org.mx/publicaciones-y-prensa/remesas/%7BD08E80AC-6031-C7CF-D792-28CBEF70658F%7D.pdf), [BEA Table 5 line 18 and footnote 3](https://www.bea.gov/sites/default/files/2025-03/trans424.pdf), [BEA June 2026 methods §14.31–14.32](https://www.bea.gov/sites/default/files/2026-06/BEA-International-Economic-Accounts-Concepts-and-Methods.pdf).]

Current transfers abroad reduce gross national disposable income with other flows held fixed. They are not directly subtracted from GDP and are not PCE. A transfer could displace saving, debt repayment or consumption; the accounts alone do not select the response. Neither a dollar-for-dollar consumption loss nor zero causal GDP effect follows from this accounting classification. The 0.2%-of-GDP comparison is scale, not an estimated fall in output or taxpayer resources. [SOURCE: [BEA personal-saving accounting, Table H/note 19](https://apps.bea.gov/scb/issues/2022/11-november/1122-regional-annual-update.htm); [IMF secondary-income framework](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/display/book/9781589068124/ch12.xml); INFERENCE. IMF full-text access was restricted; the decisive transfer/consumption distinction was independently read in BEA's accessible material.]

An additional IIMMLA raw check finds that its source-coded G3 n212 contains **199 yes and 13 “don't know”** answers to the foreign-born-grandparent question; G4+ n189 contains 188 no and one not-applicable. Other source information may explain these codes, but it was not traced. Retain “source-coded generation,” not “all observed to have a foreign-born grandparent,” and do not silently treat generic foreign birth as Mexican birthplace. [RAW CHECK: `ETHNOS10=1`, `GENERAT4`, `Q152A`.]

The published AFQT −17.72/−25.63 percentile-point coefficients use yet another classification and adjusted comparison. They are consistent with the earlier paper audit; this pass did not independently retrieve the full table. The held public NLSY analysis instead has roughly equal AFQT means in its own G3/G4 cells, with wide uncertainty. These are different estimands, not a single replicated generational trajectory. The HS ordering's sensitivity to unresolved grandparents is real; assigning them to G3 is a scenario, not observed ancestry. [SOURCE: [published paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10836839/), existing `testscores_gen_2026_09_16/RESULT.md` and `frontier_execution_2026_09_17/nlsy/RESULT.md`; explicit verification limit.]

**Withdraw “the negatively selected half of the true fourth-generation Mexican lineage.”** Pew's half refers to self-reported Hispanic ancestry in G4+, not exact Mexican G4, and neither survey observes everyone with forgotten or unreported ancestry. Selective identification is a serious explanation to test, but these rows do not identify the fraction or its contribution to the schooling gap. Pew also reports that most nonidentifiers never personally identified as Hispanic; intergenerational nontransmission differs from personal identity loss. [SOURCE: Pew report; INFERENCE / SCOPE]

**Narrow “no dataset anywhere measures fifth generation” to “these audited analyses do not isolate exact Mexican fifth-generation outcomes.”** The broader absence claim was not established. Held Pew questions already identify great-grandparent-or-earlier reported ancestry and national origin, although they do not recover exact fifth-generation birthplaces or outcome estimates. Intermarriage makes classification harder; it does not logically make descendant research impossible. [SOURCE: Pew nonidentifier questionnaire `HA6`, `Q3`, held release PDF.]

Use separate fields for own/parent/grandparent birthplace, one- versus two-origin-parent profiles, source-coded generation, unknown history, cohort and self-identification. None is a national genetic fraction. A finding about a selected self-identifying sample should retain that denominator in every transfer to a new claim. [MEASUREMENT]

## 5. Claims worth keeping and the next decisive check

Keep the large measured annual benchmark differences, the all-age extension's population coverage, the favorable senior comparison, later-generation educational shortfalls within stated samples, and evidence that country/parent profiles differ. The new [five-year LATAM comparison](immigration-latam-benchmark-comparison-2026-09-17.md) independently shows economic point estimates near the native-parent benchmark for several origins, while the regional average and Mexican-parent G2 remain below it. Neither persistent gaps nor successful subgroups isolate a genetic cause. [SYNTHESIS]

Before publishing a single burden number, rebuild the extension over all ages with one allocation/age rule, remove overlap, compute uncertainty and publish absolute balances beside reference gaps. Keep fiscal receipts/outlays, nonfiscal harms and policy counterfactuals separate until their populations and incidence agree. For remittances, a generation-resolved sender estimate could test the missing first-generation share; another total-flow/wage quotient cannot. For later descendants, country-specific lineage including nonidentifiers would test whether the observed schooling gap survives identity selection. These are discriminating improvements, not reasons to discard existing descriptive evidence. [INFERENCE / FALSIFIERS]

This audit guards against two symmetric framing errors: relabeling a negative benchmark gap as a net cost, and relabeling a positive partial balance as a complete benefit. LLM-assisted selection of comparisons and omissions can favor either narrative. Raw checks, explicit denominators and retained contrary evidence constrain that instrument bias. [QUANT-BIAS / INSTRUMENT]
