**Verdict:** 1,361 immigration-related posts out of 10,181 bookmarks were read by two Claude lanes and two Grok 4.6 reads (one per half). About 190 posts carry an extractable angle; the rest are rhetoric, single incidents or off-topic. Against ladder entries 65–81, the reads agree on five angles worth building, four that are already measured, and a handful of bookmarked claims that would fail verification and must not enter the essay. The operator's flagged angle, mixed-classroom and literacy costs, has no evidential lead in the bookmarks and is disconfirmed by the literature (ladder 81). ## Where the targets stand now (X pull, August–September 2026, and Smith's September 9 essay)

Handles verified; posts pulled with immigration keywords since 1 August. Cowen: nothing keyword-reachable. [SOURCE: `../infra/immigration-fiscal/acs_institutional_2026_09_16/x_pull_2026_09_16/x_econ_*.jsonl`]

- **Noah Smith has moved.** "Liberalism needs a new philosophy of immigration" (noahpinion.blog, 9 September 2026; 1,083 likes on the post) concedes that unenforced law is open borders in effect, that migration is not a right, that nations may select on skills and bar groups hostile to the country, and that "some of the economic costs are real, local housing shortages and strains on local government finances being the two biggest." He cites Konstantinou and Roumanias 2024 (a 1% rise in immigrant stock raises far-right voting 1.8–3.0%). What he keeps: "the economic benefits are pretty undeniable, higher tax revenue," on the national-aggregate coordinate, and the wage null. He does not mention the second generation. The essay's remaining disagreement with him is therefore narrow and exactly where the repo's evidence is: the generational unit of the fiscal claim, and that "select on skills" is the selection result the ledger already quantifies. His 13 August post that the 1900s European analogy "is good" is testable on the 1880–1940 full-count censuses (memo §9), not yet run.
- **Caplan is unchanged** and supplies the cleanest comparator trick: "virtually no immigrant nationality has worse crime rates than native-born blacks" (24 August). First-generation rates against the worst native subgroup; the second-generation rates and the white reference answer it, and the essay should quote it verbatim.
- **Nowrasteh's highest-engagement post** (15 September, 3,014 likes): "The Indian immigrant incarceration rate is 49 per 100,000. The native-born American incarceration rate is 1,195 per 100,000." First generation of the most positively selected stream against all natives pooled, the coordinate switch in one sentence; the same file gives US-born Mexican-origin at 1.7–1.9× whites. His "79 per 100,000" line is arithmetic mocking a "100 million illegals" claim, not a data point.
- **Counters on X:** none of substance were keyword-reachable; the pushback is in replies, not essays.

## Additions from the Grok read of the first half

Three angles the Claude lanes did not rank. (1) The crime cost sits in the sexual-offence tail, not the mean: the essay can say this with the repo's own numbers, since rape is 9% and murder 65% of the cost-weighted difference (ladder 78) and the London sex-offence rates by nationality were rebuilt in the Pueyo reproduction (ladder 73). (2) Shared childhood environment fades: adoption and twin designs show the family-environment correlation in adult IQ near zero, which is a mechanism reason that Americanisation of the second generation does not by itself close the ledger; the repo's own measured version is the third-generation stall (memo §4). Use as a residual-section citation (Plomin, Turkheimer), not a group-gap claim. (3) The "poor whites versus rich Blacks" incarceration comparison is the same Opportunity Insights table as the parental-income angle above, and it cuts both ways for the essay: if the second-generation gap survives at equal parental income it is not "just class," and if it does not, the selection story is the whole story. Grok's do-not-use list matches the Claude lanes' and adds the Lagos IQ "study," "85% of refugees Muslim," "$38T deficit," and the 15× juror-bias figure. Neither Caplan nor Noah Smith is named anywhere in the bookmarks. [SOURCE: `../infra/immigration-fiscal/bookmark_angles_2026_09_16/grok_angles_a.md`]

## Already measured, or contradicted by the repo

- 287(g) or deportations lowering rents: the exact claim that did not reproduce in the repeat-rent index (ladder 79).
- "Assimilation is a myth for non-Europeans": Vietnamese 5.60 → 0.30 and Salvadoran 3.01 → 0.89 institutionalisation (ladder 74).
- Mixed-classroom and literacy costs on incumbents: precise zeros and a sibling-fixed-effects flip (ladder 81); the Dutch PISA home-language gap in the bookmarks (−78 Arabic, −93 Turkish points) is a gap for the immigrant children themselves, not a cost to incumbents.
- "Which is it, cheap labour or welfare": the ledger answers it, high first-generation employment and a negative net balance because the gap is on the tax side (memo §12).
- Minority contracting set-asides: the 8(a) mechanism is real; still unpriceable to persons (ladder 76).

## Disconfirming angles the essay should carry

- AfD support is highest where immigration is lowest: restriction politics is not local exposure.
- About 40% of the federal budget goes to the over-65s: the strongest "so what" against a −$8.3k per adult-year second-generation gap.
- Trust may be a selection story (who migrates) rather than a diversity story; the meta-analysis does not separate them.

## Bookmarked claims that would fail verification

"90% of Indian H-1B applications fraudulent" (unsourced official talk); "1% of asylum pleas genuine" (Home Office grant rates run 40–70%); UK arrest rates by nationality per 1,000 (the rounded-denominator trap ladder 73 caught in London; an Albanian 21% annual arrest rate is not credible); "$165,333 per migrant child per year" (an ORR contract figure, not a per-child cost); heritability of crime "45%" (real literature, wrong use for a group gap).

What the map says. The economists' side is fully audited and nothing new appeared in it except the distributional mobility paper. The con side had three unbuilt channels with real identification behind them (automation, native fertility, return-migrant selection), and two measurable ones still in the queue (native labour-force withdrawal, the political-externality price). The automation channel is the one that can change the essay's shape: it is a productivity cost outside every fiscal ledger, it explains why wage effects are near zero (technique adjusts) and why that is not reassuring, and it is the mechanism behind the Central Valley, Belgian coal and Lancashire mill stories the restrictionist side tells anecdotally.
