**Verdict:** For Mexican-origin residents the measured record is clear on characteristics and thin on causal impact. First generation: 41–43% of adults without a high-school diploma, prime-age men employed at 89% (the highest of any group), worker median earnings 59% of third-plus non-Hispanic whites, SPM poverty 23%, household program use 60–62% driven by Medicaid and EITC with cash assistance at 4–5%, a partial annual fiscal balance $6.7k–$9.0k per adult below the all-native mean, and the lowest crime of any group. Second generation: the dropout share falls to 10%, BA attainment doubles to 25%, employment reaches the native level, worker median earnings rise to 76% of white natives, SPM poverty halves to 13–15%, household program use is 52–53% (young heads with children), cash assistance equals natives', and arrest timing is statistically indistinguishable from non-Hispanic whites, Blacks and third-plus Hispanics. Third-plus (self-identified): BA 27%, earnings 81% of white natives, poverty 11%, program use 42%; incremental progress over the second generation is small but the group is selected by ethnic attrition and its crime cannot be separated. Causal *impact* on natives is established for one channel only, and it is contested in magnitude: Borjas–Katz put the 1980–2000 Mexican inflow at about −8 points on native dropouts' wages and about −1 point on all workers in the short run, with Card- and Peri-style designs near zero. No Mexican-specific lifetime fiscal balance survives the 2026-09-05 repair. [SOURCE: tables below; linked memos] [INFERENCE for the synthesis]

# Mexican-origin population by generation: economic position, welfare, crime, and what is conclusive

## 1. Population and definitions

CPS ASEC 2025, all ages, person-weighted: Mexico-born 12.2M; native-born with a Mexico-born parent 14.4M; native-born with two US-born parents self-identifying as Mexican origin 14.4M. The Census Bureau's own generation table for 2024 gives 12.1M / 13.8M / 13.1M with a slightly different second-generation construction (Mexican self-identification plus any foreign-born parent) ([Mexican-origin incarceration memo](immigration-mexican-origin-generation-incarceration-2026-09-16.md) §4). [SOURCE: `../infra/immigration-fiscal/cps_generation_welfare_2026_09_16/mexican_origin_result_2025.txt`]

Definitions here: gen 1 = born in Mexico (`PENATVTY` 303); gen 2 = native-born with at least one parent born in Mexico; gen 3+ = native-born, both parents US-born, `PRDTHSP` = Mexican. The third-plus group is defined by self-identification, which about 17% of third-generation Mexican-ancestry children (Duncan & Trejo, cited in ladder entry 67) and up to 30% of youth drop, with the leavers positively selected. Every third-plus figure below is therefore biased toward the less assimilated part of the lineage. [FRAMING-SENSITIVE]

## 4. Economic impact on natives: the one causal channel with Mexican-specific estimates

Borjas & Katz 2007 (NBER w11281), Table 11, simulate the 1980–2000 immigrant influx in a nested-CES model with education-experience cells. Predicted change in the native weekly wage, actual influx versus a counterfactual with no Mexican immigration:

| Native group | Short run, capital fixed: actual / no-Mexico | Long run, capital elastic: actual / no-Mexico |
|---|---|---|
| All workers | −3.3% / −2.2% | +0.1% / +0.6% |
| High-school dropouts | −8.2% / −0.1% | −4.8% / +2.7% |
| High-school graduates | −2.2% / −1.2% | +1.1% / +1.6% |
| College graduates | −3.8% / −3.9% | −0.5% / −1.1% |

[SOURCE: NBER w11281 Table 11, read from the PDF this session]

So the Mexican component of the 1980–2000 inflow is about −8 points for native dropouts in either run, about −1 point for the average worker in the short run and −0.5 in the long run, and nothing for graduates. This is a structural simulation whose elasticity of substitution between dropouts and high-school graduates drives the dropout result; the Card, Peri–Sparber and Ottaviano–Peri line treats dropouts and graduates as close substitutes and immigrants as imperfect substitutes for natives, which pushes the same number toward zero, and Mariel remains "a disputed magnitude, not a proved zero" ([disconfirmers memo](immigration-economics-disconfirmers-2026-06-25.md)). Nothing in the repo identifies the Mexican-specific wage effect after 2000, when the Mexican-born workforce stopped growing. Level: **contested evidence, model-dependent sign for the average worker, negative but disputed magnitude for dropouts.** [SOURCE: memos cited] [INFERENCE on the model dependence]

Second-order channels (housing, local services, construction supply, prices) are assessed in the [second-order effects memo](immigration-second-order-effects-2026-09-05.md) for low-skill inflows generally; none is Mexican-specific.

## 5. Fiscal position by generation

- First generation: the 2024 partial account gives Mexico-born adults a balance $6,703–$8,969 below the all-native mean per adult across four constructions, before schools; adding an average-cost school scenario makes the Mexico-born balance negative in the displayed constructions ([fiscal account](immigration-fiscal-account-2024-2026-09-05.md)). This is a comparison of resident means on selected ledgers, not a complete balance or an effect of admitting anyone.
- Second and third-plus: no Mexican-specific fiscal construction exists in the repo. What the CPS tables give is the input side: adult earnings 71–81% of white natives' and 76–87% of the all-native mean, employment at the native level, Medicaid and EITC use about twice white natives', cash use equal. On the all-origin NAS 2017 accounting the second generation is the strongest net-contributing group and the first generation the weakest at the state and local level (annual 2011–13 per independent person: −$1,600 first, +$1,700 second, +$1,300 third-plus; [Hernández dismantling](immigration-dismantle-hernandez-2026-06-25.md)). Whether the Mexican second generation is net positive on a complete ledger is not established here; its earnings are below the all-native mean and its dependants' program use above it. The June Mexico lifetime-NPV synthesis was withdrawn on 2026-09-05 ([Mexico NPV](immigration-mexico-npv-population-synthesis-2026-06-15.md)). Level: **first generation measured on partial ledgers; second and third generation not measured.**

## 6. Crime by generation

From today's crime memos and the September 5 corrections, all verified against sources there:

- First generation. Texas 2018 violent-felony arrest-charge rate, unauthorized versus native-born: ratio 0.4571 ([conduct denominators](immigration-conduct-denominators-2026-09-05.md)). Mexico-born men 18–39 institutionalized 1.13% in 2023 against 2.05% for all natives and 1.12% for native non-Hispanic whites, with ICE detention inflating the foreign-born row ([Mexican-origin memo](immigration-mexican-origin-generation-incarceration-2026-09-16.md) §2). The first-generation advantage is an adult-arrival floor that does not transmit ([generational mechanisms](immigration-generational-crime-mechanisms-2026-09-16.md) §1).
- Second generation. NLSY97 (Inkpen 2024): 85% of first-generation Mexican respondents reach 18 without an arrest versus 78% of the Mexican second generation, and the second generation shows "no statistically significant differences in time to first arrest compared to non-Hispanic white or Black individuals or third/fourth-generation Hispanic respondents". This is the only US design that separates the Mexican second generation from third-plus whites on a justice outcome. Level: A for the numbers, one cohort.
- Second plus third-plus pooled. US-born Mexican-origin men 18–39 institutionalized 5.90% in 2000 (3.45× native whites), 1.92% in 2023 (1.72× raw, about 2.1× after reallocating generically coded Hispanic prison records), now below the all-native 2.05% (ladder entries 65–66). All-Hispanic sentenced-prisoner rate 606 per 100,000 adults versus 231 white and 1,218 Black in 2023 ([race/ethnicity](immigration-crime-race-ethnicity-2026-09-05.md)); the 2009–2021 decline was property and drug for both Hispanics and whites (ladder 70).
- Third generation alone: not separable in any US crime source; attrition biases the self-identified pool upward.

## 8. Disconfirmation and limits

- CPS ASEC under-reports program receipt and top-codes earnings; levels are conservative, and gaps are the informative quantity. The 2024 file reproduces every cell within sampling noise.
- Generation-2 and generation-3+ Mexican cells (about 2,500 adults each) give standard errors near 1 point on shares and about $1,500 on medians; the 2024 bracket values show the year-to-year movement.
- Self-identification defines the third-plus group; the Mexican-ancestry population with two US-born parents is larger and better off than the measured one. Nothing here corrects for it.
- The economic tables describe residents in 2024, not the 1980–2000 arrival cohorts' children specifically; second-generation adults 25–64 are the children of arrivals from the 1960s to the 1990s.
- No regression adjustment was run; "the second generation's welfare use exceeds white natives'" is a raw comparison, and the all-origin memo shows most of the generational excess is age and children.
