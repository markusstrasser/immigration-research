# How public claims about low-skill immigration mislead: 81 graded statements

**Date:** 2026-10-20 · **Evidence level:** quotes are primary text fetched in the run; grades are judgments against the
repo's measured accounts on a matched population, horizon and outcome. **Frame:** the operator asked which moves
prominent pro-immigration voices use; a restrictionist arm (23 of 81 rows) is graded by the same rules as a check on
the instrument (`notes/llm-bias-caveat.md`). [FRAMING-SENSITIVE: the grade COORDINATE is a judgment about what a
general reader hears.]

**Verdict:** The pro side seldom states a false number. In 30 of its 58 graded rows the claim is true on the axis it
states (the federal budget, the first generation, the all-origin average, the average wage, world output) and is heard
as an answer on another axis, where the repo measures the opposite sign for the Mexican-origin lineage. Only 2 pro-side
rows are false on their own terms, both on crime or skill magnitudes. The restrictionist arm states false numbers more
often (4 of 23: an unauthorized count of 20–30M, an $8bn "Medicaid fraud" line, first-generation crime) while landing
on the direction the repo measures. The commonest pro-side move is a one-sided ledger: gross taxes paid, or a Social
Security surplus, offered as the answer to whether the group costs others money. [INFERENCE from the three sweep files]

## Awareness: the caveat printed beside the headline

Motive cannot be measured. What can be recorded is that the author printed the limiting fact in the same piece and kept
the headline that it contradicts:

- ITEP: "this figure includes only the taxes borne", beside "public services receive $8.9 billion".
- Orrenius–Viard–Zavodny (AEI 2025): the abstract says low-skilled immigrants "impose a net fiscal cost"; the conclusion
  says "the evidence is clear … immigrants are a net fiscal positive".
- Hamilton Project 2022: the body says "a net fiscal cost at the state and local levels"; the title says "the positive
  fiscal benefits of immigration".
- Matthews 2015: "all of our empirical research pertains to increases in immigration that are milder than pure open
  borders", under "Open borders would make Americans richer".
- Klein 2014 concedes the safety net "would buckle" under a large influx; his 2010 "won't cost the government a dime"
  was not revisited.
- Blau–Donehower 2017: "the answer depends on what we consider the costs of an immigrant", beside the second
  generation as "fiscal powerhouses".

Two statements never reconciled: Krugman wrote in 2006 that "you'd be hard pressed to find any set of assumptions under
which Mexican immigrants are a net fiscal plus" (low-wage Mexican immigrants, NRC estimates, all levels of government)
and in 2024 that "undocumented immigrants are especially good for the budget, because they pay payroll taxes … without
being eligible for future benefits" (the unauthorized of all origins, the federal entitlement budget). These are
different objects, so this is not a sign reversal on one quantity; the 2024 sentence is moves 3, 4 and 11 (federal
scope, one program's surplus, the worker as the unit) and is heard as answering the question the 2006 one answered the
other way. The 2024 column flags his wage reversal ("myself included") and does not mention the 2006 fiscal judgment.
The 2006 sentence matches the repo on its own object: the Mexico-born with their children cost other residents
$154.8–185.9bn a year, about 0.5–0.6% of GDP.

## Limits

- The repo measures resident groups, mostly the Mexican-origin lineage. It has no all-origin unauthorized account, so
  the commonest media claims (about all unauthorized immigrants) grade COORDINATE or NO-MATCH, never HARD.
- COORDINATE depends on a reading of what the audience hears. A reader who takes "federal" as federal will find those
  rows fair.
- Coverage gaps: the FT, WSJ and NYT editorial boards, Axios, FactCheck.org, ProPublica, FWD.us, BPC, Niskanen, PIIE
  and EIG were not reached; Stiglitz, Acemoglu, Romer, Goldin, Wolfers and Rodrik had no qualifying claim in one search
  each. Furman, Summers, Stephen Miller, Bannon and Krikorian have paraphrase-only leads. Each sweep's section 4 lists
  them.
- Some quotes come from a crawler's copy of the named page where a paywall cut the fetch; the URL column marks them.
- Parent checks: Krugman 2006 and 2024, ITEP's caveat and the NAS grid were re-checked at source; the NAS check
  corrected one row (the omitted +$173k cell). Other rows were not re-fetched.
