**Verdict:** Substantial adverse spillovers are supported in particular settings. A negative overall effect of low-skill immigration on existing residents is not established by the current evidence. The strongest skeptical case concerns persistent low earnings combined with costly redistribution, rapid inflows into constrained housing/services, and difficult integration. GDP growth cannot settle that case; neither can a list of possible costs. [INFERENCE from sources below]

# What follows from the second-order costs argument

September 5, 2026. Frame: existing residents' living standards and institutional quality, with competing workers, renters, owners and taxpayers distinguished. Migrants' own gains would also count under an all-resident or global objective. This is a bounded synthesis, not a new complete welfare estimate or a change to the project's central question. [FRAMING-SENSITIVE]

The strongest version of the user's argument is coherent: employers can profit from hiring while other residents bear service, housing, compliance or coordination costs. If those uncompensated costs exceed the benefits to existing residents, the policy reduces their welfare despite increasing production. The unresolved step is the sign and size of that balance for a defined admission policy. A positive benefit channel alone does not establish a positive balance either. [INFERENCE]

## Production, living standards and the population change

GDP records domestic value added. Additional output devoted to serving newcomers is real production; its existence alone does not demonstrate an improvement for people already resident. Government purchases and payroll enter GDP, while transfers are not themselves production. Extra activity needed to maintain an existing service level can therefore contribute to measured GDP without a corresponding welfare improvement; displacement of other production still matters. BEA explicitly distinguishes GDP from distribution and nonmarket welfare. [SOURCE: [GDP accounting](https://bea.gov/index.php/news/blog/2025-06-03/expenditures-approach-measuring-gdp), [government spending](https://www.bea.gov/help/faq/552), [well-being framework](https://apps.bea.gov/well-being/archive/docs/background-materials-prototype-measures-of-economic-well-being-and-growth.pdf); INFERENCE about the policy comparison]

GDP per resident does not solve the composition problem. **Illustration only, fixed prices:** initially 100 residents each receive $60,000, with output equal to aggregate income. Ten newcomers earn $30,000 each. If each incumbent's income rises to $60,500, total output rises from $6m to $6.35m but output per resident falls to $57,727. If incumbent income instead falls to $59,500, output still rises to $6.25m. Both incumbent gains and losses are compatible with higher total output and a lower population mean. [CALCULATION: `.scratch/second-order-20260905/gdp-counterexamples.json`; not an immigration estimate]

Our recent native/Mexico-born fiscal and racial wage comparisons are descriptive resident-stock comparisons. Mexico-born, non-college, less-than-high-school, low-earning, limited-English and unauthorized populations are different. Those results do not identify a low-skill admission policy's causal effect on incumbent incomes or institutions. [SOURCE: [current findings](immigration-clarity-update-2026-09-05.md), [glossary](immigration-glossary.md); INFERENCE]

## A formal case for restrictions, with its assumptions visible

Guerreiro–Rebelo–Teles provide a serious economic version of the skeptical case. Their static native-welfare model can favor excluding low-skill entrants when optimal redistribution benefits low-skill natives and the tax/transfer system must treat newcomers identically. Different immigrant taxation can instead support entry subject to charges that cover congestion. The theory has strong substitution assumptions; households are single workers, without descendant dynamics, and the model abstracts from other externalities. Its quantitative section relaxes within-skill native/immigrant substitution and calibrates 1994–2008. This establishes a conditional mechanism for restrictions, not the empirical sign of today's complete ledger. [SOURCE: [February 2020 revision of NBER26154](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w26154/w26154.pdf), §§1–2,4.2–5; [published article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmoneco.2020.04.009)]

## Further deductions and what would settle them

1. **Private hiring incentives can diverge from the public interest.** Wages paid by the hiring firm do not necessarily cover external service/congestion costs. Taxes and supply benefits can offset them. A policy response requires the net external effect, not employer demand alone. [INFERENCE]
2. **Pace, destination and composition matter at the margin.** The next arrivals can meet full schools or scarce housing even when earlier arrivals were beneficial. Conversely, construction or care workers can relax a constraint. Past averages do not identify the next admission's value; no universal safe intake percentage was recovered. [INFERENCE]
3. **Administrative and cultural complexity should be measured separately.** Service language, work authorization, processing rules, trust, cooperation and conflict have different mechanisms. Some costs are policy-mediated; that does not erase their actual burden, but it changes which interventions can reduce it. A rule count alone has no established welfare sign. [INFERENCE]
4. **Avoid charging the same harm repeatedly.** Interpreter payroll inside a court budget is not an additional cost on top of that budget. Estimate service deterioration remaining after the actual spending response, not both full remedial spending and the deterioration it prevents. Similarly, productivity, wages, profits and resulting tax receipts cannot simply be added as separate gains. [ACCOUNTING INFERENCE]
5. **A risk-based policy judgment is possible before complete certainty.** A preference to protect vulnerable incumbents or avoid hard-to-reverse institutional risks can justify a more cautious admission rule under stated risk weights. That is a decision under uncertainty, not proof that every missing effect is negative or that the current policy has a measured negative net value. [FRAMING-SENSITIVE]

The highest-value next evidence would follow a defined admission cohort and comparable receiving areas: incumbent real disposable incomes by worker/renter status; spending together with school, court and health-service quality; housing demand and construction supply; and panel trust/cooperation outcomes. Exposure must vary for a credible reason independent of the outcomes. A consistent discounted balance, or benefits/costs bounds that establish its sign, would justify a stronger overall conclusion. Regressing local outcomes on immigrant shares without resolving selection would not. [INFERENCE]
