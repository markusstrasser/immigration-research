# Immigration — Sociology / Social-Integration Frontier (2026-06-25)

**2026-09-05 scope correction:** The 2020 meta-analysis finds a modest negative association for generalized trust as well as neighbors; the earlier claim that generalized trust is unaffected was wrong. Observational adjustment and repeated cross-sections do not by themselves identify causal effects or within-person assimilation. See [material-inference repair](../decisions/2026-09-05-material-inference-repair.md).

**Axis:** SOCIAL integration/cohesion — assimilation theory, social cohesion & trust, ethnic enclaves, residential segregation as social stratification. Explicitly NOT economics/wages/crime-rates (other agents own those).

**Instrument bias (per constitution):** politically charged topic + LLM-assisted corpus + a field that tilts left. Disconfirming evidence sought explicitly; evidence strength judged by design and source, rather than ideological surprise. Steelman before critique. Every claim tagged. Framing-dependent verdicts flagged `[FRAMING-SENSITIVE]`.

**Verdict:** Dinesen, Schaeffer & Sønderskov (2020) report a modest negative pooled association between ethnic diversity and trust, stronger for neighbors and local diversity. Figure 2 also reports a statistically significant negative generalized-trust association; out-group trust is not distinguishable from zero. These observational associations neither prove large social damage nor prove that unmeasured confounding is absent. ## (a) Papers — source-grade + what each tests/disconfirms

Grades: A = peer-reviewed meta-analysis / quasi-experiment in a top venue; B = peer-reviewed empirical with identification limits; C = review/theory, no new identification; D = advocacy/opinion. All saved to corpus unless noted.

### Social cohesion & trust (the two-sided core)

1. **Dinesen, Schaeffer & Sønderskov (2020), "Ethnic Diversity and Social Trust: A Narrative and Meta-Analytical Review," *Annual Review of Political Science* 23:441–465.** Grade **A**. `[SOURCE: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-polisci-052918-020708]` OA PDF: pure.au.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/230771020. **The current canonical meta-analysis** — 1,001 estimates from 87 studies, screened from ~4,000. **Findings (quoted):** "a statistically significant negative relationship between ethnic diversity and social trust across all studies," but the pooled partial correlation is **−0.0256 (s.e. 0.0044)** — described by the authors themselves as "rather modest." "The relationship is stronger for trust in neighbors and when ethnic diversity is measured more locally. Covariate conditioning generally changes the relationship only slightly." **Interpretation corrected:** The paper reports negative associations for generalized, in-group and neighbor trust, strongest for neighbors; out-group trust is statistically inconclusive. Similar estimates after measured controls do not rule out unmeasured confounding. A partial correlation is neither a causal effect nor a percentage-point change in trust. Public meta-data at osf.io/aecs2. `[FRAMING-SENSITIVE]`: whether −0.026 is "trivial" or "meaningful at scale" is a values call, not a data call.

2. **van der Meer & Tolsma (2014), "Ethnic Diversity and Its Effects on Social Cohesion," *Annual Review of Sociology* 40:459–478.** Grade **A**. `[SOURCE: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-soc-071913-043309]` OA PDF: repository.ubn.ru.nl/bitstream/handle/2066/133583/1/133583.pdf. The earlier, narrower structured review (90 studies, core cohesion indicators). **Findings (quoted):** "(a) there is consistent support for the constrict claim for aspects of social cohesion that are spatially bounded to neighborhoods, (b) support for the constrict claim is more common in the United States than in other countries, and (c) ethnic diversity is not related to less interethnic social cohesion." Intra-neighborhood indicators: **50% support, only 9% reject**; US support 50% vs **17% other Anglo-Saxon, 26% Europe** ("American exceptionalism"). **The 2014 review’s conclusion (not the newer meta-analysis’s generalized-trust result):** it says the harm "does not spill over to more general forms of social cohesion such as generalized trust, volunteering and informal help." Also flags that the *cross-national* studies finding consistent support "fail to meet our criteria for methodological rigor" — an against-interest admission cutting toward the skeptics on the strongest pro-constrict cross-national results.

   - *Steelman of the constrict claim (Putnam's original "E Pluribus Unum," 2007, not re-saved — landmark, training-data + widely cited):* `[TRAINING-DATA]` Putnam's own data showed diversity correlating with lower trust in neighbors AND lower trust in co-ethnics ("hunkering down"), net of SES, in US communities. The reviews support a neighbor-trust association, but the 2020 meta-analysis also detects a generalized-trust association that earlier counting-based reviews did not. The 2014 review’s quoted conclusions must not be presented as the 2020 result. Neither review alone identifies the historical effect of immigration on national trust trends. [INFERENCE]

3. **Lancee & Dronkers (2011), "Ethnic diversity in neighborhoods and individual trust of immigrants and natives: a replication of Putnam (2007) in a West-European country."** Grade **B** (single-country, cross-sectional). Saved to corpus. A direct Netherlands replication — the kind of outside-US test the meta-analyses aggregate. Useful as a worked example of the "modest, local" pattern in a European setting (where the meta-analytic effect is weaker than the US).

### Assimilation theory (segmented vs neo-assimilation)

4. **Drouhot (2024), "Assimilation Theories in the 21st Century: Appraising Accomplishments and Future Challenges," *International Migration Review*.** Grade **C** (review/theory, but the best current synthesis). `[SOURCE: https://doi.org/10.1177/01979183241274747]` Saved to corpus. A three-pronged appraisal of where the segmented-assimilation (Portes-Zhou) vs new/neo-assimilation (Alba-Nee) debate stands a quarter-century on. **What it does:** surveys whether the "downward assimilation" prediction of segmented theory actually materialized. This is a relevant review lead for evaluating broad downward-assimilation claims; the June metadata-level read did not establish a quantified field-wide consensus or a common outcome/counterfactual across the cited studies. **This is the disconfirmation-relevant synthesis to read in full.**

5. **Kasinitz (2025), "Segmented assimilation, then and now," *Ethnic and Racial Studies*.** Grade **C**. `[SOURCE: https://doi.org/10.1080/01419870.2024.2445085]` Saved to corpus. A reflective re-appraisal by an author of the major NYC second-generation study (ISGMNY). Re-examines whether the 1992 segmented framework held up. Pairs with Drouhot as the "what survived" frontier read.

6. **Haller, Portes & Lynch (2011), "Dreams Fulfilled and Shattered: Determinants of Segmented Assimilation in the Second Generation," *Social Forces*.** Grade **B** (CILS-based, identification limits). OA via EuropePMC PMC3819936. Saved to corpus. The flagship *empirical* test of segmented assimilation using CILS — models how family capital, school context, and academic outcomes drive divergent second-gen paths. **What it tests:** the *predictors* of downward vs upward assimilation (it does find a downwardly-assimilating minority concentrated by specific family/context disadvantage). Note: this is Portes's own team — i.e., the pro-segmented camp; read against Waldinger & Feliciano (below) for the skeptical counter.

   - *Skeptical counter (against-interest pairing):* **Waldinger & Feliciano (2003), "Will the new second generation experience 'downward assimilation'? Segmented assimilation re-assessed," *Ethnic and Racial Studies*** (250 citations, not re-saved — found in search). `[SOURCE: https://doi.org/10.1080/01491987042000189196]` Argues the downward-assimilation prediction is overstated once you correct for the *immigrant generation's* low starting point and selective comparison groups. This is the key disconfirming paper inside the assimilation debate.

### Ethnic enclaves & networks (help vs trap — genuinely two-sided)

7. **Martén, Hainmueller & Hangartner (2019), "Ethnic networks can foster the economic integration of refugees," *PNAS* 116(33):16280.** Grade **A** (quasi-experimental — refugees plausibly-exogenously assigned to Swiss locations). `[SOURCE: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1820345116]` OA. Saved to corpus. **The strongest causal evidence on the "enclave helps" side.** Refugees placed where co-ethnic networks were larger had **better** labor-market integration, directly contradicting the policymaker prior that co-ethnic concentration traps. **What it supports:** Beneficial labor-market effects of co-ethnic networks in this refugee-assignment setting, under its identification assumptions. This challenges an always-harmful hypothesis, but does not settle other enclave types, countries or social outcomes. (Touches labor outcomes, but the mechanism is the *social network* — squarely sociology; the wage-level magnitude belongs to the econ agent.)
   - *Counterweight (the "trap" side):* the segmented-assimilation literature (#4–6) holds that enclaves can entrench *when* the surrounding context is a disadvantaged, racialized reception — i.e., enclave quality and host context decide the sign. Schüller (2016, IZA World of Labor, `[SOURCE: https://doi.org/10.15185/izawol.287]`, OA, not corpus-saved — review) summarizes that enclave effects are **conditional on enclave quality**, sometimes positive, sometimes negative. Net: the honest reading is *conditional*, not "enclaves good" or "enclaves bad."

### Intermarriage & ethnic attrition (the integration index — and a measurement trap)

8. **Duncan & Trejo (2025), "Immigrant Age at Arrival and the Intergenerational Transmission of Ethnic Identification Among Mexican Americans," SSRN.** Grade **B**. `[SOURCE: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5077491]` Saved to corpus. **Critical methodological disconfirmation that this repo should internalize:** "ethnic attrition" — as Mexican Americans assimilate and intermarry, their US-born descendants increasingly **stop identifying as Mexican/Hispanic**. When relatively higher-attaining descendants disproportionately stop using an ethnic self-label, self-ID samples can **understate later-generation progress**. This direction is conditional on selective attrition, not automatic for all assimilation studies. `[INFERENCE]` This threatens comparisons defined by ethnic self-identification when identification is selectively related to outcomes. The direction depends on who stops identifying; it is not automatic for every group. CPS parental birthplace can classify the second generation without current ethnic self-ID, but is itself reported and does not identify all third-generation origin groups. Ordinary recent ACS records lack parental birthplace. [INFERENCE]
   - *Supporting:* **Lichter, Qian & Tumin (2015), "Whom Do Immigrants Marry?," *ANNALS***. `[SOURCE: https://doi.org/10.1177/0002716215594614]` (81 cites, not corpus-saved). Documents rising immigrant-native intermarriage as an integration signal but with strong variation by national origin and skin-tone/racialization — intermarriage-as-assimilation-index is real but *stratified by race*. `[FRAMING-SENSITIVE]`

### Residential segregation / spatial assimilation (the social-stratification reading)

9. **Denton & Massey (1988), "Residential Segregation of Blacks, Hispanics, and Asians by Socioeconomic Status and Generation," *Social Science Quarterly*** (247 cites — landmark anchor, not re-saved). `[SOURCE: https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/039cc40b9f96ef6f347b85846ff0eac2e7661320]` The foundational spatial-assimilation finding: rising SES and later generation reduce segregation for Hispanics and Asians, but **much less so for Black Hispanics** — segregation is racialized, not just immigrant-transient. **Scope:** Group/SES/generation associations in the studied setting motivate competing spatial-assimilation and stratification mechanisms. They do not by themselves establish individual residential mobility or identify racialization as the sole cause. [INFERENCE]

10. **Xiong & Pfeifer (2023), "Complicating 'Suburbanization' and Spatial Assimilation… Southeast Asian Americans, Minneapolis-St. Paul 1990–2010," *Urban Science*.** Grade **B**, OA. Saved to corpus. A recent disconfirmation of the *simple* spatial-assimilation model: Southeast Asian groups suburbanized **without** achieving racial integration — moving to the suburbs did not equal joining majority-white neighborhoods. **What it disconfirms:** the assumption that residential mobility == assimilation; place-stratification can persist through suburbanization.

## (b) Dataset cards (NEW datasets — not in this repo's register)

Format: Source / Acquired-or-gated / Official URL / Key variables / Known quirks / what-it-settles.

### CILS — Children of Immigrants Longitudinal Study (ICPSR 20520)
- **Source:** Portes & Rumbaut; distributed via ICPSR/DSDR.
- **Acquired-or-gated:** **PUBLIC-USE, pullable now.** `[SOURCE: https://www.icpsr.umich.edu/web/DSDR/studies/20520]` — "public-use data files in this collection are available for access by the general public. Access does not require affiliation with an ICPSR member institution." (Free ICPSR account, click-through.)
- **Official URL:** https://www.icpsr.umich.edu/web/DSDR/studies/20520 ; project + codebooks: https://cmd.princeton.edu/publications/data-archives/cils
- **Key variables:** 3 waves (1992 baseline 8th/9th grade N=5,262 from 77 nationalities; T2 ~1995; T3 ~2001-03 at mean age 24, N=3,613, 69% retention). Variables: parental nativity & year of arrival, language use & loss, **self-identity / ethnic self-identification over time**, educational attainment, employment, civil status, civic participation. Sites: Miami/Ft. Lauderdale + San Diego (Cubans, Haitians, Nicaraguans, West Indians, Mexicans, Filipinos, Vietnamese, Laotians, Cambodians).
- **Known quirks:** Two metro areas only (not nationally representative). Documented sampling bias in the 2nd follow-up requiring statistical correction (see Portes & Rumbaut 2005). Cohort is now ~30 yrs old — speaks to *1990s* second-gen, not post-2010 arrivals.
- **What it can study:** **A major longitudinal test of segmented assimilation** — directly measures who assimilates downward vs upward and the predictors (this IS the data behind Haller-Portes-Lynch #6). Describes observed trajectories and predictors in its sampled cohort; attrition and nonrandom exposures limit causal claims.

### NIS — New Immigrant Survey (ICPSR 38031 R1, 38061 R2)
- **Source:** Jasso (NYU), Massey (Princeton OPR), Rosenzweig (Yale), Smith (RAND).
- **Acquired-or-gated:** **TIERED.** Public-use files (demographics, most individual characteristics after top-coding/collapsing) are **pullable now** (added 2024-25 to ICPSR, no member affiliation needed). **Restricted Version 1** (unmodified country-of-origin, education, health, occupation) = Restricted Data Use Agreement. **Restricted Version 2** (state/city/zip geo-identifiers + INS class-of-admission / visa category / country of chargeability) = **secure enclave only.** `[SOURCE: https://nis.princeton.edu/data_restricted.html]`
- **Official URL:** R1 https://www.icpsr.umich.edu/web/DSDR/studies/38031 · R2 https://www.icpsr.umich.edu/web/DSDR/studies/38061 · project https://nis.princeton.edu/
- **Key variables:** Nationally representative sample of *new legal permanent residents* (INS administrative-record frame) + their children, longitudinal. Visa/admission class, country of origin, religion, health, employment, language, **and a child sample** — the only nationally representative new-LPR panel.
- **Known quirks:** Frame is **legal** immigrants admitted to permanent residence — excludes the undocumented entirely (a major scope limit for any analysis where unauthorized status matters). The variables that best settle origin/selection questions (COA, visa, geo) are the *restricted* ones.
- **What it can study:** Integration trajectories and associations with selection/admission class in its sampled new-LPR cohort. Representativeness does not make visa-class differences causal; detailed comparisons require the relevant access tier and selection assumptions.

### Add Health (Wave I–VI; UNC CPC)
- **Source:** Carolina Population Center (Harris et al.).
- **Acquired-or-gated:** **TIERED.** Public-use (subset: Wave I N=6,504 … Wave VI N=3,937) **pullable now** with a confidentiality pledge. Restricted-use (full sample + **neighborhood/contextual data, in-school friendship-nomination NETWORK data, spatial-analysis, geocodes**) = **contractual agreement, IRB, security plan, 3-yr contract, fee; no external data linkage permitted.** `[SOURCE: https://addhealth.cpc.unc.edu/restricted-use-vs-public-use-data-files/]`
- **Official URL:** https://addhealth.cpc.unc.edu/data/
- **Key variables / immigrant content:** Generation breakdown in the full sample: **1st gen 8.34% (N=1,707), 2nd gen 14.59% (N=2,987), 3rd+ 77.07% (N=15,774)** `[SOURCE: addhealth.cpc.unc.edu/.../Overview_of_Add_Health_Harris.pdf]`. Adds longitudinal *network* structure (friendship nominations) + health + later-life SES across Waves I–VI (adolescence→~40s).
- **Known quirks:** The enclave/segregation-relevant variables (network nominations, neighborhood/contextual, geocodes) are **all restricted-use** — public-use cannot settle enclave or peer-network questions. School-based sampling frame (started as adolescents in 1994-95).
- **What it can study:** Associations and longitudinal trajectories involving co-ethnic peers and second-generation attainment/health. Network selection and shared context require an identification strategy before attributing effects to peers; detailed network variables require appropriate access.

### GSS trust items by nativity (NORC)
- **Source:** General Social Survey, NORC/U.Chicago.
- **Acquired-or-gated:** **FULLY PUBLIC, pullable now** (GSS Data Explorer / direct download; no agreement).
- **Official URL:** https://gss.norc.org/ (Data Explorer: gssdataexplorer.norc.org)
- **Key variables:** Core trust battery — **TRUST** ("most people can be trusted"), **FAIR**, **HELPFUL** — crossed with **BORN** (respondent born in US?) and **PARBORN** (parents foreign-born — enables 1st/2nd/3rd-gen split), plus **ETHNIC**/region. Repeated cross-sections 1972→present.
- **Known quirks:** Trust items not on every wave; immigrant subsample small in any single year (pool waves). `BORN`/`PARBORN` give nativity/generation but **not country of origin** at public-use granularity in most years.
- **What it can study:** Repeated cross-sectional trust differences by available nativity/generation. These do not follow the same people or isolate assimilation from age, period, cohort and selective migration. Country-of-origin and parental-birthplace coverage must be checked wave by wave. [INFERENCE]

### Census/ACS segregation tabulations (dissimilarity / isolation indices)
- **Source:** US Census Bureau / derived (e.g., Brown University Diversity & Disparities, or self-computed from tract tables).
- **Acquired-or-gated:** **PUBLIC** (ACS tract-level via API; precomputed indices via Brown's "Diversity and Disparities" project).
- **Official URL:** ACS API https://www.census.gov/data/developers/data-sets/acs-5year.html ; precomputed: s4.ad.brown.edu/projects/diversity
- **Key variables:** Tract-level race/ethnicity/nativity counts → **Index of Dissimilarity (D)**, **Isolation Index**, exposure indices, by group and metro, over time.
- **Known quirks:** Dissimilarity is sensitive to tract definitions and the reference group; "foreign-born" tabulations differ from race tabulations. ACS margins of error at tract level are large.
- **What it can study:** Descriptive segregation trends for the tabulated groups. Tract boundaries, reference populations and sampling error matter. Nativity/race tables do not by themselves identify immigrant generation or within-person mobility; recent ACS lacks parental birthplace. [INFERENCE]

## (c) Genuinely NEW vs already-covered

**Already covered (do NOT re-do):** Abramitzky-Boustan intergenerational *economic* mobility (AER 2021); cluster-V cultural-transmission/deep-roots; the built first-generation synthetic-cohort income/employment profile (descriptive, not identified within-person assimilation); the pending CPS 2nd-gen-by-origin test. The repo's INDEX shows CILS / Add Health / NLSY97 already listed as *dataset-roadmap rows* (batch-4 US) — i.e., named but not yet researched or pulled.

**Genuinely NEW from this scout:**
1. **Trust/cohesion supplies a distinct evidence axis.** The 2020 pooled association is modest and stronger locally, but is also negative for generalized trust. The 2014 structured review and 2020 effect-size meta-analysis are different syntheses; the latter explicitly detects a generalized-trust association missed by the earlier counting approach. Current repository coverage was not re-counted in this repair. [SOURCE: Dinesen et al., Figure 2; INFERENCE]
2. **Ethnic attrition is a conditional measurement threat.** It can bias self-ID comparisons when identification is selective on outcomes. CPS parental birthplace already avoids that particular second-generation self-ID rule; it does not solve third-generation ancestry identification. The direction of selection must be established for the group, not assumed. [SOURCE: Duncan & Trejo; INFERENCE]
3. **Enclave evidence depends on setting and outcome.** Martén-Hainmueller-Hangartner supplies a quasi-experimental refugee labor-market result; the broader review supplies conditional hypotheses and evidence of varying quality. This is not two equally identified causal results proving that every sign is determined by enclave quality alone. [SOURCE: studies above; INFERENCE]
4. **The segmented/neo-assimilation reviews remain useful leads.** The June abstract/metadata read did not establish a quantitative field-wide consensus across outcomes. Longitudinal cohort evidence, repeated cross-sections and theory reviews answer different questions. [INFERENCE]
5. **Segregation evidence motivates competing mechanisms.** The cited group differences and suburbanization patterns do not identify racialization as the sole cause of persistence, nor individual mobility from aggregate trends. [SOURCE: studies above; INFERENCE]
6. **Five dataset leads:** CILS, NIS, Add Health, GSS and ACS can measure different pieces of integration. Access details require current verification. GSS repeated cross-sections can compare observed trust by nativity/generation, but cannot by themselves identify within-person convergence. [SOURCE: dataset cards above; INFERENCE]

## (d) Disconfirmation section

Competing hypotheses are tested against source evidence; ideological surprise alone does not increase an estimate’s identification strength.

- **Against an unqualified large-damage claim:** Dinesen et al. report a modest pooled partial association, not a measured large historical causal effect. It includes generalized trust and is stronger for neighbors. The earlier van der Meer-Tolsma review says "ethnic diversity is not related to less interethnic social cohesion" and that some cross-national studies "fail to meet our criteria for methodological rigor." Those narrower statements do not prove that contact raises trust, and the earlier generalized-trust conclusion cannot override the 2020 Figure 2 result. [SOURCE: both reviews; INFERENCE]
- **Against the strong "enclaves trap immigrants" claim:** Martén et al. (PNAS, quasi-experimental) find co-ethnic networks **improve** refugee integration. The trap story is conditional, not general.
- **Against universal downward assimilation:** The reviews and cohort studies provide counterexamples and competing interpretations, but this bounded read does not quantify the prevalence of decline across all populations and outcomes. Neither low parental starting points nor sample selection automatically explains every second-generation gap. [SOURCE: review/cohort leads above; INFERENCE]
- **Against an unqualified no-association claim:** The 2020 meta-analysis detects negative associations, including generalized trust, after the measured conditioning sets. That rejects a descriptive null in the pooled specifications; it does not rule out unmeasured confounding or identify a causal effect of a given immigration policy. [SOURCE: Dinesen et al.; INFERENCE]
- **Against careless generational measurement:** Ethnic self-ID can be selective on outcomes. The sign and size require evidence about who exits the label. Parental birthplace supports a different second-generation classification but does not solve all later-generation ancestry or selection problems. [SOURCE: Duncan & Trejo; INFERENCE]
- **`[FRAMING-SENSITIVE]` residue:** Practical magnitude requires the trust scale, a relevant exposure contrast and a causal interpretation before welfare weights are applied. Labor entry, language acquisition and intermarriage are separate outcomes; different signs across them need not be inconsistent. [INFERENCE]
