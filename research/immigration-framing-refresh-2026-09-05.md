# Immigration: what the recent evidence changes

**Assessment:** The useful question is how admission rules, legal rights and local adjustment affect particular people over time. Treating immigration as one interchangeable population shock misses mechanisms that the recent evidence makes visible. The earlier audit also found mistakes in our own rebuttals: an adverse population average can coexist with incumbent gains, while a positive average can conceal losers. [INFERENCE; sources below and [repair report](immigration-material-repair-report-2026-09-05.md)]

**Scope:** June 5–September 5, 2026 papers, revisions, news, essays and a selective X sample. The frame here is incumbent outcomes, entrant outcomes and public budgets kept separately; choosing welfare weights is a judgment, not a statistical result. This is LLM-assisted source selection, with favorable and unfavorable explanations considered explicitly. Full source and access records are in the [paper review](immigration-recent-papers-2026-09-05.md), [narrative review](immigration-recent-narratives-2026-09-05.md) and [dataset refresh](immigration-dataset-proxy-refresh-2026-09-05.md).

## The angles that matter

| Mechanism | What the evidence changes | Measurement that would distinguish explanations |
|---|---|---|
| **Work rights, bargaining and formality** | Immigration quantity alone is incomplete. The July Italy study finds consequences of the EU enlargement's change in legal access; employment shares and average wages also change with composition. It cannot isolate existing-resident legalization from new arrivals. | Incumbent workers' earnings/hours, formal versus informal employment, employer changes and entry. Compare admission quantity and work authorization as separate treatments. [NBER 35493](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35493) |
| **Adjustment over time and across industries** | The new historical H-1B study finds short-run losses for some native STEM workers alongside later gains in other occupations and downstream industries. This cuts against both fixed-job-count reasoning and “everyone wins.” | Incumbent cohorts, firms and linked industries followed through time; wages, employment levels, relocation and entry separately. Historical high-skill estimates cannot supply an all-skill, unlimited-inflow multiplier. [NBER 35560](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35560) |
| **Housing demand and housing supply together** | Immigration can raise demand while immigrant construction labor expands supply. A constrained supply curve amplifies a demand shock; zoning and migration are not mutually exclusive causes. Rising prices help some owners and hurt some renters/buyers. | Permits → completions → occupied units, rents paid, construction employment, crowding and resident moves in the same geography. Newly procured BPS measures only the first step. [housing review and source limits](immigration-recent-papers-2026-09-05.md), [current claims](immigration-recent-narratives-2026-09-05.md) |
| **Enforcement changes presence, work and production differently** | Detention, removal, staying home, firm disruption and population decline are different events. The revised ICE study's outcome is employed-and-at-work for a selected observed CPS sample; its coefficients are area averages. | Link enforcement timing to absence, employment, firm output and resident movement; distinguish releases from removals and events from unique people. The acquired June detention workbook reconciles 34,551 exits with 27,679 removal exits. [NBER 35129](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35129), [raw-anchor check](immigration-dataset-proxy-refresh-2026-09-05.md) |
| **Incumbent victimization** | A lower overall crime rate does not logically guarantee a lower victimization rate for existing residents. The offense/victim matrix matters; neither a toy counterexample nor an aggregate trend estimates it. | Compatible victim and offender populations, offenses, geography, reporting and denominators. Current national crime totals and NCVS alone do not identify unauthorized offending against natives. [crime review](immigration-crime-rates-unauthorized-vs-native-born.md), [new claim and arithmetic](immigration-recent-narratives-2026-09-05.md) |
| **Demographics and fiscal incidence** | A falling employment count can reflect population or participation changes; a rising transfer bill can reflect aging, prices or program mix. Neither automatically measures immigrant substitution or immigrant benefit receipt. Public-goods allocation can change a fiscal accounting sign without changing a causal marginal cost. | Age-standardized employment/population and labor-force outcomes; consistent population controls; tax and spending ledgers with beneficiary attribution. BLS and BEA supply components, not legal status or a net fiscal balance. [data checks](immigration-dataset-proxy-refresh-2026-09-05.md), [ESRI review](immigration-recent-papers-2026-09-05.md) |
| **Selection, institutions and policy durability** | Skilled visas, family rights, employer sponsorship, border processing and removal policy are different bundles. Recent discourse raises bargaining, redistribution, legitimacy and reversibility; an average GDP estimate does not answer them. | Explicit institutional outcomes and counterfactual policy comparisons. Current posts establish that these claims were made, not their causal magnitudes. [dated narrative review](immigration-recent-narratives-2026-09-05.md) |

## What the new data actually say

The newly accessible August neighborhood crime paper reports negative conditional associations for property crime and aggravated assault, a positive association for robbery, and an inconclusive association for its combined violence measure. That combined measure excludes rape. Its two-wave first-difference design controls for changing tract characteristics but does not identify individual offender/victim status or eliminate changing confounding. The offense differences matter; neither a blanket safety claim nor a blanket criminality claim follows. [SOURCE: [publisher article](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/07352166.2026.2699123), [primary-text assessment and table-access limit](immigration-recent-papers-2026-09-05.md)]

The BLS same-month June–August comparisons show employment counts lower in **both** native and foreign-born groups in 2026 than in 2025. Native employment/population also falls. This is inconsistent with a literal account of those months as rising immigrant headcounts replacing falling native headcounts, but it neither identifies an immigration effect nor proves native outcomes were unaffected. The series are national 16+, unadjusted for age, not seasonally adjusted, and cross revised population controls. Declining foreign-born survey population is not a count of deportations. [DATA: [BLS table and qualifications](immigration-dataset-proxy-refresh-2026-09-05.md)]

The BEA hierarchy check reconciles 780 component identities across 60 geographies. Refundable-credit memorandum lines and other nested categories cannot simply be added again. That supports fiscal arithmetic, not immigrant attribution: retirees and Medicare recipients can be immigrants too. The new building-permit vintages measure authorizations, not homes delivered; the ICE July observation is incomplete. These limitations determine which claims the data can test. [DATA; INFERENCE: [dataset refresh](immigration-dataset-proxy-refresh-2026-09-05.md)]

## What survives and what remains unresolved

The strongest favorable account combines productive matching, investment, entrepreneurship and long-run adjustment. Recent skilled-migration and legal-access evidence supports parts of that account. The strongest adverse account concerns particular substitute workers, slow local capacity adjustment, distributional losses and political or institutional costs. Those mechanisms are not refuted by national averages. Conversely, local costs do not establish a negative national or global total. [INFERENCE; primary studies and definitions above]

The most useful new synthesis is **joint effects with different incidence and timing**. This is an interpretation of the reviewed evidence, not a change to the project's central question or causal tree. The remaining hard gaps are a complete counterfactual fiscal ledger, design-valid origin/status-specific causal effects, and direct incumbent victimization. Inaccessible coefficient tables, unreplicated studies and paid previews do not fill them. [INFERENCE; GAP]
