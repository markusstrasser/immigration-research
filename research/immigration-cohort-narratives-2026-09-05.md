**Verdict:** The new sample supplies a definite unit error and several material inference errors, not an estimate of immigration's overall effects. A Somalia **crime index of 63.9** is mislabeled as crimes per 100,000. Real Minnesota fraud allegations and convictions are generalized into claims about the refugee program or recent immigration without a population denominator, arrival cohort or counterfactual. The converse error also appears: a favorable anecdote or national average cannot establish that particular local problems are negligible. [SOURCE: primary links below; INFERENCE: population and causal limits]

# Recent-cohort narratives: what the claims actually measure

Date: 2026-09-05. Scope: a bounded official X API sample, requested for the preceding 90 days, followed by primary-source checks of five salient claims. This supplements [the recent narrative scan](immigration-recent-narratives-2026-09-05.md) and the parent's [cohort analysis](immigration-cohort-clarity-2026-09-05.md). It is a claim audit, not a representative survey of public opinion.

## The population mismatch comes first

The parent's fresh analysis of official 2024 ACS person microdata estimates **7,059,716 foreign-born residents of all ages reporting U.S. entry in 2021–2024**. Of them, **7,327 were born in Somalia**, or **0.1038%**. These estimates use 52,236 and 30 sampled people respectively. The small Somalia sample makes detailed subgroup comparisons imprecise; its recent adult sample is only 22 people. [SOURCE: `.scratch/cohort-clarity-20260905/analysis/profiles_2024.csv`, rows `foreign_born/entry_0_3` and `somalia_born/entry_0_3`; source hashes in this lane's manifest]

For civilian noninstitutionalized adults aged 25–64 in that recent foreign-born cohort, the estimates are **45.83% bachelor's degree or higher**, **18.74% less than high school**, and **68.79% employed**, with a weighted population of 4,172,880 and unweighted n=30,382. These are separate descriptive margins, not a joint classification or a fiscal balance sheet. They neither establish that every subgroup performs similarly nor identify immigration's effect on other residents. [SOURCE: same extract, `civilian_noninstitutional_25_64`]

This is the surviving resident population observed in 2024, not gross admissions during those years. Birthplace is not Somali ethnicity, religion, admission category or legal status; it excludes U.S.-born descendants. A Minnesota case, a refugee award date and a crime committed in 2021 do not identify an arrival in 2021. National share also cannot resolve Minnesota's concentration or local fiscal exposure. Using selected Somali cases as a profile of the entire recent U.S. cohort is therefore unsupported; using their small national share to dismiss local losses is equally unsupported. [INFERENCE: survey universe and aggregation]

## Five consequential claims

### 1. An index became a crime rate: confirmed numerical error

On September 4, [RupertPitt1's post](https://x.com/RupertPitt1/status/2095718522437452095) describes Somalia as having “a crime rate of 63.9 per 100,000 of the population.” Another returned post repeats the wording.

The matching primary table is Numbeo's **2025 Crime Index**, where Somalia is **63.9** and its safety index is 36.1. Numbeo says its crime measure comes from website visitors' survey responses, scaled to **0–100**. It is not recorded offenses divided by population. There is no valid multiplication or division that converts this index into crimes per 100,000. [SOURCE: [2025 country table](https://www.numbeo.com/crime/rankings_by_country.jsp?region=002&title=2025), [Numbeo methodology](https://www.numbeo.com/crime/indices_explained.jsp); 2025 benchmark, methodology accessed 2026-09-05]

The same post gives a UK Somali-national conviction figure of 1,097 per 10,000 for 2021–2023. **[GAP] The underlying official numerator and denominator were not located in this bounded pass; the figure is unverified here.** Even if the numerical ratio were reproduced, determining the share of people convicted would require unique persons rather than conviction events, a matching population universe, and consistent years. Neither that UK measure nor a Somalia perception index estimates offending by recent U.S. arrivals. [INFERENCE]

### 2. One defendant becomes an entire fraudulent refugee program

On August 18, [Mark Krikorian](https://x.com/MarkSKrikorian/status/2089806064283685146) links an award recipient's alleged Medicaid fraud to the claim that “the refugee program is one big fraud.”

The Minnesota attorney general's **August 17, 2026** release confirms that **Salman Ahmed Elmi was charged with eight felony theft offenses involving over $1 million** in allegedly ineligible or unprovided Medicaid-funded services. It describes operation of Reva Health during **2024–2026**, alleges billing and supervision abuses, and distinguishes another, independent case in the same release. These are charges, not a conviction established by this source. The release also distinguishes alleged co-conspirators' separate criminal enterprises; it does not justify assigning every associated allegation to Elmi. [SOURCE: [Minnesota attorney general](https://ag.state.mn.us/Office/Communications/2026/08/17_Medicaid-Fraud.asp)]

**The case is substantial evidence of an alleged provider-control failure, warranting investigation. It supplies no estimate of program-wide fraud prevalence or refugee admission fraud.** Medicaid provider fraud and misrepresentation in a refugee application are different outcomes. To estimate the former by admission group, one would need comparable providers or beneficiaries, exposure denominators, detected and undetected fraud, and enforcement intensity. The individual's admission date and award documentation were not independently established by the primary case release read here; no recent-arrival classification is made. [INFERENCE; GAP: individual admission and award record]

This does not make the losses hypothetical. Separately, DOJ's **August 27, 2026** announcement reports three defendants sentenced to a combined **155 months** in the **$250 million Feeding Our Future scheme**, involving a different child-nutrition program. Verified wrongdoing can be serious without identifying the net effect of an entire immigration program. [SOURCE: [DOJ sentencing release](https://www.justice.gov/usao-mn/pr/three-defendants-feeding-our-future-fraud-scheme-sentenced-total-155-months-imprisonment)]

### 3. Repeated billion-dollar headlines do not establish a common loss total

Three September 5 probe posts use nearly identical wording about an alleged **$1 billion Somali fraud ring** and SBA investigations. Their IDs are `2096166582242198015`, `2096166423953285460` and `2096158845101809873`. Repetition is evidence of circulation, not three independent confirmations. [SOURCE: exact API rows in `cohort-probe.jsonl`]

SBA's **2025 annual report**, an older background source, reports suspending **6,900 borrowers tied to 7,900 loans totaling $400 million** in Minnesota and making referrals for prosecution and recovery. It describes pandemic PPP/EIDL lending and expansion of reviews nationally. This is a different program and measurement from Feeding Our Future, Medicaid charges, annual welfare expenditure or a cohort's lifetime net fiscal contribution. [SOURCE: [SBA 2025 annual report](https://www.sba.gov/files/annual-report-2025), p.21; report year 2025, outside the post sample's 90-day window]

**[GAP] A primary source establishing the new $1 billion figure and its precise scope was not found.** The older $400 million figure does not falsify a later or broader $1 billion finding. It does show why adding news totals without reconciling programs, dates, overlapping cases, alleged versus adjudicated losses and recoveries would be invalid. The SBA report does not establish that every affected borrower is Somali or that total loan face value is final net taxpayer loss. [INFERENCE]

### 4. Benefit receipt alone cannot establish a negative fiscal balance

A [September 2 post by memeticsisyphus](https://x.com/memeticsisyphus/status/2095123220869345580) associates assistance to new arrivals with an adverse net assessment. The returned text is truncated, so this memo evaluates only the visible inference and does not infer its omitted qualifications. [SOURCE: `cohort-post2020.jsonl`]

For a specified cohort, government level and horizon, an accounting balance requires **taxes and other receipts minus attributed expenditure**; changing the time horizon, public-good allocation or counterfactual changes that balance. An observed benefit payment is one expenditure component. It does not supply taxes, other spending, descendants' treatment, migration exits or a causal comparison. Fraud losses likewise cannot be added to benefits without checking overlap. [INFERENCE: accounting identity]

The converse matters: employment and education in the ACS are not measured taxes, eligibility or net fiscal contribution. The new cohort's 68.79% employment rate cannot erase verified fraud or prove a positive fiscal balance. Determining refugee or recent-arrival fiscal incidence requires the appropriately defined fiscal analysis, not nationality anecdotes or an earnings/education proxy. [SOURCE: ACS extract above; INFERENCE]

### 5. TPS, asylum and refugee admission are different classifications

On June 25, [David Bier](https://x.com/David_J_Bier/status/2070276585186357536) objects that an argument about crime and asylum is actually discussing TPS. The distinction is real. USCIS's **December 2, 2022** information sheet separately explains temporary protected status, parole and asylum; asylum concerns persecution or a qualifying fear tied to specified grounds. Its TPS discussion concerns a temporary protection for eligible people already present, under designated conditions and dates. These categories cannot be inferred from the word “migrant.” [SOURCE: [USCIS information sheet](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/fact-sheets/Information-Sheet-for-Afghans-Paroled-into-the-United%20States.pdf), pp.7–8; historical definitional background]

This source is **not used as current country-specific eligibility guidance**. The current general USCIS TPS and asylum pages returned access errors in this pass. Nor did the API retrieve the full antecedent argument: the memo validates the categorical distinction, not Bier's attribution of knowledge or intent to the other speaker. [GAP: complete thread context and current country-specific rules]

## Competing explanations considered

Before selecting claims, the sample was inspected across six mechanisms: provider oversight and fraud incentives; benefit eligibility and tax incidence; labor-market integration and selection; admission/status rules; crime reporting and prosecution denominators; and media selection or political feedback. These are candidate explanations, not effects estimated by this sample. [INFERENCE: research design]

The strongest restrictionist concern survives: a receiver can bear concentrated, substantial losses and weak program controls even when national averages are favorable. The strongest counterargument also survives: a selected defendant or alarming country statistic cannot characterize millions of recent residents.

Counterexamples were assessed with the same standard. A September 4 [post celebrating a Somali person for stopping a shooter](https://x.com/Kanoxti/status/2095849135714799914) does not establish a favorable population crime effect; the incident was not independently verified here. An August 30 [Nowrasteh post](https://x.com/AlexNowrasteh/status/2094042029906952438) favoring welfare retrenchment if immigration induced it is a conditional normative position, not proof that Minnesota ended welfare or that immigration caused that outcome. [SOURCE: API text; INFERENCE; FRAMING-SENSITIVE: welfare-policy valuation]

Posts about armed conflict in Somalia do not measure terrorism by U.S. refugees. Somali birthplace, Somali ancestry, Muslim identity, asylum status and extremist conduct are neither equivalent categories nor mutually identifying variables. An assertion that integration requires religious abandonment is a normative definition, not a measured employment or crime outcome. **This sample yields no validated U.S. cohort-specific extremism rate; that is an evidence gap, not evidence of a zero rate.** [INFERENCE; GAP]
