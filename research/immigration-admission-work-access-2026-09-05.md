Approvals divided by same-quarter receipts are not a filing-cohort approval rate; pending age is not completed processing time. The filing queue omits waiting before eligibility and people who never filed, so it cannot measure the full arrival-to-work interval. Applications are not unique people, and a pending document does not always mean absent work rights. [8 CFR 274a.12(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-274a/subpart-B/section-274a.12) distinguishes authorization incident to status, including refugees, from categories requiring a discretionary application. An EAD is also not employment. [SOURCE: regulation and filing definitions; INFERENCE: proxy boundaries.]

The [February 23, 2026 asylum-work notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-02-23/pdf/2026-03595.pdf) is a **proposed rule**; its proposed 365-day filing wait and intake pause are not evidence of implementation. This source check is not an assertion that no later rule exists. [Marbach, Hainmueller and Hangartner (2018)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6155022/) exploit a German court-triggered change: former-Yugoslav cohorts exposed to approximately seven additional waiting months had about a 20-point employment gap five years later, which later closed. Unaffected Turkish cohorts serve as a placebo. This supports a plausible mechanism; historical German effects are not estimates for 2022–2025 U.S. cohorts.

## Unauthorized residents: missing people versus unobserved status

The [Census definition, revised April 2, 2026](https://www.census.gov/topics/population/foreign-born/about.html), includes unauthorized migrants among foreign-born survey participants and population estimates. ACS/CPS public records do not identify their present legal status. These are different limitations: **status unobserved among included people** and **residents absent from coverage**. SIPP's limited first-entry field does not supply current status either. Adding the entire unauthorized stock to a survey population would double-count included residents. [SOURCE: Census definition and SIPP dictionary; INFERENCE: consequence.]

The [DHS April 2024 report](https://ohss.dhs.gov/sites/default/files/2024-06/2024_0418_ohss_estimates-of-the-unauthorized-immigrant-population-residing-in-the-united-states-january-2018%25E2%2580%2593january-2022.pdf) estimates **11.0 million unauthorized residents on January 1, 2022**, using adjusted ACS foreign-born counts minus modeled lawful residents. Printed p11 specifies **assumptions**, not measured current coverage:

| Population in that residual model | Assumed ACS undercount |
|---|---:|
| Unauthorized, most recent arrival year | 13%; declines by 7.5% for each additional year present |
| Nonimmigrants | 10% |
| LPRs, refugees, asylees | 2.5% |

The unauthorized adjustment across all tenures is **390,000**, not 13% of the entire unauthorized stock. DHS explicitly says exact undercount is unknown; lawful-stock estimates also depend on emigration and mortality models. The duration schedule comes from older report methods, not calibration to 2024/2026 recent arrivals. [SOURCE: report pp8, 11–13.]

[INFERENCE] Relative duration decay translates to `u(d)=0.13×0.925^d`, with `d=0` in the most recent arrival year; it is not a 7.5-percentage-point annual subtraction. This schedule is not an empirical prior for all foreign-born people, Somali-origin people, or civilian noninstitutional adults aged 25–64. Denominator and vintage restrictions must accompany any sensitivity parameter.

[Pew's August 2025 methodology](https://www.pewresearch.org/2025/08/21/unauthorized-immigrants-methodology-a-unauthorized-immigrant-estimates/) underlies its **14 million estimate for July 2023**. It combines a residual lawful-population model, revised population weights and coverage adjustments, then assigns likely status to survey records using individual/family characteristics. That is modeled classification, not observed status. Its reference date and revisions differ from DHS's January 2022 estimate; the difference is not a direct count of missed people. No universal current undercount percentage was established here. [SOURCE: Pew methodology.]

[INFERENCE] Where occupation or program eligibility informs status assignment, employment/benefit comparisons can partly inherit classification assumptions. An imputed-status result needs sensitivity to the assignment model, not just survey replicate-weight standard errors. Underreported earnings/benefits among respondents require separate parameters from missing residents.

[CBO's January 2026 outlook](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61994), Appendix B, uses policies through September 30, 2025 and partially projected 2025 data. Its **other-foreign-national (OFN)** category includes unlawful entrants, overstayers and lawful parolees; category exits include status acquisition. CBO uplifted modeled 2025 observed gotaways **43,000 by 15% to about 50,000**, drawing on a Border Patrol chief's 10–20% range. This adjusts a border-entry estimate, not survey coverage. It applied **54% persistence** to 482,954 potential FY2024 overstayers, using a 2016 cohort's status after 20 months, yielding about 260,000. OFN voluntary-emigration assumptions were 6% in year one, another 3% in year two, 3% in year three and 2% in subsequent years. These are model assumptions, not observed 2025 person histories or probability bounds. [SOURCE: Appendix B.]

[INFERENCE: sensitivity design] Enforcement encounters, repeat attempts, releases, departures, deaths and status transitions prevent a direct encounter-to-net-resident conversion. Release does not establish continuing residence. Residual stocks and entry/exit models constrain different parts of the accounting identity; they are not independent samples to average.

For missing share `u` of the **true target population**, arithmetic gives:

`N_true = N_observed / (1-u)`; `N_missing / N_observed = u/(1-u)`.

`mean_true = (1-u) × mean_observed + u × mean_missing`.

These identities do not determine coverage or missing residents' outcomes. Stress tests must vary both coverage and outcome selection, specifying the target age/residency/arrival group. Missing workers with unreported earnings and missing nonworkers are opposite disconfirming scenarios; neither follows from unauthorized status. Population-control revisions may already correct some survey coverage, so additional uplifts need an explicit residual-coverage interpretation. Unknown unknowns receive transparent stress bounds and tipping points, **not invented probabilistic priors or confidence intervals**. [INFERENCE: accounting and identification limits.]
