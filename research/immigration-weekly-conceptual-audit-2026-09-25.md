**Verdict:** The week's accounting became more complete, but several new interpretations outrun what their models can establish. The most consequential problems are a misconstructed ancestry instrument, a winner count whose precision depends on unvalidated incidence assumptions, and a school-cost calculation that treats an expenditure residual as lost educational output. There are also clear population, comparison-group and eligibility errors. None of the checks below establishes a replacement for the adopted **$201–246bn annual net-cost scenario**; several disputed terms are already excluded from that total. ## 1. The new ancestry instrument retains the local pull it needs to exclude

**Confirmed construction/interpretation problem; repair before causal reuse.** The [September 22 lane](../infra/immigration-fiscal/ancestry_instrument_2026_09_22/RESULT.md) calls its second instrument stronger and cleaner, reports a first-stage F of 63.9, and interprets the negative SSI response as better evidence against native displacement into transfers. But [`predicted_inflow()`](../infra/immigration-fiscal/ancestry_instrument_2026_09_22/second_instrument.py) simply sums the county–origin file's contemporaneous `PushPull_10` interactions and divides by baseline population. It does not fit the historical ancestry model. [DATA/CODE]

The authors' [data description](https://www.immigrationshock.com/ancestry-instruments) identifies these as inputs to an ancestry first stage. In their [2019 paper, equations 2–5](https://tchaney.github.io/files/Migrants.pdf), interactions enter a country–county model with origin and destination effects. Their [2026 immigration design](https://tchaney.github.io/files/ImmigrationInnovationandGrowth.pdf) first predicts historical ancestry, then combines lagged predicted ancestry with subsequent origin inflows. Summing the raw contemporary interactions does not reproduce that design. The source also supplies a distinct [immigration-shocks product](https://www.immigrationshock.com/immigration-shocks). [SOURCE]

The economic problem is simple. A local boom attracts several immigrant origins, raises housing demand and reduces benefit receipt. A destination's inflow from *other* origins can still carry that boom. Leaving out one's own origin or continent removes a mechanical own-origin component; it does not remove common local demand. The lane can therefore obtain a strong first stage and its reported outcome signs without identifying the effects of immigration. A large F establishes relevance, not exclusion. A nonsignificant baseline-level correlation, or disagreement between two instruments, does not establish which instrument is valid. [INFERENCE]

There is a separate outcome error: the lane admits its 2000 endpoint lacks a native split but concludes about **native** transfer take-up. Adding lower-receipt immigrants can reduce an all-household rate even while native receipt rises. The older displacement memo already recognizes this composition problem; the new headline fails to preserve it. [DATA: second-instrument RESULT, opening and limitations; [displacement memo](immigration-native-displacement-to-transfers-2026-09-18.md), outcome-composition discussion]

**What survives:** the first stage, reduced Mexico concentration and successful reproduction of the old regression are useful diagnostics. They do not close the exclusion argument. Retain the coefficients as diagnostics pending a correctly implemented historical design and a native-specific outcome. Trace the same instrument into the housing and congestion/wage lanes before reusing their causal interpretations. This audit identifies no honest numerical correction for the endogeneity. [INFERENCE; CODE: `housing_causal_2000_2010_2026_09_22/estimate.py`, `ancestry_iv_congestion_wages_2026_09_23/`]

## 2. A reconciled aggregate does not validate the fraction of people who win

**Material model dependence, plus two narrower implementation issues.** The [new distribution memo](immigration-winners-and-losers-2026-09-25.md) reports 23.9% winners with tax-share financing and household pooling, or 21.4% with per-person financing. The computation allocates modeled channel totals onto survey people; it does not independently observe their counterfactual gains. This is a legitimate simulation, but confidence in its sum and confidence in its sign for each person are different achievements. [DATA/INFERENCE]

The wage channel illustrates why. Its approximately $95.8bn of gross after-tax gains and $96.0bn of losses almost cancel. Small changes in the distribution of those large flows can move many households across zero without greatly changing the national total. A [reproduction probe](../infra/immigration-fiscal/conceptual_audit_2026_09_25/README.md#distribution-probe) reproduced the published central counts, then used existing production alternatives with the same other channels and the lane's convention for induced receipts. For the high-school-or-less skill split, changing the substitution elasticity gives: [CALCULATION]

| Existing wage-model setting | Winners, pooled other residents, tax-share financing | Net assigned to these people, $bn/year |
|---|---:|---:|
| Elasticity 1.5 | 29.2% | −260.1 |
| Elasticity 2.0, published central | 23.9% | −263.9 |
| Elasticity 2.5 | 18.7% | −266.1 |

These totals are the **person-allocation account**, not the adopted fiscal headline or a replacement fiscal-plus-social total. Among other differences, future taxpayers are not all in the current-person distribution. The table varies one consequential model choice; it is not a confidence interval or the full admissible range. [CALCULATION; [distribution code](../infra/immigration-fiscal/winners_losers_2026_09_24/winners_losers.py)]

A second probe makes the identification point exact. Holding the wage mean and every other channel fixed, halve each person's wage deviation from the mean: winners fall to **10.3%**, while the aggregate remains **−$263.9bn**. This is an algebraic counterexample, **not a fitted equilibrium, plausible lower bound or proposed replacement estimate**. It establishes only that aggregate conservation cannot test the distributional assumption. [CALCULATION]

Two smaller details also need attention. The purported low/high winner range stacks channels to minimize or maximize **national dollars**, which need not extremize the **number of winners**. Enumerating the existing matched channel choices gives a tax-share maximum of 30.53%; the broad published 14–30% range remains approximately right, but its construction does not prove an envelope. Household pooling also includes only other-resident members of an SPM unit. About 6.97m other residents live in units containing someone in the target group; calling the calculation complete household sharing omits the resource change of those target-group members. [CALCULATION; CODE: `build_nets()`, `spm_pooler()`]

**What survives:** the inspected alternatives still yield a minority of winners. Neither sensitivity proves a majority benefits. The finding should be “a minority in these incidence scenarios,” with the gross wage flows, financing assumptions, geography and sharing boundary carried into the headline. The next useful work is to validate those allocations and vary them jointly, not merely add more total-conservation checks. A model can be coherent and still have poorly identified individual signs. [INFERENCE]

## 3. Low school spending response does not logically imply proportional educational harm

**Unsupported production-function bridge; plausible harm remains.** The [school-dilution lane](../infra/immigration-fiscal/school_dilution_2026_09_24/RESULT.md) says most of the school cost left uncharged is diluted instruction and that low response cannot coexist with no dilution. Its calculation assigns an instructional share to the expenditure shortfall, translates that into foregone spending per pupil, then imports spending-to-score and score-to-earnings coefficients. The central result is **$16.1bn of annually accrued present-value future earnings losses** (reported cross-context range −$2.1bn to +$35.5bn), priced beside the account on September 25 and excluded from aggregate totals under the adopted option. [DATA/CODE: [`price.py`](../infra/immigration-fiscal/school_dilution_2026_09_24/price.py), lines 174–193; [adoption decision](../decisions/2026-09-25-school-dilution-priced-beside.md)]

Spending is an input price times a quantity, not educational output. Even “instruction” can have spare classroom capacity, lumpy staffing, changing teacher salaries, different pupil composition or economies of school size. A spending elasticity below one does not identify how much incumbent instruction or learning falls. Separating instructional from administrative dollars makes the proposed mechanism more plausible; it does not establish it. [INFERENCE]

The [Jackson–Mackevicius evidence](https://kirabojackson.com/pdfs/jackson-mackevicius-2024-school-spending-policy.pdf) concerns the effect of spending interventions. It does not itself show that an immigration-induced budget residual is an equivalent intervention in reverse. The cached primary article was checked. The lane's own longer-run spending response produces about **$3.6bn**, while its longer-run class-size pathway produces **$2.6bn**. These are different horizons/pathways, not interchangeable corrections or additive estimates. Using the longer-run spending response also raises the modeled school budget charge by **$33–36bn**: one cannot take its smaller dilution figure while retaining the lower fiscal charge. The alternatives make the mechanism and horizon consequential; they do not provide a cheaper corrected total. [SOURCE; DATA: school RESULT and adoption decision, alternative horizons and staffing channel]

**Strongest defense:** extra pupils can plainly strain classrooms, teachers are a useful quantity check, and causal school-finance research makes educational harm credible. The memo also discloses contrary studies and transport uncertainty. None of that establishes the claimed logical necessity. Present the figure as conditional on the fraction of the residual that is lost educational output. A decisive test measures incumbent pupils' teacher hours, occupied capacity, class composition and outcomes at the same horizon as the budget response. [INFERENCE]

One tempting objection should be rejected: discounted lifetime harm caused by **one year's exposure** can legitimately be booked as a one-year social-cost accrual. It is not automatically a stock/flow error. Label it as accrual, preserve the separation from budget cash flow, and avoid adding its future-tax consequence a second time. [INFERENCE; the lane already preserves the last distinction]

## 4. The lineage model builds in much of its conclusion that legal status hardly matters

**Confirmed missing mechanism.** The [century-lineage memo](immigration-lineage-cost-century-2026-09-19.md) says legalization after ten years changes the modeled gap by only 1.9%. In [`founder_profile()`](../infra/immigration-fiscal/lineage_cost_2026_09_19/lineage.py), the unauthorized founder inherits the pooled Mexico-born age profile, receives a $870 annual working-age adjustment, and retains the same profile at 65+. Legalization removes the working-age adjustment. It cannot change the old-age eligibility channels that the model has fixed. [DATA/CODE]

The [SSA lawful-presence rule](https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/links/0302610041) restricts monthly Title II benefits paid to noncitizens residing in the US. Lawful presence and work authorization are distinct; demographic “unauthorized” categories need careful mapping. That is a reason to model the founder's actual eligibility, covered work and residence history, not to assign pooled senior benefits and infer that status barely matters. [SOURCE/INFERENCE]

The lane's own blanket no-senior-cost sensitivity changes the undiscounted century gap by **$446,289, or 34.4%**. That is not a corrected estimate: taxes, public services and eligible care do not all vanish at retirement. It demonstrates that the omitted mechanism has room to matter. Retain the run as a pooled period-profile scenario; withdraw the legal-status conclusion until program-specific eligibility is implemented. This does not establish a comparable error in the current all-status annual account. [DATA: [lineage RESULT](../infra/immigration-fiscal/lineage_cost_2026_09_19/RESULT.md), senior sensitivity; INFERENCE]

## 5. The off-books analysis changes populations when it returns to the fiscal account

**Confirmed population mismatch.** The [rule-breaking memo](immigration-rule-breaking-competition-2026-09-25.md) reports roughly $6.43bn of employer cost advantage, including $4.53bn of payroll taxes, and maps those dollars onto the Mexican-origin account. But [`acs_cells.py`](../infra/immigration-fiscal/compliance_gap_2026_09_24/acs_cells.py) defines imputed unauthorized workers across **all origins**. The wage base used by [`edges.py`](../infra/immigration-fiscal/compliance_gap_2026_09_24/edges.py) has no Mexican-origin restriction. Non-Mexican unauthorized workers are other residents in the adopted account, not automatically inside its target group. [CODE/DATA]

Re-tabulating the exact person-level query reproduces the all-origin panel. Applying the **unchanged** central industry slopes and rates to its Mexico-born wage share gives about **$3.22bn**, including **$2.27bn** in payroll taxes. This is a population diagnostic across the same four industries, not a causal re-estimate or an exact all-generation Mexican-origin classifier. It shows that the mismatch is material, not a labeling detail. [CALCULATION: [population probe](../infra/immigration-fiscal/conceptual_audit_2026_09_25/README.md#off-books-population-probe)]

**Strongest defense:** all-origin unauthorized competition is a valid question in its own right. Preserve that result under its actual population. The unsupported step is treating all those payroll dollars as already reconciled to a different population's account and all the workers' losses as within-group. Require an explicit intersection and receipt reconciliation. Since this term was not added to the adopted headline, subtracting the difference from $201–246bn would introduce another error. [INFERENCE]

## 6. Returnees are compared with the wrong stayers to establish selective US exit

**Confirmed comparison-group error.** The [ENADID memo](immigration-mexico-return-migration-selectivity-2026-09-22.md) finds that returnees have less schooling than people who stayed **in Mexico**, then uses that to explain upward selection among Mexicans who remain **in the US**. Its limitations admit that the US-side comparison is missing. The interpretation is repeated in the [second-generation memo](immigration-second-generation-by-origin-2026-09-22.md). [DATA/CODE: `enadid_return_selectivity_2026_09_22/enadid_selectivity.py`, returnee/stayer construction]

The sign does not follow. If Mexico stayers have 12 years of schooling, returnees 9, and emigrants who remain in the US 7, the observed comparison holds while return migration *lowers* the US resident mean. Those are illustrative numbers, not estimates. To identify the proposed selection, compare returnees with the emigrant cohort that could have returned but remained abroad, matching age, sex, migration window and schooling histories as far as the data permit. [INFERENCE]

The descriptive returnee result survives, and negatively selected return migration is plausible. The local evidence does not establish its sign against US stayers. Withdraw that explanation pending the matched comparison; do not replace it with a claim of positively selected return migration or an invented fiscal adjustment. [INFERENCE]

## 7. The voting note turns incomplete detection into an upper bound, then changes geography

**Confirmed logical overclaim; no finding of decisive illegal voting.** The [September 19 note](../notes/noncitizen-voting-evidence-2026-09-19.md) labels 24,000 ballots a national upper bound and says the phenomenon is too small by orders of magnitude to have decided any federal or California contest. Yet its own source discussion records incomplete population coverage and missing detection diagnostics. A largest published flag count is not an upper bound on the underlying total when missed cases are unbounded. Correcting false positives alone cannot supply that bound. [DATA/INFERENCE]

Nor can a national rate be compared with every local margin without assumptions about geography and vote direction. California's official 2024 result for House District 13 was **105,554 against 105,367: a 187-vote margin**. That is enough to defeat the blanket orders-of-magnitude argument. It is not evidence that noncitizens cast those votes, were concentrated there, or affected the result. [SOURCE: [California Statement of Vote](https://elections.cdn.sos.ca.gov/sov/2024-general/sov/complete-sov.pdf), printed p.28]

The note's narrower statement—that its evidence does not establish a decisive contribution—is defensible. Keep adjudicated cases separate from detection flags and scenarios for undetected cases. A contest-specific decisiveness claim needs its own electorate, cast ballots, adjudicated eligibility and net directional margin. Uncertainty about detection does not establish widespread fraud either. [INFERENCE]

## 8. Debt service needs a welfare bridge before becoming another welfare cost

**Problem in proposed use, not in the compounding arithmetic.** The [debt-legacy lane](../infra/immigration-fiscal/debt_legacy_2026_09_23/RESULT.md) estimates **$28.3–36.4bn of annual federal interest** conditional on a modeled historical debt stock. It proposes putting this in the main account's currently zero-response interest row because it overlaps no existing entry. [DATA]

A previously empty budget row does not establish a new resource cost. The beneficiary set includes domestic creditors; the annual account's welfare convention nets transfers within that set and counts opportunity income. Yet switching on the interest row subtracts it dollar for dollar without a corresponding creditor return, alternative asset return, capital path or tax distortion. The annual core also holds inherited debt/history fixed, whereas this proposal changes past borrowing. They require a common counterfactual before summation. [CODE: `assumption_explorer_2026_09_21/engine.js`; DATA: `full_account_benefits_2026_09_20/README.md`; INFERENCE]

The opposite shortcut is wrong too: domestic interest being a transfer does not make debt costless. Capital crowding out, foreign claims and fiscal distortions can matter, and creditors would hold something else in a less-indebted economy. These distinctions appear in CBO's [interest primer](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/56910) and [debt stabilization analysis](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/58055). Keep the result as a conditional **taxpayer financing burden** until a dynamic account reconciles those channels. Neither zero nor the foreign-owned share is an established welfare correction. The proposed add-on remains outside the adopted headline. [SOURCE/INFERENCE]

## 9. Two useful negative findings answer narrower questions than their interpretations suggest

The [California movers lane](../infra/immigration-fiscal/movers_reasons_2026_09_24/RESULT.md) carefully reports that only 1.47% of US-born leavers choose neighborhood/crime as their single main reason. It also says the white/Mexican-origin comparison directly tests ethnic flight. That stronger implication fails: immigration could affect housing costs or school conditions, with movers correctly selecting “housing”; both groups could respond to the same mechanism. Reason shares **among movers** also cannot establish moving rates **among residents**. Keep the descriptive finding and its already explicit limits. Test the upstream mechanism with reason-specific flows per source population and an independent counterfactual. [DATA/INFERENCE]

The [street-vending lane](../infra/immigration-fiscal/vending_restaurants_2026_09_24/RESULT.md) finds no clear differential restaurant decline after the 2019 law. But its own evidence shows vending arrests had already collapsed before passage, few permits followed, and direct local vendor activity is unobserved. Without a demonstrated change in competition, this event cannot resolve the effect of **existing** vendors. Net establishment counts also allow incumbent exits to be offset by entrants. The memo acknowledges market-share limits, so “no loss detected after this law” survives; corroboration against stock displacement remains weak. Actual vendor activity and incumbent revenue/exit data would discriminate. Neither criticism establishes that ethnic composition caused moves or that vendors destroyed net restaurant employment. [DATA/INFERENCE]

## Smaller corrections and one open lead

- **New permanent residence is not first US arrival.** The NIS lane describes respondents as at the start of their US careers, roughly four months after admission, while its own anchor says 57.38% adjusted status. An adjustee indicator does not measure years already spent in the US. Narrow the timing claim and separate new arrivals from adjustees before interpreting current pay versus prior-year income. The reported conditional coefficients are not thereby disproved. [DATA/CODE: `nis2003_religion_earnings_2026_09_22/{RESULT.md,analysis.py}`; primary NIS overview cached in that lane]
- **Marginal elasticity versus finite removal.** The service-scaling memo derives `r = [1 − (1 − s)^b] / s`, but the main implementation uses the elasticity `b` as the response fraction. At the account's group share of 0.12024, `b = 0.59–0.84` corresponds to `r = 0.6054–0.8485` under a single power law. The proper function-by-function correction is small relative to identification uncertainty. Label the approximation or use the already derived formula; this is not a sign reversal. [CALCULATION; DATA: [service-scaling memo](immigration-service-scaling-test-2026-09-20.md), `assumption_explorer_2026_09_21/scaling_check.py` and `engine.js`]
- **[GAP] TANF scope bridge.** The new administrative-key lane uses $7.788bn of basic-assistance dollars to weight a $24.458bn BEA family-assistance line. Its person-versus-dollar caveat is already disclosed. The broader category reconciliation was not established by this audit; this is a question to resolve, not a confirmed error or a replacement estimate. Reconcile program components before treating the ethnicity key as an outside validation of the entire line. [DATA: `admin_benefit_keys_2026_09_24/{RESULT.md,compare.py}`]

## Objections that did not survive, and the research judgment

Several superficially attractive criticisms fail. Private after-tax income plus induced receipts preserves the model's gross-income/resource-cost identity; the tax term is not simply free money counted twice. The generation account explicitly allocates interactions with Aumann–Shapley attribution and separately reports standalone removals. Housing asset values are not blindly added to annual rents. Victim-only crime prices preserve the separation from justice spending after this week's corrections. The historical backcast, sampling-error floor, occupation-overlap elasticity sketch and scale-spillover scenario all disclose substantial limits; repeating those limits as discoveries would misrepresent the week's work. [CODE/DATA: coverage ledger and linked source families]

My main judgment is that **the weakest links now sit between models**. A budget residual becomes an educational outcome, an all-household result becomes a native result, an all-origin worker becomes a Mexican-origin worker, or a national total becomes an individual sign. None of those translations is licensed merely because both endpoints are carefully calculated. [INFERENCE]

The same discipline applies to absence claims. A mechanism the model does not permit to change cannot be found unimportant; an outcome that was not observed cannot be bounded by detected cases; a weak policy intervention cannot settle the effects of a large existing stock. Conversely, finding one such gap does not identify the opposite effect or justify filling it with a preferred number. [INFERENCE]

Priority should follow the claims at risk: first repair the ancestry design and native outcome, correct the off-books population and return-selection interpretation, and withdraw the legal-status and voting overclaims. Next test school output and gross incidence directly. Keep debt financing separate until its welfare bridge exists. The most useful new research would discriminate among these mechanisms; another layer of arithmetic reconciliation would leave them unresolved. [RECOMMENDATION]
