**Verdict:** The ways immigrant-vs-native crime statistics mislead are real, documented, and do not share a sign. The largest measured deflator (makes immigrants look better) is status-identification timing in Texas: the state's own page reports 11,000 people identified as unlawfully present only after prison entry, and a fixed 2015 conviction rate moved from 8% below to 20% above the state rate as identification accrued. The largest measured inflators (make immigrants look worse) are exposure denominators in Europe (Brandenburg's non-German suspect rate halves once non-residents leave the numerator) and immigration offences counted as crime in US federal data (noncitizen share of sentences 34.7% headline, 15.8% without immigration offences). The channel the question started from, police not recording or acting for fear of being called racist, is documented at grade A in England for one offence class (ethnicity unrecorded for two-thirds of group-exploitation perpetrators) and has no primary source in the United States, where the measured suppression of reporting comes from immigration enforcement itself. [SOURCE: primary documents cited per section; INFERENCE where labelled]

## 2. The "police won't act because racist or woke" channel

What the primary record supports, by country:

- **England.** The Jay report (Rotherham, 2014) records staff "nervousness about identifying the ethnic origins of perpetrators for fear of being thought racist" and "clear direction from their managers not to do so". Jay explicitly marks the claim that police "dared not act against Asian youths" as "not supported by specific examples". So the documentation channel is evidenced; case-level under-enforcement is asserted, not corroborated. Casey (2025) turns it into a number: two-thirds of perpetrators with no recorded ethnicity, and a conclusion that cuts both ways, against confident over-representation claims and against the "mostly white perpetrators" claim the system had been making. [SOURCE: [Jay report](https://www.rotherham.gov.uk/downloads/file/279/independent-inquiry-into-child-sexual-exploitation-in-rotherham), exec. summary and ch. 11; Casey audit]
- **Sweden.** The 16-year gap in official offender-background studies was a ministerial refusal to commission, on the record: "we already know … that persons with foreign background are over-represented" (Morgan Johansson, Riksdag, 20 Dec 2017). Police "code 291" (2015–16) was the opposite of suppression: it increased recording granularity on migrant-linked incidents and delayed publication by about four months; the released figure was about 1% of all reports. [SOURCE: [interpellation H510255](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/interpellation/brottsligheten-bland-svenskar-och-utlandsfodda_h510255/); [DN 2016-01-20](https://www.dn.se/nyheter/sverige/polisen-hemlighaller-fakta-om-sitt-flyktingarbete/); [Ygeman answer](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svar-pa-skriftlig-fraga/relationskod-291_h312684/)]
- **Germany.** The press code (Richtlinie 12.1) binds newspapers, not police records; treating it as statistical suppression is a category error. Cologne 2015/16 is a documented initial understatement ("Einsatzlage entspannt"), retracted five days later. [SOURCE: [Presserat Leitsätze](https://www.presserat.de/leitsaetze-zur-richtlinie-12-1.html); Landtag NRW Drs. 16/14450 located, not read]
- **United States.** No primary source was found for police or prosecutors declining to act against immigrants specifically out of fear of a racism charge. The de-policing literature is group-neutral and partly disconfirming: Missouri agencies made 67,000 fewer stops after Ferguson, hit rates rose from 26.7% to 28.6%, and crime did not move, meaning the dropped stops were the low-yield ones (Shjarback et al. 2017). Level reporting data run against the naive story too: Hispanic victims reported violent victimisation to police at 47.8% in 2023 versus 41.9% for non-Hispanic whites (NCVS Table 5). The one measured US reporting suppression is caused by immigration enforcement: Secure Communities cut Hispanic victim reporting 30% and raised offending, with reported crime flat (Gonçalves, Jácome & Weisburst). [SOURCE: [Shjarback et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcrimjus.2017.04.003); [NCVS 2023](https://bjs.ojp.gov/document/cv23.pdf); AER 2026 above] [FRAMING-SENSITIVE: whether "suppression" names a documentation practice, a timing failure, or an enforcement effect is a framing choice; the measured quantities are not]

## 3. What the evidence killed

- Intra-group victimisation as the least-reported channel: BJS 2012–15 shows intra-group violent victimisations reported to police at 45.9% (white), 51.7% (Black), 48.2% (Hispanic), no significant difference; and Hispanic-on-Hispanic is only 40% of victimisations of Hispanic victims, so a victim-keyed rate is not a lower bound on offending. [SOURCE: [BJS NCJ 250747](https://bjs.ojp.gov/content/pub/pdf/rhovo1215.pdf)]
- Aliases inflating counts: the only quantified source is GAO 1995 (83% of a 383-person sample used at least one alias) on name-based systems that fingerprint matching replaced; no "average aliases" figure exists in any GAO report. Not a live claim. [SOURCE: [GAO/AIMD-95-147](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GAOREPORTS-AIMD-95-147/html/GAOREPORTS-AIMD-95-147.htm)]
- The Texas arrest-to-conviction gap as evidence of removal in lieu of prosecution: DPS publishes no native baseline, counts charges not people, right-censors pending cases, hides substitution inside categories, and disclaims cross-group use ("do not attempt to allege that foreign nationals in the country illegally commit more crimes than other groups"). The 38% charge-to-conviction ratio is uninterpretable either way.
- Charge substitution as a net deflator: California Penal Code §1016.3 makes immigration consequences "one factor" prosecutors "shall consider"; Brooklyn's 2017 policy tells ADAs to offer non-removal dispositions "whenever possible". No substitution rate has been published, and the sentencing literature runs the other way (odds ratio 4.28 above). Real in law, unmeasured in effect. [SOURCE: [§1016.3](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=1016.3); [Brooklyn DA](https://www.brooklynda.org/2017/04/24/acting-brooklyn-district-attorney-eric-gonzalez-announces-new-policy-regarding-handling-of-cases-against-non-citizen-defendants/)]

## 4. Missing instruments

Each of these is an absence verified by search, not a proof of non-existence.

- No US agency publishes counts of consular notifications under Vienna Convention Art. 36 (28 C.F.R. §50.5 makes them mandatory for listed countries). A near-census of arrested foreign nationals exists in law and not in statistics.
- No Census Bureau evaluation of citizenship or birthplace item quality in correctional group quarters was found; the ACS-based incarceration literature rests on an item never separately validated in prisons. Cato's standing premise that inmate data are "collected by, or under the supervision of, correctional institution administrators" describes the 2020 decennial menu, not ACS procedure, which interviews sampled residents by CAPI with proxies allowed.
- No published IDENT no-record rate among arrestees; the DPS prison-identified stock (11,000) is a floor.
- No detainer-case dismissal rate; no DA declination-rate series by charge; no US offences-per-offender by nativity.

## 5. Consequences for this repo's own numbers

The replicated Texas violent-felony ratio of 0.4571 (2018, Light coding) is exposed to exactly one of the deflators above: unauthorized arrestees with no prior DHS encounter who self-report as citizens land in the native group. Light's self-reported citizenship field (missing in 3% of felony arrests) protects the legal-immigrant split, and their extreme-case recomputation treats every non-flagged noncitizen as undocumented, but nobody has bounded the citizen-self-report residual. DPS's own window-restricted figures for the prison-identified group are small in total (roughly 2% of charges) and concentrated in serious offences, and DPS says the group is under-represented. Any Texas ratio reported here must carry its extract date and a sensitivity line that adds the prison-identified stock; that is an analysis task, not further research. [INFERENCE from DPS caveats and Light's method text; the repo's `immigration-measurement-uncertainty-2026-09-05.md` already parameterises recording intensity]

The reusable diagnostic from the classification lane: every grounded classification bias is a residual category renamed as a group ("unknown/other" as native-born, "not identified as legal" as illegal, "not a registered resident" still counted, "not in the population register" still counted). Find the residual, find who renamed it, estimate what share of it is measurement failure, and state the extract date.

## Disconfirmation record

Searched for and not found: a US primary source for anticipatory non-enforcement against immigrants; a measured charge-substitution rate; a detainer dismissal rate. Found and contrary to the starting hypothesis: NCVS level reporting rates by Hispanic origin, Sweden's code 291, Missouri de-policing hit rates, the sign of Rumbaut's Puerto Rico coding, the sign of Cato's group-quarters imputation defect. [FRAMING-SENSITIVE] The pattern that survives is not "the statistics hide immigrant crime" but "the statistics are built from residual categories, extract dates and denominators that each side chooses, and the two biggest measured errors point in opposite directions."
