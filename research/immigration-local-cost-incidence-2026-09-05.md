## What the new calculation resolves

The [June 9, 2026 Comptroller report, Table 38](https://comptroller.nyc.gov/reports/comments-on-new-york-citys-executive-budget-for-fiscal-year-2027-and-financial-plan-for-fiscal-years-2026-2030/) separates FY2023–25 actual/accrued spending by financing source. Its figures remain subject to reconciliation. The [August 12 adopted-budget review](https://comptroller.nyc.gov/reports/comments-on-new-york-citys-fiscal-year-2027-adopted-budget/) was checked; it supplies later projections but no replacement historical financing table. |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2023 | $1,474m | $1,232m | $242m | $0m | $1,232m |

The $1m discrepancy is published rounding, explicitly retained. City financing is about **61.0%** of gross spending. The old dashboard still shows FY2023 city/state shares of $1,139m/$335m; the newer table moves another $93m from state to city financing, leaving gross spending unchanged. [DATA; SOURCE: Table 38 and [older fiscal dashboard](https://comptroller.nyc.gov/services/for-the-public/accounting-for-asylum-seeker-services/fiscal-impacts/)]

These are accrued or assigned financing shares, **not proof of collected reimbursements**. The June report records $2.19bn of state cash received by mid-May 2026 against about $3.2bn expected across FY2023–26, and treats $88m of FY2025 federal aid as at risk. The FY2025 city-financing sensitivity is therefore $1.477bn to $1.565bn for that specific loss; it is not a confidence interval or a bound on all liabilities. Do not add the unpaid state balance to this table: it spans another fiscal year and includes timing differences. [SOURCE: June report, State/Federal Aid discussion; DATA]

Intergovernmental transfers change **who finances** a service. A state reimbursement is both a city receipt and a state outlay, and cancels on consolidation. Adding $8.246bn of city-administered program spending to $3.007bn of state reimbursements would count the same services twice. Migrants' local taxes, other public services, private earnings, and the counterfactual use of resources are outside this ledger. “Net city financing after assigned grants” must not become “net cost caused by immigrants.” [ACCOUNTING IDENTITY; INFERENCE]

The [Comptroller's January–March 2026 cash report, updated June 5](https://comptroller.nyc.gov/reports/new-york-city-quarterly-cash-report/) records **$1.573bn cash outlays in July–March FY2026, versus $2.578bn in the corresponding FY2025 period: down $1.005bn, or 39.0%**. This cash measure includes payments against earlier obligations. It must not be spliced into the June report's $1.19bn of liquidations against FY2026 commitments through April. The latter tracks the fiscal year of the expense; the cash report tracks when money leaves. FY2026 full-year actuals are not inferred by annualizing either partial series. [SOURCE; DATA]

## The daily-cost denominator is a household-night

The fiscal dashboard reports **$371 per household-night for July 2024–June 2025**. The June 2026 report defines this measure as shelter plus related services divided by shelter household-nights; included costs extend beyond a hotel room to services and setup/closure expenses. Dividing $3.020bn by $371 implies **8.14m household-nights, or 22,302 average households**, *if the reported annual cost and rate have exactly the same scope*. This is an accounting consistency implication, not an independently observed census. [SOURCE: fiscal dashboard and June report; DATA]

For an illustrative mean occupancy of two or three people per household-night, $371 corresponds to **$185.50 or $123.67 per person-night**, respectively, or **$67,708 or $45,138 per full 365-day person-year**. These are occupancy scenarios, not measured population averages or statistical uncertainty bounds. With only the condition that an occupied household contains at least one person, the rate is at most $371 per person-night; no useful positive lower bound follows without occupancy information. [ALGEBRA; FRAMING-SENSITIVE]

The required empirical denominator is the sum of **person-nights in the same financed programs and period**. Neither cumulative unique arrivals, a January peak, an end-of-year shelter census, nor the CSV's “baseline shelter census” supplies it. Unique arrivals include people with very different lengths of service. The generator deliberately emits a null observed person-year cost. The June report also says household-type flow data were no longer available as of the February plan, limiting reconstruction. [SOURCE: June report; DATA LIMIT]

## School enrollment is now measured; added spending is a separate question

NYCPS reports **906,248 district students in 2024–25**, plus **148,089 charter pupils**; district grades span 3-K through grade 12. The district series in its August 2025 demographic guide is **906,977 → 912,064 → 906,248** for 2022–23, 2023–24, and 2024–25: a two-year net decline of **729, or 0.08%**. Summing the three K–12 grade bands, which removes early childhood expansion/contraction, gives **809,118 → 811,711**, a rise of **2,593, or 0.32%**. [SOURCE: [NYCPS live enrollment page](https://www.schools.nyc.gov/about-us/reports/doe-data-at-a-glance), [demographic guide pp. 2–4](https://infohub.nyced.org/docs/default-source/default-document-library/2024-25-demographic-snapshot-summary.pdf); DATA]

These are enrollment snapshots, not attendance days or migrant student-years. The guide's district register is dated October 31, with distinct rules for early-childhood providers and long-term absences. The historical series above was read in a successful official-document web extraction; later download/retrieval attempts returned 404. Its retained transcription is explicitly labeled, and the current-year district/charter totals are independently confirmed on the live NYCPS page. [SOURCE; PROVENANCE LIMIT]

The almost-flat total does **not** identify zero immigration-induced demand: enrollment without arrivals could have fallen further, and school-level pressure can grow even while the citywide total shrinks. Grade, language needs, location, teacher availability, existing classrooms, and displacement between schools matter. ELL status also does not identify nativity or asylum status. [INFERENCE]

Three different monetary objects should remain visible:

| Measure | Verified figure | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| NYC district current expenditure per pupil, FY2024 | **$35,796** | Census average across the public-school spending universe; excludes capital outlay from current spending. It is neither a migrant-specific nor marginal cost. |
| FY2024 school-level FSF funding for an ELL pupil in temporary housing | **$6,948–$7,500**, depending on grade | Formula allocation to the school; excludes other central/categorical costs and is subject to the register/adjustment schedule. |
| Targeted funding announced in October 2022 | **$12m; $2,000 per eligible pupil** in schools with at least six qualifying pupils | A supplemental allocation, not total expenditure or evidence that every newly arrived pupil received it. |

[SOURCE: [Census release May 7, 2026, revised May 12](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/school-system-finances.html); [Comptroller school-budget analysis September 12, 2023](https://comptroller.nyc.gov/reports/spotlight-school-budget-update/)]

For **1,000 hypothetical full-year pupils**, the average-current-cost benchmark is **$35.796m**; the named FSF formula would allocate **$6.948m–$7.500m** if all qualify as ELL and temporarily housed. **That contrast is not a marginal-cost interval.** The two measures cover different costs, and either omitted support demands or available capacity can matter. We do not multiply a cumulative arrivals count by the average and call the result actual spending. The same school-budget report cites approximately 19,000 asylum-family pupils enrolled during 2022–23, but does not provide their pupil-days or a separately audited total cost. [DATA; SOURCE; INFERENCE]

This supersedes using all household children aged 5–17 as actual public pupils and assigning their entire expenditure exposure across foreign-born adults. The relevant marginal-cost object is additional staffing, space, transport and services, plus foregone service quality, against a credible no-arrival baseline. It can differ from average cost in either direction. [INFERENCE]

## Where benefits and burdens fall—and what is still unresolved

Shelter occupants receive accommodation and services; hotels, nonprofits, suppliers and workers receive payments. The [Comptroller's August 2024 contract study](https://comptroller.nyc.gov/reports/comparing-per-diem-hotel-and-service-costs-for-shelter-for-asylum-seekers/) separates a **$156 average daily room rate** from **$176 of services**, giving a **$332 combined per diem** for the studied DHS arrangement. These components are already within the program ledger. They are not extra benefits to add to spending when calculating net welfare. Provider revenue is not provider profit, and neither identifies gains to native workers. Its comparison with more expensive emergency arrangements is descriptive: sites and clients were not randomly assigned. [SOURCE; ACCOUNTING/INFERENCE]

For the wider state/local frame, [CBO's June 2025 report](https://www.cbo.gov/system/files/2025-06/61256-immigration-state-local.pdf) estimates the **2023 surge**, not the NYC program: direct revenue **$10.1bn**, spending **$19.3bn**, net cost **$9.2bn**. Its alternative potential-effect ledger gives **$9.8bn net cost**, not an additional $9.8bn. School estimates are **$5.7bn direct versus $9.4bn potential**, incorporating enrollment and funding-response assumptions; they are not school invoices. NYC dollars are partly inside CBO's national accounting and cannot be added as a new channel. [SOURCE: report pp. 1–2, 10–11]

The available approaches were screened before choosing this accounting comparison: NYC program financing; actual school registers and allocations; MSA rent trends; county QWI; and the eight-state response-spending regression. The first two directly improve measured units and service scope. The local ZORI CSV extends through **May 2026**, but its metro rents and ACS foreign-born stock shares do not identify a shock or incumbent residents. QWI has no nativity variable. The receiver-node PUMA-to-county land-area bridge and court-venue exposure measures retain geography/population problems; no resident-level incumbent panel or plausibly exogenous assignment was found in these inputs. No new causal regression was run. [CODE/INPUT INSPECTION; INFERENCE]

The remaining principal gap is narrow and substantive: **matched person-days and student-days, actual incremental school spending, recipient tax payments, and a defensible counterfactual for incumbent outcomes**. The analysis establishes a municipal financing requirement and service-cost scale. It does not establish total local net fiscal cost by origin, the amount of spending displaced from other residents, or a causal native wage/rent/school-quality effect. Revenue received by local businesses and support supplied to migrant families are real incidence channels, but their net welfare value is not observed here. [GAP; FRAMING-SENSITIVE]
