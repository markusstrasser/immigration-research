# LNS public author replications: policy preferences and data limits

**Verdict:** One source-limited descriptive policy comparison is usable from the Branton replication file with its revised national weight. It shows a strong gradient in *immediate* legalization, but does not show later generations uniformly opposing legalization. None of these derivatives measures complete family lineage or repairs the exclusion of non-identifying descendants. No complete original DS1/DS3 was obtained. All three files have consequential interpretation limits; no cross-package respondent join is justified.

Local evidence: [analysis script](../infra/immigration-fiscal/new_datasets_2026_09_17/lns_replications/analyze_replications.py), [audit](../infra/immigration-fiscal/new_datasets_2026_09_17/derived/lns_replications/analysis.json), [policy table](../infra/immigration-fiscal/new_datasets_2026_09_17/derived/lns_replications/generation_policy.csv). The replication files are additional sources; the ICPSR ZIPs supplied by the user still contain documentation only.

Scope: 2026-09-17 audit of three openly posted Harvard Dataverse replication packages. All numerical findings below are **[DATA]**, generated by `analyze_replications.py`; source definitions and limitations are separately stated. No article's causal result is claimed replicated. The data describe the 2005–06 survey period, not current attitudes. This politically sensitive analysis is LLM-assisted; primary item definitions and mechanical data checks, rather than an assumed political direction, control the interpretation.

## The one descriptive table

Source: [Branton, Martinez-Ebers, Carey and Matsubayashi replication package](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/27113), version 2.1, unrestricted repository files with CC0 metadata. Use `generation`, `immpolinew`, and `wt_nation_rev` from `ajps2_replication.dta`. The accompanying do-file repeatedly applies `[pw=wt_nation_rev]`. The original LNS questionnaire/codebook verifies the policy item's ordering, and the article's retrieved p.394 defines the derivative's generation codes. The raw-to-derived generation construction is absent, so these are **author-coded groups**, not independently reconstructed lineage categories. [Article primary university copy, indexed p.394](https://lmas.unt.edu/sites/lmas.unt.edu/files/lmas/Social%20Protest%20and%20Policy%20Attitudes.pdf); [journal identity](https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.12159); [original survey codebook openly deposited by Wallace et al.](https://dataverse.harvard.edu/api/access/datafile/2440404).

Percent of each author-coded group, using the revised national weight; all five policy options stay in the denominator:

| Author-coded group | Unweighted n | Immediate legalization | Guest worker → legalization | Temporary guest worker | Close/seal border | None of these |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| First generation, noncitizen (`0`) | 3,626 | 59.0% | 22.0% | 10.3% | 1.3% | 7.4% |
| First generation, citizen (`1`) | 2,092 | 40.7% | 33.7% | 12.0% | 4.5% | 9.2% |
| Second generation (`2`) | 975 | 25.1% | 41.9% | 14.7% | 6.5% | 11.8% |
| Third generation (`3`) | 809 | 14.4% | 45.4% | 16.1% | 10.4% | 13.7% |
| Fourth-plus generation (`4`) | 667 | 15.9% | 45.8% | 14.7% | 11.6% | 12.0% |

**Interpretation:** Immediate legalization falls sharply across these categories, while the guest-worker route to eventual legalization becomes more popular. Combining the two offered legalization routes gives 81.1%, 74.4%, 67.1%, 59.8%, and 61.7%, respectively. Calling the later groups simply “anti-legalization” would misread the response options. The fourth-plus immediate-legalization estimate exceeds the third-generation estimate slightly; a strictly monotonic story is also unwarranted. Unweighted immediate-legalization percentages are 59.6%, 40.7%, 23.7%, 12.6%, and 15.1%; the broad gradient is not an artifact of this weight. These are respondent descriptions, not causal assimilation estimates or native-white comparisons.

Exact denominator audit:

- The file has 8,561 physical rows, **349 entirely missing across all 49 variables**, and 8,212 nonblank records. All nonblank records have a finite positive revised national weight and policy code 1–5.
- Another 43 nonblank records have missing `generation`, leaving **8,169** in the table. Their omitted weight is 40.5693 out of 8,239.8411 nonblank weighted units, or about 0.49%; table weight sum is 8,199.2718. This sum is calibrated survey weight, not a population count.
- Missing here means Stata system missing; there are no observed out-of-domain generation or policy codes among nonmissing records. `immpolinew=5` means **none of these**, not ordinary item nonresponse, and remains included.
- The original survey contains 8,634 people. The cause of this derivative's 422-record shortfall in usable records is undocumented in the deposited analysis do-file. No synthetic respondents were added and no blank row was assigned an ID.
- The paper's p.394 footnote gives an overall policy distribution of 41.56/33.02/12.26/4.97/8.19%; our derivative's revised-weight distribution is 42.00/31.78/12.20/4.56/9.46%. Therefore this is **not** a reproduced published benchmark. Weight/version/sample differences remain unresolved; the table is confined to this author-supplied derivative.

The dataset permits this modest table because code meanings, an explicitly used positive survey weight, and denominators can be stated. It does **not** support a national contemporary prevalence claim. No standard errors or significance claims are produced: the table lacks a verified complete survey-design specification. Kish weight-only effective sample sizes are saved solely as diagnostics, not substituted for design-adjusted precision. Age, origin, citizenship, interview timing, cohort and ethnic attrition remain uncontrolled.

## What each derivative actually contains

### Branton: useful revised-weight policy data; weak lineage and origin reconstruction

`generation` is coded 0 noncitizen immigrant, 1 immigrant citizen, 2 second, 3 third, 4 fourth-plus in the article. It mixes immigration generation and citizenship; code 0 is not “zero generation.” The deposited do-file fits models using this variable but does not derive it from birthplace/parent/grandparent fields. `PARBORN` and `GRANBORN` are absent, as are individual parent/grandparent locations. Treatment of mixed-generation parents, Puerto Rican birthplace and missing family information cannot be reconstructed. Do not rebrand code 3 as our audited exact third generation or code 4 as having a known number of foreign-born ancestors.

`immpolinew` policy codes 1–5 follow the table headings. The original questionnaire asks the preferred policy on undocumented/illegal immigration; it does not ask immigration quantity or general welfare spending. `wt_nation_rev` is the revised national weight used by the deposited models; [ICPSR's version history](https://www.icpsr.umich.edu/web/RCMD/studies/20862/version/) identifies the replacement of old weights in 2010.

The origin-like variable **`national_origin` is “ID with National Origin,” a binary identity measure, not a country code**. `american` likewise means identification with Americans. Origin indicators `cuba`, `pr`, `dr`, `south`, and `central` are present, but neither a raw ancestry code nor an explicit Mexican indicator survives. An all-zero residual must not silently be labeled Mexican without additional construction evidence. No party-affiliation variable and no `RESPID`/`CASEID` appear. [DATA: Stata labels and exact column inventory in analysis.json; SOURCE: deposited Stata do-file.]

### Wallace: original-sized, uniquely identified projection, but transformed fields and no weight

[Wallace, Zepeda-Millan and Jones-Correa package](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/SZK4NF), v3.1: **8,634 rows, 30 columns, 8,634 distinct nonmissing `respid` values, zero duplicate IDs**. The original codebook defines RESPID as the respondent identifier. This is a full-original-N-sized derivative with IDs, not a small respondent subsample or a complete raw data release. Original-ID set equality cannot be verified without DS1/DS3. Matching N and unique IDs alone do not prove every original person survives unchanged.

It includes `firstgen`, `partyid7`, origin indicators `mexican`, `prican`, `cuban`, `dominican`, `salvadoran`, and `CentralAmerican`; government attitudes `bigintst`, `sayso`, `complic`, `nocontac`, `govtrust`; and demographic/media/protest variables. **No survey weight is present.** `firstgen` is labeled first-generation dummy (5,717 ones, 2,917 zeros), but the provided do-file/codebook do not specify its exact birthplace treatment. It cannot distinguish second from third-plus or support exact-lineage analysis.

The deposited codebook and do-file explicitly derive `partyid7`: 1 strong Republican, 2 Republican, 3 Republican leaner, 4 independent, 5 Democratic leaner, 6 Democrat, 7 strong Democrat. It uses PARTYID, STRDPART and INDPARTY and leaves the other PARTYID responses unclassified. **3,144 values are missing (36.4%)**; original codebook counts show these exactly equal 1,410 “don't care” plus 1,734 “don't know/other party.” Omitting them produces a selected partisan denominator, not a whole-sample party balance. [Original codebook pp.69–70; deposited do-file opening section.]

The code converts K3 response code 9 (DK/NA) to missing. Missing counts are 971 `bigintst`, 864 `sayso`, 668 `complic`, and 795 `nocontac`. `govtrust` has no system missing and is coded 1 never through 4 just about always. `hhinc` has 1,801 missing; the supplementary codebook discusses a separate imputed `hhinc2`, **which is absent from this 30-column file**. Thus do not call the whole derivative either raw or wholly imputed. No weighted policy/party table is produced from it. [Study-specific codebook](https://dataverse.harvard.edu/api/access/datafile/2440403).

### Perez: interpretable party categories and five origins; third means third-plus and weight vintage is unresolved

[Perez replication package](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/1KWH3E), v1.0: **7,688 rows × 22 columns**, the five largest origin groups rather than all LNS respondents. The full [author-posted paper](https://eoperez.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/perez_origins-and-implications-of-language-effects.pdf), pp.441–442 and footnotes 13–14, defines the sample and coding. All-zero origin dummies are legitimately Mexican here because the paper explicitly defines that baseline and the sample contains only Mexican, Cuban, Puerto Rican, Dominican and Salvadoran respondents. The script verifies mutually exclusive origin dummies and records counts.

`second` means native-born with foreign-born parents; `third` means native-born with native-born parents. **The latter is third-or-higher under our terminology, not exact third.** Both zero indicates the paper's first-generation reference category, but raw birthplace and parental fields are absent; the precise mixed-parent and Puerto Rico rules remain unverified. This is not comparable to Branton code 3 without relabeling and a harmonization decision.

The paper defines `dems`, `reps`, `indys`, and the misleadingly named `noparty` as Democrat, Republican, Independent, and **Don't Care**, respectively; all four zero is its **Don't Know reference**. Original LNS PARTYID code 5 combines “don't know/other party,” so the safest source-faithful residual label retains that combined wording. The README variable list calls `noparty` “other party,” conflicting with the paper and original questionnaire; do not follow that text silently. Exactly 1,583 respondents are all-zero, and 6,105 have one indicator; no respondent has multiple positive party indicators. None of these five categories should be dropped as if structural nonresponse.

`weight` is labeled Nation-Level Weight and is positive/nonmissing for all 7,688. It is **not identified as the revised weight** in the README or paper. Its minimum 0.607729 and mean 1.1592 closely match the original WT_NATION's documented minimum 0.607729 and full-sample mean 1.159299, rather than the revised-weight calibration. [INFERENCE] This appears to be the old national weight; a byte/value comparison to originals is unavailable. I therefore did not add a second weighted party table. `amid`, `inter` have 287 missing; all party/generation/weight fields have none. `knows`, `america`, `solid` are constructed scales, not unprocessed answers to single policy items. There is no respondent ID.

## Identity and join verdict

The script searches exact case-insensitive `CASEID` and `RESPID` names in every file. Only Wallace exposes `respid`. Consequently **cross-package ID overlap is not measurable** and **zero joins were attempted**. No row-order merge, invented ID, demographic-match merge, or weight-match merge is used. A later authorized original file could potentially be joined to Wallace by RESPID after matching study/version, ID representation and overlap and checking unchanged source fields. That is a possible future validation route, not current join authorization or a verified linkage. Original RESPID is documented as a character field, whereas Wallace stores it numerically; preserve the original format and inspect any leading zeros before a future linkage.
