# When Mexican-origin findings transfer to other migration streams

**Verdict:** Transfer a measured mechanism to comparable migrants, not a country-wide verdict to a region. The Mexican/Central American low-education concern transfers much less well to established Cuban, Colombian, Peruvian and Brazilian populations. Distance predicts some educational selection, but neither distance nor poor Somali outcomes establishes a large cognitive selection effect or a genetic explanation. [INFERENCE]

Date: September 17, 2026. US-focused; European selection evidence is labeled. Extends the [regional and trust review](immigration-latam-southeast-asia-trust-2026-09-17.md). The welfare frame separates migrants' outcomes, fiscal balances and effects on incumbents. These politically charged interpretations are vulnerable to LLM framing bias; adverse outcomes and evidence against blanket pessimism are both retained.

Two additional primary tabulations fill important gaps:

- **Brazil:** in 2024, 48% of immigrant adults 25+ had BA+, civilian labor-force participation was 73%, and poverty was 13%. In FY2023, 49% of Brazilian green-card grants were employment based. That last denominator is grants, not all arrivals or today's immigrant stock. These observations weaken a Mexican low-education extrapolation without proving a fiscal surplus. - **Venezuela:** in 2023, 48% of immigrant adults 25+ had BA+, participation was 75%, but poverty was 17% versus 12% among the US-born. Higher education coexists with unfavorable income outcomes. **Transfer judgment:** Guatemala/El Salvador/Honduras warrant testing similar low-education earnings and fiscal mechanisms. Dominican outcomes look intermediate in these measures. Cuban/Colombian/Peruvian/Brazilian outcomes warrant their own estimates; importing the Mexican magnitude would ignore large observed differences. Ecuador and Haiti show why lower parental-generation education does not mechanically determine one descendant outcome. None of these education tables validates an origin-specific crime or trust verdict. [INFERENCE]

## 2. Distance, selection and absolute skill are different objects

The distance intuition has evidence: Belot and Hatton find stronger educational selection relative to origins for more distant migration, especially from poorer countries. Their bilateral-stock study is observational; distance also accompanies language, historical links and different policies. The June 2009 manuscript's sample is not silently equated with the final publication's. It supports a useful prior, not a universal rule or cognitive-SD conversion. [SOURCE: [author manuscript, Tables 3–5](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/12436879/BELOT_Immigrant_Selection_in_the_OECD.pdf), [2012 publication](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9442.2012.01721.x).]

Del Carmen and Sousa compare recent adult migrants with origin residents and generally find positive selection on predicted origin wages for Guatemala, El Salvador and Honduras, weakening across the 2000/2014 cohorts. Yet these migrants have low absolute schooling. Honduras illustrates the measurement problem: in 2014 migrants had a lower 16+-years share than residents, 5% versus 7%, but also a lower no-schooling share, 4% versus 10%. [SOURCE: [World Bank WPS8334, methods and Table 2](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/653631518451652402/pdf/WPS8334.pdf).]

**Correction to our earlier terminology:** immigrant BA share minus origin BA share measures a difference at one educational threshold. Its sign is not the sign of selection throughout the skill distribution. Our ecological model's high fit does not identify “selection explains 91%,” actual parental ability, or genetic transmission. This interpretation is now narrowed in the [decision record](../decisions/2026-09-17-selection-measurement-scope.md); the original numerical outputs are preserved. [INFERENCE]

A useful counterexample comes from Europe: Aksoy and Poutvaara find positive educational selection among conflict-motivated migrants and women, but negative selection among men migrating for other reasons, despite similar journey-cost distributions across motivations within routes. These are survey-defined motives, not US legal admission categories. The finding challenges the claim that expensive travel selects every stream similarly. [SOURCE: [full IZA manuscript](https://docs.iza.org/dp12800.pdf), [published study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdeveco.2021.102681).]

The leading framework is **origin × arrival cohort × migration route × measured resources × destination conditions**. A professional recruitment channel, family sponsorship, protection referral and independently financed irregular journey impose different filters. Networks and policy can change those filters while geographic distance stays fixed. That is a testable alternative to a fixed origin effect, not proof that every residual is environmental. [INFERENCE]

## 3. Somalia: retain the unfavorable evidence, reject the missing premise

The latest checked national tabulation, 2024 ACS, reports Somalia-born poverty of 25% versus 12% among US-born people, and household median income of $46,800 versus $81,400. Civilian labor-force participation is 70% versus 63%; this includes people seeking work and is not age standardized. Poor income outcomes coexist with substantial participation. These are immigrants, excluding descendants. Older figures of 37% poverty and 14% BA+ describe 2019, not the latest checked population. [SOURCE: [MPI 2025 analysis of 2024 ACS](https://www.migrationpolicy.org/journal/spotlight/sub-saharan-african-immigrants-united-states), [older release](https://www.migrationpolicy.org/journal/spotlight/sub-saharan-african-immigrants-united-states-2022).]

Minnesota provides stronger youth follow-up. Its March 2026 administrative study identifies Somali-background students by Black race and Somali/Maay home language, combining immigrant and US-born students. BA completion was 15% versus 24% for white English-speaking peers; any postsecondary credential was 27% versus 42% for 2006–20 high-school cohorts. Older cohorts also had less full-time and less stable work. The 85% versus 80% with recorded Minnesota work 8–11 years after high school is a multi-year state measure, not an annual employment rate. Differential outmigration matters. [SOURCE: [MN DEED linked education/wage records](https://mn.gov/deed/newscenter/publications/trends/march-2026/somali.jsp).]

These disadvantages are substantial. They do not measure the migrants' selection relative to people remaining in Somalia. UNHCR referral prioritizes protection needs; education, work experience and language are not its selection criteria. Receiving governments decide admission under additional rules. This does not establish zero positive selection, but a long-distance resettlement journey cannot be treated as a measured +2-SD cognitive screen. [SOURCE: [UNHCR referral explanation](https://help.unhcr.org/global/resettlement/), checked September 17, 2026; INFERENCE.]

“+2 SD” requires a named trait, a reference population, a common measurement scale and observations of the actual selected cohort. Even if stipulated on cognitive tests, lower earnings would not identify why: skills, language, health, credentials, institutions and labor-market treatment can affect the same outcome. Their relative contributions are not estimated here. [IDENTIFICATION]

## 4. What the genetic hypothesis would need

Genetic contributions to individual cognitive differences are supported; dismissing them would be incorrect. Okbay et al.'s family analyses distinguish direct genetic effects from relatives' effects and correlated environments, and find substantial direct effects for cognitive performance. Their educational-attainment score also illustrates how population prediction combines several channels. These studies do not estimate Somali-versus-other migrant group differences. [SOURCE: [2022 full paper, within-family analyses and methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-022-01016-z).]

Newer evidence was checked rather than assuming old prediction limits: Wolfram et al. (2026) report a cognitive-score association of 0.355 within UK Biobank sibling pairs versus 0.406 between people, with another sibling sample and cross-ancestry prediction. This supports individual prediction; it does not explain national-origin mean differences or measure Somali migrants. The predictor is partly proprietary and all authors report company employment/equity, limiting independent reproduction of the exact score. [SOURCE: [published paper, Table 1 and methods/disclosures](https://icajournal.scholasticahq.com/article/158459.pdf).]

The inference being tested has two missing steps: **distance does not establish +2-SD cognitive selection; poor group outcomes do not decompose genetic and environmental causes.** Persistence over generations is compatible with genetic transmission, but also persistent family resources, schooling, networks, norms and institutions. Within-population heritability is not the fraction of a between-population mean gap explained by genes. These are identification limits, not an estimate that genetic contributions are zero. [INFERENCE]

The same applies to trust. Economic disadvantage, lower reported generalized trust and dishonest behavior are distinct outcomes. This pass did not establish a comparable Somali-specific generalized-trust estimate, much less genetic mediation of it. Use the [separate trust evidence](immigration-latam-southeast-asia-trust-2026-09-17.md) for cooperation mechanisms; do not use an education or income rank as a substitute. [GAP]

## 5. When each claim can travel

| Claim | Conditions needed for transfer | What remains insufficient |
|---|---|---|
| Lower earnings / fiscal pressure | Comparable skills, ages, dependents, employment, eligibility and local costs | A country name, distance, or copied Mexican dollar total |
| Descendant educational disadvantage | Actual parental resources and comparable childhood/cohort exposure | First-generation adults who are not the sampled children's parents |
| Crime effect | Same age/sex/status, offense, exposure and detection definitions | Home-country violence, schooling or all institutional residence |
| Trust/cooperation effect | Comparable survey/behavioral measures and an incumbent counterfactual | Lower income or an origin-country label |
| Genetic explanation | Directly relevant data and a design separating inherited variants from correlated environments | Residual country differences, persistence, or travel distance |

These conditions sharpen interpretation; they are not a new mandatory analysis protocol. [INFERENCE]

The practical priority is to separate recent Venezuelan, Brazilian and northern Central American cohorts by route and measured skills, then estimate common outcomes. For mature descendants, linked income and parent-birthplace evidence is more useful than another ecological origin regression. The genetic claim remains unestablished; immediate fiscal or integration questions can be investigated without first resolving it. [INFERENCE]
