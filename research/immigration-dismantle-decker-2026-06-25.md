# Nicholas Decker's pro-immigration arguments — corrected assessment

**Date:** 2026-06-25; materially revised 2026-09-05. **Section of:** consolidated dismantling memo.

**Verdict:** Increasing returns and innovation can strengthen the case for immigration, but neither alone proves an unrestricted welfare conclusion. The June memo also made two invalid rebuttals: CBO's population average is not incumbent welfare, and GDP rising while GDP per person falls is compatible with constant returns. Those rebuttals are withdrawn. [INFERENCE / formal checks below]

**Frame:** Evaluate the population, horizon and outcome actually claimed. A positive net effect need not be a Pareto improvement; a harmed renter or locality does not by itself prove aggregate harm. LLM agreement, political alignment and the repo's prior verdicts are not independent evidence. See [instrument caveat](../notes/llm-bias-caveat.md).

## Corpus and quotation provenance

The June pass recorded reading Decker's [Yes, Immigrants Must Make Us Richer](https://nicholasdecker.substack.com/p/yes-immigrants-must-make-us-richer), [The Gains From Trade Are Large](https://nicholasdecker.substack.com/p/the-gains-from-trade-are-large), [Increasing Returns and Immigration](https://nicholasdecker.substack.com/p/increasing-returns-and-immigration), [Should We Restrict Immigration To Encourage Innovation?](https://nicholasdecker.substack.com/p/should-we-restrict-immigration-to), [Brain drain is probably just totally wrong](https://nicholasdecker.substack.com/p/brain-drain-is-probably-just-totally), [On the Measurement of Human Capital](https://nicholasdecker.substack.com/p/on-the-measurement-of-human-capital) and [The Moral Duty To Marry An Immigrant](https://nicholasdecker.substack.com/p/the-moral-duty-to-marry-an-immigrant). The retained excerpts below are not a new certification of every quotation.

## 1. Increasing returns does not settle welfare; trade does not require it

> "I think the assumption of constant returns to scale is nonsense. Not only that, it is obvious nonsense… There are three main channels by which returns simply have to be increasing: specialization, ideas, and fixed costs… of course immigrants make us richer." [SOURCE: yes-immigrants-must-make-us-richer]

**What survives:** specialization, non-rival ideas and fixed costs are plausible positive mechanisms. Omitting them can matter. But complementarity is not evidence that a production function has increasing returns: `F(H,L)=sqrt(HL)` has constant returns and a positive cross-partial, so more of one labor type raises the other's marginal product. Ricardian trade can likewise generate gains with constant-returns technologies and comparative advantage. The June blanket endorsement of trade-implies-increasing-returns was incorrect. [FORMAL COUNTEREXAMPLE]

Constant returns means `F(tK,tL)=tF(K,L)`, not that increasing labor alone leaves average output unchanged. With `F(K,L)=sqrt(KL)`, `K=L=100` gives output 100; holding capital fixed and raising labor to 121 gives output 110 but output per worker `110/121=0.9091`. This directly invalidates the June claim that CBO's GDP-up/GDP-per-person-down combination is impossible under constant returns. [RECALC]

The [February 2024 CBO Outlook](https://www.cbo.gov/system/files/2024-02/59710-Outlook-2024.pdf), Box 2-1, projected roughly +2% real GDP and −0.8% real GDP per person in 2034. Its denominator includes newcomers. For example, 100 incumbents rising from income 100 to 101, plus 20 newcomers earning 50, yields a population average of 92.5 while every incumbent gains 1%. Thus CBO's average is not an empirical refutation of an incumbent-gain claim. [SOURCE; FORMAL COUNTEREXAMPLE]

The narrower objection is valid: the existence of increasing returns does not fix their magnitude or establish net gains for a specified population after adjustment and other externalities. It also does not guarantee every subgroup gains. These are distinct claims, and a defense of average gains need not meet the stronger Pareto standard. [INFERENCE]

Colas–Sachs ([10.1257/pol.20220176](https://doi.org/10.1257/pol.20220176)) models an indirect fiscal benefit of roughly $750 per additional low-skilled immigrant per year. That is evidence for a modeled complementarity channel, not proof every static cost estimate is an upper bound. The [July 2024 CBO analysis](https://www.cbo.gov/system/files/2024-07/60165-Immigration.pdf) already incorporates indirect economic and productivity effects. They cannot simply be added again. Nor may productivity gains, wages, profits and taxes be summed without a non-overlapping welfare account. [SOURCE: conceptual audit §§3,7,8; INFERENCE]

## 2. Doubling world GDP is a model result, not a settled forecast

> "estimates of the removal of transnational labor barriers, or open borders, would approximately double world GDP." [SOURCE: the-gains-from-trade-are-large]

Clemens's [2011 review](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.25.3.83) separates full from partial liberalization and stresses the large movements underlying the largest numbers. The published [Docquier–Machado–Sekkat 2015 paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/sjoe.12097) gives a 11.5–12.5% medium-term benchmark; ~4% belongs to a 2012 draft scenario. The revision changes the reference point without making any one model a policy forecast. [SOURCE: conceptual audit §5]

Decker himself qualifies structural estimates:

**"the structural estimates of immigration are likely high estimates. They generally assume that labor is identical… Obviously, if labor is not identical, this breaks down! And especially since we would expect the most skilled people to move first…"** [SOURCE: the-gains-from-trade-are-large]

That is a qualification, not a logical self-contradiction: reporting a model estimate and then explaining why it may be high can be accurate. A critique of the essay's presentation should acknowledge the caveat and distinguish it from a claim that doubling will occur. This audit does not infer the latter solely from the retained headline excerpt. [INFERENCE]

The repo's fixed-gain arithmetic gives 1.36%, 6.82% and 20.45% of a $110T world economy for 200M, 1B and 3B movers at $7,500 each annually. Under those same fixed inputs, doubling requires 14.67B additional movers. These calculations do not independently refute models with different wage gains, selection or dynamics. [RECALC]

The US housing-stock/start comparison is not a measurement of worldwide destination capacity; current construction rates are not a permanent physical maximum. Adjustment costs warrant analysis, but the June claim that housing must bind in year one of every large-flow scenario is not established by that comparison. [INFERENCE; conceptual audit §5]

## 3. Coherent economic objections need not be objections to net aggregate gains

> "The only coherent anti-immigration view is that they will vote for bad things… What is incoherent and irrational are the economic arguments against immigration." [SOURCE: yes-immigrants-must-make-us-richer]

Distribution, adjustment costs and fiscal incidence supply coherent economic questions without invoking immigrants' votes. CBO's [June 2025 state/local report](https://www.cbo.gov/system/files/2025-06/61256-immigration-state-local.pdf) retrospectively estimates costs exceeding revenues by about $9.2B for the surge population in 2023, or $9.8B with the additional channels it considered. This is evidence about that budget and period, not the net welfare effect of immigration generally. [SOURCE: conceptual audit §3 and effects matrix]

The [Dallas Fed housing study](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/papers/2026/wp2607) supplies evidence of housing-market adjustment. Its estimated price effects are conditional on its identification design; a statistically insignificant supply estimate is not proof supply is zero or cannot respond later. Rent increases harm tenants and benefit landlords before secondary effects, so renter losses cannot be treated as wholly lost national output. [SOURCE; INFERENCE]

The economic coherence of an objection does not establish that restriction is the best response. Construction, local transfers, compensation or different pacing could change the comparison. Conversely, a positive aggregate balance does not automatically compensate a losing group. [INFERENCE]

## 4. Innovation: strong evidence, with versioned and properly labeled magnitudes

> "they are directly responsible for 23% of all inventive output, and plausibl[y] responsible [for] 36% of all innovation through their spillovers… There is no solid argument against allowing high-skilled immigration." [SOURCE: should-we-restrict-immigration-to; on-the-measurement-of-human-capital] *(Decker's original reads "plausible responsible 36%"; "[y]" and "[for]" are editorial completions of his grammar, meaning unchanged.)*

The primary [Bernstein et al. manuscript, February 2025](https://web.stanford.edu/~diamondr/BDMP.pdf) finds immigrants represent 16% of inventors and authored 23% of patents; its model including collaborator spillovers attributes 32% of aggregate innovation to them. The 36% figure belongs to an earlier version, not a raw authored-patent share. The direct and modeled spillover measures are different and must not be added together. [SOURCE: paper abstract and introduction; [NBER record](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30797)]

The evidence supports an important innovation mechanism. It does not identify an optimal high-skill inflow or imply that local congestion is absent. Equally, the existence of a local cost is not enough to overturn a strong net case for admission. The June label unambiguously good was too broad, while its attempt to rebut net benefits merely by naming housing costs was also too broad. [INFERENCE]

## 5. Brain drain and the welfare frame

> "skilled emigration would actually leave the nation more skilled in the long run, not less… Immigration is just so big and so good, it is insane that we restrict it." [SOURCE: brain-drain-is-probably-just-totally]

Emigration opportunities can induce skill investment, and Decker's Filipino-nurse example addresses that mechanism. A selected country/occupation does not establish that every origin country gains from every skilled departure. Decker's own phrase "officially, the evidence is mixed" should be treated as a qualification; neither favorable examples nor a general statement of mixed evidence resolves each country-specific effect. The exact primary decomposition of this example is outside the present repair. [SOURCE: Decker's brain-drain essay; SCOPE LIMIT]

Origin welfare is relevant to a cosmopolitan policy argument. It is a scope error only if it is presented as evidence of a different outcome, such as incumbent destination income. The June assertion that origin gains are irrelevant to destination policy silently imposed an incumbent-only welfare objective. That value choice must be explicit. [FRAMING-SENSITIVE / INFERENCE]

## 6. Selection, scale and composition

Decker's examples include inventors, nurses, software engineers and lower-paid service workers. Evidence for one population or pace does not establish a universal result for every inflow. But uncertainty about extrapolation does not itself prove the sign reverses at a larger scale. Labor demand, capital and housing supply can also adjust. [INFERENCE]

Fiscal results from different countries and cohorts demonstrate that accounting balances can differ. They do not establish that skill or origin alone causes each difference, or determine the overall welfare sign of a US policy. Similarly, individual Texas offending comparisons cannot be replaced by county-level unauthorized-share coefficients; generational convergence does not make the individual comparison invalid. [SOURCE: conceptual audit §§4,6,7; INFERENCE]

The July 2024 CBO estimate is a projection of the incremental 2021–2026 surge's effects on 2024–2034 outcomes: approximately $0.9T lower covered federal deficits and $8.9T additional nominal GDP. The headline deficit estimate excludes discretionary appropriations and state/local budgets. These are not realized totals, additive welfare benefits, or an estimate for every kind of immigrant. [SOURCE: CBO July 2024, summary]

## Assessment and limits

The strongest defensible criticism concerns universal claims and unspecified welfare objectives. The mechanisms of specialization, complementarity and innovation survive; the June purported empirical refutation of incumbent gains does not. Claims about policy optimality need magnitudes, a counterfactual and explicit distributional weights. The production-function critique must be checked against actual model documentation, not inferred from an output sign pattern. No absence of a response from Decker is treated as evidence against his argument.

The India/bus essays were not reviewed because they are tangential to this immigration question. The cited studies' full microdata, each brain-drain comparison and every Decker quotation are not recertified here.
