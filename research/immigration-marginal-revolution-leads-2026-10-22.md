# Two October 2026 papers from Marginal Revolution, read in full

Date: 2026-10-22. [SOURCE / CALCULATION / FRAMING-SENSITIVE] Reading record; narrative authorship remains

**Result:** neither paper moves the main case ($368–437bn, v9). Tyler Cowen posted both as arguments that
immigration's economic gains outweigh its fiscal cost. Read in full, each one cuts the other way for the
Mexican-origin group. In Battisti, Peri & Romano's model, natives in the US lose from an inflow less skilled
than residents, and the group is less skilled than the model's low-skill case. In Hassan, Kalyani & Lopez Del
Valle, ancestry carries ideas only from the origin country's own technology stock, and Mexico is the origin of
at most 2 of the 1,448 foreign technologies they find.

## 1. "The net fiscal impact of immigration should include gains from trade"

Cowen, 2026-10-20, on Battisti, Peri & Romano, NBER w35901: "the gains from trade effect can be small, and it
still will overwhelm what otherwise appear to be net fiscal costs." He rejects the paper's own mechanism and
puts market size, increasing returns and innovation in its place.

**Steel-man.** A fiscal ledger misses what natives gain as workers, consumers and owners of capital. A model
that joins the labor market and the budget finds natives gaining in 14 of 17 OECD countries from inflows with
the skill mix of 2019–24 arrivals, and both skill groups gaining in most of them.

**What the paper finds for the US** [SOURCE: w35901 Tables 6 and 8, pp. 4–5, 21, 32]. Each scenario adds one
percentage point to the labor force; welfare is natives' steady-state consumption equivalent.
- With the 2019–24 arrivals' mix (38.6% with a tertiary degree against 30.6% of residents): **+0.07%**;
  low-skilled natives +0.18%, high-skilled −0.04%.
- Same skills as residents: 0.00%.
- An inflow twice as intensive in low- and medium-skilled workers relative to natives (Scenario 3):
  **−0.10%**; low-skilled natives −0.20%.
- The authors: the US effect "operates almost entirely through the labor market rather than through the fiscal
  channel".

The labor-market gain comes from immigrants accepting lower wages "because they bargain from a weaker outside
option", so firms post more vacancies (p. 4). That is the mechanism Cowen says he does not accept. The fiscal
block holds pure public goods, defense and debt service, 20.7% of US spending, fixed in aggregate (Table 1,
p. 27); the main case holds the same lines at zero response, so the two agree there.

**Against the group.** [CALCULATION from the account's earnings file] Among positive earners, 19.0% of the union
hold a BA against 43.3% of other earners [DATA: `matched_benefits_2026_09_19/derived/skill_composition.csv`,
`below_ba`]. The union's ratio of BA to non-BA earners is 0.31 of everyone else's, below Scenario 3's 0.5. In the
paper's own terms, the group sits past the case where US natives lose [INFERENCE: the paper's tertiary category
and the CPS BA split are close but not identical; the model was not run on a shock the size of the group].

**Cowen's claim, in magnitudes.** [CALCULATION] Offsetting the main case takes $368.0–436.6bn, 1.26–1.49% of
2024 GDP ($29,300bn, BEA). The National Academies put the surplus from all US immigrants, 16.5% of hours, at 0.31%
of natives' income with capital fixed, and call it an upper limit (NAS 2017, p. 171). The offset needs four to
five times that. The channels Cowen names are priced (FAQ entry 4): the production gain, $8.2–12.5bn, is inside
the main case; city size net of the schooling mix ($15.3bn) and consumer-side scale ($2.3bn) are in the
fiscal-plus-social total; the patent term, +$37–57bn inside an interval of about ±$490bn, is not added
(ladder 201). The claim holds for skilled inflows, where the fiscal cost is small. For this group it needs
gains several times the published surplus.

## 2. "The causal effect of foreign ideas on U.S. innovation"

Cowen, 2026-10-21, on Hassan, Kalyani & Lopez Del Valle, NBER w35900 (with Burchardi, Chaney and Hassan, the
same research line as the ancestry instrument in ladder 182 and the patent null in ladder 201).

**Steel-man.** Immigrants carry their countries' ideas. Counties with more ancestry from a country, instrumented
by historical settlement, absorb more of its technologies and then invent more US technologies on top of them;
54% of US technologies build on a foreign input.

**What carries over.** [SOURCE: w35900 pp. 7, 11, 58] The channel runs through the origin country's own
patented technologies. Of 20,786 technologies, 1,448 were born abroad: Japan 582, Germany 356, the UK 162. Mexico
is not listed; it is inside "Others (≤ 2 each)". The authors also find that "ancestry itself has no effect once
those inputs are there." [INFERENCE] For a group whose origin supplies at most 0.14% of foreign technologies,
the channel is about zero. It says nothing about the Mexican-origin group's own inventiveness, which ladder 201
already treats through Burchardi et al.'s schooling gradient.
