## Household adjustment: the denominator changes the story

In Kreider and Werner's January 2025 US working paper, the Medicaid subsample comprises adults aged 65 and older with daily-living difficulties. Table 9 estimates a **7.5 percentage-point increase (SE 3.5)** in having family as the primary helper, **conditional on receiving help**. The unconditional probability of having a family helper changes **−2.0 points (SE 2.6)**. The positive conditional share therefore does not measure more caregiving households, caregiver hours or lost employment. It also does not establish that those losses are zero. [SOURCE: [author paper](https://amandakreider.github.io/files/Immigration-Home-Care-2025.pdf), §6.3.1, Tables 8–10]

Austrian care-reform evidence supports a labor-supply mechanism, while US youth evidence points toward additional work after enforcement-related income pressure. Neither identifies v9's remaining-household population. The precise code boundary is item 11's fixed claimant incomes during statutory recalculation, not a claim that every income in v9 is fixed. Unpaid-care labor supply was already explicitly left unpriced. [DATA/SOURCE: [household evidence](../notes/v9-behavioral-feedback-2026-10-16/households.md)]

## Production: observed disruption is not observed task reassignment

The Korean guest-worker interruption paper measures closures, reported production setbacks and changes in firm-average wages. Its Table 10 does **not** track the same worker's task assignment. Section 5.3 allows either reassignment or retention of less-skilled workers to explain the wage pattern. That is useful disruption evidence, but weaker evidence for a particular organizational mechanism than its abstract suggests. The pandemic setting and selection of surviving firms also limit transport. [SOURCE: [Lee, Peri and Yang, March 2026](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34927/w34927.pdf), §5.3 and Tables 9–10]

The US H-2B lottery evidence supports productive complementarity. Its authors explicitly describe their estimated elasticity as including market imperfections; adding a generic coordination penalty could count part of that response twice. The international-team experiment establishes coordination difficulties in newly formed remote teams, with no employer facilitating coordination, not a permanent US economy-wide penalty. [SOURCE: [Clemens and Lewis, July 2026 reprint](https://www.piie.com/sites/default/files/2026-07/wp26-11.pdf), §8.2; [Lyons author manuscript](https://gps.ucsd.edu/_files/faculty/lyons/lyons_research_072016.pdf), conclusion; [production evidence](../notes/v9-behavioral-feedback-2026-10-16/production.md)]

Losing complementary workers and incurring communication costs while working together are different mechanisms; they need not favor the same counterfactual. Neither source establishes how much v9's actual parameters absorb. The overlap warning is a reason to reconcile the effects before adding them, not evidence that they are already counted. [INFERENCE]

## Coping: stable outcomes can conceal effort, but movement is not itself harm

The updated Danish neighborhood paper finds native exits concentrated in particular housing types and considerable replacement by other natives. Its §5.5/Figure 6 finds **no accelerating exit threshold** in the studied setting. This weakens an automatic runaway-exit story without ruling out avoidance costs elsewhere. The Spanish evidence also shows why construction and housing competition can generate native outflows without identifying a net loss. [SOURCE: [Boje-Kovacs et al., August 2026 author paper](https://www.vpsantanna.com/files/research/immigrants_and_native_flight.pdf); [Moraga et al., 2017 working paper](https://docs.iza.org/dp11143.pdf), §§3.2/4.3]

A bounded feedback can persist without accelerating because stabilizing forces offset it. The exit result therefore does not settle the exit–service feedback as a whole. [INFERENCE]

The Danish refugee-school study reports an original-cohort check that follows children who move. Thus not every outcome null can be dismissed as “only stayers were measured.” That check still does not value the effort or money used to adapt; its supplementary table was not independently retrieved. Modern Oslo school-border estimates remain inconclusive. [SOURCE: [Hassan et al.](https://academic.oup.com/esr/article/39/3/352/6843667), analytic strategy and robustness discussion; [coping evidence](../notes/v9-behavioral-feedback-2026-10-16/coping.md)]

## Institutions: evidence for parts of the chain, not the whole chain

UK tax-letter experiments observe actual payments by overdue self-assessment debtors. They establish a behavioral margin, but cannot cleanly separate new revenue from earlier payment. An Argentine experiment provides counterevidence: fairness/peer messages had no significant average effect, while deterrence worked. Historical US city evidence observes tax and spending policy changes after immigration, not merely attitudes; it does not identify contemporary tax noncompliance. These are separate links, not an estimated modern ethnic-composition effect on institutional performance. [SOURCE: [Hallsworth et al. accepted manuscript](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/88777/1/WRAP-behavioralist-tax-collector-Vlaev-2017.pdf), Table 4 and persistence; [Castro and Scartascini working paper](https://publications.iadb.org/publications/english/document/Tax-Compliance-and-Enforcement-in-the-Pampas-Evidence-from-a-Field-Experiment.pdf), §4; [Tabellini manuscript](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/19-005_a4261e39-175c-4b3f-969a-8e1ce818a3d8.pdf), §5.1]

No identified contemporary lobbying or coalition-to-policy pathway was established in this bounded pass. That adjacent question remains open, not negative. [GAP: [institutions/local evidence](../notes/v9-behavioral-feedback-2026-10-16/institutions-local.md)]

## Local finance: the source has changed materially

The July 2026 Cornaggia–Cornaggia–Israelsen–Zimmerschied paper replaces the previously audited January 2025 version. It now reports an ancestry-based instrument with first-stage diagnostics, total revenues and expenses, transfers, operating margins and an enforcement comparison. The old “no first stage/no total expenditure/no inward-transfer regression” objections cannot be applied to it. [SOURCE: [current author paper](https://kylezim.com/media/Immigration%20and%20Municipal%20Bond%20Yields.pdf), title page, Tables 3/9–11]

Its benchmark IV estimate is **−4.8 basis points (SE 1.5)** in bond yield spread for a one-percentage-point rise in all-origin five-year immigrant inflow relative to prior population. Later operating margins improve; current margins are imprecise. State transfers matter. The authors' “77%” decomposes only the combined implied sales-tax and state-transfer increase, not total national benefit. The instrument's exclusion restriction, population mismatch and national financing remain unresolved. [SOURCE: Table 3 col. 8, Table 9, p31 n34]

This is evidence against assuming inevitable local fiscal deterioration. Transfers can stabilize recipients while shifting costs elsewhere; consolidation cancels the transfer, not the services or financing consequences. The skill-specific results in Mayda et al. point in different directions, so no uniform local response follows. [INFERENCE; SOURCE: [Mayda et al.](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/swp2023-57.pdf), Tables 6/9c; detailed comparison in the institutions/local note]

## What would independently settle the remaining gap

The highest-value next evidence is a linked panel following the **same nonmember residents, firms and governments**, including movers: work and care hours, worker tasks and value added, coping expenditure, taxes and services, and intergovernmental financing after a credible exposure change. Each candidate needs its own outcome; these data cannot be collapsed into a single “social dysfunction” proxy. [INFERENCE]

For a new dollar term, the decisive requirements are a relevant population and horizon, an identified change beyond the existing modeled response, and fiscal or welfare accounting that avoids duplication. For rejecting the strong omission story, useful evidence includes adaptation that removes persistent losses, outcomes *and coping resources* remaining unchanged, or fiscal/service stabilization rather than accelerating exit. Source tables and replicable administrative outcomes would adjudicate these claims; agreement between models would not. [INFERENCE]

No numerical v9 change follows from this pass.
