# Latin America, Southeast Asia and social trust

**Verdict:** Some Mexican-origin findings transfer to similarly selected Central American streams; a single Latin American fiscal/crime verdict does not. Established Filipino and Vietnamese communities support a favorable economic judgment, with substantial variation elsewhere in Southeast Asia. Trust is a credible cost channel, including effects on local cooperation, but the evidence does not establish inevitable national decline or a centuries-long recovery clock. [INFERENCE]

Checked September 17, 2026. Scope: US immigration and descendants; international studies inform mechanisms, not US origin-specific effect sizes. This extends the [executed frontier](immigration-frontier-execution-2026-09-17.md). No new microdata estimates were run. LLM framing bias matters here: economic success must not silently become complete host benefit, and lower reported trust must not become dishonesty or permanent cultural incapacity.

These are model-based estimates, not a September 2026 census or border encounters. The definition includes temporary/liminal categories such as pending asylum, parole and TPS; it does not mean every person entered illegally or lacked temporary permission to remain. MPI revises earlier estimates using its updated method, so subtracting Pew's 2023 estimate from MPI's 2024 estimate would not isolate population growth. [SOURCE: [MPI Tables 1–3 and methodology](https://www.migrationpolicy.org/commentary/us-unauthorized-immigrant-population-new-peak).]

The strongest transferable concern is **low formal education and earnings capacity in particular streams**, with consequences depending on age, dependents, employment, eligibility and receiving-area capacity. Our held CPS table puts foreign-born BA attainment near 9–11% for Mexico, Guatemala, Honduras and El Salvador, versus about 32% for Cuba and 47% for Colombia. These all-status adult stocks do not identify education at arrival, unauthorized residents or the latest arrivals. Generation here uses **CPS-native citizenship categories and reported parental birthplaces**, not ethnicity alone; a foreign-born father's country takes precedence, otherwise the mother's. Native includes birth in US territories and birth abroad to American parents. Third-plus is the parent-based comparison, not verified grandparent ancestry. Repeated CPS participation means records are not unique people; small cells lack design-based intervals in this output. These are descriptive point estimates, not significance-ranked countries or matched parent–child change. The pooled earnings series is nominal, so it is not used for dollar comparisons here.

**Claim to keep:** the Mexican/Central American low-education pattern is real in these stocks, but Colombian/Cuban profiles and differing descendant outcomes refute treating LATAM as interchangeable. **Claim to reject:** the Mexican fiscal estimate or a Mexican crime ratio applies to all LATAM or all unauthorized residents. The checked data do not identify that joint origin/status estimand. The held ACS institutional table also lacks exact G2 and includes non-prison institutions; it cannot rescue that extrapolation. ## 2. Is Southeast Asian immigration likely positive?

**A favorable assessment of economic incorporation is warranted, particularly for Filipinos and Vietnamese.** The CPS table defined by reported parental birthplace strengthens this beyond a generic “US-born Asian” comparison; it does not link parent records. Independent tabulations of another survey are consistent: Pew's pooled 2021–23 ACS analysis finds Vietnamese BA attainment of 29% among immigrants and 59% among US-born adults, with English proficiency of 36% and 90% respectively. Filipino poverty is 7% overall; immigrant adult BA attainment is 52%. [SOURCE: [Vietnamese](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/fact-sheet/asian-americans-vietnamese-in-the-u-s/), [Filipino](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/fact-sheet/asian-americans-filipinos-in-the-u-s/), published May 1, 2025. INFERENCE: favorable economic judgment.]

Counterevidence matters: US-born BA attainment is 31% for Cambodians and 24% for Laotians in the Pew sheets; Burmese poverty is 21%. Fully 83% of US-born Burmese are under 18, limiting claims about mature descendants. Smaller-group sheets use origin-alone definitions, unlike the broader Filipino/Vietnamese identity coverage; US-born also includes later generations. [SOURCE: [Cambodian](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/fact-sheet/asian-americans-cambodians-in-the-u-s/), [Laotian](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/fact-sheet/asian-americans-laotians-in-the-u-s/), [Burmese](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/fact-sheet/asian-americans-burmese-in-the-u-s/).]

The favorable judgment is strongest for established communities' productive incorporation. **A complete regional net fiscal balance or causal gain to incumbent Americans remains unmeasured here.** Education does not establish low offending, high trust or every future intake's benefit. India and China are not Southeast Asia. Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and the smaller national groups were not separately assessed; no regional weighted aggregate was calculated. [FRAMING-SENSITIVE; GAP]

## 3. Trust: a serious concern, without a fixed historical clock

**The strongest adverse evidence is more than a compositional artifact.** Dinesen, Schaeffer and Sønderskov's review covers 87 studies and 1,001 estimates. Its pooled partial correlation between ethnic diversity and social trust is −0.026, with stronger associations for local settings and neighbor trust. This is a modest average association; most underlying designs are observational and the authors explicitly reject interpreting the aggregate as a causal effect. Diversity is also not identical to immigration volume or national origin. [SOURCE: [2020 review, results and causal-identification discussion](https://pure.au.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/230771020/Ethnic_Diversity_and_Social_Trust_Final_version_2020.pdf).]

A stronger mechanism study uses constrained allocation in French public housing: Algan, Hémet and Laitin find weaker collective upkeep and worse shared spaces in more diverse blocks. Assignment is argued to be conditionally exogenous, not a researcher-run random allocation; building characteristics matter. This supports a local cooperation cost under particular conditions, not a LATAM/SEA-specific national effect. [SOURCE: [2016 published study](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/686010); relevant identification/results read in the [February 2015 author manuscript](https://www.yann-algan.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Algan-et-al-2015-3.pdf).]

**Persistence is real; “centuries” is not an estimated minimum.** Algan and Cahuc's inherited-trust work supports intergenerational persistence, without establishing genetic determination or a recovery duration. Danish survey trust rose from 47% in 1979 to 79% in 2009. Denmark was already comparatively trusting; this does not show a low-trust society can be rebuilt from scratch in 30 years or identify immigration's effect. It does demonstrate material change within decades. [SOURCE: [Algan–Cahuc](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faer.100.5.2060); [Danish study](https://lanekenworthy.net/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/reading-sonderskovdinesen2014.pdf).]

Structured contact offers a possible moderator: randomized Norwegian military room assignment produced suggestive evidence of improved outgroup trust after eight weeks. In the read working-paper version, the main binary-treatment estimate was significant at 10%, not 5%. Intensive equal-status cooperation cannot be equated to ordinary neighborhood mixing. [SOURCE: [published study record](https://www.ffi.no/en/publications-archive/trust-ethnic-diversity-and-personal-contact-a-field-experiment), [February 2019 manuscript, Table 2](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/198875/1/cesifo1_wp7515.pdf).]

Our corrected GSS results also deserve to remain visible: Hispanic G1/G2/G3+ trust is approximately 10–12 percentage points below the white third-plus comparison after age, education and survey-year adjustment. The direct adjusted generational contrast is too imprecise to establish convergence or its absence. These are pooled 2000–24 opinion differences, not national-origin estimates or measured trustworthiness. [DATA: [full-denominator/design-corrected results](../infra/immigration-fiscal/frontier_execution_2026_09_17/social/RESULT.md).]

Separate three propositions:

1. Newcomers report less trust, lowering the population average through composition.
2. Immigration changes incumbents' trust or cooperation.
3. These changes durably damage institutions and welfare.

For a hypothetical population of 900 people with 70% trusting and 100 newcomers with 40% trusting, the combined rate is 67%, with no change among incumbents. The compositional change is real and potentially relevant; it does not by itself demonstrate the second or third proposition. Reporting distrust is also different from behaving dishonestly. [ARITHMETIC / MEASUREMENT]

**Practical implication:** preserving trust is a legitimate objective. Carry a plausible, unpriced cooperation cost rather than silently assigning zero; do not turn it into an invented national dollar loss. The useful discriminator is change in the same incumbents and newcomers after plausibly exogenous arrivals, alongside observed cooperation and public-service performance, compared across inflow pace and institutional conditions. This can distinguish composition, spillovers and lasting damage. [INFERENCE; FRAMING-SENSITIVE]

## Coverage and what would change the judgment

The review covered the new MPI table, held CPS origin outputs and generator, six Pew origin sheets, corrected GSS results, and the trust papers above. No new origin-specific crime/fiscal account or full regional weighting was built. Border encounters, ACS institutions as incarceration, and education as a trust proxy were deliberately not substituted for the missing outcomes.

The main explanations are selection/cohort differences for economic outcomes, and both composition and context-dependent spillovers for trust. Durable adverse incumbent outcomes following credibly assigned inflows would strengthen the cost case; convergence in behavior and maintained public goods would weaken it. Neither outcome requires waiting centuries. These are discriminating research questions, not changes to the project's central question or an adopted new analysis protocol. [INFERENCE]
