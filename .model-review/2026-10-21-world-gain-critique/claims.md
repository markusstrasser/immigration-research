# Claims to attack (written by the analyst, Claude; attack them)

Context: a "world ledger" compares, for one year (2024), the Mexican-origin lineage in the US (44.90M people incl.
US-born descendants) against the counterfactual where they live in Mexico. Parties: the group, other US residents,
Mexico's residents. Readings R1/R2/R3 are defined in the attached RESULT excerpt; executed totals in readings_subset.csv
(world_total_bn = group + other US residents + Mexico's residents, equal dollar weights; lambda variants charge payers
the marginal cost of public funds).

The analyst's claims:
1. Most defensible pay counterfactual is R3 (Mexican Migration Project same-person hourly gain 2.0 for the first
   generation), because it follows the same people and handles selection better than choosing a percentile (R1 p56,
   R2 p70). The selection-corrected place premium for Mexico (Clemens–Montenegro–Pritchett) is higher, ~2.5x, so R3
   is if anything conservative on the group's gain.
2. Household consumption PPP is the right deflator for welfare (vs output service prices in R2).
3. Public goods: average cost (R1/R3) probably overstates the group's valuation of general administration; no WTP
   measure exists; unsettled.
4. lambda = 1 is the least defensible central; 1.16–1.29 is defensible (all sources > 1; 1.29 implied by Hendren's
   welfare weights).
5. Therefore the most defensible world gain is about $45–170bn a year, central near $100bn, and it turns negative at
   lambda 1.5 under R2 or R3.
6. Other US residents lose $340–401bn a year at lambda 1 and $419–499bn at lambda 1.29 in every reading; the world
   total is a large transfer with a thin remainder.
7. The figure excludes any loss of US output through institutions/politics (unpriced; a persistent 1% of GDP is
   ~$293bn) and innovation/agglomeration effects; the other-US-residents line includes native wages (−$0.7bn long
   run), rents, induced taxes on the production gain (+$13.2bn), crime, congestion, unreimbursed care.
8. Irreversibility: the stock (citizen second generation) is irreversible; under uncertainty an irreversible decision
   needs expected gain above cost by an option-value margin, which a thin and sign-uncertain world gain does not clear;
   the decision-relevant margin is future inflows, each admitted cohort becoming irreversible once its children are born.

Task: attack each claim from first principles and the literature. For each: is it right, wrong, or incomplete; the
strongest counter-argument; what parameter value you would defend and why (cite sources you are confident exist; say
when unsure). Check internal consistency against the attached numbers (e.g. does applying lambda to the whole
other-residents line double count; is lambda applied symmetrically to Mexico's fiscal side; does R3 combine its pay
parameter with R1's other choices coherently). Name anything material the analyst left out, in either direction
(e.g. omitted gains: agglomeration, innovation, remittance welfare, consumer surplus; omitted costs). End with your own
defensible central and range for the world gain, and say whether claim 8's conclusion follows.
