**Verdict:** Five original media pieces add two useful policy-design leads and one new fiscal study; most underlying economic mechanisms are already integrated. The strongest correction to a possible hostile reading is that Boustan explicitly acknowledges Mexico's unconditional income gap in her podcast, and Pritchett explicitly distinguishes rotational work visas from open borders. The new pieces do not establish a complete welfare ranking of immigration policies. [INFERENCE; source ledger below]

## Primary checks actually completed

**P1 — Ozimek and Eckhardt, [The Near-Term Fiscal Impact of H-1B Workers at the Federal and State-and-Local Levels](https://eig.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/H-1B-Fiscal-Impacts.pdf), EIG, March 2026.** Read PDF opening results, Table 1, “Defining Who, What, and When”, and wage construction, PDF pages 1–7. The household model uses a 2023 near-term snapshot, includes dependents, and bases its wage input on median approved-extension wages, treated as a midpoint of a six-year term. These are constructed typical-household results, not linked observed tax records for the entire visa population. Table 1 gives $30,049 federal and $5,040 state/local; the latter is an average across states. Its model projects $65,017/$10,650 with both reforms. The report includes near-term housing/business responses but excludes longer-term hospital expansion and explicitly separates lifetime analysis. These distinctions qualify the headline numbers. **[GAP]** The remaining tax/spending appendices and behavioral reform calibration were not audited or reproduced; do not grade the numerical model verified end-to-end. [SOURCE: P1, PDF pp.1–7]

**P2 — Abramitzky, Boustan, Jácome and Pérez, [Intergenerational Mobility of Immigrants in the United States over Two Centuries](https://elisajacome.github.io/Jacome/ImmigrantMobility_AER.pdf), AER 111(2), 2021.** Read introduction and data sections, printed pp.580–586. The equal-parent-rank comparison reports a pooled 5–6 rank-point advantage at parental percentile 25; this does not imply universal unconditional parity. Historical father-son links use income scores; modern Opportunity Insights data link tax records and exclude pairs lacking Social Security numbers. The separate GSS sample includes some undocumented-parent families. Modern within-county comparisons reduce the average mobility advantage by about one quarter. This supports the podcast's distinction, while limiting cohort transfer. No econometric reproduction conducted. [SOURCE: P2, pp.580–586]

**P3 — IRS, [Foreign agricultural workers](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-agricultural-workers).** Current official text confirms exemption from Social Security/Medicare taxes for covered H-2A services; lack of mandatory income-tax withholding is not exemption from income-tax liability. This verifies C1's legal baseline, not the effect of his proposed reform. Also checked [Publication 5144](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p5144.pdf), Q3–Q4; current webpage governs over the older leaflet. [SOURCE: P3]

The abstract reports industry/downstream income gains and no detectable patenting response. The full study was previously inspected in the local September 5 paper review, which also records short-run native STEM losses. ## Local integration anchors and novelty limits

**Skill selection and beneficiary flags:** P1/C2/C3 deliberately concern employer-sponsored skilled H-1B households; this is policy selection, not evidence for average unauthorized arrivals or low-skilled permanent settlement. P1's estimand is modeled near-term public-budget balance; the beneficiary is government budgets, not every native worker or renter. P4's selected high-skill historical cap shock concerns incumbent earnings by industry/occupation, not all-skill admission. P2 concerns observed children of immigrants conditional on parental income, including low-income parents, but is neither a randomly selected entrant experiment nor a fiscal study; families' upward mobility is its outcome. C1 concerns temporary agricultural work and a proposed tax regime; C5 concerns broader rotational labor, with migrant, sending-country and host benefits asserted separately but not measured jointly. A literature dominated by H-1B evidence cannot settle a low-skill policy claim. [SOURCE: primary scope definitions above; INFERENCE]

## Disconfirmation and remaining gaps

The contrary tests were: find explicit author concessions rather than infer universal claims; inspect the original fiscal model's population, wage statistic and horizon; check whether local research already treats temporary status differently; distinguish one-time costs from annual earnings. These tests narrowed the criticism of C3/C4/C5 and weakened the certainty of C2's fee claim. [INFERENCE]
