1. **“Every instrument is weak, rejected, or both” is contradicted by its own table.** `RESULT.md:109-117` includes the foreign-born/value shift-share arm with first-stage F **38.4**, coefficient **+0.428 (0.304)** and no J statistic. `src/analyze.py:89-92` passes one aggregated shift-share instrument for one endogenous treatment. `src/estim.py:87-95` correctly leaves J unavailable when the number of excluded instruments equals the number of endogenous regressors. No overidentification statistic is possible for that arm; blank J is neither rejection nor validation. The result is imprecise and exclusion remains unverified, but this is different from weak or rejected. The approximate 95% interval +0.428 ± 1.96×0.304 is **[−0.168,+1.024]**; it spans zero without identifying a zero effect. Distinguish this arm from gravity specifications with low first stages and/or rejected overidentification tests.

2. **A single-origin instrument is not invalid merely because its shift is a scalar.** `RESULT.md:119-123` repeats the earlier scalar-shift fallacy. `src/prep.py:112-120` constructs growth scalars and initial local exposure shares; `src/analyze.py:65-69` constructs `bartik_mex = shares['mex'] * g_all['mex']`. With a nonzero national shift this is a rescaled baseline-exposure instrument and still varies across localities. It may be weak or endogenous; it does not cease to be an instrument mathematically. Its exclusion restriction depends entirely on the baseline-exposure comparison, rather than being rescued by independent within-period national-shock variation. National Mexico-born stock decline also cannot establish that no neighborhood diffusion occurred: local relocation, enclave expansion, differential settlement and offsetting exits can coexist with aggregate decline. The gravity instrument uses local spatial distributions; the sign of a national net change does not by itself falsify that mechanism. Correct the explanation while retaining observed weak/rejected gravity diagnostics.

3. **Metro +1.32/+3.43 are descriptive associations.** `RESULT.md:19-21` says an inflow “raises” rents and values; `:234-240` calls the demand effect unambiguous. `src/analyze2.py:151-173` forms weighted averages of tract ACS medians, constructs foreign-born stock change divided by base population, and calls the regression with **no controls and period fixed effects only**, clustering on CBSA. There is no excluded instrument or local demand-shock adjustment. Those coefficients cannot separate immigration-caused housing demand from migrants selecting growing labor/housing markets. The exposure is a net population-stock change, not observed gross entry. It is also assembled over price-valid tracts (not necessarily all metro residents), so “metro's initial population” should retain its sample construction. Recommended: “In these pooled metro comparisons, a one-point higher foreign-born stock change/base-population ratio is associated with about 1.32% higher rent growth and 3.43% higher value growth.” No empirical causal confirmation follows.

4. **The $1,000 ceiling is not an identified bound.** `RESULT.md:261-265` first says sign is unresolved, then declares what natives pay must be under roughly $1,000 per group resident-year. `src/dollars.py:40-60` selects a set of OLS and matched-measure arms; `:63-83` converts their point coefficients to dollar scenarios; `:96-100` reports extrema across that chosen set. The standard errors are carried as metadata but are not propagated into a confidence bound, and no restriction bounds omitted-variable or measurement bias. The same draft `RESULT.md:191-198` says neither raw nor corrected magnitude is credible. A finite range across selected specifications is not a finite bound on the causal amenity cost. Replace “bound” with “illustrative range across these estimated associations”; do not report “under $1,000” as a ceiling.

5. **Related accounting caution if it reaches the index:** `RESULT.md:267-273` and `src/dollars.py:4-8` infer that within-metro relative price changes are only transfers and nearly net to zero by construction. Fixed-effect demeaning constrains residual averages, not the underlying welfare loss. Amenity deterioration can change capitalized rents while causing real utility loss; relative prices alone neither quantify it nor prove it sums to zero. Landlord–renter transfers are a component, not a complete welfare accounting. This reinforces the lane's sound decision to leave the amenity unpriced.
