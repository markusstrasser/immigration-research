## 3. Ed West: diversity, trust and institutional capacity

**Source:** Ed West, [It was ten years ago today](https://www.edwest.co.uk/p/it-was-ten-years-ago-today), 4 April 2023. **Access:** public full text. **Locator:** paragraphs following the discussion of David Goodhart's “Too Diverse?” and the paragraph on liberalism failing in diverse societies. **Prior coverage:** no specific essay audit located.

West argues that less-selective large-scale immigration tends to erode high-trust norms and that diversity can impair common institutions. He expressly allows selective immigration to augment desirable social qualities. The essay is a retrospective polemic rather than a new study. [SOURCE: article.]

**Verdict: negative trust association integrated; generalized institutional-collapse inference overstated.** Dinesen, Schaeffer and Sønderskov's [2020 full-text meta-analysis](https://pure.au.dk/ws/files/230771020/Ethnic_Diversity_and_Social_Trust_Final_version_2020.pdf), pp.450–451, covers 1,001 estimates from 87 studies and reports pooled partial correlation −0.0256 (SE 0.0044). [SOURCE: paper, Figures 1–2.]

**Disconfirmation:** the same paper, p.460, reports heterogeneous national trends, including rising Danish trust during diversification, while acknowledging negative local associations. This rejects an inevitable national-decline story without proving the local cost zero. [SOURCE: paper's time-trends discussion; INFERENCE.]

This is a genuine administrative-data route for distinguishing composition; it is not Bek's unidentified 60%→21% table.
