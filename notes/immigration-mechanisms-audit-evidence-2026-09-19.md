## 1. Confirmed: single-origin IV explanation is mathematically wrong

**Priority: high.** `research/immigration-native-displacement-to-transfers-2026-09-18.md:403-411` treats a negative national Mexico-born stock change as a reason the displacement-looking coefficient is a failure, and calls other windows well identified. `research/immigration-employment-entry-displacement-2026-09-18.md:5` attributes weak pooled strength and changing results to the near-zero or negative national shift; `research/immigration-school-flight-and-public-goods-2026-09-18.md:794-798` inherits the claim that the instrument is weak because inflow stopped.

The principal implementation is `infra/immigration-fiscal/displacement_transfers_2026_09_18/estimate.py:100-107`: Z is the baseline share divided by baseline population, multiplied by a single national shift constant. Lines 132-145 fit the first stage and 2SLS. Within one window, multiplying this single instrument by any nonzero constant leaves its first-stage F and the 2SLS estimate unchanged. Changing the sign of Z changes both first-stage and reduced-form coefficients, leaving their ratio unchanged. At exactly zero the instrument vanishes; near zero does not mechanically imply low F. For pooled windows changing their relative shifts can alter pooled variation; this algebra does not claim otherwise.

**Independent numerical verification:** `/private/tmp/immigration-iv-scale-probe-20260919.py` reconstructs the 2008–2018 Mexico-born SSI model from the principal derived panels using NumPy/pandas rather than the original estimator. With 341 metros and national stock change −240,775, instrument scales 1, −1 and 0.001 each produce first-stage F **44.00252051** and IV **+0.2130079222**. Thus the actual negative-shift arm retains the positive transfer association under sign reversal and a thousand-fold rescaling. [CALCULATION]

**Correction:** preserve the observed low F, placebo failures and wide intervals, but explain these through the correlation of baseline settlement exposure with local changes, changing samples/windows, dynamic confounding or pooled-shock composition. Do not use the sign or small nonzero magnitude of the single national scalar as an identification argument. A positive-transfer coefficient with failed placebo remains unidentified; this correction does not rescue it as causal.

Related source: [Jaeger, Ruist and Stuhler, NBER 24285](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24285), official abstract verified, says persistent settlement patterns can mix current and lagged responses. Its full PDF returned an internal web-tool error in this audit; the algebra and numerical check above do not depend on that source.

## 2. Confirmed: failed identification is promoted to a null; the exclusion test is mislabeled

**Priority: high.** Displacement memo `:337-352` calls correlation between Z and the baseline outcome a direct test of the exclusion restriction. The code confirms that is exactly what is tested at `estimate.py:150-155`: regression of `y0` on `Z`, separately from the prior-change placebo. Exclusion for a differenced outcome concerns correlation with the unobserved outcome change; a baseline-level association alone is not a direct test. Conversely, a nonsignificant baseline association does not validate exclusion. Prior-change placebos, dynamic confounding and implausible extrapolation are substantive warnings and can support withholding a causal conclusion.

The same memo `:337-338`, `:792-793`, and the local-spending memo `research/immigration-local-spending-composition-2026-09-18.md:3,32-42` say invalidated opposite-signed estimates become a null. That is incorrect. They become **unidentified**. An invalid confidence interval cannot exclude a harmful causal response merely because its positive endpoint is small. The introductory displacement caveat `:18-20` already correctly says SSDI is unmeasured and the hypothesis untested on its most likely margin.

**Correction:** use “the measured associations do not establish displacement onto transfers; the causal effect remains unresolved.” Rename the baseline test “baseline balance/mean-reversion diagnostic.” Retain the actual negative associations as descriptions, with their exact population and period.

**Related overclaim:** employment memo `:5` says a flat college-control group means the no-college result is not metro-demand bias, while conceding the two effects are not distinguishable. A nonsignificant control cannot establish absence of demand confounding; skill-specific demand shocks and power remain possible. Conversely displacement memo `:398-401` assumes low-skill supply cannot affect graduate employment at all, which is not a general-equilibrium implication. Do not make the college control an exclusion oracle.

**Local spending estimand mismatch:** local-spending `:38` compares the confidence interval at its own mean stock-share change with the paper's effect at a different mean unauthorized-arrival flow. It cannot statistically refute the paper's estimand. Section `:42` already acknowledges the treatment mismatch and should govern the headline. The paper-table claim that education's own budget-share coefficient was not negative is a separate, more defensible criticism; I read the repo's transcription but did not independently reopen the original two PDF tables.

## 3. Confirmed: school tuition's welfare interpretation omits randomized benefits

**Priority: medium-high if exported to a headline or dollar ledger.** School memo `:633-665` calls the 2012–2014 DC voucher cohorts the only clean US experiment and uses test-score results to classify private tuition as largely defensive expenditure. It also says this evidence decides whether tuition is defensive (`:635`). This is stronger than the experiment supports.

The same federal evaluator's **earlier** randomized DC evaluation assigned about 2,300 applicants in 2004–2005 and reported high-school graduation **82% versus 70%**, based on parent reports, with higher parent satisfaction and perceived safety. It found no conclusive overall test-score effect. [SOURCE: [IES 2010 final report official record](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/resource-library/report/evaluation-report/evaluation-impact-dc-opportunity-scholarship-program-final-report), full official record opened, randomized design and outcome table summary verified. This audit did not read the entire underlying 2010 PDF.]

The **same later experiment** cited by the memo reports chronic absenteeism **22% among scholarship offers and 20% among users versus 29% among controls**, and improved student satisfaction and perceived school safety at three years. [SOURCE: [IES SOAR evaluation, official structured abstract and findings](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/evaluations/evaluation-effectiveness-scholarships-opportunity-and-results-act-soar-program), opened and verified.]

These sources do not establish benefits for self-paying suburban switchers, much less immigration-induced switchers. They do disprove the “only clean experiment” presentation and the inference that test-score nulls mean tuition buys no substantive benefit. The repo itself concedes the extrapolation `:664-665` and has no clean flight estimate `:831-835`.

**Correction:** retain the correctly reported short-run math losses and year-three null from this later trial; add the other outcomes and earlier randomized cohort. State that immigration-attributable defensive expenditure is unpriced because neither the switch's cause nor its net benefit is identified. Consumer safety, satisfaction and graduation are outcomes, even when test scores do not change. Headline `:16` “the public-goods response is real” also exceeds the memo's own demand-versus-funding-formula warning at `:814-822`; describe the revenue association instead.

## 4. Norms: survey differences survive; the causal response-style correction does not

**Priority: medium.** `research/immigration-institutions-and-liberal-norms-by-generation-2026-09-18.md:329-337` calls police-scenario differentiation and within-person institutional-confidence dispersion two measures that are not attitudes, then infers that some convergence is convergence in survey competence. Both measures are themselves constructed from substantive attitudes. Someone could sincerely oppose police force even against an attacker, or sincerely have similar confidence in different institutions. Reduced dispersion is compatible with response style, but does not identify it. The lead `:5` “a large share ... is response style rather than attitude” is unsupported, especially when `:337` calls the share unknown. This overclaim cuts in both political directions.

**Correction:** “Response style is a plausible alternative explanation; these checks cannot quantify how much of the difference it explains.” The separate interviewer-mode comparison can be retained with its design caveats. Likewise `:15` promotes selective Hispanic identification into upper bounds on all nonconvergence: selection direction on economic outcomes does not mathematically bound every norm outcome. Say lineage/identity selection may bias comparisons, with outcome-specific direction unresolved.

**Political violence:** headline +10.1 pp adjusted Hispanic third-plus gap (SE 2.6), and Mexican-origin +13.7 pp (SE 4.6), are consistently represented in memo tables `:396-406, 521-544`; code `infra/immigration-fiscal/norms_gen_2026_09_18/anes_spec.py:44-46` declares ANES 2020 V201602 and 2024 V241579 and the low-bar item. I inspected principal specification and published derived-result references, but did not rerun the full raw ANES stack. The +10.1/2.6 signal is not so weak that “many tests” alone refutes it. The white-liberal contrast is only +5.8 (2.9), so the stronger “outside the white range” declaration is more sensitive to multiplicity and benchmark selection than the white-overall contrast.

The memo already says `:566` the political-violence item should carry no substantive weight until replicated on a behaviorally validated instrument. Honor that instruction in every downstream summary. [Westwood et al. 2022](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2116870119), full publisher text opened through methods, directly supports concern about vague items and disengagement inflating levels and associations; it does **not** show the Hispanic gap is spurious. Valid narrow claim: “This survey item differs; whether it reflects actual acceptance or behavior remains unresolved.” Cross-sectional generation differences are not longitudinal assimilation.

## 6. Apportionment: retain as mechanical counterfactual, no numerical defect found

`research/immigration-apportionment-seats-attributable-2026-09-18.md:3-9,13-31` defines the operation as changing **counts**, holds election winners fixed, separates all Mexican origin (24 seats), Mexico-born (7) and unauthorized all-origin estimates (2), and reports independent official-baseline gates and source sensitivities. This is a useful descriptive calculation and I found no arithmetic contradiction in the displayed tables. I did not independently rerun Huntington–Hill from raw source downloads.

Keep “under the fixed-location counting counterfactual.” The 24-seat figure is not 24 seats won by immigrants, not the effect of recent immigration policy, and not an election outcome model. Mexican-origin includes US citizens and long-established US families. It is not wholly “arithmetic on certified counts”: the Mexico-born and unauthorized arms use estimates, and the children arm a ratio proxy, as the memo does disclose. The proportional-removal placebo is scale invariance built into divisor apportionment, so it validates implementation but cannot prove population size is irrelevant; the result depends on both group size and uneven geography. “Concentration, not size” should become “uneven geographic concentration at this population size.” No need to bury the correct 24-seat calculation.
