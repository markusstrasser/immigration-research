**Verdict:** Private avoidance is real in some settings, but this search does not establish a material omitted v9 cost. The strongest source update improves the behavioral mechanism while weakening a generic runaway-exit story: a 2026 Danish study finds additional native exits concentrated in large multifamily buildings, considerable replacement by other natives, and no accelerating exit threshold. Modern school-border evidence remains inconclusive. None of the four inspected primary texts identifies net coping resources plus residual harm for v9's US nonmember stayers.

## Hypotheses before new searches

1. **Time-intensive coping:** families may change commuting, school routes, supervision or unpaid assistance without buying a replacement service. Stable test scores/crime victimization after this effort do not identify unchanged welfare. This could be offset by cheaper immigrant-supplied services or benefits of the alternative.
2. **Selective arrival rather than visible exit:** higher-income native families may avoid entering an exposed locality/school; unchanged outmigration misses this margin. That can alter observed resident composition and local revenues, without establishing an aggregate loss.

Three reasons a supposed omission could be false or already included: (a) crime fear/security and actual public-service resources have existing rows, and house prices can capitalize them; (b) moving/private enrollment may purchase positive amenities or reflect labor/housing demand, so gross outlays and native flight are not net harm; (c) outcomes for stayers may be poor counterfactual evidence, but a stationary equilibrium account already includes some endogenous wages, housing costs and services—transition adjustment costs cannot simply become a permanent annual charge.

## What changed after reading the evidence

**The initial question needs a narrower formulation.** Aggregate neighborhood or school outcomes can conceal turnover among people with different preferences. That is not automatically concealed deterioration: replacement without displacement, price adjustments and voluntarily purchased improvements can generate similar aggregate patterns. The consequential object is the effect on the same incumbent/nonmember people, including movers and people who would have entered, jointly observing their resources spent, destination benefits and residual outcomes. [INFERENCE from S1–S4]

**There is useful evidence for avoidance, but no measured dollar bridge.** The current Danish paper adds unusually fine individual/building data and a refugee-placement-based instrument. It also finds strong housing-type heterogeneity and explicitly fails to find accelerating exit tipping. This is a better reason to investigate specified settings than to add a uniform national coping premium. The Oslo paper does not rescue a general school-flight causal claim: its preferred first-order spatial result is imprecise, and measured treatment is a bundle of school characteristics. [S1, S2]

**The adaptation objection cannot dismiss all outcome nulls.** The Danish refugee-school study follows students from their original cohort in a robustness exercise even if they change schools. Its central result remains a small, imprecise academic effect. This addresses selection of observed pupils, although it does not measure the costs of moving, private effort or a national common effect. [S4]

## Four primary texts: result, design, uncertainty and transport

### S1. Boje-Kovacs, Mulalic, Saiz, Sant'Anna and Schultz-Nielsen, August 21, 2026 working paper

**Actual current title:** *Ethnic Minorities and Neighborhood Change: White Flight or White Churn?* The author's [live PDF](https://www.vpsantanna.com/files/research/immigrants_and_native_flight.pdf) now has this title/date, whereas search results and SSRN metadata still identify *Immigrants and Native Flight: Geographic Extent and Heterogeneous Preferences* dated February 12, 2026. This is an updated version of a paper already mentioned in `research/immigration-research-coverage-matrix-2026-06-25.md:76`; the mechanism is not a new discovery. Working paper, not verified as peer-reviewed.

Denmark, 1987–2017, linked individual/property registers; approximately 53 million household-head/year observations in baseline Table 3. Table 4 Panel A col. 2 instruments neighborhood non-Western immigrant share using quasi-random refugee placement plus modeled national movement transitions: coefficient 0.024, SE 0.010, first-stage F 460.938. A 10 percentage-point share increase corresponds to 0.24 percentage points higher annual exit probability (SE 0.10 points), a linear conversion—not a v9 prediction. Table 3's single-family coefficient is −0.002 (0.005); large-building coefficient 0.036 (0.008). Figure 6/§5.5 finds no accelerating exit tipping point. [SOURCE; CALCULATION of units only]

Causal qualification: this is a local response to refugee-driven exposure, conditional on the instrument assumptions; it is not literal random assignment of all immigrants. Table 4 Panel B's large-building overidentification test rejects at p=.032, although the full-sample tests do not. The authors pursue within-neighborhood building variation as a further check. Non-Western foreign-born arrivals are not Mexican lineage or US-born descendants. Movement/preference results do not measure social damage, dollar moving cost or net welfare. [SOURCE; INFERENCE]

### S2. Rogne, Borgen and Nordrum, 2025, European Societies

*School segregation and native flight: evidence from school catchment area borders*. [Publisher/full text, DOI 10.1162/euso_a_00021](https://doi.org/10.1162/euso_a_00021).

Oslo administrative records, 2012–2014; native-origin families with oldest child aged over 2 and under 6, near elementary catchment borders. Geographic RD uses the quarter of neighboring school pairs with largest minority-composition contrast (about 33 percentage points). Table 3's linear controlled specification has approximately 4,134 family observations, coefficient 0.058, SE 0.031; higher polynomial specifications change sign. The authors call the overall causal evidence ambiguous and specification-sensitive. [SOURCE: Data, Model specifications, Table 3 and Discussion]

They lack actual enrollment for the main exposure, using school-age residents of catchment areas; they exclude ambiguous border grid cells (about 10%); the treatment includes correlated school attributes, not minority share alone. This supports neither a definitive zero nor an attributable net moving-cost estimate. No prices or destination welfare are observed. [SOURCE]

### S3. Fernández-Huertas Moraga, Ferrer-i-Carbonell and Saiz

*Immigrant Locations and Native Residential Preferences: Emerging Ghettos or New Communities?* Full text inspected as [IZA DP 11143, November 2017](https://docs.iza.org/dp11143.pdf); later journal article identified as *Journal of Urban Economics* 112 (2019), 133–151, [DOI 10.1016/j.jue.2019.06.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jue.2019.06.002). **Numerical anchors below belong to the 2017 working paper, not an asserted reproduction of final journal tables.** This source family was already noted in the June 25 coverage matrix, line 78.

Spain, address-register and spatial microdata; 28,521 established grid neighborhoods, 2001–2008 long differences. Table 5 col. 2: non-Western inflow/native-change coefficient −0.323 (SE .059), conditional shift-share IV. But additional construction and higher residential density accommodate arrivals; new suburban neighborhoods attract both groups. The paper's §§3.2/4.3 show why an outflow/inflow coefficient between −1 and 0 does not identify dislike or welfare harm: housing competition can produce it. [SOURCE: WP pp. 9–13, 18–21, 27–28; Table 5 p.43]

The instrument and new-neighborhood interpretation are not randomized evidence; the construction/suburbanization setting differs from constrained US metros. This directly challenges translating every native displacement into a net loss or assuming that an established-neighborhood estimate describes the whole urban equilibrium. [INFERENCE]

### S4. Hassan, Hvidtfeldt, Andersen and Udsen, 2023, European Sociological Review

*Do refugee children impair the academic performance of native children in the school? Informative null results from Danish Register Data*. [Full text, DOI 10.1093/esr/jcac059](https://academic.oup.com/esr/article/39/3/352/6843667). Already reviewed for systemwide-school scope in `infra/immigration-fiscal/school_systemwide_2026_09_27/design_table/NOTES_eu.md:50`; newly re-read here for its mover-selection check.

Danish school cohorts observed during 2007–2015, national tests, DiD around refugee entry. Table 3 col. 3: Danish score effect −.010 SD (SE .009), mathematics −.010 SD (.010). Crucially, Analytic Strategy p.8 fixes children to their first-test cohort even if they move, and p.10 reports robustness to this selection check (supplement Table A12). Main-table figures and text directly verified; **A12's underlying estimates were not independently retrieved**. The observation counts in Table 3 are repeated test records, not numbers of unique children. [SOURCE]

Scores are standardized within grade-year and the design is relative across cohorts; it cannot rule out a nationwide common shift. Tracking movers addresses sample composition, not time/money spent coping. Refugee pupils and Danish comparison children are a different population/counterfactual from v9. [SOURCE; INFERENCE]

## What v9 can and cannot take from this

1. **Retain an unpriced uncertainty, not a new cost term.** None of these studies estimates the annual US resource/welfare difference between the existing economy and an economy without the full Mexican-immigrant lineage. None establishes an amount large enough to materially revise v9. This is absence of a calibrated result, not evidence of zero harm. [INFERENCE]
2. **Do not identify “native” with “stayer.”** US-born descendants belong to v9's removed group. A native/citizen outcome in a US study can include both members and nonmembers. The European classifications also differ. A transfer requires the member/nonmember beneficiary split, not just the study's native label. [FRAMING-SENSITIVE; v9 scope supplied by parent]
3. **Separate four objects:** additional real moving/commuting/security/educational inputs; taxes/fees/house-price transfers; residual harm or benefit after adaptation; and changes in outcomes of those who stay or enter. Gross tuition or a house-price decline is not an additional welfare loss. Capitalization may already bundle school quality, safety and preferences; generic avoidance can overlap existing security/fear rows. [INFERENCE; established local accounting boundary]
4. **Transition is not stationary cost.** A one-time move induced by a shock cannot be multiplied into a permanent annual ledger without an explicit flow/equilibrium model. Conversely, equilibrium outcomes do not reveal the welfare path or the resources used to get there. [INFERENCE]
5. **No intrinsic ethnic claim follows.** The behavioral results are conditional on income, housing, schools, institutions and choice sets. Measured preferences do not establish a deterioration of service quality. How preference satisfaction enters welfare is separately framing-sensitive. [SOURCE; INFERENCE]

## Decisive falsifiers / next evidence if this link is pursued

- The strong omission claim would weaken if an identified exposure change leaves **both** incumbent resource use (including time and moves) and their outcomes unchanged, with movers followed and substitution benefits counted. A stayer-only null does not suffice.
- The runaway-locality claim needs an actual accelerating response and deteriorating fiscal/service state after initial exposure. S1's nonaccelerating exits and S3's construction response are genuine counterevidence, not mere “more research needed.”
- A positive correction requires an identified additional coping resource cost or compensating variation for v9 nonmember stayers, net of replacement benefits and overlaps, in a US-relevant population and horizon. A sorting coefficient alone fails this test.
- Best raw material is linked longitudinal address/property/school enrollment records with original exposure assignment and outcomes after moving; add actual moving costs, travel/time use and private-service purchases. Current public metro counts cannot recover all those margins. Restricted Danish administrative microdata were **not** acquired or reanalyzed here. [INFERENCE]
