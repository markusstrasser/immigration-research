**Verdict:** The mechanism is real enough to investigate, but this bounded check establishes no additional signed correction to v9. Direct experiments support both productive complementarity and costly coordination. The strongest apparent new bottleneck evidence is short-run, partly selected, and does not directly measure the individual task downgrading claimed in its abstract. Generic complementarity is already modeled; supervisor-time losses or network amplification beyond that term remain unmeasured.

Scope: task/supplier bottlenecks and scarce supervision or coordination attention. One bounded production mechanism review, 2026-10-16. No repository edits or model changes.

## Pre-search hypotheses

Two overlooked mechanisms to test, not findings:
1. A supervisor's attention can be the binding input: hiring workers can either consume expert time through translation/error correction or free it by taking routine tasks. The relevant effect is the change in joint output and remaining workers' earnings, not the headcount of supervisors.
2. A small critical task or supplier can constrain an entire production chain. Removing its workers may impose a larger short-run output loss than their wage bill, but substitution, inventories, entry and investment may dissipate that loss in a stationary comparison.

Three ways the omission could be false or already absorbed:
1. Competitive wages, profits and the production elasticity can already embody complementarities and supervision costs. Explicitly naming a mechanism does not justify adding it twice.
2. A firm-level complementarity estimate can include organizational and institutional frictions; it is not automatically evidence missing from the model's generic elasticity.
3. An emergency labor withdrawal can reveal replacement delay or network disruption without identifying the long-run national counterfactual. Surviving/expanding firms, changed techniques and displaced demand may offset the initial effect.

## What changed after reading the sources

**The useful distinction is between an observed outcome and an organizational explanation for it.** A fall in firms' average wages after workers disappear can arise from task reassignment, changed employee composition, bargaining, or demand; it does not itself observe a supervisor being pulled onto the production line. Conversely, a team experiment can observe coordination failure without showing that real firms fail to adapt or that its cost is missing from the measured wage/output relationship. [INFERENCE]

### 1. U.S. H-2B lottery: complementarity is already evidence behind the production term

Michael A. Clemens and Ethan G. Lewis, *The Effect of Low-Skill Immigration Restrictions on US Firms and Workers: Evidence from a Randomized Lottery*, AEJ Applied Economics 18(3), July 2026, pp. 43–82; DOI 10.1257/app.20250049. Read the full relevant methods/results/limitations in the [July institutional reprint](https://www.piie.com/sites/default/files/2026-07/wp26-11.pdf); publication identity independently verified at the [AEA record](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/app.20250049). Page anchors below use the reprint's internal paper pagination. Its elasticity range differs slightly from the final AEA abstract, so no claim of complete numerical identity between versions is made.

- **Population/design:** 472 responding firms in the 2021/2022 H-2B lotteries, with pre-registered tests and randomized certification access. Mostly seasonal nonfarm service work. Outcomes observed 3–9 months after the lottery; only surviving firms surveyed. “U.S. workers” includes citizens and lawful permanent residents, not merely non-Mexican natives. Reprint §4, pp. 11–14; footnote 1. [SOURCE]
- **Measured:** Table 2, p.18: H-2B employment's revenue elasticity is 0.218 (SE 0.080, Anderson–Rubin p=.008) with the win instrument and 0.198 (SE .069, p=.004) with expected share. U.S. temporary employment estimates are +.188 (SE .149, p=.219) and +.061 (SE .125, p=.630). Thus revenue effects are clear; a positive aggregate U.S.-hiring effect is imprecise and the intervals include negative values. These are firm-level revenue/employment outcomes, not measured national welfare or supervisor time. [SOURCE]
- **Already absorbed:** §8.2, p.33 explicitly defines its elasticity as an *effective* firm elasticity incorporating imperfect input/output markets, not a purely technical coefficient. Footnote 39 excludes substitution between firms. The model-calibrated elasticity is not an independent observation of supervision, but an additional coordination penalty could double-count the very frictions that produce the reduced-form response. [SOURCE; INFERENCE]
- **Disconfirmation of inevitable cascading closure:** footnote 21 says lottery losers were not substantially less likely to exist/respond about a year later, which weighs against a large short-run exit effect in this setting. It does not establish no long-run exit or remove survey selection. [SOURCE]
- **Transport:** the H-2B program changes temporary foreign labor at applicant firms. V9 removes a whole Mexican-immigrant lineage, including many people inside this paper's U.S.-worker outcome. National adjustment, demand loss, other firms' expansion and family-member removal are different estimands. [INFERENCE]

### 2. Korea sudden stop: relevant disruption evidence, but task downgrading is inferred

Jongkwan Lee, Giovanni Peri and Hee-Seung Yang, *The Effects of a Sudden Stop in Low-Skilled Immigration: Evidence from Korea's Guest Worker Program*, NBER WP 34927, March 2026; DOI 10.3386/w34927. [Full working paper](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34927/w34927.pdf), §§3–5, Tables 3–11, printed pp.10–31. Not peer-reviewed in this version. Funding: KDI and World Bank, as stated on the title page.

- **Population/design:** Korean manufacturing firms participating in the Employment Permit System; 761 firms in closure analysis, 517 surviving respondents in the balanced panel, 512 with relevant wage data. The January 2020–January 2021 interruption coincides with COVID. Identification uses pre-pandemic statutory worker quotas and firm-size variation, with region/firm-size controls; surviving-firm outcomes instrument worker losses with predicted exposure. [SOURCE: §3, Tables 3–5]
- **Observed:** 33 confirmed closures, with additional contact-loss cases considered separately. Table 6, column 4: predicted EPS-share exposure is associated with closure, coefficient .154 (SE .073), with size fixed effects. Among survivors, Table 9's reduced-form production-setback coefficient is .409 (SE .171); its 2SLS coefficient is 1.365 (SE .708), first-stage F=9.55. This is employer-reported disruption, not measured lost physical output or a supplier-network cascade. [SOURCE]
- **Crucial distinction:** Table 10 observes Korean employment, new hires, tenure and firm-average wages. Its wage coefficient is −.619 (SE .285) in 2SLS and −.168 (SE .061) reduced form. The employment coefficient is positive, while new hiring is imprecise and tenure increases. **The table does not follow the same person's task assignment before and after.** Section 5.3, p.29, explicitly allows either reassignment toward lower-paying tasks **or** retention of relatively less-skilled workers. Therefore the abstract's task-downgrading story is a supported candidate explanation, not uniquely identified observed reassignment. [SOURCE; INFERENCE]
- **Limits that change its use:** first-stage F is 9.55–9.58 in outcome models (Table 10), and 9.22/6.38 in alternative size-controlled first stages (Table 5); conventional 2SLS magnitudes are consequently fragile. Closure results are reduced-form and are not invalidated by that particular issue. Nonresponse and survival select the intensive-margin sample. Pandemic demand/credit shocks remain a substantive competing explanation despite controls and checks excluding exporters/hard-hit regions. No long-run stationary estimate follows. [SOURCE; INFERENCE]
- **Coverage:** this paper was already listed in `research/immigration-recent-papers-2026-10-06.md:271–274` as abrupt-stop evidence outside the stationary account. This pass deepens the mechanism assessment; it is not a newly discovered omitted study. [DATA]

### 3. Randomized international teams: coordination costs exist, with sharp limits

Elizabeth Lyons, *Team Production in International Labor Markets: Experimental Evidence from the Field*, AEJ Applied Economics 9(3), July 2017, pp.70–104; DOI 10.1257/app.20160179. [AEA metadata](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/app.20160179); full text read from the [author's July 2016 manuscript](https://gps.ucsd.edu/_files/faculty/lyons/lyons_research_072016.pdf), §§2–4 and conclusion. The following numerical anchors refer to that manuscript, not independently verified final-publication tables.

- **Population/design:** 324 English-speaking remote programmers, paired into 162 pairs, from India, Pakistan and Bangladesh. Random assignment crossed same/different country with teamwork/independent work; one PHP and one Javascript programmer per pair, an eight-hour task, no previous relationship. This is international contracting, **not a U.S. immigration experiment**. [SOURCE: §§2.3–3.1, pp.12–15]
- **Measured:** Table 5, p.24, column 3: teamwork raises correct joint feature output by .318 (SE .164) for same-country pairs; the different-country interaction is −.669 (SE .236). The joint effect for different-country pairs is negative (test p=.008). No fiscal conversion is justified. [SOURCE]
- **Mechanism evidence weaker than outcome:** Table 8, p.31, reports a .349 (SE .191) increase in discrepancies between reported and completed work among different-country teams (81 pairs). Only 16 nonrandomly observed chat conversations support the discussion of extra communication. Coordination difficulty is plausible, but this is not an experimental manipulation of communication or a measurement of manager-hours displaced. [SOURCE]
- **Disconfirmation/transport:** the author explicitly states the experiment concerns new teams, offers no long-term adaptation estimate, and has **no employer facilitating coordination** (conclusion, p.35). Firms can reorganize, train, use shared routines, or avoid the particular team arrangement. The finding supports the existence of a coordination problem; it cannot establish a continuing national productivity penalty from Mexican-origin residents or their U.S.-born descendants. [SOURCE; INFERENCE]

## Exact implication for v9

The inspected production core (`infra/immigration-fiscal/matched_benefits_2026_09_19/model.py:7–76`) is a competitive two-skill CES with capital adjustment and wage-induced hours. The prior caller trace also inspected the nativity nest and case/engine linkage; neither contains manager attention, worker-task assignment, supplier links, replacement delays or redundant capacity as states. That supports “not explicitly represented,” **not** “zero implicitly represented.” The existing H-2B-based complementarity rationale and observed earnings may embody some of these effects. [DATA; INFERENCE]

**No defensible new dollar term emerged.** The most consequential unresolved question is whether a persistent organizational effect on the *remaining, correctly classified population* exists beyond the production response already calibrated. A short-run firm shock cannot be simply multiplied by the lineage's wage bill. Revenue is not value added, lost total output is not remaining residents' welfare, and citizen/native groups are not necessarily the complement of the lineage. Enforcement fear/chilling and abrupt-stop effects must be excluded from a stationary lineage-absence comparison unless explicitly modeled as a separate policy scenario. [INFERENCE]

What would establish or falsify the gap independently: matched worker–firm–task evidence on supervisor hours, task reassignment and error/rework rates, with output/value added and worker earnings, before and after a credible labor-supply change; follow replacement, learning and firm entry/exit. A claim of an *additional persistent* coordination cost fails if the observed losses disappear after adjustment or are accounted for by the existing wage/output elasticities. A network-amplification claim requires downstream effects and substitutes, not only exposed-firm revenue loss. This names evidence needed; it does not authorize a new model or empirical lane. [INFERENCE]
