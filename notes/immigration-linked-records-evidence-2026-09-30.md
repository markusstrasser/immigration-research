# Linked survey–administrative records: what they would change in the account

Date: 2026-09-30. Working note answering "why don't we use linked tax and benefit records, as the
Dutch and Danish accounts do?" The access route and its gates are in
[fiscal_access_2026_09_20](../infra/immigration-fiscal/fiscal_access_2026_09_20/RESULT.md).

Provenance: a researcher subagent (claude-opus-5-5) wrote this on 2026-09-30 from primary papers
and Census tables it downloaded. The source files sat in the session scratchpad and are not kept.
The lead re-read two items: the NEWS 2018 median-income rows (Hispanic $51,450 → $55,890, +8.63%;
white non-Hispanic $70,640 → $74,480, +5.43%; foreign born +11.05%, from `money_q50.xlsx`, sheet
"Comparison") and Kim & Tamborini's statement that Hispanic workers under-report more than whites
at the top of the earnings distribution. The dollar effects are [INFERENCE] transports of published
ratios onto the case's lines, not engine runs.

**Verdict:** Published linked records do not support the worry that the CPS overstates the group's taxes. Among SSN-holding workers, Hispanic and immigrant survey earnings fall 4.1 and 5.5 log points *short* of their W-2s relative to whites and natives (Kim & Tamborini 2014, SIPP–SSA), and linked household income rises more for Hispanics (+8.6% vs +5.4%, n.s.) and immigrants (+11.1% vs +4.8%) than for others (Census NEWS 2018). Survey income above tax records is documented only for self-employment (already priced, ladder 254) and among noncitizen Hispanics, who mostly cannot be linked (44% link rate). On benefits: Hispanics under-report UI (0.72–0.77, matching the admin-keys lane's ρ 0.71–0.80) and Social Security slightly (receipt 0.97 of whites', cash set only); Medicaid shows a 7% relative under-count in the 2002 linkage but none on current administrative records (ρ 1.03); the Census EITC simulator over-assigns Hispanics (31.6% vs 27.8% of dollars), where the repo's Treasury OTA check already measures the residual at −$0.3–0.4bn. Net: linked data would more likely *lower* the adopted case, by about $5bn (range −$15bn to +$8bn), mostly income tax (≈ −$4bn, and its sign on the adopted key is unsettled against Treasury OTA); cash set about −$8bn. Confidence low to moderate: the evidence is from 2002–2018, Hispanic-wide, never Mexican-specific.

# Linked survey–administrative evidence on Hispanic / Mexican-origin / foreign-born misreporting (CPS ASEC, ACS, SIPP)

Scope: published record-linkage results (CPS ASEC / ACS / SIPP linked to IRS, SSA, SNAP, Medicaid records) on how Hispanic, Mexican-origin or foreign-born respondents report earnings, income and benefits relative to everyone else; implications for the main case's survey-keyed lines (income tax within AGI bins, payroll tax, Medicaid enrollment, EITC, Social Security benefits). SSI is bounded by another agent; only misreporting rates are noted here if met.

### F1. Social Security (OASDI) receipt, 65+ — Bee & Mitchell 2017 as tabulated by Meyer, Mittag & Wu 2024 (NBER w32860)
- Table 2 (printed p. 32): OASDI, 2013 CPS ASEC, false-negative rate white non-Hispanic **6.4%**, Black 13.6%, **Hispanic 14.3%**, Asian 15.5%; false-positive rate white 42.9%, **Hispanic 39.6%** [SOURCE: `admin_benefit_keys_2026_09_24/_cache/lit/nber_w32860.txt` lines 1184, and text p. 8–9: "13.6%, 14.3%, and 15.5% of true OASDI recipients among Black, Hispanic, and Asian individuals do not have payments recorded in the survey, more than twice as high as the rate for whites (6.4%)"]. The FP rates are w32860's own computation from Bee & Mitchell Table 6 Panel A: Hispanic .084/(.084+.128), white .051/(.051+.068) (appendix p. A-10) — i.e., among 65+ Hispanics 8.4% are false positives and 12.8% true negatives as shares of all 65+ Hispanics. Hispanic here is "any race"; white is "White, not Hispanic".
- [CALCULATION] Receipt capture (survey reporters ÷ true recipients) = (1−FN) + FP·(non-recipients/recipients). Hispanic 65+: true recipients share = 1 − .084 − .128 − FN-share; using the rates: recipients r_H satisfy FN 14.3% ⇒ capture_H = 0.857 + 0.084/r_H; white: 0.936 + 0.051/r_W. The unconditional cells needed for r are in Bee & Mitchell Table 6 (not yet opened) [GAP → F2].
- Elderly poverty correction (context, same review p. 19): with admin records poverty falls 38% for whites vs **9% for Hispanics** (Bee & Mitchell 2017) and 25% vs **6%** (Dushi & Trenkamp 2021) — i.e., linked records raise the incomes of elderly whites far more than of elderly Hispanics, the opposite of the working-age transfer pattern.
- Main-case exposure [INFERENCE]: the adopted case puts OASDI and Part A on an accrual basis keyed to the group's payroll tax and covered worker-years (ladder 257, decision 2026-09-29-main-case-v4.md), so survey-reported benefit receipt does not enter it; only the cash set ($294.7–361.8bn) charges 2024 benefits ($56.3bn / $53.0bn on the September 27 case). Net sign there depends on FN vs FP; see F2.
