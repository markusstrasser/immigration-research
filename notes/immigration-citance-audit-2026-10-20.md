### Method (fixed before classifying) [INFERENCE]
- Pull: scite `citation_graph(direction=in, include_snippets=true, max_edges=2000)` per paper; dump parsed by a script (`scratchpad/citances.py`, not committed). scite returns edges in **DOI-alphabetical order**, capped at 2000, and attaches snippet text only for open-access/unrestricted citing papers. - Filter: snippet sentence names the author with the paper's year (or scite's year-stripped "Author ( )" form, which scite has already resolved to this DOI) and contains a finding word (paper-specific regex below). Sort newest first; read in order; classify every citance that states something about the paper's *finding* until ~30 are classified. - Multi-paper citations (e.g. "(Card 2001; Borjas 2003)") are classified against what the sentence attributes to the group, if the claim cannot hold for this paper it is OVERSTATED for this paper.

## Coverage and limits [INFERENCE] [FRAMING-SENSITIVE]
- **Not random.** scite's `citation_graph` returns citing edges in DOI-alphabetical order (capped at 2000 for Card 2001 and Borjas 2003, so their frames cut off later publisher prefixes) and attaches text only for open-access/unrestricted citing papers (snippet coverage 4–49% of edges). Within that, I took finding-bearing citances newest first (2017–2026 mostly) from the first ~48 filter-matching rows. Closed-access journals (much of AER/QJE/JPE) are under-represented; SSRN/NBER/Wiley/Springer are over-represented.
- **Filter bias.** The author-name + finding-word regex favours sentences that say something about results; method-only citances (most JRS and Card 1990 citers) were skipped, so shares are "of finding-bearing citances", not "of all citations".
- **Academic frame only.** Policy writing, op-eds and advocacy, where the canon audit located most over-citation, are outside scite. The con-side misuses it predicted (Borjas 8.9%, BFK 44%) may be common there and invisible here; this audit cannot clear them.
- **Classifier.** One LLM rater (me), no second coder, rules fixed before classification (Method section). The repo's instrument note says the LLM tilts pro-immigration; the rules were taken from the canon audit's list, which names 8 pro-side and 4 con-side misreadings, so the search for overstatement was itself asymmetric. To counter it I ran the restrictionist-side targeted greps (Borjas `8.9`) and classified OP's "modest negative" and Card 2001's "depresses wages for all workers" as restrictionist overstatements. A second, blind rater on the 32 OVERSTATED and the 71 UNCLEAR rows is the obvious check.
- **Truncation.** Snippets were read cut at 400 characters (full context fetched for 18 cut rows); several UNCLEARs are cut sentences.
- **Duplicates.** SSRN/published duplicates of the same citing paper were dropped where seen; some boilerplate repeats across distinct papers by the same authors were kept as separate citances (noted).
- **Papers skipped / thin:** NAS 2017 (scite indexes 18 statements; n=3); Card 1990 (6% snippet coverage). Abramitzky et al. is AER 2021, not JPE. Butcher & Piehl 1998 and 2007 pooled (both resolved).
