**Verdict:** Non-citizen voting in US federal and California elections is real, non-zero, overwhelmingly caused by defective registration systems rather than intent, and too small by three to four orders of magnitude to have decided any federal or California contest. Point estimate after the second pass: roughly 300 to 3,000 non-citizen ballots per federal election nationwide. Upper bound: 24,000, the Census Bureau's August 2026 figure taken entirely at face value, which is 0.015% of the 2020 vote and smaller than the margin in every single state that year. The one federal list ever subjected to case-by-case review shrank by 98.8%: DHS told Nevada there "may be as many as" 15,903 non-citizens and confirmed 185. That Census brief reports no false-match rate, no confidence interval and no authors, and its former chief scientist says the false match rate likely exceeds the effect it measures. Where states adjudicate their own flagged lists the numbers collapse: Texas proved 578 of 2,724 SAVE flags were citizens (21.2%) and referred 117; Michigan charged 6 of 38 referrals; Ohio charged 12 of 633. The largest confirmed ballot count anywhere in this file is New Jersey's fewer than 400, produced by a motor-vehicle software defect that registered people who had answered "no" to the citizenship question.

## Block 1 — Survey evidence: Richman 2014 vs Ansolabehere 2015 (appended 2026-09-19)

**Richman, Chattha & Earnest 2014**, "Do non-citizens vote in U.S. elections?", *Electoral
Studies* 36:149–157. Uses the Cooperative Congressional Election Study (CCES) 2008 and 2010.
Headline: **6.4% of non-citizens estimated to have voted in 2008**, 2.2% in 2010; the
non-citizen subsample is ~339 (2008) / ~489 (2010) self-identified non-citizens out of ~32,800
(2008) and ~55,400 (2010) respondents. [SOURCE: Electoral Studies 36:149-157, doi
10.1016/j.electstud.2014.09.001] — note: the 6.4% is a **model-adjusted estimate**, not a raw
count; the raw count of self-reported non-citizens with validated votes is single digits.

**Ansolabehere, Luks & Schaffner 2015**, "The perils of cherry picking low frequency events in
large sample surveys", *Electoral Studies* 40:409–410. Primary text read:
<http://web.stanford.edu/group/bps/cgi-bin/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/The-Perils-of-cherry-picking-low-frequency-events-in-surveys.pdf>
[SOURCE, full text]. Numbers, with denominators, from the 2010–2012 CCES **panel**:

| Quantity | Value | Denominator / date |
|---|---|---|
| Citizenship-item reliability (said citizen 2010 → citizen 2012) | 99.9% | 18,878 panel citizens, 2010→2012 |
| Expected citizens misclassified as non-citizens | 19 | 0.1% × 18,878 |
| Respondents self-identifying non-citizen in 2012 | 105 | 2012 panel wave |
| Consistent non-citizens (both 2010 and 2012) | 85 | of those 105 |
| **Validated 2010 votes among the 85 consistent non-citizens** | **0** | 2010 election |
| Respondents who said citizen 2010 → non-citizen 2012 (clear misclassification) | 20 | panel |
| Catalist-validated 2010 voters among those 20 | 3 | 2010 |
| Non-citizen 2010 → citizen 2012 with a 2010 vote (may be a lawful naturalization) | 1 | 2010 |
| Validated citizen vote rate in CCES panel | 70% | incl. unmatched; 78% among Catalist-matched |

Their conclusion, verbatim from the abstract: "the likely percent of non-citizen voters in recent
US elections is **0**." Mechanism: with 97.4% of the sample citizens, a 0.1% misclassification
rate puts ~19 citizens into the 519-person non-citizen cell (3.7% of it); at a 70% citizen vote
rate that alone manufactures ~13 apparent "non-citizen voters" out of 519 (2.8%) with zero real
ones. Observed misclassified voters were 4, *below* the 13 expected, so P(the apparent
non-citizen voters are misclassified citizens) is near 1.

[INFERENCE] The decisive design point is the **panel**, not the rebuttal's arithmetic: respondents
whose citizenship answer is stable across two waves show zero validated votes. Richman's estimate
rests entirely on the cell that the panel identifies as measurement error. This is not a
he-said-she-said; the rebuttal tests a prediction the original could not.

- [GAP] Later peer-reviewed work 2016–2026 not yet checked (Richman 2017 reply; Grimmer/Eckles-type
  replications; any post-2020 CCES/CPS work).

## Block 2 — State administrative checks (appended 2026-09-19)

**Georgia, first citizenship audit, announced 2022-03-28.** [SOURCE: GA SoS press release,
<https://sos.ga.gov/news/secretary-raffensperger-refers-1600-noncitizen-registrants-local-das-gbi-state-election-board>]
- ~4,500 registrations in pending-citizenship status run through USCIS SAVE.
- **1,634** could not be citizenship-verified; 88 counties; attempts span 1997–2022-02-24; 1,319
  (80.7%) since 2016. Top counties DeKalb 345, Fulton 275, Gwinnett 221, Cobb 143, Clayton 141.
- 2,258 total applications in pending status (the extra 624 lacked data for a SAVE check).
- **Ballots cast by any of them: 0.** "None of these individuals have cast ballots in Georgia
  elections." [SOURCE: GA SoS release + AllOnGeorgia 2022-03-29 quoting it]
- Denominator: ~7 million+ GA registered voters at the time. 1,634 = ~0.02%.

**Georgia, second audit (registered voters, not pending applicants).** Found **20 non-citizens on
the rolls out of 8.2 million registered**; **9 of the 20 had ever cast a ballot**, 11 never voted;
most of the 9 voted before 2012. Registrations cancelled, referred to DAs. [SOURCE: Atlanta
Journal-Constitution, <https://www.ajc.com/politics/election-audit-shows-20-noncitizens-registered-to-vote-in-georgia/NRORAL7Z3RDSNMQ3QDLVEARPSY/>]
Rate: 20/8,200,000 = **0.00024% of registrations**; 9 ballots across ~two decades of elections.

**Texas, SAVE era.** [SOURCE: TX SoS press releases]
- 2025-06-05: **33** potential non-citizens who voted in Nov 2024 referred to the AG. Denominator:
  **>11 million** ballots cast in Texas in Nov 2024 → **0.0003%**.
  <https://www.sos.state.tx.us/about/newsreleases/2025/060525.shtml>
- 2025-10-20: full roll of **>18 million** voters run through SAVE → **2,724 potential
  non-citizens** = **<0.02%** of registrations. Sent to counties for investigation, not a finding
  of non-citizenship. <https://www.sos.state.tx.us/about/newsreleases/2025/102025.shtml>
- **False-positive rate on that list:** ProPublica/Texas Tribune surveyed the 177 flagged counties
  and got data from 97; **more than 5% of SAVE-flagged voters proved to be citizens**; at least 87
  erroneous flags across 29 counties. Travis 11/97 had DPS citizenship proof; Potter 3/9; Moore
  6/11; Erath 2/3. [SOURCE: ProPublica/Texas Tribune, 2026-02-13, via
  <https://www.click2houston.com/news/texas/2026/02/13/a-federal-tool-to-check-voter-citizenship-keeps-making-mistakes-it-led-to-confusion-in-texas/>]
- Counter-direction finding: in ≥7 counties, people who **correctly marked "not a citizen"** on
  the form were registered anyway **by county staff error**; 94 such people identified, **none had
  voted**. Bexar and Denton each confirmed exactly **1** actual non-citizen registrant, neither had
  voted. [SOURCE: Votebeat 2025-10-31]
- Precedent for caution: Texas's 2019 claim of **95,000** potential non-citizens with "more than
  half" having voted collapsed — most were naturalized citizens flagged on stale data; the state
  settled the resulting suit. [SOURCE: Texas Tribune 2025-12-02]

**Louisiana, SAVE.** **403** non-citizens identified out of **2.96 million** registered (0.014%);
**83 of the 403 cast at least one vote, going back to the 1980s**. Even if all 83 had voted in
2024, that is 0.004% of the 2,006,975 Louisiana presidential ballots. [SOURCE: LA SoS spokesman
via Daily Signal 2026-07-24; figures repeated in FactCheck.org 2026-03-30. DHS's own count for
Louisiana was 419, the state's was 403.]

**National SAVE totals — two incompatible framings, both recorded:**
- **~10,000** cases referred to investigators out of **49.5 million** registrations checked, per a
  USCIS spokesman (2026-01-14 NYT). Denominator context: 174 million registered nationally for
  2024, so under a third of the rolls were checked. [SOURCE: FactCheck.org 2026-03-30,
  <https://www.factcheck.org/2026/03/flaws-in-government-tool-to-id-noncitizen-voters/>]
- **28,000 non-citizens** (plus 400,000 dead) across **68 million** records in 25 states, per DHS;
  10 states fully processed found >10,000 non-citizens. [SOURCE: DHS via Daily Signal 2026-07-24]
- **Independent cross-check that contradicts the DHS framing:** 7 states with ~35 million
  registered voters publicly reported their own SAVE results; those searches identified **roughly
  4,200 people — about 0.01% of registrations** — as non-citizens, *before* subtracting the >5%
  false positives. [SOURCE: ProPublica/Texas Tribune 2026-02-13]
- [INFERENCE] The DHS aggregate (28,000/68M = 0.04%) is ~4× the states' own published aggregate
  (0.01%) and is a count of **flags**, not verified non-citizens, and not at all of ballots cast.
  Georgia's SoS office said most of its ~2,500 DHS-listed records were people **already** held in
  pending status who never reached the rolls. Treat DHS totals as an upper bound on flags.
  [UNVERIFIED] DHS has not published a per-state confirmed-non-citizen or ballots-cast count.
- DHS had to **correct** data it gave **at least five states** after SAVE misidentified voters;
  Missouri's flagged list fell from >1,200 in its 7 largest counties to reduced revised lists in
  January 2026 (Boone County 74 → 33). [SOURCE: ProPublica/Texas Tribune 2026-02-13]

## Block 1b — Richman's own raw counts, and the 2016/17 reply (appended 2026-09-19)

Primary text: Richman's author version, <https://ww2.odu.edu/~jrichman/NonCitizenVote.pdf> [SOURCE].
The paper's own table for **2008, n = 339 self-identified non-citizens**:

| Measure | Count | % of 339 |
|---|---|---|
| Self-reported **or** Catalist-verified vote | 38 | 11.3% |
| Catalist-verified vote | 13 | 3.5% |
| **Self-reported AND verified** (most conservative) | **5** | **1.5%** |
| Model "adjusted estimate" (the headline) | 21 | **6.4%** |
| Same adjusted estimate, 2010 | 8 | 2.2% |

The paper's own stated range of national non-citizen votes in 2008 is **"just over 38,000 at the
very minimum to nearly 2.8 million"** against a 19.4 million adult non-citizen population (CPS
2011) — a spread of nearly two orders of magnitude, which is the paper conceding it cannot
identify the quantity. The famous "7.9%–14.7%" figure is the confidence interval on the *least*
conservative measure only; the full interval across measures is bounded below at **0.2%**.

**Richman has repudiated the number used in public debate.** On his own site, 2017-03-10: the
Trump team took "the high end of the confidence interval for the highest and least certain
estimate from the paper as if it is actually a point estimate" and the high figure is "almost
certainly way way way too high." [SOURCE: <https://fs.wp.odu.edu/jrichman/2017/03/10/why-i-would-sign-the-open-letter-if-it-were-true/>]

**Richman, Earnest & Chattha, "A Valid Analysis of a Small Subsample"** (working paper dated
2016-10-19, ODU DigitalCommons 2016-12-10, <https://digitalcommons.odu.edu/politicalscience_geography_pubs/48>)
[SOURCE]. Their reply concedes the direction and shrinks the claim to "**a small percentage** of
non-citizens vote." Their strongest technical point is a **power** objection: the 2010
cross-sectional CCES contains only **7** non-citizens with validated votes, so the panel test
Ansolabehere ran on 85 consistent non-citizens is underpowered to detect a small true rate.
[UNVERIFIED] whether this reply was ever published in a peer-reviewed journal; I found it only as
an institutional-repository working paper.

[INFERENCE] The power objection is correct as stated but does not rescue the headline. It converts
the dispute from "6.4%" versus "0" into "somewhere between 0 and a fraction of a percent, not
resolvable by this instrument." Nothing in the 2016/17 reply defends a rate that could move a
federal election, and the administrative record below is a far better-powered instrument than
either paper.

## Block 4 — Prosecutions and case counts (appended 2026-09-19)

**Brennan Center, "Noncitizen Voting: The Missing Millions", 2017-05-05.** [SOURCE:
<https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/noncitizen-voting-missing-millions>]
44 administrators in 42 jurisdictions across 12 states, including 8 of the 10 jurisdictions with
the largest non-citizen populations. Officials oversaw **23.5 million** 2016 ballots and referred
**~30** suspected non-citizen incidents = **0.0001%**. **40 of the 42** jurisdictions reported
**zero** known incidents. In California, Virginia and New Hampshire — the three states named by
the President — **no official reported a single incident**. The survey asked for *suspected but
not referred* incidents too; in 40 of 42 that answer was also zero. Frame: this is a survey of
detection by officials, so it bounds *detected* voting, not all voting.

**Heritage Foundation Election Fraud Database.** [SOURCE: <https://electionfraud.heritage.org/>,
last updated 2025-12-12; a "sampling," not a census, by its own description]
- 2017 Brennan audit of the then-749-case version: **41 cases** of non-citizens registering,
  voting, or attempting to vote, across **more than four decades**; in ≥5 of the 41 no ballot was
  cast. Only 10 in-person impersonation cases in the whole database. [SOURCE: Brennan,
  <https://www.brennancenter.org/sites/default/files/2019-07/Report_HeritageAnalysis_Final.pdf>]
- 2024 audit of the then-1,546-case version: **68 total non-citizen-voting cases back to the
  1980s**, under 5% of the database; of those, **10 involved unauthorized immigrants** (several of
  those 10 were people voting under an assumed US-citizen identity, i.e. identity fraud rather
  than non-citizen registration). [SOURCE: American Immigration Council 2024-08-01]
- [INFERENCE] Heritage is the most motivated searcher for these cases in the country and its own
  lifetime total is ~68. That is the single most informative number in this file: the ceiling on
  *provable* cases assembled by the side with the strongest incentive to find them.

**Ohio, the best longitudinal administrative series.** [SOURCE: OH SoS via Ideastream 2024-08-27,

**Executive Order of 2025-03-25, "Preserving and Protecting the Integrity of American Elections"
— court status:**
- 2025-04: Judge Kollar-Kotelly (D.D.C.) preliminarily blocks the directive to add documentary

- **2026-06-24: Judge Denise Casper (D. Mass.), on suit by 19 state attorneys general, permanently

  post-Election-Day mail-ballot counting ban, the military/overseas changes, and the withholding of

  Quoted: "The Constitution does not grant the President any specific powers over elections." Only

  it, on authority grounds rather than on the empirical question. The SAVE America Act, being a
  statute, would not carry that defect — but Kansas's equivalent state law was struck down on
  different grounds in *Fish v. Schwab*, where the trial court found "no credible evidence" of
  significant non-citizen fraud and weighed **39** confirmed cases against **35,000+** blocked
  eligible registrations.

**The DHS/USCIS SAVE overhaul itself** is covered in Block 2. Summary of its reported numbers:
free and bulk-searchable from spring 2025 after the March 2025 EO; Social Security data, later
passport data, folded in; 25 states, 68 million records checked; DHS claims 28,000 non-citizens;

flags were citizens; DHS corrected data for at least 5 states. [GAP] **California's participation

## Assessment of scale (appended 2026-09-19)

Converting every administrative source to a common unit — **non-citizen ballots per million
ballots cast** — because the sources otherwise use incompatible denominators (registrations,
ballots, flagged records, decades):

| Source | Ballots | Denominator | Rate per million | Nature of the count |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Georgia roll audit | 9 | 8.2M registrants, ~2 decades | <1 | confirmed, referred to DAs |
| Georgia pending-registrant audit 2022 | 0 | 1,634 flagged | 0 | confirmed zero |
| Brennan 42-jurisdiction survey, 2016 | ~30 | 23.5M ballots | 1.3 | suspected, referred |
| Texas 2024 via SAVE | 33 | 11.3M ballots | 2.9 | referred to AG, unadjudicated |
| Ohio 2020 | 13 | 5.9M ballots | 2.2 | referred |
| Ohio 2018+2020+2022+2024 | 167 | ~20M ballots | 8.4 | referred, not charged |
| Louisiana via SAVE | 83 ever | 2.96M registrants, back to the 1980s | ≪1 per election | flagged, some unverified |

**Best-supported point estimate: a few hundred non-citizen ballots per federal election
nationwide.** At the 1–3 per million that the Brennan, Texas and Ohio-2020 figures agree on,
155 million ballots in 2024 implies roughly **150 to 500 ballots**. Using Ohio's highest
multi-cycle referral rate as the upper anchor gives about **1,300**.

**Upper bound: ~10,000 ballots per federal election, and that requires stacking every assumption
against the null.** Derivation: the 2025–26 SAVE bulk checks are the best-powered detection
instrument ever pointed at this question. Seven states publishing their own results found ~4,200
flagged people in ~35 million registrations (0.012%). Scaled to 174 million national registrations
that is ~21,000 flagged; net of the >5% Texas false-positive rate, ~20,000. The share of flagged
registrants who ever cast a ballot is 21% in Louisiana and 45% in Georgia, and near zero in the
Texas county returns; taking the most unfavorable 45% gives ~9,000 people **who ever voted, across
elections spanning four decades**. Compressing all of them into one election — which the Louisiana
and Georgia date distributions rule out — yields ~9,000 ballots, **0.006% of the 2024 electorate**.
The realistic figure is roughly two orders of magnitude below that ceiling.

**Does any evidence support a decisive share in a California or federal contest? No.** Three
reasons, in descending strength.

First, the arithmetic does not reach. Even the 0.006% ceiling would require a contest decided by
under six votes per hundred thousand *and* near-unanimous directional voting by the non-citizens
involved. No 2024 statewide or presidential contest was within that margin. In California
specifically there is no documented non-citizen ballot count at all: the Brennan survey covered
California jurisdictions and found zero incidents in 2016, and the California Secretary of State's
own 2016 complaint file — 948 complaints against 23 million voters — produced 73 items worth
investigating and **one** incident of fraudulent voting, not identified as a non-citizen case.

Second, the one published decisive claim is the one the evidence killed. Richman, Chattha and
Earnest's 2014 abstract asserts that non-citizen participation "has been large enough to change
meaningful election outcomes including Electoral College votes, and Congressional elections," and
that non-citizen votes "likely gave Senate Democrats the pivotal 60th vote" in the 111th Congress.
That claim is a downstream product of the 6.4% adjusted estimate. The estimate rests on 5
respondents who both said they voted and had a vote validated, out of 339; the CCES panel shows
zero validated votes among the 85 respondents whose non-citizen answer was stable across two
waves; and the lead author has since written that the figure used in public debate is "almost
certainly way way way too high." [FRAMING-SENSITIVE] Richman's power objection is legitimate — the
panel cannot distinguish a true rate of zero from a true rate of a few tenths of a percent — but
that surviving interval is still an order of magnitude too small for the decisiveness claim.

Third, the administrative record's *composition* removes the mechanism. Where states have looked at
why non-citizens appear on rolls, the answer is overwhelmingly administrative error rather than
intent: Ohio's investigators found that in "every instance" the registrant had truthfully checked
the non-citizen box and had the registration rescinded on request, which is why 633 referrals
produced 12 charges and 6 indictments. In Texas, 94 people across seven counties were registered
by county staff despite correctly declaring non-citizenship, and **none of them voted**. California's
1,500 wrongly registered people in 2018 were a DMV data-entry defect; the DMV stated none were
unauthorized immigrants, the registrations were cancelled before the midterm, and mailed ballots
were voided by barcode. A process that enrolls people who told the truth about being non-citizens
is not a process that produces motivated bloc voting.

**Steel-man of the other side, stated at full strength.** Ohio's Secretary of State makes the
strongest available argument: "a single vote can turn an election," detection is necessarily
incomplete because enforcement depends on self-disclosure at registration, and the SAVE bulk checks
found real non-citizens on real voter rolls in every state that ran them — the null hypothesis of
literally zero is false and was always false. That is correct. The 2025–26 checks are also the
first instrument with enough power to say so. What they do not support is any claim about
magnitude: the same checks put the rate at 0.01–0.02% of *registrations*, of which a minority ever
voted, in a country where the smallest federal margins are three to four orders of magnitude
larger. The honest statement is that non-citizen voting is real, non-zero, overwhelmingly
inadvertent, and too small by orders of magnitude to have decided any federal or California
contest on the record.

**[FRAMING-SENSITIVE] and instrument caveats.** Per `notes/llm-bias-caveat.md`, this is a topic
where the model's post-training disposition runs toward the consensus finding, so the disconfirming
material was weighted deliberately: Richman's power objection is presented in his own terms, the
Heritage database is used as the strongest pro-prevalence evidence rather than dismissed, and the
DHS aggregate is reported alongside the state figures that contradict it rather than in place of
them. The reverse bias is also present in the sources: DHS's 28,000 figure counts flags and has
never been decomposed into confirmed non-citizens or ballots cast, and Brennan's survey measures
official detection rather than underlying behavior. Both instruments fail in their sponsor's
preferred direction. The state-level counts of **ballots actually cast** — Georgia's 9, Ohio's 167,
Texas's 33, Louisiana's 83 — are the least corruptible numbers in this file, and they agree.

# Revised assessment (second pass, 2026-09-19)

Three of the five new items move numbers that were load-bearing in the first-pass assessment. I
state the change explicitly rather than quietly editing the earlier text.

**Change 1 — the Texas false-positive rate quadruples, and it came from the state, not a critic.**
Prior position: ProPublica's survey of 97 Texas counties found ">5%" of SAVE-flagged voters proved
to be citizens. New evidence: the Texas Secretary of State's own release of 2026-09-15 reports
**578 of 2,724 flagged voters demonstrated citizenship — 21.2%** — with 506 of them flagged only
because passport data reached SAVE after the initial run. Threshold cleared: yes, this is the
state's own adjudication of its own list. **UPDATE.** Every SAVE-derived flag count in this file,
including the national ~4,200, should now be read with a fifth to a quarter of it likely erroneous,
and the 2,724 resolved to 117 referrals, **4.3% of the original list**.

**Change 2 — New Jersey supplies the largest confirmed ballot count in the file, and it raises my
point estimate.** Prior position: 150–500 non-citizen ballots per federal election nationwide,
built from state rates of 1–3 per million. New evidence: New Jersey's Governor confirmed **fewer
than 400 ballots** from first-time registrants created by a single twelve-month MVC software
defect. Against ~4.3 million New Jersey ballots in 2024 that is on the order of tens per million,
several times the Ohio and Texas rates, and it went undetected for two years. Threshold cleared:
yes — this is a state executive confirming a count against its own records. **PARTIAL-UPDATE.**
Revised point estimate: **roughly 300 to 3,000 non-citizen ballots per federal election
nationwide.** The low end still holds if New Jersey was an isolated defect; the high end follows if
a handful of other states ran comparable automatic-registration bugs without noticing. Nobody has
established which, so the range stays wide. What does not change is the causal reading: the New
Jersey cohort answered "no" to the citizenship question on a keypad and were registered anyway,
which is the same mechanism as California 2018, Texas's 94 county-staff registrations and Ohio's
"they did not lie" finding.

**Change 3 — a better-sourced upper bound replaces my constructed one.** Prior position: a
~10,000 ceiling derived by scaling SAVE flags. New evidence: the Census Bureau brief of 2026-08-18
puts **24,000+** on the record as the most aggressive federal estimate anyone has published. It
carries no false-match rate, no confidence interval and no named authors, its former chief
scientist says the false match rate likely exceeds the effect, Justin Levitt notes a 0.019%
false-positive rate would zero it out, and no case in it was individually verified. **HOLD** on my
assessment of its quality, **UPDATE** on which number to use as the ceiling: it is cleaner to adopt
the opposition's own maximum than to construct one. **Upper bound: 24,000 ballots per federal
election, taking the most aggressive published federal analysis entirely at face value.**

**The decisiveness conclusion does not move, and now rests on the strongest available arithmetic —
which is in the critics' own hands, computed from the largest pro-prevalence number.** 24,000
ballots is **0.015% of all votes cast in the 2020 presidential election, smaller than the margin of
victory in every single state that year** [SOURCE: House Oversight Democrats letter, 2026-09-04].
The brief's own state figures make the same point concretely: 500 in Wisconsin, which was decided
by more than 20,000 votes, and 1,100 in Arizona, decided by more than 10,000. And the analysis
cannot say which way any of them voted. **No evidence in this file supports a decisive share in any
federal contest.**

**California specifically is, if anything, better supported than in the first pass.** The DHS
letter of 2026-07-16 asserts "may be as many as 190,832" but claims database matches for only
81,336 and **alleges no ballots cast at all**. Its stated matching key includes Social Security
number; California's public voter file contains none, California says it never gave DHS the private
file, and the state is in active litigation to keep it. Applying the Texas SAVE rate to
California's roll implies roughly 2,800 registrants, about 1.5% of the DHS figure. California's own
answer is that VoteCal does not track whether a cancellation was for non-citizenship, so the state
has **no responsive records** — which is a real gap in California's list maintenance and should be
named as one, but it is the absence of a count, not a large count. The only California contest ever
decided in part by lawfully cast non-citizen ballots remains the 2018 San Francisco school board
election under Proposition N, with **56** registrants.

**What the second pass strengthens on the other side.** Three findings deserve to be carried
forward by anyone arguing this matters more than the totals suggest. Texas, Michigan and New Jersey
all show that **detection depends on an administrative accident**: New Jersey's 6,600 surfaced only
because a new administration went looking, two years late; Michigan's cases reach back to 2009;
Louisiana's 83 span four decades. Second, **prosecution rates understate incidence by construction**
— Michigan closed 6 of 10 cases not because the people were citizens but because intent was
unprovable or the government itself had caused the registration, and Michigan law bars prosecuting
someone registered "through human or mechanical error." Third, Virginia's now-paused removal
programs mean a known-defective flag is being neither acted on nor replaced with the individualised
review the Attorney General's opinion says is lawful year-round. None of these move the magnitude
estimate; all three are legitimate grounds for wanting better instrumentation than currently
exists.

originate with a party to the dispute: DHS, the Census Bureau under a specific administration,

## 7.1 CORRECTION — Prince William County. My "43 of 162 were likely citizens" was wrong, and the truth is stronger.

Prior position (Blocks 4 and 6.3, and the summary table): "at least 43 of the 162 people removed in
Prince William County were likely US citizens, a 27% false-positive rate," sourced to the DOJ's
2024 NVRA complaint. **That mixed up two different quantities and understated the finding.**

Primary now read: the sworn review by **Eric Olsen, Director of Elections and General Registrar,
Prince William County**, filed as Exhibit 3,
<https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.vaed.561740/gov.uscourts.vaed.561740.9.4.pdf>
[SOURCE, full text]. Covering May 2023 through February 2024, 162 voters cancelled for the VERIS
reason "declared non-citizen":

| Finding | Value |
|---|---|
| Cancelled for VERIS reason "declared non-citizen" | 162 |
| Of those, never cast a ballot | roughly three quarters |
| **Had a voting history** (mostly 2012–2020) | **43** |
| Of the 43, had a registration application **affirming US citizenship** | **43 — all of them**, many reaffirmed 2–5 times |
| Of the 43, supplied a Social Security number | **43 — all of them** |
| Of the 43, cancelled **after updating information at the DMV** | **43 — all of them** |
| Of the 43, subsequently moved, re-registered or affirmed status | **26** |
| Took no action (which Olsen says does not indicate non-citizenship) | 17 |
| **Ballots shown to have been cast illegally** | **0** |

So **43 is the count who voted, not the count of errors.** Olsen's own words: the VERIS reason "is
a VERIS classification and is **NOT dispositive** of the individuals not being citizens"; the
cancellations happened "**due to an omission at the DMV, a form with known shortcomings in design
and data transfer limitations**"; and "**No evidence exists that demonstrates any ballots were cast
illegally.**" The mechanism he identifies: the citizenship question sits **outside the main
application** on the DMV form, ELECT receives only transferred data and never form images, so a
missed checkbox on an update triggers a mandatory cancellation under Va. Code § 24.2-427 even for
someone who affirmed citizenship on every prior registration.

Olsen said the same thing on the record at the Virginia Board of Elections hearing of 2024-09-30,
quoted in the plaintiffs' second amended complaint,
<https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.vaed.561386/gov.uscourts.vaed.561386.182.0.pdf>
[SOURCE, ¶61]: "We looked at 162 individuals that were listed as noncitizens in the VERIS system.
Forty-three of those have voted. We looked at all forty-three of those. **Every single one of them
had verified their citizenship previously. Some by as many as five times. All had Social Security
Numbers.** And we had to cancel them because of state protocol, but we also didn't see any issue
that they had done anything illegal."

Two other county error rates from the same filing (¶ following): **Fairfax** reported that of 26
cancellations sent by ELECT, **7 were sent in error and one voter re-registered immediately — at
least a 30% error rate**. The **Prince William** registrar separately estimated a **50% program
error rate** and flagged the cancellation of a US-born citizen resident in the county for 14 years.
**Arlington** identified a distinct failure: registrants who update an address at the DMV are
flagged, but the county never receives the new address, so the intent-to-cancel notice goes to the
old one.

**[INFERENCE] This is the disconfirming evidence for the EPEC Virginia number, and it is decisive
at the county level.** EPEC's 6,111 ballots come from exactly this population: registrations
removed with the VERIS reason "declared non-citizen." In the one county where a registrar
individually reviewed every such voter, **all 43 had affirmed citizenship, all had Social Security
numbers, and none was shown to have voted illegally.** EPEC's grade stays C, but the reading
changes from "probably heavily inflated" to "the only line-by-line audit of this exact flag found

## 7.3 The 449,000 figure — resolved, and the lead's account is correct

[SOURCE: PolitiFact 2019-11-11,
<https://politifact.com/factchecks/2019/nov/11/viral-image/no-evidence-449000-noncitizens-were-registered-vot/>,
reporting Judicial Council of California jury data obtained directly; corroborated by Snopes
2018-12-11 and a Gigafact/AZCIR fact brief updated 2026-05-13]

| Fiscal year | Total disqualified from jury duty | Disqualified for non-citizenship |
|---|---|---|
| **2016–17** | 1,096,478 | **449,404** |
| 2017–18 | 1,063,784 | 425,814 |
| Jul 2008 – Jun 2017 (cumulative) | ~11.3 million | ~4.7 million |

**It is a jury-pool number, not a voter-roll number.** California courts draw jurors primarily from
the **DMV licensed-driver and ID-holder list**, with the voter registration list as a secondary
source; San Diego Superior Court confirmed the DMV list is most courts' primary source. Non-citizens
including lawful permanent residents can hold California licenses, and unauthorized immigrants have
been able to since AB 60 took effect in January 2015. So non-citizens appear in the jury pool
through the DMV path without ever touching the voter rolls. The Secretary of State's office stated
flatly that it is "not true that 449,000 noncitizens were registered to vote," and said it knew of
no recent convictions for non-citizens registering. PolitiFact rated the claim **False**; Snopes
found that in San Diego County, non-citizenship accounted for only 29% of jury disqualifications,
against the claim's implied ~41% statewide. **Resolves the Block 6.4 [GAP]. Do not use 449,000 as a
registration figure in either direction.**
