**Verdict:** SIPP already provides usable raw data for a bounded descriptive test of remaining adults' work around a co-resident's exit. It does **not** identify deportation, the removal counterfactual in the fiscal model, or a causal work response. The practical next step is a small measurement audit, not a new data pipeline. NLSY97 is a plausible second panel for separations and household transitions; CPS is weaker because it follows addresses. PSID and HRS are currently closed to this agent's microdata workflow by their explicit AI-use terms. No observed work response was estimated here.

Overlap: 20,891 people between 2023/2024 releases, 18,801 between 2024/2025, 11,040 across all three. This is an ID-join check, **not a retention rate**: annual files combine changing panels/waves. The 2025 file contains 2022–2025 panels. `PNUM` alone and interview household `SHHADID` are wrong longitudinal keys. Preserve panel/wave checks, person identity, reference year and monthly residence separately. `[CALCULATION: data/sipp_probe.py, data/sipp_probe.json; SOURCE: guide 3.4]`

Two different feasibility screens were executed within reference year 2024:

1. **Type-2 slot disappearance, weaker screen.** Focal age 18–64 in both adjacent months, same residence; an adult Type-2 slot populated in t−1 becomes empty in t. After excluding household statuses outside 1–4: **599 focal person-month candidates, 544 focal people**. Of these, **47 candidates involve 45 Mexican-identifying focal adults** (`EHISPAN=1`); only 10 Mexican candidates have no focal job in t−1. Requiring observed employment in t−3, t−2, t−1, t, t+1, t+2 leaves **453 candidate person-months**, including **36 Mexican-identifier candidates**. These are slot transitions, not stable-person set differences, distinct departures, deportations or independent observations. Multiple adults may report the same person. `[CALCULATION: data/sipp_support.py, data/sipp_support.json]`
2. **Tracked split-residence, stronger screen.** Two interviewed adults share `(SSUID, ERESIDENCEID)` in t−1, both have records in t, one stays at the old residence and the other's residence differs. Focal age 18–64 in t: **286 directed focal/other pair transitions**, **232 focal person-months**, **214 focal people**, **160 departing-adult/month combinations**. This is a candidate split-home pattern, **not a verified event count**. Only adjacent months are required; universe/imputation/relationship audits remain. `[CALCULATION: data/sipp_probe.py, data/sipp_probe.json]`

The initial Type-2 `sipp_probe.json` result was 600/545 before status filtering; `sipp_support.json` supplies 599/544. These are not competing estimates. The two screens cannot be added: they may overlap and count different units.

**Type-2 entries matter.** They lived with a respondent during the reference year but not at interview and have no individual person-record. Up to ten slots (`ET2_LNO1`…`10`, codes 60–69) carry proxy age, sex, ever-worked-while-together, annual income and monthly relationship. Fields populate only in co-residence months. This recovers a channel a difference of interviewed-person rosters would omit. But stable Type-2 identity across months/households/waves and complete person-set transitions are not yet established. `[SOURCE: guide 3.5.7; dictionary ET2_LNO universe]`

**Exit date and reason are partial.** `ERH_BMONTH`/`ERH_EMONTH` date residence spells; `ERESIDENCEID` is a monthly address identifier within original SSUID that can persist across waves. `EEHC_WHY` describes the mover's reason for moving **to** a residence—marital change, family, job, housing, disaster, eviction, other. It is not the stayer's report of why someone left and has no deportation code. `THHLDSTATUS=3` combines deceased, institutionalized, active-duty and moved-outside-US partial-year members. `EMS_EHC`, `EPNSPOUS_EHC`, `EPNCOHAB_EHC` identify some partnership changes; these are not random removal. `[SOURCE: dictionary pp.1245,3244; guide 3.4.4]`

**Mover attrition remains.** Original adult sample members are followed to US addresses; later entrants remain in sample only while living with an original member. Unreachable movers drop out. People outside the US, in military barracks or institutions are not interviewed, although tracked for possible return. A focal adult who later moves within the US must remain in the analysis if found; conditioning the final outcome sample on staying at the old address selects a response to the loss. The current same-address rule is a measurement screen. `[SOURCE: guide 2.3–2.4]`

**Earnings NaN is not simply nonresponse or zero.** `TPEARN` is in universe when at least one job on lines 1–8 is held during the month, and includes negative profits/losses. Therefore blanks often represent nonemployment; the initial 444/600 with numeric earnings on both sides understates zero-inclusive support. But the full-file diagnostic also finds 822 records with `RMESR=1` and missing `TPEARN`, while some `RMESR=6/7/8` have numeric earnings. A final zero rule must check job-month universe, allocation flags and absence reasons. The selected extract lacks the full job-spell/`APEARN`/`RIN_UNIV` fields to resolve discrepancies. Do not complete-case away nonworkers or zero-fill indiscriminately. `[SOURCE: dictionary TPEARN p.2581; CALCULATION: data/sipp_earnings_missing.csv]`

**Window and weights.** Main screens only cover within-2024 adjacent months. The six-month check is t−3…t+2, requiring an April–October first-absent month. Held files allow at most reference years 2022–2024 for overlapping people, not three years for everyone. The [official directory](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/sipp/data/datasets/2025/) lists `lgtwgt2025yr2_csv.zip` (about 192 KB), `lgtwgt2025yr3_csv.zip` (113 KB), and separate replicates. `FINYR2` spans 2023–2024; `FINYR3` spans 2022–2024; merge on `(SSUID,PNUM)`. These weights were not downloaded or used. Monthly replicates are already held. Target population and attrition treatment must precede weighted event estimates. `[SOURCE: guide 7.3; official directory; DATA: rw2025 archive]`

## Bounded next test and stop conditions

`[INFERENCE]` SIPP can ask a narrower question: how do continuously observed adults' employment and unconditional hours change around an identified co-residence split, by relationship, prior earnings contribution and children/care needs? First reconcile monthly interviewed-person sets and Type-2 relations, separate whole-household relocation from loss of one adult, retain later focal moves, and audit reported versus imputed changes. Do not condition on post-exit marriage, work or staying at the old address.

Stop the deportation branch if cause remains unobserved. Stop a national Mexican-lineage estimate if public fields cannot define that population: `EHISPAN=1` is reported identity, not all descendants, and Type-2 slots lack full origin/status histories. Stop estimation if event validation fails. If decision-relevant precision fails, retain the imprecise descriptive estimate but do not use it for calibration. A successful generic-exit result adds measured descriptive information but cannot directly replace the fiscal counterfactual parameter.
