## Gaps worth filling

Ranked by how often the claim recurs in public debate, then by how cheaply held data can answer it.

1. **A3, the removal docket priced.** The "13,099 murderers" and "80% reoffend" lines are among the most repeated in this debate, and the repo cites the ICE letter without its totals. The fix needs documents only. Add the letter's table (violent convictions ≈ 105k national, ≈ 157k with pending charges, against 435,719 total convictions) and BJS violent rearrest (43.4% in 9 years for released violent offenders, against 78.7% for any offense). Then price Hankinson's actual argument: violent convicts on the docket × violent rearrest rate × the repo's victim cost per violent crime, as a harm-averted-by-removal bound beside the Secure Communities null.
2. **A1, SCAAP custody share by state.** FAIR's 2019 ratios recur, and the repo holds FY2023 and FY2024 SCAAP by jurisdiction plus Pew 2023 state counts. One script can compare state-prison custody-day shares (statewide catchment) with population and adult shares, with and without unknown-status days. The scratch check above gives CA 0.9–1.4 and NJ 0.5–0.7, against FAIR's 3.3× and 5.4×. The script still needs an AZ denominator and an explanation of NY DOCCS's partial reporting.
3. **A6, homicide and legal-immigrant rates by status in Texas.** "Illegal immigrants commit less crime" is the headline dispute. The held Light replication zip (`light_texas/124923-V1.zip`) carries offense-level tables, but the derived table kept only four broad categories. The scratch extraction under A6 shows legal noncitizens' homicide arrest charges at 1.25× natives and all noncitizens at 0.74× (2012–18). Tabulating this properly, under the headline and extreme-recode codings, answers both halves of Caplan's claim on Texas data the repo already holds.
4. **A2, origin violence against US outcomes.** "Importing violent cultures" is common. The ACS institutional cells by origin are held; aligning them with origin homicide rates for the period each cohort left (UNODC), and adding Ecuadorian and Venezuelan cells from the 5-year file, would test the claim on its own ground. The outcome would still be institutional residence rather than violence (§6 angle 14 in the mechanisms memo).
5. **A4, licence route and crash risk.** This is topical after the 2026 Indiana crash and the non-domiciled commercial-licence rules. FARS, already used in the crash lane, records licence status and licensing state for large-truck fatal crashes, so the unlicensed or foreign-licensed share of large-truck fatal crashes is a moderate pull. Home-country record checks remain outside public data.
6. **A9, the deterrence elasticity and budget path.** This is frequent but the most expensive gap: it needs encounter, re-entry and 2025–26 stock estimates the repo does not hold. A cheap first step is to add Bazzi et al. (2021) and a note that the fiscal account takes the unauthorized stock as fixed, so it cannot price a surge that lowers the future stock.

Out of scope and not ranked: A7 and A8 (policy positions). Their empirical pieces are already in the repo; see the ACS table and the Secure Communities and homicide-cost memos.
