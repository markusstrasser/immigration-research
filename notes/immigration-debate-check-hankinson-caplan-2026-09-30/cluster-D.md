Against "never because": Alesina–Harnoss–Rapoport (2016) find the birthplace variety of *skilled* immigrants positively related to income and TFP in rich countries, with a 2SLS gravity instrument. Unskilled variety and ethnic fractionalization do not robustly correlate [EXTERNAL: https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w18699/w18699.pdf]. Verdict: the rich-country evidence favours Caplan's "not near civil war". Hankinson's "never because" is contradicted for skilled birthplace variety and untested for the rest.

### D12 — Would accept a 1986-style deal (status for long-resident, crime-free, tax-paying unauthorized in exchange for real enforcement); 1986 and 1996 were not enforced (Hankinson)
- Grade: PARTIAL (the repo covers what legal status changes, not whether the enforcement half was carried out or whether amnesty draws new entrants)
- Our answer: On what legalization does to the people and children involved, the repo's evidence is extensive. Children of unauthorized Mexican parents do worse, by 10–20 points on SPM poverty and 12–14 points less often in college at 18–24. Once the parents legalize, the children match those of legal entrants, at 13.5 years of school in IIMMLA (ladder 186). IRCA- and Italy-based designs find lower recorded crime after legalization. The Sept 23 audit rates Baker's 3–5% on property crime "sound, imprecise". It rates Pinotti's −0.6 pp from a 1.1% base, 95% CI about −1.2 to 0.0, "best design, imprecise". On today's routes, 55% of 2003's new Mexican green-card holders had once entered without papers, and 77% of those used 245(i), which closed in 2001. About 1% of unauthorized Mexicans legalized a year, and under 2026 law there is almost no in-country route (ladder 187). Hankinson's filters bite on ladder 185: 38–51% of the 3.7M Mexico-born parents of US-born children are imputed unauthorized. The repo does not test his premise that 1986 and 1996 were not enforced.
- Where: `research/immigration-confidence-ladder.md:301` (185), `:303` (186), `:305` (187), `:837-839` (legalization–crime cluster, revised by the audit); `research/immigration-policy-frontier-2026-06-25.md:5-16`; `research/immigration-study-integrity-audit-2026-09-23.md:65-66`.
- Fit: The fit is good for the Mexican-origin population and for the legalization half. The enforcement half of the bargain, and whether amnesty invites more entry, are outside every lane.
- Weakness: The restrictionist's strongest point is that the deal failed on implementation, not on its legal text. After IRCA legalized nearly 2.7M, the unauthorized stock rebuilt to 10.99M by Jan 2022 (DHS OHSS), and the repo holds no series in between. Nothing in the repo shows a credible enforcement commitment is possible.
- External: GAO-05-813 finds notices of intent to fine employers fell from 417 in FY1999 to 3 in FY2004, and worksite arrests fell 84% (2,849 → 445, FY1999–2003). Worksite enforcement's share of agent time fell from 9% to 4% [EXTERNAL: https://www.gao.gov/assets/a247577.html]. Orrenius–Zavodny (2003, Demography) find that IRCA legalized "nearly 2.7 million", and that border apprehensions fell about 11% for six months, then returned to normal. The amnesty "did not change long-term patterns" of undocumented migration from Mexico in either direction [EXTERNAL: https://www.dallasfed.org/~/media/documents/research/papers/2001/wp0103.pdf]. Verdict: "not enforced" holds for the employer-sanction half, at least by the late 1990s. The same study cuts against the view that amnesty itself pulls in new illegal entry.

### D13 — Open labour markets and open borders threaten the living standard natives built (Hankinson)
- Grade: PARTIAL (the repo measures the current stock's costs and wage effects, not open borders)
- Our answer: On the fiscal side, the adopted main case puts the Mexican-origin population's net cost to all other US residents at $371–435bn a year, $9.4–10.9k per member [CALCULATION: 371.4 and 434.8bn over the account's 39,712,493 people]. That is a stationary comparison, not the effect of any admission rule (FAQ 11). On wages, the effect of Mexican immigration on native dropouts' wages is "contested: −8 points (Borjas–Katz) to about zero (Card, Peri)". The Ottaviano–Peri model gives about +0.6% for average native wages and −6.7% for earlier immigrants. On the account's own data, a native/foreign-born nest moves natives +$54bn and other foreign-born residents −$46bn (FAQ 14, ladder 176). Rents rise about 1% per 1% of population (0.8–2.2), paid by renters of low-quality units and capitalised by owners (ladder 79). Ladder 23/39: whether open borders raise welfare turns on the weight given to immigrants' welfare, a value choice, plus empirical cost inputs.
- Where: `CLAUDE.md` headline and `research/immigration-objections-faq-2026-09-21.md:339-350` (entry 11); `research/immigration-mexican-origin-by-generation-2026-09-16.md:205`; `research/immigration-INDEX.md:654` (Card–Peri); `research/immigration-objections-faq-2026-09-21.md:419-440` (entry 14); `research/immigration-confidence-ladder.md:87` (79), `:717`/`:787` (23/39), `:743` (29).
- Fit: Everything measured concerns the current resident stock or marginal policy changes. Ladder 29 says the Card-side wage findings are bounded to marginal variation, and the 2021–24 surge already lies outside it. Open borders is far outside observed variation, so no estimate here extrapolates to it.
- Weakness: "Living standard" is undefined, and the verdict turns on the measure. Measured as average native wages, the evidence sits near zero and closer to Caplan. Measured as other residents' net fiscal position plus rents, it supports Hankinson for this low-skill stock, with the losses concentrated on renters and low-skill workers. [FRAMING-SENSITIVE]
