### B8 — In European welfare states the typical native is also a net burden
- Grade: PARTIAL
- Our answer: The repo concedes the US form of the point. ### B9 — Europe errs by letting people live without working and by delaying work permits
- Grade: PARTIAL (both halves have a European causal test in the repo, but no memo assembles them into an answer, and the US account does not model either policy)
- Our answer: Work permits: Marbach, Hainmueller & Hangartner 2018 exploit a German court-triggered change; former-Yugoslav cohorts that waited ~7 months longer had a ~20-point employment gap five years later, which later closed (Turkish cohorts as placebo). Living without working: Andersen, Dustmann & Landersø 2019 find Denmark's 2002 Start Aid cut (~50% lower benefits for refugees) raised employment 10% → 19% in year one but cut disposable income 40%, roughly doubled first-year convictions (mostly shoplifting), and harmed children (childcare −15 points, a year less schooling for boys 10–13 at arrival, convictions doubled for teenagers). The Marginal Revolution audit marks Cowen's reading, that welfare and immigration can be complements, as AGREE and as evidence against Caplan's keyhole answer.
- Where: `research/immigration-admission-work-access-2026-09-05.md:78`; `research/immigration-generational-crime-mechanisms-2026-09-16.md:73-79`; `research/immigration-marginal-revolution-claims-audit-2026-09-18.md:47`
- Fit: Right populations (European refugees) and outcomes (employment, crime). The repo's own fiscal account is US and does not price either policy lever.
- Weakness: The work-permit half is well supported and holds. The benefit half is contested by the repo's own evidence: cutting support raised work as Caplan predicts, but it also raised crime and damaged children, so the saving is not free. Hankinson could add that Denmark's employment gap, not benefit design, explains its MENAPT deficit (B7).
- External: Fasani, Frattini & Minale, JEEA 2021: exposure to an employment ban at arrival cuts refugees' post-ban employment probability by 15%, rising with ban length and lasting up to 10 years, concentrated among the less educated; they estimate a €37.6bn output loss for the 2015 arrivals [EXTERNAL](https://economics.uc3m.es/?p=5652) (search excerpts, paper not opened). Not in the repo (0 `rg` hits for "Fasani").
