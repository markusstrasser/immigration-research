## 1. Pronatal “treasury-equivalence budget” fails its own allocation sensitivity

**Claim and location.** `infra/immigration-fiscal/pronatal_equivalence_2026_09_18/README.md:3` gives $202k for a white-reference child versus a Mexico-born arrival aged 25, $243k versus a Mexican second-generation child, and $161k versus a third-plus child, at 3%. Line 26 says moving shared household receipts back to actual taxpayers leaves lifetime sums “nearly unchanged.” Line 33 says the budgets already carry one generation of descendants.

**Verified defect.** The generator explicitly chooses the `all_age_shared` account (`lifetime_profiles.py:25-49`), then sums each age-band balance through age 82 with 3% discounting (`:52-59`). This gives a white child positive attributed taxes while under 18; README lines 15-26 show a +$4,965/year child balance because parents' taxes are shared with children. Adding a child does not create that attributed slice of parental tax revenue. More directly, the claim of allocation robustness is false on the repo's own alternative. I independently reused its estimator and replaced the shared component matrix with its existing personal-source matrix, keeping the sample, donor model, bands, rate, horizon and population weights fixed. No original file was changed.

At zero discount, the personal-source white-child-minus-Mexico-born-adult difference is $364,543 rather than approximately $509k. So this is not just a small discount-rate issue. Independently reading upstream stored personal-source target profiles reproduced the target NPVs to the dollar (`all_age_ledger_2026_09_17/derived/age_profiles.csv:66-89`). The reference profile was regenerated from the unchanged upstream estimator.

The only estimator substitution is `shared, personal, renter = original(state); return personal, shared, renter`.

**Rebuttal and surviving claim.** Household sharing is legitimate for describing how a current household's resources are distributed. It is not a legitimate invariance argument for marginal childbirth versus immigration. The person-source result also is not an identified policy NPV: current age profiles are treated as a single life, everyone survives to 83, and there are no induced-child selection, policy-response or migration paths. **Materiality:** HIGH, demonstrated sign reversal for the child-versus-adult comparison. **Correction:** withdraw the $202k as an actionable pronatal budget; replace with allocation-sensitive period-profile comparisons and state that a real birth-versus-arrival counterfactual remains unestimated. Remove “nearly unchanged” and the explicit descendant-coverage assertion. The $128k/$87k alternative child-to-child gaps remain descriptive, not validated subsidy budgets.

## 2. Federal grants are counted twice in parts of the “complete” account

**Claim and location.** `research/immigration-fiscal-gap-incidence-who-pays-2026-09-18.md:3` headlines 89% state-local; lines 18-27 say the account is partitioned by the government writing the cheque. Lines 218-224 acknowledge federal grants inside state-local costs are also inside federal functions, but characterize the repair primarily as moving spending between buckets.

**Verified objection.** Some of this is an actual consolidated double count, not merely the choice of payer. The code charges gross state-local general expenditure less selected functions (`ledger_residual_agg_2026_09_16/residual_agg.py:123-129`; retained functions include highways), and also charges the entirety of federal transportation function 400 (`ledger_absolute_2026_09_17/absolute_ledger.py:1046-1049`). The incidence code puts G in state-local and R in federal (`gap_incidence_2026_09_18/incidence.py:281-286`). No subtraction of transportation grants occurs in that path.

**Primary check.** The [Census government-finance glossary](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/state/about/glossary.html) defines direct expenditure as payments to final recipients and federal intergovernmental revenue as aid/grants received. Direct expenditure is not “expenditure financed only by own-source revenue.” The [FHWA official 2026 funding/spending chapter](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/policyinformation/pubs/our_nations_highways_2026/funding_spending.cfm) explicitly states state/local highway expenditures include federal reimbursements, while consolidated direct federal expenditure excludes transfers. It identifies about **$64bn of federal highway intergovernmental payments in 2024**, already funding state/local spending. The [underlying FA-5 table](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/policyinformation/statistics/2024/fa5.cfm) shows $60,999.0m to states plus $2,866.6m to local governments. Thus there is a concrete overlap channel, not a speculative accounting complaint. Different state-local/federal vintages prevent treating this $64bn as the exact correction to the current model.

**The national comparator has the same conceptual defect.** `ledger_absolute_2026_09_17/derived/national_reconciliation.csv:25-29` computes federal receipts minus federal outlays **plus state/local own-source revenue minus state/local direct expenditure**, yielding −$2,590.8bn. Since federal outlays include grants, own-source revenue omits the receiving counterpart while state-local direct expenditures include spending financed by those grants. A consolidated identity must net the transfer from federal outlays or add the corresponding state-local receipt; omitting both creates two charges for the same public purchase. This undermines using that comparator to assess “coverage.”

**Rebuttal and scale.** The builder correctly nets some school and Medicaid overlaps (`absolute_ledger.py:984-1015`), so it is wrong to subtract the full federal-grant total again from every item. And a common per-person correction may barely alter an age-standardized relative gap. Nevertheless, successful component-addition gates only reproduce the double-counted construction; they do not validate it.

**Materiality:** HIGH for “complete,” absolute dollar totals and payer shares; correction to the target total remains [GAP]. **Correction:** net intergovernmental transfers consistently by function and year, rebuild the national reconciliation and federal/state-local split, then rerun dependent incidence/interest figures. Until then suppress “89%” as a measured payer split. Do not claim this audit proves a sign reversal of the full account.

## 4. $2,246 per native household and compounded debt are scenarios

**Arithmetic checked:** $263,224,141,000 / 117,200,000 = **$2,245.94**. With r = 879.879 / ((26,330.142473 + 28,307.312291)/2) = **0.03220790587**, borrowing $263.224141bn at each year-end for ten years gives **$3,048.434bn debt**, of which **$416.192bn is accumulated interest**. This reproduces the reported $3.05tn/$416bn (the probe uses the same displayed constants).

**Objection:** the native payer base is imposed, not estimated (`gap_incidence/incidence.py:8-15,426-457`); other immigrants also finance governments. The memo itself admits the entire residual is assigned to natives (`:225-227`). Some Mexican-origin descendants are native householders too; the terminology must not imply two disjoint populations. The same matrix applied to white residents' own modeled deficit gives $3,334 per native household (`memo:3`), demonstrating why these quantities cannot mean “tax savings if that group disappeared.”

The interest code applies a chosen federal-financed share to a fixed repeated flow (`gap_interest_2026_09_18/interest_on_gap.py:44-63`), and the share inherits the unnetted-grant split. Its per-person terminal-value extension imposes a uniform age shift taken from each group's crude complete-minus-partial difference (`:67-86`), not a newly built complete age profile. It is inappropriate to present this as an independently measured third-order effect or cohort forecast. Nominal rates and held annual-dollar flows also require a stated price path.

**Materiality:** MEDIUM/HIGH if used in the main report; arithmetic survives, causal rhetoric fails. **Correction:** retain only as explicit financing illustrations after repairing the input account, with the all-native financing assumption, nominal/real convention and repeated-flow assumption adjacent to the number.
