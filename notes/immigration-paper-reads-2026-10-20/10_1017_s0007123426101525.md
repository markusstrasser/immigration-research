## 2. Question, population, period, unit and estimand

The question is “the smallest immigrant-native crime differential (r) that any consistent estimator of the aggregate slope can detect at conventional levels of statistical power.” The empirical illustration uses “German county-level data around the large-scale 2015 refugee inflow (2014–16).” Units are **390 counties** after 11 exclusions, with two endpoint observations and county first differences; population is the pre-existing population plus refugees, not Mexican-origin US residents or their descendants. [SOURCE: main Dilution Problem, pp.4,7–8; SI E and footnote 14]

The estimand distinction is explicit: “the total effect of changes in the immigrant population share Sit on overall crime in an area.” Total response θ = ∂y/∂S = r(S) + (1−S)∂cN/∂S, where r=cI−cN is offenses per person per period, **a rate difference, not a rate ratio**. The immigrant crime propensity is held fixed for a given inflow. With muted native behavior, θ≈r and a consistent aggregate slope identifies r. If cI also changed with S, another S∂cI/∂S term would enter; the paper holds that channel fixed. [SOURCE: main pp.5–6, Eq.2; SI C.1; INFERENCE for the additional derivative]

There is **no automatic attenuation bias from a small immigrant share** in the correctly specified slope. The problem is small exposure variation relative to outcome noise, hence a wide interval for r. Small initial immigrant stock alone is not a general impossibility theorem: n, unit population, treatment variance and residual volatility matter. [SOURCE: Eq.1, slope consistency statement, SI C.5; INFERENCE]

## 4. Identification and assumptions

This is an algebraic decomposition and calibrated power simulation, with a secondary confidence-bound translation, not a newly identified causal immigration effect. Simulated county shocks and errors are independent normal draws; Δyi=rΔSi+Δεi; OLS with a two-sided 5% test. For each grid point there are **1,000 repetitions**, n=390. Mean shock varies 0–.01; gap k×cN varies k=0–30, hence ratio cI/cN=1+k. Native response is muted, immigrant rate fixed, gap constant across counties, and no measurement error in the main simulation. A valid causal application additionally needs exogenous exposure/orthogonal shocks and the relevant design's identifying assumptions. The simulation grants these rather than proving them for real Germany. [SOURCE: main Eq.4, p.8; SI C.1,E]

Author-named threats: heterogeneous immigrant demographics/context; native behavioral mechanisms bundled into θ; tiny policy changes; measurement error; constant-gap assumption; 11 unmatched counties; preperiod aggregate crime used as a native proxy; and original refugee-ban identification potentially failing. SI E treats total preperiod crime as a proxy because longer-settled immigrants are included; the author regards this as conservative for the detectable **ratio**, since a lower native baseline would require an even larger ratio with the same absolute noise. SI C.5's Poisson intuition ignores over-time covariance and uses representative populations; E's actual calibrated Monte Carlo instead adds normal residual noise. [SOURCE: main pp.3,5,7–8, fn7; SI C.5,E,fn14,fn16]

Disconfirmation/limits to the critique: very large Greek-island shocks are an explicit counterexample; broad native behavioral changes can be estimable; outcomes narrowed to hate crime avoid diluting that particular channel; SI B acknowledges that Chalfin and Masterson–Yasenov do discuss precision for **aggregate** outcomes. These are restrictions on what the paper overturns. [SOURCE: main fn1, Discussion; SI B]

## 9. Misuse risks and strongest limits

The clearest author limit is: “Crucially, this paper takes no position on whether immigrants are more, less, or equally prone to crime than natives.” [SOURCE: Introduction p.3] Accordingly:

- **22.5/28.1× are compatibility bounds**, not observed criminality; **15× is a hypothetical calibration threshold**, not a measured effect.
- A weak test does not establish parity and does not establish elevated offending. The paper weakens one inferential route; it does not invalidate individual-level or disaggregated evidence.
- Do not say small immigrant shares bias every regression to zero: correctly specified slopes remain consistent under the benchmark. It is mainly a precision/exposure-variation issue; measurement error is an additional problem.
- Do not turn suspect/citizenship or institutional/custody records into true offending by Mexican ancestry. The repo's reporting rule still governs.
- Do not reinterpret the descriptive AfD variable-importance plot as a causal estimate, or as proof that crime perceptions are accurate/inaccurate.

[SOURCE: Eq.1–3, Discussion, SI A,C–F; INFERENCE]
