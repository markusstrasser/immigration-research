## Identification and assumptions

Static closed-economy monopolistic-competition model; nested CES labor substitution; endogenous number of firms/varieties, Pareto productivity and free entry; utility linear in final consumption. The modeled variety value uses a CES **love-for-variety** elasticity imported from trade estimates. Productivity and factor shares recalibrated each year absorb changes outside model. No design identifies the political intervention that caused the2016 stock deviation; educational upgrading, return migration, demographics and COVID also affect the stock/ratios. No bootstrap, statistical parameter distribution, confidence interval or out-of-sample validation is supplied. [SOURCE: §§2–5; INFERENCE]

Calibration limitations authors state: **“More precise results would involve a proper estimation of these parameters, and this is beyond our scope.”** Their description of independent literature parameters as “unbiased” does not establish unbiased structural welfare estimates. H-1B substitution need not transport to all high-skill immigrants; trade-derived variety elasticity need not measure domestic population-driven product gains. [SOURCE: §4; INFERENCE]

Additional reproduction question: §3 imposes **σ<1+θ** for the Pareto moments, while calibratedθ=2.36 givesσ<3.36; AppendixC plotsσ up to5. Unlessθ/support changes, some sensitivity points violate their stated condition. §3 changesθ to3.5 for illustration, but no AppendixC reset sufficient forσ=5 is reported. **[UNVERIFIED implementation]** Without code this is a traceable consistency question, not proof of how the actual solver handled tails. [SOURCE: §3/fn2, Table1, AppendixC chart; INFERENCE]
