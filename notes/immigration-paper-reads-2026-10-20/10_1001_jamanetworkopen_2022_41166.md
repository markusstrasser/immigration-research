## Question, population, period, unit and estimand

“Do insurers and US government programs pay more for the care of immigrants than immigrants contribute to the health care system in insurance premiums and taxes?” Main outcome: “total contributions minus total expenditures by third-party payers on their behalf”. [SOURCE: Key Points; Methods]

Main period **2017**, additional repeated annual accounts2012–16; **2018US dollars**. Community-dwelling residents plus an ACS/NHEA nursing-home allocation. Population = all foreign-born, broken into citizen/imputed documented/imputed undocumented; comparison **US-born citizens**, including immigrants' US-born children. No Mexican-origin or lineage tabulation. Unit = person/year and group sums, separately estimated across datasets; combined210,669 is CPS180,084+MEPS30,585, **not210,669 linked individual records**. NHIS links only to the MEPS frame; CPS supplies separate tax/premium estimates. [SOURCE: Main Data Sources; Tables1–3; INFERENCE]

Estimand = health-sector current financing incidence, public **and private** payer flows; not all-government fiscal balance, lifetime entitlement accrual, measured resource shortage or a causal removal effect. Own out-of-pocket payments/care are excluded as equal offsetting flows; private insurer net contributions are a different budget from Treasury. [SOURCE: Methods/Limitations; INFERENCE]

## Construction, assumptions and identification

Weighted cross-sectional accounting, SAS9.4 complex-survey procedures, two-sidedP=.05. No instrument, randomization or policy comparison. Expenditure-use inputs observed in MEPS; household/employer premiums partly survey-imputed; legality **imputed** using arrival/citizenship/benefits/licensure/veteran/federal-employment/spouse rules; tax receipts **allocated/modelled** using wage×2.9% Medicare tax and CBO/ITEP incidence; NHEA residual amounts redistributed by stated keys. Nursing-home costs assigned proportional to resident counts; uncompensated hospital care proportional to uninsured counts, financing proportional to other payer contributions. Observed national total calibration does not establish correct group allocation. [SOURCE: Methods; INFERENCE]

Status inference uses Medicare/Medicaid receipt to assign documented status, so undocumented payer costs are not independent measurements of a directly observed legal group. Authors admit Emergency-Medicaid users can be classified documented. This can shift the **89% undocumented share** and subgroup balances while leaving the **all-foreign-born aggregate** essentially unchanged under pure status relabeling. A causal claim that undocumented status itself generates the surplus is unsupported. Wage×tax rates also require payroll-compliance assumptions; actual group tax records are not linked. [SOURCE: Immigration Status/Limitations; INFERENCE]

Excluded/incidence limits: no direct incarcerated-immigrant medical spending; most military/VA/IHS and some other public programs excluded; state-only ACA subsidies absent; post2017coverage rules absent. Private employer premiums count as earned compensation of workers; that is an incidence convention, not direct employee cash. Annual younger migrants pay while older native residents draw; no age standardization, future earned Medicare claims or descendants' costs follow from current surplus. [SOURCE: Discussion/Limitations; INFERENCE]

## Misuse risk

“Our estimates ... are not expected to match, in part because some federal spending is funded by borrowing rather than current revenues.” [SOURCE: Limitations] “Immigrants subsidize the health-financing system” is a descriptive current-flow result conditional on allocations; it does not establish the entire fiscal balance, that no hospitals experience unpaid costs, future solvency, or the effect of admitting/removing a person. Conversely, gross unpaid-care costs alone cannot rebut a net premium/tax financing estimate. [INFERENCE]
