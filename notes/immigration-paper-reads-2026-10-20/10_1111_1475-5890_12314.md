## Identification, assumptions and what is measured versus modeled

No experimental instrument or causal comparison. Accounting assigns observed/simulated liabilities/benefits to cross-sectional respondent records; the migrant extension allocates in-kind spending, and VAT is constructed from HBS consumption. Country- and group-specific fiscal profiles condition on age, education and labor status, then CEPAM changes population composition. Neither simulated eligibility nor a fixed fiscal profile is observed actual collection/take-up for all migrants. [SOURCE: §§3.1–3.3; INFERENCE]

Table1 baseline: immigration10M every5 years; future arrivals' education like recent inflows; integration average2010–2015; constant labor entry/exit rates; fertility1.6→1.8; constant country-specific emigration by age/sex; improving mortality; education trends continue; intra-EU mobility average2013–2016. Text calls this continuation of2008–2014 policy,2M third-country nationals annually, about30% labor admissions, lower employment than natives for20 years. These are assumptions, not empirical forecasts with calibrated probabilities. [SOURCE: Table1; §3.3]

The main paper warns that consumption, productivity, wages, job opportunities and housing conditions are **not** allowed to respond to arrivals; policy/integration and migrant selection are fixed. Distinct mortality and origin-paid education complicate the synthetic LCD. Cohort differences can masquerade as age profiles; not identified here as within-person aging. Exact uncertainty and country sampling fragility are deferred to appendix. [SOURCE: §§4.2–4.4/5; INFERENCE]
