## Identification and assumptions

There is no single review-level identification design, formal search inclusion protocol, quality-weighted meta-analysis, standard error or pooled causal parameter. The authors themselves call Table A1 “a non-exhaustive summary of key studies”. They distinguish observational correlations, administrative panels, natural experiments and fiscal projections, but broad prose is not a replacement for tracing those studies. [SOURCE: II(ii); III(i); INFERENCE]

Main threats expressly named: welfare changes can be responses to immigration; migrants select on preferences/risk/skills; networks and labor markets confound destinations; “who counts” changes fiscal comparisons; fiscal horizons, descendants, discount rates and policy assumptions drive projections; attitudes can cause misperceptions rather than the reverse. Fiscal balance also depends on whether current deficits are closed by future generations. [SOURCE: II(iii); III; IV; V; A2]

## Misuse risk

The main risk is turning a narrative “small overall effect” into a universal theorem that a particular low-income lineage is fiscally neutral, or turning a positive working-age skilled-visa result into a completed-family lifetime gain. The review expressly says “their outcomes vary depending on assumptions made” and “fundamental questions about how to define who 'counts' as an immigrant”. Its affirmative recommendation to reconcile attitudes with evidence is a policy/taste frame, not itself an empirical falsification of voter concerns.
