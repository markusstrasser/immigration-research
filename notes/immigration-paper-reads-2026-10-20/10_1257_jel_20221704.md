## 3. Identification and assumptions

OLS with county residence and birth-year fixed effects, female and census-wave controls; SEs clustered by county. Thirteen observed high-propensity counties versus five low-propensity counties; middle50% omitted. Identification requires parallel educational trends absent desegregation and no cohort-specific high-versus-low county shocks.1940 ethnicity concentration is a **proxy** for segregation; current adult residence proxies childhood school exposure decades earlier. California birth does not ensure attending childhood school in today's county. No instrument or actual school-treatment observation resolves these gaps. [SOURCE: §§3–4/FN15/18] [INFERENCE]

The authors test an older-cohort placebo, birth-year event studies, alternative1930 exposure definitions and tercile cutoffs. They argue partial treatment of the1931–35 comparator makes main effects conservative and older1921–30 comparison provides an upper estimate. These are **conditional interpretations**, not mathematically identified bounds: differential cohort trends/migration or exposure error need not bias one way. “The identification strategy assumes that no other contemporaneous factors can explain the differences observed over this time period.” With only18 observed county clusters and sparse low-county Hispanic observations(143young/77old), conventional cluster inference needs careful small-cluster validation. None is shown in supplied main text. [SOURCE: §4/Table1/FN15] [INFERENCE]

## 7. Relevance, direction and arithmetic

The study supports the proposition that institutional treatment can change measured Hispanic schooling; it does not decompose today's Mexican-origin education gap into discrimination, selection or other causes. Smaller White losses are evidence against a universal claim that these particular historical reforms had zero incumbent costs, while remaining compatible with modern null school-quality estimates for **different interventions and cohorts**. The matching rule forbids using1947desegregation to refute2003–19 immigration-share effects or today's2024conditional fiscal balance. [DATA: FAQ matching rule; school-systemwide lane] [INFERENCE]

If current matched educational improvements raised lifetime earnings, the account's tax/benefit composition would improve. ## 8. Misuse risk and disconfirmation

Misuse: “segregation explains the current fiscal gap,” “immigration lowers White schooling,” or “desegregation is established to be a resource transfer.” Limiting passage: “we are not able to directly tie individuals to schools”. Another: “absent data on school resources within schools explicitly identified as Mexican or White-only, we cannot definitively say whether this is the mechanism”. The favorable Hispanic effects, adverse White effects, placebo uncertainty and exposure misclassification must travel together. [SOURCE: Conclusion/§5]
