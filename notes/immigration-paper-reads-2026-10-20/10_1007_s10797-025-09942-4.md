## Identification, construction and assumptions

OLS: NFP on migrant indicator, then country/year/country×year effects, then demographic controls. Household-clustered robust SEs. Decomposition uses pooled coefficients with migration indicator as reference; endowments versus coefficient/unobserved differences. Exact sample weighting, survey-income reference-year alignment, harmonization code, and tax calibration-to-receipts checks are **not specified in the supplied working paper**; do not assume them. Repeated cross-sections cannot separate within-person ageing/assimilation from cohort selection/return migration. [SOURCE: §§3/4; INFERENCE]

Important tax approximation in fn9: **“Some countries apply different personal income tax rates and thresholds depending on demographic characteristics ... and labour market parameters ... Our data do not allow us to make these distinctions.”** They distinguish employee/self-employed but cannot implement every family/hours rule. Benefit allocation: contributory cash benefits at person level; noncontributory child/housing/social-assistance household benefits divided by **number of adults**. Public services/education/healthcare, consumption taxes and dynamic/native-economy effects are outside this cash-transfer NFP; under18s are not separately priced. [SOURCE: §3, fn9; §4]

Authors acknowledge selection into destination/staying, country variation in origin/skills and institutions, inability to separate refugee/family reasons, and an unexplained component combining returns and unobservables. They propose discrimination, language or social capital as **possible**, not identified causes. A coefficient decomposition contribution near zero is not a zero individual return to schooling. [SOURCE: §§3/5.2, fn8; INFERENCE]
