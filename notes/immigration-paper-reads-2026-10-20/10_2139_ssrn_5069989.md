## Question, population, period, unit and estimand

The underlying common task was whether **“greater immigration reduces support for social policies among the public.”** This paper instead asks whether **“the dispersion depends on researcher priors about immigration.”** Its primary outcome is an analyst-produced estimate, not the respondent's welfare attitude itself. [SOURCE: §§1–3]

The call went online April2018. The experiment eventually obtained161 researchers in73 teams; two teams produced no numerical effect and were excluded, leaving **158 researchers,71 teams,1,253 model estimates**. Teams first reproduced Brady–Finnigan2014 and then extended the analysis using five ISSP waves spanning **1985–2016** and country-level immigration/macroeconomic inputs. Original replication concerned17 European countries; extensions could change countries, waves and outcomes. These are participating volunteers, not a probability sample of immigration scholars. [SOURCE: §§1–2; INFERENCE: sampling limit]

Primary unit is **team–model**, with inverse-number-of-model weights to give teams equal total weight, team-clustered SEs, team-size/discipline fixed effects and skill/topic factors. Alternative researcher–model expansion gives **2,680 dyads**, weighted by inverse(team size × number of models). These duplicate common team outputs; they do not create2,680 independent research experiments. [SOURCE: §3, Tables2–4, fn7/12; INFERENCE]

“The ‘average marginal effect’ (AME) gives the change in the probability that the government should be responsible for providing social programs resulting from a one-percentage-point increase in the immigrant share.” **Normalization is unresolved:** the same passage quotes percentile AMEs −.071 and+.052, then translates a **ten-point** immigrant-share rise to **−7.1/+5.2 support percentage points**. ## Identification: controlled task, observed ideology

The strongest evidence is a common question and supplied dataset, **immigration preferences measured before analysis**, repeated association across operationalizations, equal-team weights and blind peer-review information. Teams could not choose the overarching question/data, but could choose how to analyze them. This substantially narrows many cross-paper confounders. [SOURCE: §§1–3]

**Ideology was not randomized.** Random assignment concerns reviewers. The ideology–estimate regression remains observational: ideology may covary with unmeasured expertise, prior methodological commitments, topic selection or other team attributes. Survey-before-analysis reduces reverse causation from this particular output; it does not identify the causal effect of changing a researcher's politics. “Bias” relative to a known true parameter is not measured, since there is no outcome oracle. [SOURCE: design; INFERENCE]

Teams are defined asymmetrically because anti-immigration responses are rare: pro requires **more than half** of members answering5/6; anti requires **at least one** member answering1/2, unless pro classification takes precedence. Result:31 pro,31 moderate,9 anti. Two mixed three-person teams are classified pro despite one anti member; removing their40 models reportedly changes little, without a full coefficient table. Different definitions are also examined, but the9-team anti tail constrains precision. [SOURCE: §2/fn5]

Most participants are sociologists(55.4%) or political scientists(27.4%);57/71 teams are multi-person and36/57 cross-disciplinary. Controls approximate discipline composition, and alternatives use lead discipline or28 combinations. Statistical skills and topic experience are themselves measured factors with imputation, not complete objective measures of ability. [SOURCE: §3/Data Appendix]

**Referee score is perceived adequacy, not verified truth.** Random blind reviewer assignment reduces sorting of favorable reviewers to particular teams; it does not eliminate a common ideological or methodological preference across the reviewer pool. The source's claim that scores are “not affected by potential ideological bias on the part of referees” is stronger than random assignment warrants. [SOURCE: §4; INFERENCE]

**Five design dimensions were selected from103 decisions**, producing58 occupied specification cells. Expected AME is the mean of actual estimates in each cell, reused in the same sample. The68% figure is an in-sample explanatory decomposition, not a randomized mediation estimate or held-out forecast of analyst behavior. Post-selection and correlated decision rules remain. [SOURCE: §5/fn13; INFERENCE]
