## Question and estimand

“We examine the long-term associations between human capital and racial and ethnic disparities in labor market outcomes for both men and women using the High School and Beyond 1980 cohort”. “We employ Gelbach's (2016) decomposition method ... providing correlational insights rather than causal relationships.” [SOURCE: introduction]

Population: students in US10th/12th grades in1980 followed to2021, not all current US residents or all birth-cohort members. Outcomes at mid-50s/approximately57–59: annual individual/household income, employment, occupational prestige, financial fragility, occupation tasks/requirements; income in2012 dollars. Male sophomore comparisons add age28 in1992. Unit is person, separately by gender; school-clustered SEs, 2021 longitudinal survey weights. Hispanic analyses distinguish fathers in US **>20 versus1–20 years**, which is a family-duration proxy, not a Mexican-specific generational code. [SOURCE: §§1/2/4/5, fn15; Tables1/5/6]

“Our decomposition answers the question: ‘What proportion of the racial gap is uniquely associated with each human capital factor after accounting for correlations with other predictors?’” Pooled coefficients decompose the unconditional–conditional gap into predictor groups; they do not identify race-specific returns, causal contributions, or the effect of equalizing schooling. [SOURCE: §2, fn8]

## Identification and named threats

Weighted pooled OLS/Oaxaca–Gelbach accounting; four predictor groups: demographics/family, educational attainment, “test scores” (also GPA/courses/track), school context. Gelbach removes dependence on order of covariate insertion; it does **not** remove confounding, selection, reverse channels within controls, or justify changing predictors as an intervention. Context variables may act through networks/noncognitive skills as well as measured academics. School value-added BLUPs control **prior scores only**, not race/sex/SES; their “school contribution” is not randomized school quality. [SOURCE: §2; fn8/9; AppendixA.2; INFERENCE]

Authors name school-based sampling (98% eventual high-school completion), attrition and missing income (~24%), zero-income exclusion (~1.1% of income reporters), missing covariates2–3%/achievement10–13%, absence of2021 hours, limited recent-immigrant samples, race-specific-return imprecision, cohort-specific gender labor attachment and a1992–2021 observation gap. Attained education is measured in adulthood (Table6 at each outcome date), so “end-of-high-school human capital explains X” is broader than the actual temporal controls. The developmental claims about origins of achievement are interpretation, not identified by the decomposition. [SOURCE: §§1/2/conclusion, fn5/6/9/10/13/15; AppendixD; INFERENCE]
