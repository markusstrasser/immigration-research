## 4. Identification and threats

Poisson regressions with agency fixed effects, month-by-year effects, evolving controls and state-clustered SEs; event study around staggered adoption, omitted pre-period represented by one year before. Additional models use state/county linear trends, county quadratic trends and region-by-year effects; negative-binomial robustness. CPS mechanisms use weighted OLS with state trends. Neighbor-policy indicators test geographic displacement. This is policy variation, **not an IV for the number of unauthorized immigrants**. [SOURCE: §3.4; Tables2–7; Appendix4,8]

Authors' key assumption: **“Interpreting our estimate of β1 causally requires that the common trends assumption be satisfied.”** Event-study pre-coefficients do not reject equality; that is a diagnostic, not proof of exogenous enactment. Other named threats: contemporaneous immigration and social policies; selective NIBRS coverage/reporting; policy-induced population movement; victim-reporting changes; heterogeneous effects and geographic trends that can themselves introduce bias; few state clusters. They use wild-cluster-bootstrap p-values, not just conventional stars. [SOURCE: §§3.1,3.4,4.1; fn17]

Additional inference limits: arrests can change through reporting/clearance/enforcement rather than actual incidents; propensity to report property versus violent offenses need not respond identically. Composition controls may condition on a mediator, so persistence after those controls is suggestive, not an identified mediation decomposition. Separate CPS employment/composition results do not link a particular worker to an offense. Staggered timing, heterogeneous effects, repeal and public→universal transitions warrant a cohort-specific reanalysis before transporting the average effect; this paper supplies no such completed modern reanalysis. [INFERENCE based on the stated design]

## 9. What it changes, and what it cannot change

**It checks a policy margin:** restrictions on unauthorized hiring can reduce recorded property crime across a broad Hispanic population while improving salaried employment for citizen Hispanics. This is evidence against a blanket assertion that such restrictions necessarily increase aggregate property crime. It is also a caution against assuming all citizen workers gain equally: the paper's white/Black estimates are largely null. [SOURCE: Tables2,8–10; INFERENCE]

**It measures something else than the repo's main number.** The current declaration is `infra/immigration-fiscal/live_case.json`: **oct15, v8**. INDEX Core State and FAQ anchor price the **45.23M Mexican-origin lineage**, alive in2024, at **$366–456bn per year conditional cost to other residents**; v9 is not adopted. FAQ's rule is exact: **“A result refutes a claim only when population, horizon and outcome match.”** Here the treatment is marginal E-Verify adoption2004–2015, outcome Hispanic-arrestee-linked counts, population all Hispanic origins and both citizen/immigrant groups, geography selected NIBRS agencies. The repo's main case is neither that policy nor that population or outcome. [DATA: live_case; INDEX Core State; FAQ “Before combining numbers”]
