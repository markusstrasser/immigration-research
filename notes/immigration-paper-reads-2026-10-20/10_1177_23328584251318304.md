## 3. Identification and threats

Descriptive stable-cohort design, with discrete-time logistic survival hazards and optional cubic initial-score control. It addresses endogenous removal of high scorers from the current-EL group and later entry of newcomers. It does not randomly allocate school funding, TK, bilingual instruction or teacher training. Policy timing, changing composition, linked-test scaling and migration/exit selection remain threats. They inspect initial classification stability (missing FigureA1) and condition on initial proficiency; neither establishes that policy caused residual improvement. Demographics are partly imputed backward from2008–09; private-school and out-of-state outcomes disappear. [SOURCE: Methods/Introduction/Notes4–8]

Their strongest attribution argument is that initial language differences explain little of the improvements and school environments plausibly changed. The competing explanation need not be changing **observed** initial score alone: unobserved cohort conditions, pre-K selection, selective exits and test-scale linking remain. A survival estimator's censoring assumption does not follow from a low annual attrition rate. [SOURCE: Introduction/Discussion] [INFERENCE]
