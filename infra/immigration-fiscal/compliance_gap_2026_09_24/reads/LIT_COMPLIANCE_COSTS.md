**Verdict:** The usable numbers are these. The IRS net misreporting rate is 1% for withheld wages versus 57% for nonfarm-proprietor income (Pub 1415 Table 5, TY 2014–2016, still the newest measured estimate). The employer FICA+FUTA underreporting gap is $29bn in TY 2014–16, projected at $40bn for TY 2022 (Pub 5869 Table 3), and it excludes agricultural and household employers. In construction, payroll fraud touches 12.4–20.5% of workers (2017) and saves 12.5–23.5% of legal labor cost (Ormiston–Belman–Erlich 2020 Table A). On one Michigan project, workers' compensation plus employer FICA alone come to 4.9% of project cost and 16.1% of labor cost (ICERES 2025). The 2008 three-city survey puts minimum-wage violations at 37.1% for unauthorized, 21.3% for authorized and 15.6% for U.S.-born low-wage workers, and lost pay at about 15% of earnings (Bernhardt et al. 2009). DOL-published WHD totals for the data gate: FY 2023 has 20,215 concluded actions, $212,325,391 in back wages and 163,768 employees; FY 2024 has 17,300, $202,676,115 and 151,989. About a third of FLSA cases are directed (GAO-21-13), and about half of OSHA inspections are programmed (FY 2024: 17,170 of 34,625). No source read measures that immigrant-employing or immigrant-owned firms break these rules more than other firms; the immigrant link rests on worker-side violation rates and anecdote.

**What targeting does to the lane's numbers** [INFERENCE]
- About two-thirds of cases start from a complaint, so they are sampled on workers' willingness to complain. That is lower for unauthorized workers, whom GAO's priority-industry rationale names as "unlikely to file". Complaint cases therefore under-sample exactly the group in question.
- The directed third is aimed at 20 priority low-wage, high-violation industries. They took 75–80% of FLSA actions, and violations were found in 80% of FY 2019 FLSA cases, so per-case findings overstate the population rate in those industries.
- Both biases work against reading WHD back wages per covered worker as prevalence. Survey prevalence (§4) is the better base, with WHD as a detected-and-assessed floor.
- GAO-21-13 also gives the fiscal-year convention for the data gate: FLSA cases are counted in the fiscal year the case closed.

## 8. Disconfirmation: is off-books work concentrated in immigrant-employing firms?

Evidence read that cuts against concentration:
- Bernhardt et al. (2009) find a 15.6% minimum-wage violation rate among U.S.-born low-wage workers. That is lower than 37.1% for the unauthorized, but far from zero. They state the abuses are "not limited to unauthorized immigrants" (§4a).
- Ormiston–Belman–Erlich (2020) report stakeholder accounts that payroll fraud is "prevalent in all parts of the construction sector", middle-class trades included (§3b).
- The IRS 57% misreporting rate applies to every nonfarm proprietor, and no IRS publication splits it by nativity (§1).
- GAO-08-962T (in the cached excerpt) names agriculture, accommodation and food, manufacturing, and health care and social services as WHD's directed-case industries. That is a low-wage-industry pattern, not an immigrant-firm one.

Evidence for concentration is weaker than usually claimed. Bernhardt's worker-side rates double with unauthorized status. The payroll-fraud literature mentions "undocumented laborers" as anecdote and as a reason Southern and Texas surveys may run high. No source read measures misclassification or off-books pay by the immigrant share of a firm's or industry's workforce.
