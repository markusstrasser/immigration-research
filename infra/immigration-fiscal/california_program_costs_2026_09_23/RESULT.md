**Verdict:** The article's "$11 billion" is Medi-Cal alone. It is not a sum across programs. The
article's link for it is an October 2025 Los Angeles Times story in which the Department of Finance
(DOF) put FY2025-26 Medi-Cal for undocumented immigrants at "just over $12.5 billion", of which
"State money accounts for $11.2 billion". That figure is a mid-year projection quoted by a
spokesman; no government document itemizing it was found. The primary documents agree on its
scale. The Legislative Analyst's Office (LAO) put the May 2025 estimate at **$10.8bn General Fund
for 2025-26**, including in-home care (IHSS), and in October 2025 at **about $10bn General Fund a
year, a quarter of Medi-Cal's General Fund**. So claim 3 holds in state dollars for this one
program, with framing caveats [FRAMING-SENSITIVE]. The figure counts children, the state's share
of emergency care that federal law requires, and some lawfully present people in the
"unsatisfactory immigration status" (UIS) category.

Claim 1 ($180bn) holds only in total funds. The state's own budget puts health and human services
at $307bn in 2025-26, of which $84.4bn is General Fund. Medi-Cal alone is $194–197bn in total funds
but $45–49bn General Fund. Most of the remainder is federal money.

Claim 2 holds on eligibility for health care, college aid, tax credits, low-cost car insurance and
phones. Two of those are not paid from taxes, however: Low Cost Auto is funded by premiums, and
LifeLine by a surcharge on phone lines. The rental-assistance example is a San Francisco city
program. CAPI requires the pre-1996 SSI immigration criteria, so "illegal immigrants can qualify"
overstates it.

Claim 4 cannot be checked against documents. The CalWORKs and Medi-Cal rules the officers
described match the official rules, but the CAPI officer's "I don't care about legal or not"
contradicts CDSS's eligibility rule.

## 4. What the article's $11bn most plausibly sums

It is the DOF's $11.2bn state share of FY2025-26 Medi-Cal for undocumented immigrants. The
hyperlink says so, and the article's own Medicaid paragraph later gives the cost as "an estimated
$10 billion annually", citing LAO. The other programs the article lists are not in the $11bn.

"At least" is defensible, but the documents give little above it. For FY2025-26, the unauthorized
share outside Medi-Cal is either zero or unmeasured. CFAP pays nothing to undocumented people
until 2027-28 at the earliest. CAPI serves lawful and PRUCOL immigrants. LifeLine and Low Cost
Auto do not draw on the General Fund. Legal services run $64–69M, for a mixed population
[CALCULATION: 43.6 + 10 or 15 + 10]. The ITIN share of CalEITC/YCTC and the Cal Grant dollars paid
to Dream Act students are not published. The only documented additions in the UIS category are the
+$1.1bn GF of claiming corrections in 2025-26 (819.3 + 288.9 [CALCULATION]). Those raise state
cost while lowering federal cost, and part of them repays earlier years.

## 5. Steel-man and framing

The strongest reading of the article is this. California's treasury spends about $10–11bn a year
giving comprehensive coverage to people without lawful status. Federal law requires only
emergency care, so this is a state choice. LAO says the cost ran "more than double the initial
estimates" [SOURCE: LAO 5083]. The state enacted the coverage while running structural deficits,
and it has since had to freeze and trim the program. LAO's own documents support that reading.

Choices that change the number [FRAMING-SENSITIVE]:
- **Children.** Undocumented children have had full scope since 2016, and they are inside the
  figure.
- **Required emergency care.** Medicaid must cover emergency services for otherwise-eligible
  unauthorized immigrants (42 U.S.C. §1396b(v)) [TRAINING-DATA]. The state's share of that care is
  inside the $11.2bn but is not a California policy choice. LAO's ≈$10bn "expansions" figure is
  closer to the discretionary increment.
- **UIS is not the same as unlawful presence.** UIS "primarily consists of undocumented
  immigrants, as well as certain documented immigrants" [SOURCE: LAO 5092]. From October 2026,
  H.R. 1 moves refugees and asylees into UIS, so later UIS figures include lawful refugees.
- **Mixed-status households.** CalWORKs child-only aid and prorated Section 8 go to US-citizen
  children. The article counts them as subsidizing their parents.
- **A projection, not an actual.** The $11.2bn is an October 2025 mid-year figure.
