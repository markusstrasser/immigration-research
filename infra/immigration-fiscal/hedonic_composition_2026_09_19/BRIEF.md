# Lane: what natives pay to live away from, or near, Hispanic and immigrant neighbours — hedonic capitalisation within metros (2026-09-19)

## Goal
The repo prices fiscal and crime channels and carries trust and "compositional amenities" as narrative only. Card, Dustmann and Preston (2012, JEEA 10(1):78–119) show compositional concerns explain 2–5× the attitude variation that wage and tax concerns do, but give no dollar figure. The one revealed-preference route to a dollar figure is housing: within a metro, if natives value neighbourhood composition, the change in a neighbourhood's Hispanic or foreign-born share should be capitalised into its rents and prices relative to the rest of the metro (Saiz and Wachter 2011, REStat 93(1):169–188, "Immigration and the Neighborhood": within-metro immigrant density predicts relatively lower subsequent native price growth; verify the paper's actual design and magnitudes from the full text before reproducing). This is distinct from the metro-level demand effect (Wilson and Zhou 2026, ladder revisions: +2.2% prices, +1.4% rents per 1% employment inflow), which is positive and must be netted out with metro-year fixed effects.

## Data routes, in order
1. ACS 5-year tract and ZCTA tables via the Census API (key in config.local.env): B03002 (Hispanic origin), B05002 (nativity), B25064 (median gross rent), B25077 (median home value), B25003 (tenure), B19013 (income), B15003 (education), B25034 (housing age). Vintages 2009–2013, 2014–2018, 2019–2023 (tract boundaries change in 2020: use ZCTA or the NHGIS/Census 2010→2020 tract relationship file, state your choice). CBSA delineation fixed at 2013 (the repo's convention).
2. Zillow ZIP-level ZORI and ZHVI monthly CSVs from files.zillowstatic.com (public, under 100 MB each); metro-level files are already at `~/research-data/immigration-fiscal/data/external/urban_housing/zillow/` and must not be modified.
3. Optional check: HUD Fair Market Rents by ZIP (SAFMR) for the same years.

## Design
Long differences within CBSA: Δlog(rent) and Δlog(value) on Δ(Hispanic share) and Δ(foreign-born share), CBSA-period fixed effects, controls for initial income, education, tenure, housing age, and initial rent level (mean reversion). Identification arms: (a) OLS long difference; (b) shift-share instrument using 2000 or 2010 enclave shares × national growth of the origin group (state what the instrument assumes and run the Goldsmith-Pinkham–Sorkin–Swift exposure check on the largest shares); (c) Saiz–Wachter's own specification, lagged density predicting subsequent growth, reproduced as closely as the paper allows. Report separately for Hispanic share, foreign-born share, and Mexico-born share where B05006 permits. Heterogeneity by metro housing-supply elasticity (Saiz 2010 elasticities if obtainable; else permit-based proxy) and by initial share (tipping non-linearity, Card, Mas and Rothstein 2008).

## Outputs
(a) Coefficients: percent change in rent and value per 10-point change in group share, all arms, with SEs clustered by CBSA. (b) Dollar translation: the implied annual rent differential per household and per resident of the group, and the capitalised stock effect on native-owned housing per metro; state the aggregation explicitly and give it as a range, never a point. (c) A placebo: pre-period rent growth on later share change. (d) The metro-level demand effect from the same data, to show the two operate at different levels. (e) A one-paragraph comparison against Card–Dustmann–Preston's weights and against the repo's per-resident fiscal gap (ladder 123: about −$7,100 per person-year vs whites) so the parent can say how large the amenity channel is relative to the ledger.

## Limits to state
Rents capitalise everything correlated with composition (school quality, crime, income), so this is a bundled amenity price, not a taste for ethnicity; sorting of natives is the mechanism, so it is a valuation by movers; ZIP and tract are not neighbourhoods; ZORI covers rentals in Zillow's sample only. Do not write about groups' worth; the quantity is a market price of neighbourhood change.
