**Verdict:** Inspection records show that LICENSED food trucks and carts in Los Angeles County (2009–July 2012) drew fewer health-code violations per inspection than restaurants (3.6 and 2.4 vs 7.8), with establishment fixed effects; it says nothing about unpermitted street vendors, who are not in inspection data at all, which is the group the "scruples" claim concerns. Grade: B- for the licensed comparison (administrative data, fixed effects, but no adjustment for food risk in LA and different inspection intensity), not applicable to unlicensed vending.

## Population, period, unit

Seven cities; for Los Angeles, LA County Department of Public Health inspections 2009–July 2012: 2,928 inspections of 601 food trucks, 42,089 of 7,542 restaurants, 594 of 236 carts; unit: inspection.

## Design

Mean violations per inspection by establishment type; OLS with establishment fixed effects plus weekday, month and year dummies; Poisson with time dummies and establishment-clustered SEs. Food-risk controls only in Seattle and DC.

## What it says about restaurants or formal businesses

Licensed mobile vendors are not dirtier than restaurants under the same inspection regime. [INFERENCE] This does not answer the operator's claim, which is about vendors outside the permit system: in 2012 LA County inspected only 236 carts, while the city alone had an estimated 10,000 food vendors (CLA 2014), so almost all sidewalk food vending was uninspected. Carts also sell lower-risk menus than restaurants, and LA has no risk control in the model, so the gap partly reflects menu, not conduct.

## Stated limitations

"When looking at the rate of violations, keep in mind that the average numbers of violations were low for all types of food service in all cities. Thus, some eye-popping comparisons are not as dramatic as they may appear."
