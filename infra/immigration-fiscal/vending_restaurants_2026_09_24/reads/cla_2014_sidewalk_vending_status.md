**Verdict:** The primary government source behind the widely repeated "50,000 Los Angeles street vendors": an unsourced Bureau of Street Services estimate relayed by the Chief Legislative Analyst, with no method, date of count or sales figure; usable only as an order-of-magnitude administrative guess for about 2014. Grade: C- for the count (official but methodless), no restaurant evidence.

Source: Sharon M. Tso, Chief Legislative Analyst, City of Los Angeles, "Sidewalk Vending Status Report", report to the Economic Development Committee, Council File 13-1493, dated 26 Nov 2014 (9 pp., scanned).

## Population, period, unit

Sidewalk vendors in the City of Los Angeles (not the county), about 2014; unit = vendors. Food trucks are excluded ("this report does not address food trucks or other food vending vehicles affecting street parking", p. 2).

## Design

None stated: a figure attributed to Bureau of Street Services representatives. No survey, count date, or definition of "vendor" (full-time, seasonal, or anyone cited once) is given.

## What it says about restaurants or formal businesses

Nothing measured. The report notes that the Bureau of Street Services was developing criteria to "determine proximity to other businesses" (p. 8), and that stakeholders preferred a program "rather than limit the number of permits" (p. 7).

## Stated limitations

"While we do not have a dollar amount with respect to the cost of operating and enforcing a sidewalk vending program" (p. 7); the vendor count is attributed, not derived.

## Consequence for the lane

Every later "50,000 vendors" and "$504 million" figure (Economic Roundtable 2015, press coverage) traces to this one unsourced estimate. The 80% non-food share here (40,000 of 50,000) differs from Sidewalk Stimulus's "three-quarters" wording [INFERENCE]. Food vendors, the group that competes with restaurants, are ~10,000 on this estimate.
