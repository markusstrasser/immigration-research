**Verdict:** A vendor-advocacy legal report (UCLA Law clinic with Public Counsel and vendor organizations) documenting that food vending stayed effectively unpermitted after SB 946 (165 food permits in the City of LA by mid-2021 against ~10,000 food vendors); its counts are secondary or from an aid-program intake survey, and its claims about brick-and-mortar complementarity cite other reports, not new data. Grade: C for permit counts, D for restaurant claims.

Source: UCLA School of Law Community Economic Development Clinic, Community Power Collective, Inclusive Action for the City, Public Counsel and others, *Unfinished Business: How Food Laws Criminalize Sidewalk Food Vendors and Harm Low-Income Communities of Color*, June 2021, 53 pp.

## Population, period, unit

City of Los Angeles sidewalk food vendors, 2020–2021; permit counts (StreetsLA/DPH), and an intake survey of 2,653 vendors who received emergency cash from the Street Vendor Emergency Fund (not a random sample; selected on applying for aid during COVID).

## Design

Legal and policy analysis with interviews; descriptive counts.

## What it says about restaurants or formal businesses

Asserts "Sidewalk vendors are a benefit, not a threat, to other brick-and-mortar businesses" (p. 11), citing a 2016 study, a Portland business survey, Sidewalk Stimulus 2015 and the Maxwell Street closure. None is causal. The 73% high-risk share matters for the "scruples" question: most food vending is the kind of hot or cooked-to-order food that the health code regulates most tightly, and almost none of it was permitted in 2021 [INFERENCE].

## Stated limitations

"There is no available comprehensive demographic data on the vending population" (p. 10).
