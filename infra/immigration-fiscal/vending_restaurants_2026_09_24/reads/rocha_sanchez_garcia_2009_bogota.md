**Verdict:** The closest thing found to a quantitative estimate of street vendors' effect on nearby formal shops: a cross-sectional OLS on 694 surveyed Bogotá establishments (2004) finds that more vendors on the same block go with slightly lower shop sales (elasticity −0.044, p = 0.08) and employment (−0.05, p < 0.01), with 2003 sales/employment as controls; not a natural experiment, one city, mostly retail rather than restaurants. Grade: C (cross-section with lagged-outcome controls; the authors concede reverse causality is not solved).

Source: Ricardo Rocha, Fabio Sánchez and Leonardo García, "Ventas callejeras y espacio público: efectos sobre el comercio de Bogotá" (Street vendors and public space: the effects on commerce in Bogotá), *Desarrollo y Sociedad* 63 (first semester 2009): 245–268. Study carried out by Universidad de los Andes (CEDE) for the Cámara de Comercio de Bogotá (the city's chamber of commerce, i.e. the formal businesses' association).

## Population, period, unit

Four commercial corridors of Bogotá (Centro, Restrepo, Kennedy and a fourth), census of 12,759 establishments and 4,143 street vendors, and a survey of 717 establishments, Dec 2004–Jan 2005; unit: establishment; outcome years 2004 (controls 2003).

## Design

Cross-sectional OLS: log 2004 sales (or employment) of each establishment on log number of street vendors in its block (from the vendor census), establishment traits (modernity index, branches, sector dummies), 2003 employment (sales) as a scale control, zone dummies, public works and local robbery rate. No instrument, no panel, no policy shock.

## What it says about restaurants or formal businesses

This is the steel-man of the restaurant side: vendors in the same block are associated with lower formal sales and jobs even after controlling for the shop's own 2003 scale. The authors argue the remaining bias runs toward zero, so the true harm would be larger: "como se espera un efecto positivo del comercio establecido hacia la presencia de ventas callejeras, el coeficiente obtenido estaría sesgado hacia arriba, lo cual implica que el verdadero efecto sería aún más negativo" (p. 264) [since formal commerce is expected to attract vendors, the coefficient would be biased upward, so the true effect would be more negative]. [INFERENCE] The mechanism they name is congestion of public space (sidewalk crowding), not only price competition. The sectors are retail (textiles, footwear, sundries, fruit), not restaurants. A 100% removal simulated from a log-vendor elasticity is an extrapolation, and the overall-commerce line (employment −2.1%) shows that removal destroys more vendor jobs than it adds in shops.

## Stated limitations

"la metodología econométrica puede no corregir del todo la doble causalidad … lo que ameritaría una adecuada instrumentación de la variable de interés" (p. 264) [the method may not fully correct two-way causality, which would call for proper instrumentation]. Cross-section; spatial externalities not modelled.
