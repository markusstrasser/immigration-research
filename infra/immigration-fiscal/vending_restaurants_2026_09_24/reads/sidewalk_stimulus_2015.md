**Verdict:** An advocacy-commissioned size estimate (vendor count × one student-survey revenue figure) plus a descriptive, uncontrolled same-block comparison of businesses near and far from 284 vendor arrest locations; the size arithmetic is usable as an order of magnitude for 2012–2014, the brick-and-mortar comparison says nothing causal. Grade: size C, restaurant effect D.

## Population, period, unit

City of Los Angeles street vendors (food and merchandise), sales year about 2012–2014 (IMPLAN 2012 data); brick-and-mortar comparison uses QCEW establishments 2007–2011 in Boyle Heights, Downtown and Hollywood; vendor locations are 284 LAPD/StreetsLA arrest records, 1 Jul 2011–23 May 2012.

## Design

- Size: 50,000 vendors (Bureau of Street Services estimate, via a Nov 2014 Chief Legislative Analyst report) × $10,098 revenue per vendor per year (a 2014 UCLA urban-planning student survey of Westlake/MacArthur Park vendors, not a representative sample). Multipliers from IMPLAN.
- Restaurants: establishments on the same Census block as a vendor arrest location ("near") against all others ("far"), 2007–2011; raw means, no controls, no pre-trends; vendor locations are arrest locations, so they mix vending with enforcement.

Arithmetic check [CALCULATION: 50,000 × $10,098 = $504.9 million]: the $504 million total is the count times the one per-vendor figure. "Three-quarters" merchandise implies 12,500 food vendors, not the stated 10,000; the report is internally inconsistent here [INFERENCE]. It also gives two stimulus totals ($517 million on p. 5; $434 million on p. 20) and two tax figures ($124 million potential, p. 6; $83 million lost, p. 20) without reconciling them.

## What it says about restaurants

The report states both sides. Steel-man of the restaurant complaint, as the report itself summarizes the literature: "Vendors don't pay rent, taxes, and payroll, according to businesses interviewed in a 1980 study, and therefore threaten the livelihoods of stores, especially smaller ones" (p. 9). Its own evidence for complementarity is thin: few restaurants shared a block with a vendor arrest (3 of 80 in Boyle Heights), and restaurants near vendors added "one more job" than those far away, with no test reported. Vendors reportedly avoid selling food in front of restaurants (Kettles 2004 observation, p. 10). [INFERENCE] The near/far comparison cannot separate vendor effects from vendors choosing busy blocks; the sign it reports is what selection on foot traffic would also produce.

## Stated limitations

"To date, there is no comprehensive survey of Los Angeles street vendors. … We recognize the limitations of this data and also its concentration in certain geographies" (p. 1). "A multitude of factors contribute to business profit and loss, including but not solely the presence of street vendors" (p. 14).
