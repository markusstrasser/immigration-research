**Verdict:** Confirmed in the NBER June 2015 version: recipients' welfare benefit is "about $0.2 to $0.4" per dollar of government Medicaid spending. The value is about $0.4 under the complete-information approach and the consumption-proxy optimization approach, and about $0.2 under the other two. Under sensitivity analysis the range is $0.15–$0.85. About $0.6 per dollar is a transfer to external parties, the providers of implicit insurance to the uninsured: $2,222 of G = $3,596 per recipient per year. The population is Oregon adults aged 19–64, below 100% of the federal poverty line, uninsured and not categorically eligible. The paper names no single "preferred" estimate. The JPE 2019 version was not reached, and its headline normalization may differ.

## Corrections to the brief's figures

- The brief's description of the population is confirmed: low-income (below 100% FPL) uninsured adults aged 19–64 in Oregon who are not categorically eligible for traditional Medicaid (quote 7).
- The brief asked for a "preferred estimate". None exists (quote 9). The baseline is $0.2–0.4 per $1 of government spending, and the sensitivity range is $0.15–0.85.
- The external-party share is about $0.6 per $1 of government spending: N = $2,222 of G = $3,596, or 62% (quote 5).
- Not verified: whether the JPE 2019 version reports the same numbers, or whether it normalizes by resource cost instead of government spending. Check before citing the JPE version.

    figures differ slightly from the working paper's: G $3,600 against $3,596; N $2,152 against $2,222; C $1,448
