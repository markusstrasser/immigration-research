**Verdict:** The tax on the return to a marginal investment runs from 9.3% (CRS, economy-wide excluding land,
2024 law) to 29% (CBO, business capital, 2014 law). The two models differ even under similar law: for pre-TCJA law
CRS gives 11.0% economy-wide and CBO 18%. OMB's base-case 7% "approximates the marginal pretax rate of return on
an average investment in the private sector".

Used for: τ and m in claim 2's productive-use reading (`world_ledger.py` `CAPITAL_TAX`, `RETURN_OVER_DISCOUNT`,
`saving_leak`).

## Quotes

1. CRS, PDF p. 5: "The METR is the share of the return on the investment that is paid in taxes. ... It also
   captures taxes paid by creditors and shareholders (both U.S. and foreign) on interest, dividends, and capital
   gains."
2. CRS, summary (PDF p. 2): "The overall (marginal) tax rate on investment in the economy was 11.0% before the TCJA
   in 2017, 7.6% in 2018, and 9.3% in 2024, and will be 11.1% in 2027."
3. CRS, Table 6 (PDF p. 16), 2024 column, excluding land: economy-wide 9.3%, corporate 13.6%, noncorporate 17.0%,
   owner-occupied housing −2.3%. The text: "the appropriate rate for considering investment is the one excluding
   land because land is not reproducible."
4. CBO, publication page: "The ETR on capital income is, on average, 18 percent; The ETR on income from
   owner-occupied housing is close to zero; and The ETR on capital income generated by businesses is, on average,
   29 percent." The page also states that CBO covered "both corporate and individual taxation—but only with
   respect to the permanent features of federal income tax law in 2014".
5. OMB A-94 (1992), PDF p. 9, §8.b(1): "Constant-dollar benefit-cost analyses of proposed investments and
   regulations should report net present value and other outcomes determined using a real discount rate of 7
   percent. This rate approximates the marginal pretax rate of return on an average investment in the private
   sector in recent years."

## Use

- τ: 0.093 (quote 3, the current economy-wide rate) and 0.29 (quote 4, business capital before the 2017 corporate
  rate cut). The high end favours the productive-use reading: the rich's saving goes mostly into financial claims
  on business and debt, and the rate is from 2014 law.
- m, the pre-tax return over the rate at which the forgone tax revenue is discounted: 1 when discounting at 7%
  (quote 5), 3.5 at 2%, the main case's low-end real cost of government funds (OMB A-4 2023; Treasury's 2024 real
  yields 1.94–2.15%; `decisions/2026-09-27-main-case-capital-return-and-long-run-responses.md`).
  - Corrected 2026-10-07: 7% over 2% implies a 71% tax on capital, inconsistent with τ above, and Newell, Pizer
    and Prest reject that pair (`newell_pizer_prest_2023.md` quote 3). The upper m is now 1/(1 − τ), discounting
    at the after-tax return the tax implies: 1.10 at τ 9.3%, 1.41 at 29%.
