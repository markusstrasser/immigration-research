reason is simple assault.  NCVS violent victimisation is dominated by simple assault,
which the UCR index excludes.  Adding 'other assaults' (the UCR simple-assault line) to

  UCR index violent only (murder, rape, robbery, aggravated assault)           2.77x

On the NCVS-comparable basis the arrest ratio falls from about 2.8x to about 1.9x, which
brackets the 1.37x victim-report ratio far more closely.  The residual gap, and the
larger gap to the 2.1-2.9x prison ratio, is the enforcement, charging and sentencing
wedge PLUS the offence-severity mix.  Nothing here separates the two, and the cost
weighting used in this lane deliberately AMPLIFIES severity: the Hispanic arrest share
is highest for the most expensive offences (murder 43%, robbery 49%, rape 42%) and
lowest for larceny (21%) and fraud (22%).  [FRAMING-SENSITIVE]

CROSS-CHECK against a lifetime career-criminal valuation
====================================================================================================
Cohen & Piquero (2009), J Quant Criminol 25:25-49, abstract (publisher page read this
session): 'We estimate the present value of saving a 14-year-old high risk juvenile from
a life of crime to range from $2.6 to $5.3 million.  Similarly, saving a high risk youth
at birth would save society between $2.6 and $4.4 million.'
The often-quoted $4.2-7.2m 'career criminal' figure is NOT in the abstract and was not
verified here. [UNVERIFIED]

Implied scale check.  Route A2's difference is a FLOW per adult-year; over the 40 years
from 25 to 64, undiscounted:
  base arm        $  1,421/yr x 40 = $    56,846 per adult 25-64
  adjusted-coding arm $2,023/yr x 40 = $    80,912 per adult 25-64
Against the by-generation memo's synthetic-cohort lifetime fiscal gap of about $250,000
per adult (undiscounted), crime cost is a 23-32% addition on the base arm.  Cohen &
Piquero's $2.6-5.3m is the lifetime cost of ONE high-risk offender, not a population
average, so it is not comparable to these per-adult figures and is quoted only to show
the order of magnitude that a single career offender carries. [INFERENCE]

====================================================================================================
FRAGMENTATION / SOCIAL COHESION — NO DEFENSIBLE DOLLAR ESTIMATE
====================================================================================================
Asked for, and refused on the evidence.  There is no per-adult dollar figure for lost
social cohesion that survives the standards this repo applies to the lines above.  What
exists, and why none of it converts:

1. Putnam (2007), 'E Pluribus Unum', Scandinavian Political Studies 30(2):137-174.  The
   canonical finding: in the short run, ethnic diversity is associated with lower trust,
   less altruism and fewer friendships, including within one's own group ('hunkering
   down').  It reports no dollar value and proposes none.  It is also a CROSS-SECTIONAL
   association on US localities, with a long replication dispute about whether the effect
   survives controls for deprivation and residential sorting. [SOURCE: doi 10.1111/
   j.1467-9477.2007.00176.x] [UNVERIFIED: the replication literature was not read here]

2. Algan & Cahuc (2010), 'Inherited Trust and Growth', AER 100(5):2060-2092, and Algan &
   Cahuc (2013, Handbook of Economic Growth ch. 2): about a fifth of the cross-country
   variance in income per head 1980-2009 co-varies with generalised trust, and a one
   standard deviation rise in trust is associated with income per head about 6.8% higher.
   [UNVERIFIED: read from secondary summaries this session, not the papers]

3. Wellbeing-valuation attempts, e.g. 'Valuation of Trust in Government: The Wellbeing
   Valuation Approach', Sustainability 13(19):11000 (2021), doi 10.3390/su131911000, price
   trust by the income equivalent of its life-satisfaction effect. [UNVERIFIED: not read]

Why none of these becomes a line in this ledger, in order of severity:
  (a) None is identified as a CAUSAL effect of any specific group's presence.  Putnam is
      a diversity-index correlation; Algan-Cahuc is a cross-country growth regression
      instrumented by ancestors' inherited trust.  Neither licenses attributing a dollar
      cost to US-born Mexican-origin adults, who are the group in question.
  (b) Any trust->income elasticity applied to a US subgroup would double count the wage
      and tax effects the ledger already measures directly.
  (c) The sign is contested even in principle: the same literature finds diversity raising
      innovation and variety.  A one-sided cost line would be a framing choice, not a
      measurement. [FRAMING-SENSITIVE]
  (d) Any number produced would have an uncertainty band wider than the entire -$8,286
      fiscal gap, so it could not change a decision.
Verdict on this item: [UNVERIFIED]/speculative.  Stated as a qualitative consideration,
never as a dollar figure.
