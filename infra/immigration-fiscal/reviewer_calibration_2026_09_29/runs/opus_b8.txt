**CASE K345C**
VERDICT: ERROR
LOCATION: `founder_profile`: only `p.benefits` changes on legalization, and earnings and taxes stay on `unauthorized_profile`
WHY: Legalization switches on eligibility for Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid and SSI, but earnings and taxes stay at the unauthorized level. The cited studies show wage gains of 6–15% and a large shift of work onto payroll records. The model counts the extra costs of legal status but leaves out the extra revenue, so the 34% rise is biased upward and could be much smaller.
CONFIDENCE: 0.88

**CASE K1CF8**
VERDICT: ERROR
LOCATION: Claim: "This is the with-against-without effect", measured by assigning at-fault crashes to the group
WHY: Charging the group for all damage to other residents in crashes its drivers cause is a fault-based accounting, not a counterfactual. The account's own elasticities (about +0.07 for non-fatal crashes, −0.21 for fatal ones) say that removing the group's vehicle-miles would barely change other residents' crash risk per mile. That puts the with-against-without effect near zero, far below $42.3bn. The comparison to crime fails because other crashes would largely replace the removed ones.
CONFIDENCE: 0.75

**CASE K8A79**
VERDICT: ERROR
LOCATION: Round 3 birthplace imputation, and different handling of missing birthplaces across rounds
WHY: Round 3 imputes birthplace for 64% of institutionalized Mexican-origin men and marks 91% of those as foreign-born. Among men who reported a birthplace, only 38.5% were foreign-born. Because the donors were household residents, the imputation roughly doubles the foreign-born count (72% vs 38.5%; the ratio falls to about 0.6 if imputed cases matched reporters), while rounds 1–2 simply dropped missing birthplaces. The jump to 1.10 is therefore mostly a data-processing artifact, and the step from 0.45 to 0.48 is within noise (SE of the difference ≈0.07).
CONFIDENCE: 0.9
