CASE K893A
VERDICT: ERROR
LOCATION: The 2000 data point (0.13), because of how the 2000 census imputed birthplace for institutionalized men (QBPL flag)
WHY: In 2000, 68% of institutionalized Mexican-origin men had an imputed birthplace, and 98% of those were assigned a US birth, while 38.5% of the men who reported a birthplace were foreign-born. If the imputed men resemble the reporters, the census counts only about 0.32×0.385 + 0.68×0.02 ≈ 0.14 of the group as foreign-born against a true 0.385, an undercount of about 2.8×, which moves the 0.13 ratio to about 0.37. The 1980–1990 change (0.45 to 0.42) is within its SEs, and the censuses handle missing birthplace differently, so no cohort-by-cohort improvement is shown.
CONFIDENCE: 0.9

CASE K1FC8
VERDICT: ERROR
LOCATION: The step from equal skip rates (18.1% vs 18.2%) to "no bias", given that the income hot deck does not match on Hispanic origin
WHY: Hot-deck bias depends on who the donors are, not on how often people skip. Without Hispanic origin in the match, the group's nonrespondents mostly get donors from outside the group. As a result, the fill-ins keep only 9% of the group's wage gap (DiD +0.139, about 3 SE) and raise Social Security, pension and interest income by 19–66 log points. Running those incomes through a tax calculator overstates the group's taxable income and tax.
CONFIDENCE: 0.9

CASE K3059
VERDICT: SOUND
LOCATION: none
WHY: The evidence rules out the ASEC oversample and the January 2025 controls. It places the gap in the split between Mexico-born and other Hispanics under a Hispanic control that applies only nationally, and the CPS actually counts more Hispanics in total. CASE KEECB
VERDICT: SOUND
LOCATION: none
WHY: The price line counts only other residents' consumer savings. It leaves out the group's own consumption and firms' profits, whose taxes sit in receipts, and wage effects are booked separately. No dollar appears in both the price line and the receipts line. Counting other residents' wage losses and their price gains separately is the standard split of transfers and surplus, not double counting.
CONFIDENCE: 0.65
