CASE K3023
VERDICT: ERROR
LOCATION: The premise that a low spending response "cannot coexist with no dilution", and the calculation that multiplies all uncharged spending by the 0.61 instructional share.
WHY: Fixed costs and spare capacity make an extra pupil cost less than the average pupil, which by itself produces an enrollment elasticity below 1 with no dilution. Those fixed costs are mostly non-instructional, so applying the 0.61 share overstates the harm. The $16.1bn assumes no economies of scale and uses only the first-years response. The long-run response gives $3.6bn, the direct class-size pathway gives $2.6bn, and the range crosses zero, so neither "most" nor the central figure is supported.
CONFIDENCE: 0.68

CASE K1FDB
VERDICT: ERROR
LOCATION: The step from "returnees have less schooling than never-migrants" to "the US stayers' average schooling rises"
WHY: Whether return migration raises the stayers' average depends on how returnees compare with the emigrants who stay in the US, not with people who never left Mexico. Mexican emigrants as a group already have less schooling than non-migrants, so returnees at 7.9 years could be above the stayers' mean. The survey does not observe the stayers, as its stated limitation says.
CONFIDENCE: 0.87
