CASE K32D1
VERDICT: ERROR
LOCATION: The inference from the returnee-versus-non-migrant gap (+1.2 years) to "US stayers' average schooling falls"
WHY: Return migration lowers the US stayers' average only if returnees have more schooling than the migrants who remain in the US. Comparing them with people who never left Mexico cannot show that, and the limitation says the survey does not observe emigrants still in the US. If stayers average more than 10.8 years, return migration raises their mean.
CONFIDENCE: 0.9

CASE K042E
VERDICT: ERROR
LOCATION: The outcome, which is receipt across all households with no nativity split, combined with the group-specific rates (6.9% for immigrant households, 4.2% for native ones)
WHY: Composition alone predicts this result. A 1-point rise in immigrant share raises the all-household rate by 0.01 × (6.9 − 4.2) = 0.027 pp, which is nearly the whole 0.03 estimate. The implied effect on natives is about 0.003 pp (SE 0.012), so the data give no evidence that natives are pushed onto transfer programmes.
CONFIDENCE: 0.9

CASE K76EF
VERDICT: ERROR
LOCATION: Adding the aggravated-assault and robbery unit costs, which include a risk-of-homicide component, to the murder costs
WHY: Assaults and robberies that end in death are recorded as murders, so those deaths are already in the $924. They are counted again through the homicide component: 76% of $380 plus 42% of $60 ≈ $314. With that removed, the gap is about $1,107 and murder's share is about 83%, not 65%.
