CASE KE5B7

WHY: The method change alone moved the 31 Dec 2018 count from 168,900 to 121,500, a 28% drop, which is larger than the whole claimed 26% decline. CASE K1989
VERDICT: ERROR
LOCATION: Equating the "with-against-without effect" with damage in crashes where group drivers are at fault, net of their liability insurance.
WHY: Fault attribution is a liability rule; it does not measure what would happen without the group's driving. The account's own elasticity of +1.4, on the networks carrying 65% of the group's driving, means the group's vehicles raise the crash rate for all other drivers, including in crashes where group drivers are not at fault or not involved, and liability insurance does not cover that. Removing about a tenth of urban traffic would cut other residents' crash costs by far more than $9.6bn, so the figure likely understates the effect several times over.
CONFIDENCE: 0.85

CASE K7572
VERDICT: ERROR
LOCATION: The outcome variable, which is the SSI receipt rate across all households because the 2000 file has no nativity split.
WHY: Immigrant households receive SSI at 1.9% and native households at 4.6%, so a 1-point shift toward immigrant households lowers the all-household rate by about 0.027pp even if natives' receipt does not change. That accounts for nearly all of the −0.03 estimate. The implied effect on natives is about zero, with an interval reaching roughly +0.02pp. Arrivals after 1996 were largely ineligible for SSI, so the composition effect could exceed 0.03pp and hide some displacement. The estimate is not better evidence against displacement.
CONFIDENCE: 0.8
