# Lane: calibrating the Clemens–Pritchett institutional-transmission model with the repo's own generation data (2026-09-19)

## Goal
Clemens and Pritchett (2019, JDE 138:153–164, doi:10.1016/j.jdeveco.2018.12.003; open working-paper version CGD WP 423, 2016) formalise the "migrants carry low productivity or institutions" fear as a dynamically efficient migration model with three parameters: transmission τ (fraction of origin TFP embodied per migrant), assimilation a (annual rate at which migrants' productivity determinants converge to natives'), congestion c. Their calibration (a in 0.026–0.128, τ in 0.157–0.497, c < 0.5) yields an optimal migration rate above current levels. The repo has measured, for the Mexican-origin population specifically, quantities that are assimilation rates in this model's sense: earnings 71–81% of white natives' for the Mexico-born and the second generation (generation memo 2026-09-16), fiscal gaps that narrow at a common age but not at a common age and place (ladder 125), attitudes and civil-liberties convergence by generation (ladder 87, 104, 110, 117, 135), and G2→G3+ stalls in several outcomes. The question: what does the model imply when a and τ are set from the repo's Mexican-origin measurements rather than from the paper's pooled poor-country census figures, and does the paper's conclusion survive?

Penn World Table or the paper's own figures for Mexico/US TFP or GDP per worker ratio (state which).

## Design
(a) Reproduce the paper's baseline m* and transition time T from its parameters. (b) Estimate a for Mexico-born → second → third-plus on each repo outcome (earnings, fiscal balance at common age, civil-liberties index, institutional confidence, economic-role-of-government items) as an annual convergence rate over a 29-year generation, with the paper's definition; report the range and which outcomes stall. (c) Estimate τ for Mexico from the paper's own method (immigrant productivity relative to origin and destination) using the repo's earnings ratios; compare to the paper's Mexico row. (d) Solve m* over the repo's (a, τ) grid at c = 0, 0.5, and the paper's upper bound; report the migration rate and the level of Mexican-origin stock at which the model turns the sign. (e) Disconfirmation: the outcomes that do not converge (economic role of government, fiscal balance at common age and place) as a lower bound on a; the outcomes that over-converge (institutional confidence above whites) as an upper bound; show the model's conclusion at each. (f) State plainly what the model cannot represent: assortative geography, political externality (ladder 97), fiscal channels the model treats as TFP.

## Outputs
`derived/`: parameter table with sources, m* grid, reproduction gate log. `RESULT.md`: verdict on whether the repo's measured assimilation rates fall inside the paper's range, and what m* they imply relative to the observed Mexican migration rate (state the observed rate and its source). No policy advice; the output is the model's implication under measured parameters.

## Limits to state
The model is about global efficiency, not the host country's fiscal balance; a and τ are defined on TFP, and the repo's outcomes are proxies; the paper's calibration is cross-country and the repo's is one origin.
