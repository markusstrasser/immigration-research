# Who are the IR-5 (parent of a US citizen) adjusters? Published evidence (2026-09-27)

**Verdict:** No published table gives prior status or time in the US for parents of citizens who adjust, for
Mexico or for all countries, so the published record cannot fix the split between visitor-type and
long-resident Mexican IR-5 adjusters. The closest measured proxies:
(1) INS FY1997 Table 10, all classes: of 94,446 Mexican adjusters, 16.1% entered as visitors for pleasure,
71.9% entered without inspection (the 245(i) era) and 9.6% had unknown status. FY1998-2002 are unusable, with
56-89% unknown.
(2) INS FY1997 Table 11: 61% of Mexican adjusters with a known year had last entered in 1992 or earlier, and
18% in 1996-97. These figures cover all classes, not parents.
(3) NIS surveys, all countries: in FY1996, 20.8% of parents of citizens had any prior illegal experience,
against 73.7% of Mexicans (Jasso et al. 2008). In 2003, only 25-34% of parents adjusted (Jasso 2011), far below
the ~80% of Mexican IR-5 who adjust in FY2019-24.
(4) Immediate relatives aged 55+ in FY2023-24: 44-46% adjust, and adjusters skew younger within 55+.
Under the law, a parent who crossed without inspection cannot adjust without 245(i) or parole. Adjusters
therefore entered on a visa or border crossing card (visitors, or overstays from such entries) or were paroled.
Mexicans received 513,763 port-of-entry paroles, October 2018 to May 2025.
NOT FOUND: any class x prior-status table after FY2002, Mexican IR-5 age by channel, recent 245(i) counts,
military parole-in-place counts, and the PPIC appendix giving pathways by visa class.

## Q1. DHS/INS tables of adjustments by status at entry

**What exists.** INS Statistical Yearbooks FY1986-FY2002 carry Table 10, "Immigrants admitted who were adjusted
to permanent resident status by selected status at entry and region and country of birth". Table 11 gives
"calendar year of entry, type of admission, and region and country of birth". Both cross status or entry year
with **country of birth only, never with class of admission**. No IR-5 split exists in any yearbook checked
(FY1997-FY2003). The FY2003 yearbook no longer has Table 10: its "status at entry" hits are enforcement tables
only [SOURCE: `_cache/who/Yearbook_Immigration_Statistics_{1997..2003}.txt`, from
https://ohss.dhs.gov/sites/default/files/2023-12/Yearbook_Immigration_Statistics_{year}.pdf; the 1996 URL
returned 404].

**Table 10, Mexico-born adjusters, all classes** [SOURCE: quote ids yb1997-t10-mexico ... yb2002-t10-mexico;
FY1997 on PDF p.47]:

| 1998 | 76,475 | 111 | 6,268 | 103 | 204 | 136 | 318 | 26,037 | 43,103 |

Exchange visitors (FY1997-2000) and intracompany transferees are omitted from the table but included in the
totals; the quote rows give every column.

- FY1997 [CALCULATION]: visitors for pleasure 16.1% of Mexican adjusters (17.8% of those with known status),
  entered without inspection 71.9% (79.6% of known), unknown 9.6%. All countries, FY1997: visitors for pleasure
  22.4%, entered without inspection 24.6% of 417,659 [SOURCE: yb1997-t10-all; CALCULATION].
- Unknown status grows to 56.4% (FY1998), 68.2%, 69.5%, 88.9% and 72.8% (FY2002) [CALCULATION]. Among known
  cases, visitors for pleasure run 19-30% (FY1998-2002, excluding the anomalous FY2001) [CALCULATION].
- Bearing on the split: FY1997 is the only clean year, and it describes all Mexican adjusters under the
  1994-97 version of 245(i), when people who had entered without inspection could adjust for a $1,000 fee.
  Most Mexican adjusters then had crossed without inspection. A parent who crossed without inspection cannot
  adjust today unless grandfathered under 245(i) or paroled (see Q3). The 1997 mix therefore bounds nothing for
  FY2019-24 parents [INFERENCE]. The 15,202 visitors for pleasure show that the B-2/border-crossing-card route
  into adjustment was already large for Mexicans in 1997 [INFERENCE].

**Table 11, FY1997, year of latest entry of adjusters** [SOURCE: yb1997-t11-mexico, yb1997-t11-all]. The table
note defines the year:

> NOTE: The year of entry for new arrivals is the year entering as an immigrant. The year of entry for
> adjustments is the latest year of entry as a nonimmigrant or the latest year of entry in another temporary status.

- Mexico: 94,446 adjusters. Latest entry 1996-97: 15,064; 1995: 7,539; 1994: 5,447; 1993: 5,098; 1992 or
  earlier: 52,466; unknown: 8,832. Of those with a known year, 17.6% entered in 1996-97 and **61.3% in 1992 or
  earlier** (5+ years before adjustment) [CALCULATION].
- All countries: 22.9% in 1996-97, 27.5% in 1995 and 33.1% in 1992 or earlier [CALCULATION].
- FY2002 Table 11 has 148,031 of 166,056 Mexican adjusters with unknown year (89.1%), which makes it unusable
  [SOURCE: yb2002-t11-mexico; CALCULATION].
- Bearing on the split: in the one clean year, Mexican adjusters were mostly long-resident, at least 5 years
  since their latest entry. The "latest entry" definition understates residence for circular migrants. The
  figures cover all classes, not parents.

**Not found for Q1:**

  (2008) used. They would give the IR-5 x Mexico x status-at-entry cross-tab for the 1990s.

## Q2. Published NIS results on prior US experience

**Jasso, Massey, Rosenzweig and Smith (2008)**, NIS-Pilot, FY1996 cohort. Source: IZA DP 3441, the preprint of
*IMR* 42(4):803-843, `_cache/who/iza_dp3441_jasso_2008.txt`.
- Table 4, share of adults with any previous illegal experience (entry without inspection, overstay,
  unauthorized work or apprehension) [SOURCE: jasso08-t4-parent, jasso08-t4-mexico, jasso08-t4-total]:
  - parents of US citizens: 37.5% (men), 12.5% (women), **20.8% (all)**;
  - Mexico-born: 85.3%, 67.2%, 73.7%;
  - whole cohort: 38.7%, 26.1%, 31.7%.

> Table 4 also provides evidence that previous illegal experience is strong among men who immigrate as parents
> of U.S. citizens and as employment principals (both 38%)

> Sample sizes unfortunately do not permit a finer look within visa category and

- Bearing on the split: in 1996 about four parents in five had no illegal history. The parent row pools new
  arrivals and adjusters and all countries, and the paper cannot cut parents by country.

**Jasso (2011), "Migration and Stratification"**, NIS-2003 adult sample (n = 8,573). Source: IZA DP 5904, the
preprint of *Social Science Research* 40(5).
- The PDF crops Table 4's right-hand columns. They were recovered by re-extracting p.91 with a widened crop box
  (`_cache/who/iza_dp5904_jasso_2011_p91_wide.txt`).
- Table 4, parents of US citizens (11.9% of adults): 66.2% female; mean age 65.5 (men) and 62.7 (women); mean
  schooling 8.75 and 6.93 years; **% adjustees 25.3 (men), 33.6 (women)**; English fluency 20.8% and 19.7%. All
  immigrants: % adjustees 57.9 and 57.0 [SOURCE: jasso11-t4-parent, jasso11-t4-all].
- Table 11, average years of visa processing for parent principals: new arrivals 2.65 (men) and 2.38 (women),
  adjustees 2.54 and 2.58 [SOURCE: jasso11-t11-parent].
- Previous illegal experience, whole 2003 cohort: 39.6%. Mexico: 71.3% from the immigrant record alone, 77.6%
  with survey measures [SOURCE: jasso11-illegal-total, jasso11-mexico-record].
- Bearing on the split: in 2003 about 70% of parents of citizens (all countries) came as new arrivals. The
  Mexican IR-5 adjuster share of ~80% in FY2019-24 is far above that. Either the Mexican parent flow differs
  from the 2003 worldwide flow, or adjustment by parents has grown since [INFERENCE]. Q5's all-country 44-46%
  for immediate relatives 55+ in FY2023-24 fits growth. The parent session's NIS-2003 microdata can test this.

**Hayes and Hill (PPIC 2008)**, NIS-2003, the Massey-Malone taxonomy, all LPRs. Source:
`_cache/who/ppic_CC_608JHCC.txt`, Table 2 [SOURCE: ppic-pathways-*].
- New arrival 37.6%, illegal border crosser 20.1%, visa abuser (overstay or unauthorized work) 21.5%, **nonresident
  visitor 11.4%** (prior visits on a visa, no violation), student/exchange 5.1%, refugee/asylee 2.2%, nonresident
  worker 2.2%.
- The main report splits pathways only for broad groups ("Immediate family: 48%" of LPRs). The by-visa-class
  Table A.1 is in a technical appendix that could not be retrieved: ppic.org redirect-loops on it, and the
  Internet Archive was offline on 2026-09-27, although its CDX index lists 2011-2015 PDF captures of
  `608JHCC_technical_%20appendix.pdf`.
- Bearing on the split: the survey distinguishes the visitor type (11.4%) from the visa-abuser type (21.5%),
  which is exactly the needed split. The parent-row values are NOT FOUND; retry the Wayback capture.
- Massey and Malone (2002) on the NIS pilot was not fetched (budget).

## Q3. Recent channels (FY2019-FY2024)

**(a) Parole counts as the entry adjustment needs** [SOURCE: `_cache/who/uscis_pm_volume-7-part-b-chapter-2.txt`,
https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-7-part-b-chapter-2]:

> If an alien has not been inspected and admitted or inspected and paroled before filing an adjustment
> application, the officer must deny the adjustment application.

**Port-of-entry parole of Mexicans** (sources are Exa extracts; direct curl returned 403, see
`_cache/who/exa_extract_gao-26-107765.txt`):
- GAO-26-107765: from October 2018 through May 2025, paroles to Mexicans made up 22% of the ~2.4 million CBP
  paroles at the southwest border, and 513,763 (about 32%) of paroles by the Office of Field Operations
  [SOURCE: gao-mexico-ofo].

  > Almost all (97 percent) appointments in fiscal years 2023 and 2024 resulted in paroles.

- House Homeland Security majority release, 2023-10-23 (partisan source quoting internal documents): 58,772
  CBP One appointments by Mexican nationals, of whom 55,099 (93%) were paroled [SOURCE: house-cbpone-mexico].
- CBP: Mexicans are among the top three nationalities processed with CBP One appointments; 936,500+ appointments
  were scheduled from January 2023 to December 2024 [SOURCE: cbp-dec2024].
- Bearing on the split: a Mexican parolee with a citizen child aged 21+ meets 245(a) and can adjust as IR-5.
  This is a third type: a recent entrant who lived in Mexico but whose counterfactual is not clean departure,
  since parolees are in removal proceedings and many would stay [INFERENCE]. No count of IR-5 adjusters who had
  been paroled was found (NOT FOUND).

**(b) Military parole in place** [SOURCE: same Policy Manual chapter]:

> In general, USCIS grants parole in place only sparingly.

> The fact that an alien is a spouse, child, or parent of an active duty member of the U.S. armed forces, a
> member in the Selected Reserve of the Ready Reserve, or someone who previously served in the U.S. armed forces
> or the Selected Reserve of the Ready Reserve ordinarily weighs heavily in favor of parole in place.

- A parent of a service member or veteran who crossed without inspection can be paroled in place and then
  adjust. Such parents are long-resident by construction [INFERENCE].
- Count of military parole-in-place grants to parents: NOT FOUND (not searched in depth).

**(c) Visitors and border-crossing-card holders who adjust as immediate relatives.**
- *Matter of Cavazos*, 17 I&N Dec. 215 (BIA 1980). The respondent was a Mexican who entered on a border crossing
  card and married a citizen the same day. Headnote (2): notwithstanding preconceived intent, an application
  "for ad- justment of status should as a general rule be granted in the exercise of discretion in the case of
  an immediate relative" [SOURCE: `_cache/who/bia_matter_of_cavazos_2750.txt`, quote id cavazos-holding].
- USCIS on the State Department's 90-day rule [SOURCE: `_cache/who/uscis_pm_volume-8-part-j-chapter-3.txt`,
  technical updates of 2019-06-05 and 2021-07-16]:

  > While this "rule" does not apply to USCIS because it is DOS policy, USCIS is clarifying that it may also find
  > that an applicant made a willful misrepresentation due to a status violation or conduct in the United States
  > that is inconsistent with the applicant's prior representations, especially where the violation or conduct
  > occurred shortly after the consular interview or admission to the United States.

  > This technical update to Volume 8 removes all references to the U.S. Department of State’s 90-day rule.

- Bearing on the split: the law leaves the visitor-type route open. A parent in Mexico can enter on a B-2 visa
  or border crossing card and file an I-130/I-485 package, with misrepresentation and discretion as the only
  checks. No count or share of immediate-relative adjusters who adjusted soon after a visitor entry was found
  (NOT FOUND).
- USCIS Policy Manual vol. 7 part A ch. 10 (discretion) was fetched but contains no "preconceived intent" text.
  The current Policy Manual passage on preconceived intent was not located.

**(d) 245(i) adjustments per year, recent: NOT FOUND.**
- Vol. 7 part B ch. 8 (245(i)) was fetched: `_cache/who/uscis_pm_volume-7-part-b-chapter-8.txt`.
- No DHS table flags 245(i) after FY2002.
- The large FY2001-02 "unknown status at entry" counts for Mexico (127,107 and 120,920) coincide with the LIFE
  Act 245(i) window [INFERENCE, not verified].

## Q4. Unauthorized residents who adjust, and time in the US

- Warren (2020, *Journal on Migration and Human Security*, CMS). For 2010-2018 on average, 155,000 undocumented
  residents a year adjusted to LPR status, all countries; for Mexico, 45,000 a year. Both are "based on DHS data
  on removals and adjustments" [SOURCE: `_cache/who/exa_extract_warren_2020_jmhs.txt` (Exa extract; curl 403),
  quote ids warren-fn5, warren-fn8].
- Bearing on the split: 45,000 Mexican undocumented adjusters a year across all classes (2010-18) is of the same
  order as Mexican IR-5 adjustments alone in FY2019-24 (27.3k to 51.3k, sister lane §2). If Warren's figure is
  right and roughly stable, a material part of the recent Mexican IR-5 adjusters cannot have been counted
  undocumented residents: they were visitors, parolees or otherwise in status [INFERENCE; fragile, because
  Warren's adjustment component is itself derived from DHS totals by assumptions not retrieved here].
- NOT FOUND: any DHS, USCIS, GAO, CBO, Pew or MPI estimate of the share of immediate-relative adjusters who were
  overstays, or of their years in the US. Warren's class and duration detail (2013 IMR paper, CMS tables) was not
  fetched.

- Mexico's IR-5 flow adjusts at ~80% (sister lane §2) against this all-country 44-46%, so the age mix is a weak
  proxy for Mexican parents.
