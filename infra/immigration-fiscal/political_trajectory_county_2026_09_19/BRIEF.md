# Lane: does Mexican-origin composition move political outcomes — a county panel with the California–Texas contrast (2026-09-19)

## Goal
Ladder 97 and 102 (`research/immigration-confidence-ladder.md`) hold that the political externality is unpriced and that Mayda, Peri and Steingress 2022 (low-skill immigrant share raises the Republican vote) cannot be turned into dollars. The operator's question is different and measurable: over 2000–2024, has the growth of the Mexican-origin share of a county's population changed the county's political trajectory — presidential vote, turnout, and the laws that bubble up from local majorities — and does the California–Texas contrast (same immigrant stock, opposite state politics) show composition driving outcomes or outcomes driven by something else? Measure the trajectory; do not price it.

## Arms
1. **County vote panel.** Presidential returns by county 2000–2024 (MIT Election Data and Science Lab county presidential file on Harvard Dataverse, DOI 10.7910/DVN/VOQCHQ, or the Tony McGovern GitHub mirror; validate row counts against the documented 3,1xx counties per year) merged to Hispanic and Mexican-origin shares from Decennial 2000/2010/2020 (SF1/DHC P004 / P9 Hispanic by origin) and ACS 5-year B03001 by county (Census API, key in config). Long-difference and two-way fixed-effects regressions of Democratic two-party share and turnout (votes / citizen voting-age population, B05003I and B29001) on the change in Mexican-origin share, with state-by-year fixed effects, population weights, and SEs clustered by state. Run three specifications that could reverse the sign: (a) a shift-share instrument (1990 or 2000 Mexican-origin settlement × national growth), (b) dropping the four border states, (c) 2016–2024 alone, where the Hispanic vote moved toward the Republican side (report the 2020→2024 swing in Mexican-origin-majority counties in the Rio Grande Valley by name, from the same file).
2. **Composition vs conversion.** Decompose the county-level Democratic share change into (i) the Mexican-origin share of citizens of voting age rising with the group's own partisanship held at its 2000 level, versus (ii) the group's partisanship moving. Use CPS November voting supplement 2000–2024 (Census API `cps/voting/nov`, `for=state:*`) for Hispanic citizen turnout by nativity and state, and the published exit-poll and Catalist/Pew validated-voter Hispanic Democratic shares by year as the partisanship series (cite the primary tables; grade Pew's validated-voter reports as primary, exit polls as secondary).
3. **California–Texas contrast.** Both states have a Mexican-origin share near 30%. Build the state-level series 2000–2024: Mexican-origin share of citizens of voting age, Hispanic share of the electorate (CPS November), presidential and gubernatorial margins, and legislative control. Test whether the divergence is explained by the Mexican-origin share (it cannot be, if equal) versus the white vote (compute the white two-party share in each state from CPS/exit polls) and the non-Hispanic composition. State which of the two mechanisms the data supports and what it implies for the "bubbling up" claim.
4. **Laws that bubble up.** From the Correlates of State Policy Project (MSU IPPSR, public CSV) or the NCSL immigration-law database, take the state laws most directly tied to the group: driver's licences for the unauthorized, in-state tuition, E-Verify mandates, sanctuary/anti-sanctuary statutes, 287(g). Cross-tabulate adoption year against Mexican-origin share and against white partisanship; report whether share predicts adoption once white partisanship is controlled (a logit or a simple ordering test; n is about 50, say so).
5. **Apportionment already measured.** Ladder 147 has the House-seat effect; cite it, do not redo it.

## Outputs
`derived/`: county panel (under 5 MB or lane-ignored with a rebuild script), regression tables per specification, the composition/conversion decomposition, the CA–TX series, the law-adoption table. `RESULT.md` opening `**Verdict:**` answering: per percentage point of Mexican-origin share growth, how many points of Democratic two-party share and turnout, under which specification the sign holds, and whether the CA–TX divergence is a composition effect or a white-vote effect.

## Limits to state
Ecological regressions on counties do not identify individual votes; the partisanship series is survey-based; state laws are few and endogenous; 2024 may be a level shift or a cycle. The "vortex" is a growth-rate claim over 60 years; a 24-year panel bounds the level effect only.
