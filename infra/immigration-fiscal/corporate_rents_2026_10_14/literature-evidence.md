## Contradictions and missing bridges

- **Residual versus rents:** Barkai interprets the residual under his required-return assumptions as pure profits. KN show that changing rental rates or introducing omitted capital produces different allocations of similar residuals. CE's valuation approach produces a more modest rise, with user-cost and discount-rate differences central. This is substantive model dependence, not a reason to average estimates. [SOURCE: cited methods above]
- **SG&A:** KN critique earlier De Loecker–Eeckhout estimates by including SG&A in the variable-cost proxy; their exact replication attempt also reports a mismatch. The published DLEU reply separately examines overhead and profit measures. Calling the KN critique a conclusive refutation of the later published DLEU result would overstate it. [SOURCE: KN pp. 182–183; DLEU pp. 593–596, 605–606]
- **Different denominators:** Pure profits/value added, profit/sales, price/marginal cost and the present value of rents in Q are distinct. The corporate taxable supernormal slice is another estimand. None of these papers provides a tax-return reconciliation or a weighting across taxable corporations for the target year. [INFERENCE from the documented constructions]
- **Required returns versus taxes:** A required return to intangible capital can be economically normal yet appear in a tax-based supernormal slice because investment is expensed. A risk premium can also be outside a risk-free normal-return benchmark. Which components Treasury's slice contains must be resolved from Treasury's own definition, not by renaming these papers' residuals. [INFERENCE; Treasury definition supplied by the parent worker]
- **Level versus removal response:** A positive rent level does not identify its demand elasticity, scale elasticity, relocation response or who loses it when a population is removed. Neither jobs nor the group's consumption is causally identified as the correct removal key by these papers. [GAP]

## Recommended premise and next evidence

Suggested decision wording: “The low end assumes that corporate source-tax receipts associated with the group's jobs leave the US while resident owners preserve their worldwide after-tax capital income. This requires replacement returns elsewhere, including risk premia and returns to intangible capital. The literature does not identify how much of the taxable supernormal slice is pure rent or whether those rents disappear with demand; vanishing-rent and fixed-rent readings therefore remain separate sensitivity arms.” [ASSUMPTION; INFERENCE]

This wording states the conditional reading clearly; it does not claim the literature establishes the replacement return. Labeling the low end as rents that scale with the US market adds an unsupported causal premise and requires the residence-tax and private-owner-loss entries computed by the parent. Treating all supernormal returns as fixed rents is likewise an assumption. [INFERENCE]

[GAP] To narrow φ requires matched economic capital costs and tax treatment for the taxable corporate population in the target year, including risk compensation, expensed intangible investment and multinational source/residence distinctions. To choose the arm requires evidence for rents' response to the particular removal counterfactual. Suggested next source queries: taxable residual profit reconciliation; economic versus taxable return to expensed intangibles; corporate rent response to demand shocks. Do not convert historical aggregate ratios into φ while those bridges are absent.
