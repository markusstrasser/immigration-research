**Verdict:** (A1) BEA's state/local highways-and-streets stock does **not** include subdivider-built streets: it is built from Census GF government construction outlays, and GF excludes donated property (Census GF manual §7.1, U50, §8.1; Kornfeld BEA WP2022-2 p.6). (A2) Land under local subdivision streets has been dedicated on recorded plats since the 19th century, and since about 1958 developers have also built the streets (Smith 1987; Ridgefield 1928). No source gives a national stock share, so the inferred shares are local streets 0.9 (0.75–1.0), collectors 0.6 (0.4–0.85) and parks 0.3 (0.15–0.5), all [INFERENCE]. (B) Raw land per gross acre ÷ finished-lot price per lot acre: central **0.20**, range **0.10–0.46**. The deciding source is Munneke, Sirmans & Slade (JREFE 2023, Maricopa CoStar 1998–2012): 0.26 on unconditional means, 0.46 with location controls at equal parcel size, and 0.19 with controls at the mean parcel sizes [CALCULATION]. Practitioner rules give 0.15–0.39 per lot, which becomes 0.10–0.25 per lot acre.

**A1 reading [INFERENCE, firm]:** BEA's state/local highways-and-streets net stock is built from GF capital outlays for construction by governments (A1.1, A1.12); GF excludes donated property in revenue and expenditure (A1.8–A1.10), and BEA nets but does not itemize private-to-government asset transfers (A1.2). So a street built by a subdivider and deeded to the city does not enter FAAt701 state/local highways and streets at construction, and no primary text found adds it on transfer. Its construction cost sits in private investment (single-family construction factor A1.6, or private "highway and street" A1.13) or in the lot value treated as land (A1.4), depending on a Census procedure I could not verify in a primary document. Consequence for the case: r × BEA highway net stock **understates** the structures under subdivision streets; the land under them is likewise not bought (A2). Developer-paid streets are capitalized into lot prices, so in a land-and-structures account they look like part of the residential lot, not the government stock. [GAP] No BEA statement found that explicitly says "donated subdivision streets are excluded"; this is a reading of the source chain.

What dedication changes [INFERENCE]: the land is still a resource kept from other use. But a dedicated parcel was never a government outlay, and its value is capitalized into the lot prices buyers paid. In an account of other residents' budget saving, dedicated land is not a budget item. In a resource-cost account it is a cost the subdivision's buyers already bore through lot prices.

**B recommendation:** central **0.20**, range **0.10–0.46**, for raw land per gross acre ÷ finished-lot price per lot acre in the same market. B1 and B2 are the only transaction-based measurement found, and both are already in this form: the raw acre is gross (it includes the future streets and open space), and the finished-lot acre is lot area. B2's 0.19 keeps the plattage (size) discount, which is part of what an englobo tract is. Location controls move the equal-size ratio to 0.46. Per-lot practitioner shares (B3–B5: 0.15, 0.29–0.33, 0.39) must be multiplied by the lot share of gross area (≈0.65 per the NAHB acreage split r1 mis-cited) to reach this form, giving 0.10–0.25. Applied to the lane: the r1 factor 0.65 was an acreage share. Read as a price ratio, the nearest evidence-based number is ≈0.2 × ATF finished-lot price per acre, and ≈0.3–0.46 if one rejects the plattage component.
