**Verdict:** The statutory anchors hold: the registry cutoff is still Jan 1, 1972; non-LPR cancellation is still capped at 4,000 a year and U visas at 10,000; IRCA approved nearly 2.7 million, 75% Mexican-born; and no legalization law has passed (the Dignity Act has sat in committee since July 2025). The administrative routes changed materially since 2024. Keeping Families Together (an estimated 500,000 spouses and 50,000 stepchildren) was vacated on Nov 7, 2024. DACA still grants no initial requests, and the district court had not implemented the Fifth Circuit's Jan 17, 2025 ruling (relief limited to Texas, work permits severable) as of the latest dated status found (Feb 2026). USCIS's Feb 28, 2025 memo PM-602-0187 ended categorical exemptions from NTA referral (DACA excepted), and ICE began arresting applicants at San Diego USCIS interviews on Nov 12, 2025. The I-601A waiver is still open and faster (median 24.3 months in FY2026 vs 41.2 in FY2024; 89% of FY2026 completions approved), but third-quarter completions fell by more than half and 77,755 are pending. Pending U principals reached 288,243 (June 2026), and the share of bona fide determinations found ineligible rose from 15% (FY2024) to 34% (FY2026). Military parole in place is still offered, but its median processing time rose from 4.5 to 12.8 months. The September 2026 Visa Bulletin is the latest (October is not yet posted). Its Mexico final action dates are F1 01JAN08, F2A 22AUG25, F2B 15FEB09, F3 01JUL01, F4 08APR01 and EB-3 Other Workers 01APR22. DOS attributes the 2026 advances to lower issuance under the now-ended 75-country pause, which never covered Mexico, and warns of retrogression, so these dates overstate steady-state speed. Mexican asylum claims succeeded 16.6% of the time in FY2024, the latest Mexico-specific figure found.
