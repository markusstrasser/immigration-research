**Verdict:** The published evidence agrees with this lane's IIMMLA and CPS results: what hurts US-born children is parents who never obtain legal status, not unauthorized entry as such. Bean et al. (2011) find 2.04 years less schooling for Mexican-origin children of mothers who remained unauthorized (or of unknown status), 1.51 after controls and 1.24 with an IRCA-timing instrument; this lane's seven never-legalized mothers show 2.03 years unadjusted. The quasi-experimental studies run the same way, each imprecise (integrity audit 2026-09-23, `../study_audit_2026_09_23/C_children_status.md`): mothers' age eligibility for DACA cut their citizen children's diagnosed adjustment and anxiety disorders by 4.3 points (95% CI 0.6–7.9) from 7.9% (Hainmueller et al. 2017; the 4.5 is a graphical fit of the same data; receipt unobserved); IRCA application rates raised Mexican-born mothers' birthweights 34 g in the average county, 96 g extrapolated per applicant, 65 g with composition fixed (Cascio, Cornell & Lewis 2024); DACA moved children of eligible mothers 5 points from private coverage to Medicaid with no clear gain in total coverage (Tran 2025) and cut eligible households' poverty 38% (95% CI 13–62%; near-poverty not significant; Amuedo-Dorantes & Antman 2016); enforcement shocks raise poverty, grade retention, dropout and foster-care entry (B-grade panels). Against it: Bean's "unauthorized" side mixes in mothers of unknown status or who never migrated (rows 1a–1b); Landale et al. (2015) find more behaviour problems than among children of documented mothers but none relative to children of US-born mothers; the immigrant-mobility results (Abramitzky et al. 2021) exclude children whose parents never obtained SSNs (about 21% of Hispanic children; IRCA-legalized parents are included), which flatters Mexican-origin mobility.

## Bean et al. classification recipe

Bean et al. fit latent classes to both parents' steps (entered authorized, became a permanent resident, naturalized) reported by the adult child in IIMMLA, pooling the 1.5 and second generations (Mexican N 935). Their headline contrast collapses mothers into two groups: permanent resident or citizen at the interview, against unauthorized or of unknown status at the interview; the second group includes mothers scored as never migrated or status unknown (rows 1a–1b). This lane's `iimmla_parent_entry.py` differs on purpose: second generation only (n 553), mother's entry from Q127A–Q128, "not legal by 2004" only when she is explicitly neither a citizen (Q122 = 2) nor a green-card holder (Q124 = 2). That group is purer and much smaller (7 mothers, 12 fathers) and gives 11.43 against 13.46 years (legal-entry mothers), a 2.03-year gap, beside Bean's unadjusted 2.04.
