# Q2–Q3 reads: pay parity, Medicare GME, FICA, H-1B costs (fetched 2026-09-28 JST)

## Pay
- RGH IM program page (see q1): "The following is a list of benefits granted to all residents" ... "The salaries for
  the 25-26 academic year will be: $73,000 PGY-1 / $76,000 PGY-2 / $80,000 PGY-3". One scale, no visa distinction.
- 20 CFR 655.731(a) (eCFR, current as of 2026-09-24; `_cache/ecfr_655_731.txt`): H-1B LCA wage "shall be the greater
  of the actual wage rate ... or the prevailing wage"; "(1) The actual wage is the wage rate paid by the employer to all
  other individuals with similar experience and qualifications for the specific employment in question." "The wage
  requirement includes the employer's obligation to offer benefits and eligibility for benefits provided as compensation
  for services to [U.S. workers]".
- 22 CFR 62.27(b) (`_cache/ecfr_62_27.txt`): ECFMG "must sponsor alien physicians who wish to pursue programs of graduate
  medical education"; (b)(7) the physician must "Submit an agreement or contract from a U.S. accredited medical school,
  an affiliated hospital, or a scientific institution to provide the accredited graduate medical education." No wage
  floor or parity rule appears in 62.27 [searched: stipend|salary|compensation|wage].
  62.27(g)(1): clinical J-1 physicians are "automatically subject to the two-year home-country physical presence
  requirement of section 212(e)".
- DOL LCA aggregator (secondary): RGH "Medical Resident Physician" H-1B LCAs median $65,693 (FY2020–26, all years
  pooled; older years' scales were lower).

## Medicare GME (statute and regulation)
- 42 CFR 413.75(b) (`_cache/ecfr_413_75.txt`): "Foreign medical graduate means a resident who is not a graduate of a
  medical, osteopathy, dental, or podiatry school ... accredited or approved ... by ... (1) The Liaison Committee on
  Medical Education ... (2) The American Osteopathic Association ..." — defined by school, not citizenship or visa.
- 42 U.S.C. 1395ww(h)(4)(D)(i) (`_cache/usc42_1395ww.txt`): an FMG "shall not be counted as a resident on or after July
  1, 1986, unless— (I) the individual has passed the FMGEMS examination ... or (II) the individual has previously
  received certification from, or has previously passed the examination of, the Educational Commission for Foreign
  Medical Graduates." (ECFMG certification is already required to start residency, so certified FMGs count fully.)
- 42 CFR 413.79(b)(1): "If the resident is in an initial residency period, the weighting factor is one." (b)(2): 0.50
  beyond the initial residency period. Applies to all residents alike.
- 42 U.S.C. 1395ww(h)(4)(F)(i): FTE residents "may not exceed the number (or, 130 percent of such number in the case of a
  hospital located in a rural area) of such full-time equivalent residents for the hospital's most recent cost
  reporting period ending on or before December 31, 1996." Same cap in 42 CFR 413.79(c)(2)(i); IME cap at
  1395ww(d)(5)(B) (line 797 of the cached text: "may not exceed the numbe[r]" as of 1996).
- No provision in either text conditions DGME or IME on citizenship or visa status [searched: citizen|alien|visa].

## FICA
- 26 U.S.C. 3121(b)(19) (`_cache/usc26_3121.txt`) excludes from "employment": "Service which is performed by a
  nonresident alien individual for the period he is temporarily present in the United States as a nonimmigrant under
  subparagraph (F), (J), (M), or (Q) of section 101(a)(15) of the Immigration and Nationality Act, as amended, and which
  is performed to carry out the purpose specified in subparagraph (F), (J), (M), or (Q), as the case may be;"
  FUTA mirror: 26 U.S.C. 3306(c)(19), same words.
- 26 U.S.C. 7701(b)(5)(C): "teacher or trainee" = individual "temporarily present in the United States under
  subparagraph (J) or (Q) ... (other than as a student)". (E)(i): not exempt "for the current year if, for any 2
  calendar years during the preceding 6 calendar years, such person was an exempt person". (E)(ii) students: "For any
  calendar year after the 5th calendar year ..." not exempt unless no intent to reside permanently.
- IRS, "Taxation of alien individuals by immigration status – J-1": "alien physicians, au pairs, short-term scholars,
  and summer camp workers in "J" visa status ... are all within the definition of "teacher or trainee"." And: "J-1
  aliens who become U.S. tax residents or change their visa status to other work visa types (other than F, M or Q
  visa) are subject to Social Security and Medicare taxes on their wages."
- IRS, "Alien liability for Social Security and Medicare taxes of foreign teachers, foreign researchers, and other
  foreign professionals" (page dated 2026-08-18): "Foreign scholars, professors, teachers, trainees, researchers,
  physicians, ... in J-1, or Q-1 nonimmigrant status for less than 2 calendar years are generally NRAs ... exempt from
  Social Security Tax and Medicare Tax"; "Exempt Employment includes: ... Employment as a physician"; "Teachers,
  trainees, and researchers in H-1b status ... are liable for Social Security/Medicare taxes from the very first day of
  U.S. employment, regardless of whether they are nonresident or resident aliens ... unless the provisions of a
  Totalization Agreement relieve such aliens".
- IRS Pub. 519 (2025) (`_cache/p519.txt`): "services performed by you as a nonresident alien temporarily in the United
  States as a nonimmigrant under subparagraph (F), (J), (M), or (Q) ... are not covered under the social security
  program if the services are performed to carry out the purpose for which you were admitted ... generally include only
  on-campus work, practical training, and economic hardship employment." "Social security and Medicare taxes will be
  withheld from your pay for these services if you are considered a resident alien ... even though your nonimmigrant
  classification ("F," "J," "M," or "Q") remains the same." "You will not be an exempt individual as a teacher or
  trainee in 2025 if you were exempt as a teacher, trainee, or student for any part of 2 of the 6 preceding calendar
  years." "You will not be an exempt individual as a student in 2025 if you have been exempt as a teacher, trainee, or
  student for any part of more than 5 calendar years". Refund attachments list: "If you are engaged in optional
  practical training, attach Form I-766."
- IRS FAQ, Medical Resident FICA Refund Claims (2010): regulations effective April 1, 2005 exclude full-time (40h+)
  employees from the 3121(b)(10) student exception, which "excludes medical residents from the student exception"
  (upheld in Mayo Foundation v. United States, 562 U.S. 44 (2011) [TRAINING-DATA citation; opinion PDF 404'd]).
  So US-citizen and green-card residents pay full FICA.

## H-1B employer costs and the 2025–26 proclamations
- USCIS H-1B page (updated 2026-09-21): Proclamation of Sept 19, 2025: "certain H-1B petitions filed at or after
  12:01 a.m. Eastern on Sept. 21, 2025, must be accompanied by an additional $100,000 payment"; applies to beneficiaries
  "outside the United States" without a valid H-1B visa; not to change of status/extension granted inside the US.
  "On June 8, 2026, the U.S. District Court for the District of Massachusetts issued an order that vacated agency
  guidance implementing the $100,000 payment requirement ... State of California v. Mullin, 1:25-cv-13829 (D. Mass.)
  ... On July 24, 2026, the First Circuit denied the government's motion. ... will comply with the court's order".
- Proclamation of Sept 2026 (Federal Register 2026-19554, 2026-09-23): extends the 2025 Proclamation "until 12:00 a.m.
  eastern daylight time on September 21, 2027"; "since then, the $100,000 payment has been made for over 700 petitions."
- DHS NPRM "Fee for Certain H-1B Petitions" (FR 2026-17324, 2026-08-25): "a $103,265 fee ... for all H-1B cap-subject
  petitions"; "not to all H-1B petitions such as cap-exempt filings".
- AMA press release 2025-09-25: AMA and 53 societies urged DHS "to exempt physicians ... including medical residents,
  fellows" from the $100,000 payment. No categorical physician exemption found in the USCIS text above
  (exceptions only "in the extraordinarily rare circumstance" by the Secretary).
- Standing I-129 fees were not re-fetched this lane [GAP]; from the 2024 USCIS fee rule [TRAINING-DATA]: H-1B I-129
  $780 ($460 small employer/nonprofit), Asylum Program Fee $600 ($300 small, $0 nonprofit), ACWIA $750/$1,500 (exempt
  for higher-education institutions and affiliated nonprofits), Fraud Prevention $500, optional premium processing.
