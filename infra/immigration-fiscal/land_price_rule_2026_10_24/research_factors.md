# Land price rule for roads, right-of-way and parks: primary-source factors

**Verdict:** Recommended central factor for road/ROW land = 1.0 × ATF (acquisition/market-value rule: FTA 2018 p.13, Yellow Book market-value standard, Guerra–Duranton–Ma and Millard-Ball convention), range 0.10 (Australian current-use fair value: NSW OLG p.11, Dandenong 0.05–0.15) to 1.16 (FTA corridor sales median, p.11); 0.35 (englobo raw-land share) as the release-value sensitivity. No statistical agency publishes an ATF factor: OECD-Eurostat gives none, CBS excludes road land (1996: farmland price), ONS prices built-up road land near dwelling land, ABS passes through the 85–95% discounts.

Scope: price rule (factor on across-the-fence, ATF, adjacent-land value) for land under public roads,
highway right-of-way and public parks, from appraisal practice and national accounts; plus US street-land
share/value and rural land $/acre. Researcher run 2026-10-24. Source tags per repo CLAUDE.md.

## Notes

- Yellow Book 2016 (the edition requested; a 2024 edition may exist, not checked) [SOURCE: UASFLA 2016, pp.169, 195–198]. Its rule for land
  under an existing road is market value of the land as burdened, which federal courts put at nominal; for an undedicated strip it
  forbids assuming the ATF value. It never names an ATF multiplier or a corridor enhancement. For a land-under-roads
  *opportunity-cost* computation (land freed if the road were closed), this is the "burdened" end; the unburdened alternative-use
  value is the fee value net of removal costs (Rasmuson, quoted p.197: failing to consider removal costs "will result in an
  artificially inflated value").
- Corridor literature (rows 5–13): the enhancement factor (>1) is a premium for an *assembled, functioning corridor
  whose highest and best use is still a corridor* (sale to another corridor user). It is not a land-component factor for
  land under a road valued at its alternative use. Federal transit practice sets it at 1.0 unless market-proven; the one
  documented FTA sale set gives 1.10–1.30. Liquidation (selling strips to abutters) sits below ATF: row 12's one observed
  sale is 0.226 × ATF, and FTA (p.8) says abutters may view such strips as of "negative value" net of maintenance and taxes.
  [INFERENCE] For the lane's question (opportunity cost of land kept in streets), the relevant bracket is
  liquidation/strip (<1) to plain ATF (1.0); the >1 enhancement belongs to corridor-as-corridor valuation.
- National accounts (rows 14–20): no statistical agency found so far publishes an ATF factor. The OECD-Eurostat guide
  endorses valuing at comparable private-land prices but gives no number and reports most countries omit the land. The
  only published numeric rule found is Australian public-sector financial reporting, which ABS consumes: 85–95%
  discount on average site value (factor 0.05–0.15, central 0.10). That discount is fair value *in current use* (land
  restricted to road use, sold englobo), not the opportunity cost of the land if released.
- Statistics Canada [GAP]: two attempts (NBSA methodology pages 13-022-x; CSMA User Guide 13-606-g 2016 PDF, cached) give
  no land-under-roads rule; StatCan says its land "methodology ... is dated and needs revision" (13-605-x/2013002/article/11782,
  web, not cached). No factor found.

## Synthesis for the price rule

Three different objects get called "the value of road land", and the factors differ by an order of magnitude:

| Object | Factor on ATF | Best sources |
|---|---|---|
| Fair value in current use (land restricted to road use) | 0.05–0.15, central 0.10; US federal: nominal | rows 1, 18–19 |
| Release value of an existing strip (sold to abutters, or raw englobo land if replatted) | 0.23 observed sale; 0.35 englobo example | rows 12, 18; vacation statutes |
| Acquisition cost of new or wider right-of-way (market value of the taken land, before-and-after) | 1.0 (FTA rule; federal market-value standard; research convention in Guerra et al. and Millard-Ball; ONS built-up) | rows 3–5, 21; US street table |
| Existing corridor sold as a going corridor | 1.10–1.30 (FTA sales, median 1.16); 1.25–2+ practitioner; up to 2.5 litigated | rows 6, 9, 13 |
| Cost of assembling a new corridor | 3.7–14 × bare land | row 10 |

[INFERENCE] For an account that charges the land a group's roads and parks use when budgets respond in the long run, the
responding margin is new or wider right-of-way bought at market value: factor 1.0 on ATF. Current-use fair value (0.10) answers
a balance-sheet question about land locked into road use and understates the resource cost; enhancement and assemblage factors
price the corridor's going-concern or assembly cost, not the land. Parks are not corridor strips: the national-accounts rule
(OECD guide 8.100: comparable private land) points to 1.0 on adjacent value, and CBS 1996 priced parks at sports-ground prices.
