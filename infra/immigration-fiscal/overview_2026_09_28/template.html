Summary

  • Other US residents pay about $355bn a year (322–387). That is about $8.7k per group member (7.9–9.5). Only 2024 is measured.
  • Public services decide the sign. The group pays about $65bn more in taxes than it gets in benefits (60–70). Schools and other services reverse the sign. Schooling is the largest line.
  • Assumptions matter more than data noise. Sampling noise is about ±$21bn. Single assumptions move the total by $25–85bn.
  • Two accounting choices move the total most. If property taxes follow people, the total falls to about $323bn (290–356). If pensions count when earned, the total rises by about $75bn.
  • Costs outside the budget add {{SOCIAL_ADD}}. These are pollution, road crashes, crime victims, traffic and fear, net of gains from a larger economy. The total with them is {{SOCIAL_TOTAL}}.
  • State and local taxpayers pay about 85%. About one other resident in six comes out ahead, mostly landlords and high earners.
  • All three generations cost others. The second generation closes most of the gap in finishing school but little of the college gap.
  • Against as many whites, the group costs about $320bn a year more (315–330).
  • The frame matters. The group itself gains much by living here. With equal weight for every person, the world gains.

One year

The account covers 2024 only. It is the only year with a full measured account. It takes the people who live here now and asks what their presence costs others in that year. Economists call this an annual cross-section.

All governments, not the federal budget

The total covers federal, state and local budgets together. About 85% falls on state and local budgets, mostly through schools, police and local services. The federal part is 13–19% of the 2024 cash gap.

Deficit

States and cities must balance their budgets. Their part of the cost shows up as higher taxes or fewer services in the same year. Only the federal part can become debt. So the total is not a deficit number.

Interest

If the federal part of past gaps was borrowed, taxpayers pay about $36bn a year in interest on it (31–42). This is a legacy cost. Removing the group in 2024 does not remove the old debt. So the interest stays beside the total.

Return on public capital

Schools, roads and public buildings tie up capital that could earn a return in another use. National accounts charge only wear, so they set the return to zero. The total charges 2–3% a year. Economists call this the opportunity cost of capital. It is a resource cost, not a payment. It never enters the deficit or the debt.

Pensions: when paid or when earned

On a cash basis, a worker's payroll tax counts as income in the year it is paid. But the tax also buys a claim on future benefits. This young group pays about $65bn more payroll tax than it draws (63–68). Counting the promise when it is earned adds about $75bn under current law. Counting it replaces the cash pension lines. It is not an extra line.

Earlier years

Years before 2024 come from a model. It carries the 2024 position back on each year's national data. Do not multiply the annual number by a number of years.

Lifetimes

Lifetime numbers come from a separate ledger. They compare the group with whites and discount future years at 3%. They agree in direction with the annual total. Never scale one onto the other.
