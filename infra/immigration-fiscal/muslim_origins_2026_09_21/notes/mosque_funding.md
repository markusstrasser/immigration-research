claude-opus-5[1m]

**Verdict:** No complete ledger of US mosque funding exists, and the parent's expected reason is
confirmed from the statute, not inferred: 26 U.S.C. § 6033(a)(3)(A)(i) exempts churches (and the
IRS applies "church" to all houses of worship, per Pub. 1828) from the Form 990 information
return, so there is no filed, auditable income statement for mosques as a class. The one
systematic instrument — Bagby's US Mosque Survey (2000/2010/2020, n=470 completed telephone
interviews from a 700-mosque stratified random sample of a 2,769-mosque census) — measures budgets
and fundraising *methods* but has **no foreign or overseas category at all**: its six income
categories are Friday collections/pledges, Ramadan/Muharram fundraising, events, online giving,
endowments/investments/rentals, and "other." So the survey cannot answer the foreign-share
question either way; treating its "52% from Friday collections" as a bound on foreign money is
unsupported by the instrument. What the primary record does establish, from court judgments rather
than surveys, is *specific* foreign-state control: SDNY obtained summary judgment that the Alavi
Foundation — which holds real property in NY, MD, VA, TX and CA — was acting for the Government of
Iran, and DOJ's civil forfeiture reached those properties. The Saudi channel is documented only at
a lower evidentiary grade: Freedom House's 2005 study is an explicitly non-representative
collection from "more than a dozen" mosques, and its own text concedes "not all the mosques in the
study may receive Saudi support"; its only quantified funding figures are Saudi self-reports from
King Fahd's official website and are worldwide, not US. Where the evidence is well measured
(budgets, income methods) it points to locally raised, small-scale finance — median mosque budget
$80,000; participants give $674/yr against $1,732/yr for church-goers. Where the evidence is
thinnest (foreign share) there is no measurement at all, only a handful of adjudicated cases.
Gaps this epoch did not close: Diyanet Center of America (site is JS-rendered, "about" URLs 404),
NAIT property holdings (nait.net returned HTTP 500), Qatar Charity, GAO, the 2011 survey wave, and
whether Alavi's forfeited properties were themselves Islamic centers (DOJ releases say only
"properties in California, Virginia, Texas, Maryland, and Queens").

## Against concern / For concern

- **Against concern:** A5, A6, A8, A9, A10 (budgets are small, median $80k; income is overwhelmingly congregational; per-participant giving is 39% of the church rate, i.e. no sign of an external subsidy inflating mosque finances); A13, A14, A16 (the most-cited Saudi-influence document is a non-random convenience collection of *publications*, not of money, says so itself, and its material is mostly 1980s–90s vintage collected in 2003).
- **For concern:** A1, A2 (the disclosure hole is statutory and total — absence of evidence here is guaranteed by law, not by measurement); A7 (the only systematic instrument does not ask about foreign money, so no survey wave can rebut the claim either); A11, A12 (a federal court judgment, not an allegation, that a foundation holding US religious/charitable real property in five states was operating for a foreign state); A14 (two named, prominent US mosques acknowledged as receiving Saudi royal/official support).

## Datasets with funding observed

- **US Mosque Survey 2020** (Bagby; ISNA / Center on Muslim Philanthropy / ISPU). Access: free PDF, Reports 1 and 2. n = 470 completed telephone interviews, stratified by state, from 700 sampled of a 2,769 census; ±5% design target. Variables: total budget, zakah collected, budget-over-$100k share, six fundraising-method shares, full/part-time staff and salaries, purpose-built status, imam nativity. **No variable for foreign, overseas or embassy funding.** Microdata not offered publicly. Earlier waves 2000 and 2010 (2011 report) give comparable budget series (Table 14) — not fetched this epoch.
- **IRS Form 990 / ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer**: structurally unavailable for mosques themselves (A1, A2). Usable only for *non-church* Islamic 501(c)(3)s that do file (NAIT, Alavi, ISNA, ICNA and similar), which is the one open route to a partial ledger — untried this epoch.
- **DOJ civil forfeiture record** (SDNY 650 Fifth Ave, 2008–2026): property-level, adjudicated, single-entity. Not a population ledger.
