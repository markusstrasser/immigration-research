## The two bases

- **Cash-benefit convention** compounds modeled federal gaps under the all-borrowed assumption. Social Security and Medicare follow benefits paid rather than promises earned. Other NIPA accruals remain, including public-employee pension compensation, so this is not a complete conversion to Treasury cash borrowing. National interest and debt inputs are measured; the group attribution is modeled. This is the original debt lane's headline convention.
- **Accrual** charges Social Security and Part A at the promises each year's payroll taxes earn, valued at payable benefits, in place of the benefits paid. The adopted case does the same for 2024. The stock is therefore partly **implicit** debt: promises counted when earned, compounded at the Treasury's effective rate as if borrowed. It is the lane's benchmark `main_with_accrual`, now run for every group. **The accrual basis matches the adopted $371–435bn**, since that case charges the 2024 accrual. Cash matches the cash set, $295–362bn.

The basis matters most for an old group. On cash, the white slice's 2024 federal gap is $55.2 / 71.3bn, above the union's $36.3 / 61.3bn (engine), because its retirees draw Social Security and Medicare now. On accrual it is −$18.3 / −2.2bn, against the union's $113.0 / 134.3bn. [CALCULATION: `derived/federal_gap_by_group.csv`, year 2024]

**Where the cash difference comes from.** Nominal federal gaps summed by period, low end. The union (rough) against A1: 2008–2012 $228bn against $173bn; 2013–2019 $163bn against $38bn; 2020–2022 $342bn against $308bn. Before 2008 both ran federal surpluses on these lines: 1990–2004 −$111bn against −$344bn. The union's receipts are scaled down by its measured relative income in the earlier years (0.519 in 2008 against 0.611 in 2024). The white slice's relative income is flat, so its gap follows national budgets alone. ## Method

`legacy.py` imports `debt_legacy_2026_09_23/debt_legacy.py` read-only and calls its own functions: `federal_shares`, `v4_shares`, `case_split`, `History`, `programme_federal`, `stock`, `rate_paths` and `member_count`. It replicates only the orchestration in `main()` that builds the sept29 case. The lane is unedited.

- **Group amounts.** `group_lines.py` imports the white lane's `rekey_sept29.py`, runs its `setup()` (151 gates, row-4 frame) and `run29()`, and writes every line's 2024 amount and response for four groups, both bases and both ends to `derived/group_lines_sept29.csv`. Gate: each group's cost equals `rekey_summary_sept29.csv` to 5e-5 (union rough $294.88 / 343.98bn cash, $375.48 / 424.58bn accrual; A1 $97.75 / 147.58bn cash, $24.24 / 74.06bn accrual; all residents $176.45 / 225.28bn cash, $166.43 / 215.25bn accrual).
- **One code path.** `programme_federal` reads a corner's lines through `lines_at`. A corner here may carry `_table`, the case's lines at that corner with the group's amounts. A patched `lines_at` returns that table and otherwise calls the lane's function. Everything else is the case's: responses, federal shares by year, national BEA series, OMB rates and the capped programmes (rental assistance, LIHEAP and public housing, never compounded). Gates: the group's line set is the corner's wherever either side responds, the dump's responses are the corner's (1e-9), and the engine union's dump amounts are the corner's (1e-6).
- **Replacement framing.** Every group follows the union's measured headcount path (`History.share`, the back-cast's series), so only the per-capita position differs. The 2024 per-programme relative use is held in every year, as the lane holds the union's.
- **Relative income** scales receipts, as in the lane's income rule:
  - the union keeps the back-cast's ACS S0201 series;
  - A1 takes ACS S0201 POPGROUP 451, "White alone, not Hispanic or Latino", over 001, 2008–2024. It runs from 1.1687 in 2008 to 1.1729 in 2024. [DATA: `derived/acs_inputs.csv`] The ACS has no parent birthplace, so the third-plus slice (94% of NH whites in the CPS frame) takes the NH-white path. Only the path relative to 2024 is used;
  - the all-residents slice is 1 by definition;
  - the 2010 and 2020 profiles are interpolated, as in the back-cast. **Before 2008 every group is held at its 2008 value**, as the back-cast holds the union.
- **Union-only terms** stay with the union: the use-keyed justice increment and its federal part (ICE interior 100% federal), the uncompensated-care increment, the lane constants, school reprice, college re-key and the induced receipts F. The comparators carry their justice and Medicaid lines at the lines' average federal shares, and have no F. The white lane scores no production term for a native group.

## Bias from holding the white slice's 2024 age structure

Non-Hispanic whites' median age exceeded the nation's by 4.2 years in 2008 (41.1 against 36.9) and by 5.2 years in 2024 (44.4 against 39.2). [DATA: `derived/acs_inputs.csv`, ACS S0201] The slice was relatively younger in the past, so its relative use of Social Security, Medicare and Medicaid long-term care was below the 2024 value the rule holds. [INFERENCE]

- On **cash**, holding 2024 overstates the white slice's past federal gaps. Its legacy is biased up and the union − white difference biased **down**.
- On **accrual**, Social Security follows taxes, not age, so the bias shrinks to Medicare B and D (62.5% of the line) and long-term care. Its direction is the same.
- The school lines run the other way: a younger past means more pupils. K-12 is 5–10% federal by year, so this is small in a federal legacy.
- The income rule already carries the slice's measured relative income, which was flat. That absorbs part of the receipt side.
- **Net comparison bias is unquantified.** The white-age mechanism alone does not establish that the union-minus-white difference is conservative: both groups aged, median age does not identify programme use, and school and receipt channels can offset. Historical group-specific profiles are needed.

The lane's own limit is unchanged for the union. It was younger in 2008 (median 25.7 against 29.8 in 2024), and the direction for it is unknown.

## What this is not

- **Never add a stock to an annual figure.** The stock is a balance. Only the 2024 interest is an annual line.
- **The legacy never enters the 2024 with/without headline.** The $371–435bn compares 2024 with and without the group; removing the group in 2024 leaves past debt in place (the debt lane's 2026-09-25 ruling). The legacy is a separate historical line beside the account, like the social costs. Its like-for-like reading is the difference against a comparator, not the union's level.
- **Capital return.** The return on public capital ($34.4 / 57.2bn for the union) is an imputed resource cost. It is never compounded here: the compounded gaps are the cash set less the capital return and less the displaced beneficiaries. The gaps do include depreciation inside current spending (the lane's `pre_existing_gap`), so the legacy and the capital return both touch public capital through different dollars. Read them beside each other, never summed as one capital charge.
- **Pension accrual.** The accrual legacy compounds past years' accruals. The adopted case charges 2024's accrual ($76.7 / 73.0bn for the union). These are different years, but the accrual legacy is interest on promises, not on Treasury borrowing. **Pick one basis.** Cash and accrual legacies are alternatives and never add.
- **Not debt held by the public.** On accrual, part of the stock is an implicit liability. The Treasury's effective rate is an assumption for it.
- **Not a policy scenario.** It is an accounting attribution on a resident-stock account, with the per-capita positions of 2024 carried back. [FRAMING-SENSITIVE]
- **Not established as additive to public-employee pension interest.** The simulation compounds NIPA consumption containing accrued public-employee compensation. Separate interest categories do not reconcile the underlying financing. State both rows separately under the [decision](../../../decisions/2026-09-30-legacy-comparisons-separate.md).
