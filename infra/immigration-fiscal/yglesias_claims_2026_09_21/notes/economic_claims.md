claude-opus-5[1m]

**Verdict:** 21 economic claims and 5 concessions collected from 8 primary pieces I fetched and grepped myself, spanning 2020-08 to 2026-05: the *One Billion Americans* book excerpt he bylined at New York magazine (2020-08-31), a PBS/Amanpour interview transcript (2020-11-18), and six Slow Boring posts (2021-08-10, 2021-11-15, 2023-07-13, 2023-09-06 preview, 2025-01-08, 2025-11-06, 2026-05-01). What is **thin**: I found no passage in his own voice citing the National Academies 2016 fiscal report, and no passage in his own voice putting a number on the federal fiscal balance of immigration — the two Slow Boring posts that carry the heavy CBO/fiscal-scoring material are **guest posts by other authors** (Jed Kolko, 2024-09-12; Doug Elmendorf and Heidi Williams, 2023-08-28) and are recorded here only as exclusions. His single dedicated wages post ("The truth about immigration and wages", 2021-01-04) is paywalled to a 396-character preview that contains no claim. What **moved**: the 2020 book is maximalist and undifferentiated ("immigrants of virtually all stripes... make native-born Americans richer"); by 2021-08 he already writes that "less-educated immigrants are both less economically beneficial" than skilled ones and says effective-altruism arguments moved him off the humanitarian case for low-skill admission; by 2025-01 he calls the restrictionist housing-scarcity argument "factually accurate"; by 2026-05 he grants a conditional automation caveat. The direction of travel is toward conceding costs on housing and on low-skill economics while holding the aggregate-benefit claim fixed. ## Position over time

- **2020-08-31** — undifferentiated aggregate claim: immigrants "of virtually all stripes... make native-born Americans richer" (E-01); costs named only as traffic, rent, water, pollution (C-01).
- **2020-11-18** — native labor-market competition is explicitly minor: benefits "so far outweighs small problems of labor market competition" (E-05).
- **2021-08-10** — first differentiation by skill in his own voice: less-educated immigrants "less economically beneficial" (E-08/C-02); says EA arguments moved him from the humanitarian case for low-skill admission toward Denmark's aid-heavy model (C-03), and "all things considered" favors closing the asylum door.
- **2021-11-15** — reframes low-skill immigration as an anti-inflation supply-side tool, with the gain running through consumer prices and native occupational upgrading rather than through wages (E-10 to E-13).
- **2023-09-06** — asserts the working-class-harm perception is simply false (E-14).
- **2025-01-08** — concedes the housing-scarcity argument as "factually accurate" (C-04) and concedes skilled immigration is *worse* than unskilled for housing (C-05), while calling the current system "good enough" and larger-is-better (E-16).
- **2025-11-06 / 2026-05-01** — holds the aggregate-benefit line, accuses the left of overstating the anti-immigration economic case (E-19), and grants a conditional automation caveat (C-06).
