**Verdict:** Outside Howard–Wang–Zhang, enforcement studies with construction outcomes are few and weak. In the preferred contiguous-county design, 287(g) moved construction employment −2.0% (SE 1.6, not significant) and construction weekly wages −2.3% (SE 0.9); wages fell rather than rose (Bohn and Santillano 2017). The 2025–26 surge studies put construction at the top of sectoral job losses (Brookings: nearly 4% versus 1.48% overall; Cox and East: figure only, mostly insignificant by sector), but none measures starts, permits or building costs. The E-Verify and Arizona papers use construction only as a control or a descriptive check. No construction-specific study links immigrant labour to slower mechanisation or prefabrication, and the main construction-productivity paper (D'Amico et al.) contains no immigration term.
