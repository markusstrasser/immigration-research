# Lane: the real size of the Mexican-origin population across generations — self-identification, ancestry, attrition and coverage (2026-09-19)

## Goal
The repo's Mexican-origin union is 40.9M on CPS ASEC 2025 (12.2M Mexico-born, 14.4M with a Mexico-born parent, 14.4M native-born with two US-born parents self-identifying as Mexican) and the Census Bureau's 2024 generation table gives 39.0M (ladder 67). The third-plus group is defined by self-identification, and Duncan and Trejo show ethnic attrition: descendants of Mexican ancestry who no longer identify as Mexican or Hispanic, positively selected, running mostly through mixed parentage (ladder 67: second-generation adult attrition 2–3%, third-generation children 17%, higher for youth). Separately, surveys under-cover the Mexico-born. The operator's question: what is the true total number of people of Mexican origin in the United States, by generation, under each defensible definition, and how much larger than 40M is it? Then: because attriters are better off, what does the larger population do to the per-person and aggregate fiscal gap?

## Arms
1. **Self-identification baseline, reproduced.** CPS ASEC 2025 (on disk under `~/research-data/immigration-fiscal/data/external/cps/`, the same file the all-age ledger reads; reuse `infra/immigration-fiscal/all_age_ledger_2026_09_17/` generation coding read-only) and ACS 2024 1-year PUMS (`acs_pums_2024_1yr/`): Mexico-born; native-born with at least one Mexico-born parent (CPS only); native-born self-identified Mexican (HISP = Mexican). Report the counts with replicate-weight SEs and the Census Bureau's published 2024 generation table beside them.
2. **Ancestry definition.** ACS ANC1P/ANC2P Mexican-ancestry codes (list them) against HISP = Mexican: how many report Mexican ancestry without Mexican Hispanic origin, and the reverse; and how many report any Hispanic origin with Mexican ancestry. This gives a second self-report definition, not an attrition correction.
3. **Attrition correction.** Fetch Duncan and Trejo's primary papers (JOLE 2011 "Tracking intergenerational progress for immigrant groups: the problem of ethnic attrition"; AEA P&P 2025 115:451–56 / IZA DP 17579; Duncan and Trejo 2017 on the complexity of immigrant generations) and Emeka and Vallejo 2011; extract their attrition rates by generation and age, and the CPS method they use (parents' and grandparents' birthplace where the CPS carries it for co-resident children). Reproduce what is reproducible on CPS ASEC 2025: for children living with parents, Mexican ancestry via parental birthplace and parental Hispanic origin against the child's own Hispanic identification; report the attrition rate by generation and by whether parents are both Mexican-origin or mixed. Apply the resulting rates to adults, stating that this is an extrapolation from children, and give the corrected third-plus and total counts; carry a mixed-parentage arm that counts a person with one Mexican-origin grandparent as fractional (one quarter) as well as whole.
4. **Coverage.** Census 2020 Post-Enumeration Survey Hispanic net undercount, the Bureau's foreign-born coverage work, and the coverage multipliers the unauthorized-population literature uses; apply them to the Mexico-born and to Hispanic natives separately. The parallel lane `unauthorized_population_size_2026_09_19/` is working the unauthorized stock; do not duplicate its flow accounting, and read its `derived/` if it exists for the coverage multipliers it settles on.
5. **Fiscal implication.** Using the all-age ledger's per-person balances by generation (`infra/immigration-fiscal/all_age_ledger_2026_09_17/derived/`, read-only) and Duncan–Trejo's evidence on attriter selection (education and earnings of non-identifying descendants relative to identifiers), compute how the per-person gap to third-plus whites and the aggregate gap move when attriters are added back at their own characteristics; state the assumption on attriter characteristics explicitly (fully converged; Duncan–Trejo's measured gap; halfway) and give all three.

## Outputs
`derived/`: counts by definition and generation with SEs; ancestry cross-tab; attrition rates reproduced and applied; coverage-adjusted counts; fiscal implication table. `RESULT.md` opening `**Verdict:**` with the total under each definition (self-ID; ancestry; attrition-corrected whole and fractional; coverage-adjusted) and a one-paragraph answer to "is 40M too low, and by how much".

## Limits to state
Attrition rates are measured on co-resident children and extrapolated to adults; ancestry is a self-report with its own drift; coverage multipliers are assumptions; mixed-ancestry fractional counting is a convention and the memo must give both.
