**Verdict:** The auditors' record does not support calling California's ~$11bn of state-only Medi-Cal
"fraud". That spending is lawful state policy, enacted and budgeted openly, and no audit, CMS
document or charge calls it fraud. What the record does document is **improper federal claiming
around those state programs**: federal Medicaid money billed for services the states must pay for
themselves. California returned **$1.108bn** of it in 2025-26, HHS-OIG found **$52.7M** for
2018-19 (refunded), and CMS's 2025 preliminary review put six jurisdictions at **$1.35bn**, almost
all California and partly disputed. Across HHS-OIG, CMS, the Illinois Auditor General, TIGTA,
SSA-OIG, USDA and DOL-OIG I found **no charged or adjudicated fraud** tied to unauthorized
immigrants' eligibility for benefits. Improper payments documented in primary sources total about
**$1.18bn**, 94% of it California's 2025-26 return. The lawful spending critics call waste is about
ten times larger each year: California ~$10.8bn General Fund in 2025-26 alone. Every dollar here is
already inside our BEA-based account. A refunded federal claim moves cost from the federal budget to
a state budget; total government spending does not change.

## 3. The operator's word "fraud" for California's $11bn

The evidence places the $11bn in the **lawful** category. It is state money spent under state
statute, and federal law permits it. CMS's own administrator says so: states can "legally build
Medicaid programs for illegal immigrants using their own state tax dollars, so long as no federal
tax dollars are used" [SECONDARY: KFF/PolitiFact quoting Oz's X post]. The documented problem is the
boundary between state and federal money. In 2018-19 and in the periods corrected in 2025-26, California
billed federal Medicaid for costs that belonged on the state side ($52.7M found by OIG, $1.1bn
returned in 2025-26). That is an improper payment, and it was repaid. Fraud requires intent. The only
official statement on intent is CMS's "Whether willful or not", and no charge exists. Calling the
$11bn "fraud" is not supported by any auditor, inspector general or CMS document found here.
[FRAMING-SENSITIVE: whether lawful state spending on unauthorized residents is "waste" is a policy
judgment, not an audit finding.]

## 4. Steel-man of both readings

**Critics who see systematic abuse.**
- The misclaiming was structural, not random. California used an early-2000s proxy for 15+ years
  without reassessing it (M4); OIG checked only nine months, and CMS's 2025 review then found
  $1.3bn more. The earlier years were never quantified. A state that runs a large state-only program
  has a standing incentive to load shared costs (administration, capitation) onto the federally
  matched side, and CMS found the same pattern in six jurisdictions.
- Illinois' controls were weak: more than half of the checked "seniors" under 65 were enrolled in
  error, there were 478 duplicate IDs, and 6,098 "undocumented" enrollees had SSNs (M9).
- Federal matching through SAVE sent over 170,000 names to five states for re-verification
  [SECONDARY: KFF].
- TIGTA found $4.2bn a year of ACTC going to ITIN filers without any check at processing that the
  children met the residency rules; the IRS said it lacked authority to make one (T1).
- Money is fungible: provider-tax arrangements lawfully draw federal funds that backfill state-only
  costs. CMS has moved to close them, and HHS-OIG opened an audit of "health care-related taxes as
  the State share" on 2026-02-17 [SOURCE: OIG work plan, via Exa].

**The programs' defenders.**
- The state-only programs are authorized by statute and budgeted in public documents; the federal
  government's own statement confirms their legality.
- The federal-claiming errors are cost-allocation mistakes that the states returned or did not
  contest: California did not contest OIG's refund and booked the 2025-26 corrections itself, and
  DC is repaying. Colorado, Washington and Illinois call CMS's preliminary numbers wrong.
- SAVE non-matches are often data errors such as maiden names, and many names are emergency-Medicaid
  patients who are lawfully covered. No count of confirmed-ineligible enrollees has been published.
- USDA ran SAVE on 29 states' SNAP data and published no immigration figure (N1).
- SSA-OIG found SSA compliant with the lawful-presence rule (S2). Later sample errors are in the
  hundreds of thousands of dollars.
- The larger documented flow runs the other way: $1.2 trillion of suspended wages, and $12bn net to
  the trust funds in 2010 alone (S1).
