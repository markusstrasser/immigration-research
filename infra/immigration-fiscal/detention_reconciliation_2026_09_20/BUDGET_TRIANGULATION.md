## Civil-custody scope and double-counting boundary

**ICE detention and CBP short-term custody are distinct operating scopes, but their funding is not perfectly separate.** [GAO-26-107425](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-107425/index.html), lines175–177 and209–233, describes CBP's initial short-term holding and processing. Crucially, lines479–483 says CBP historically charged offsite medical care to ICE's annual O&S through the ICE Medical Payment Authorization Request system; ICE obligated about$76m for medical costs of people in CBP custody inFY2023. This is evidence of an interagency attribution issue, **not anFY2024 cash figure**. If the estimand is spending for ICE-held detainees, ICE-account payments can include other custody populations. If it is all civil immigration custody, add CBP scope only after eliminating reimbursements and other overlaps.

[GAO-25-107346](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-25-107346/index.html), lines241–250, reports about$1.4bnFY2024 obligations for CBP soft-sided-facility wraparound contracts, and explains other support contracts overlap permanent and soft-sided facilities. The report's methodology says GAO inspected actual expenditures, but the published headline and annual comparison use **obligations**. This does not supply a cash-spending add-on. Avoid converting that$1.4bn to expenditures.

ORR/HHS unaccompanied-child care and custody is a separate program, outside ICE custody operations; the official [HHS FY2027 congressional justification](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/fy-2027-acfc-cj.pdf), Refugee and Entrant Assistance, describes responsibility for care and placement of children apprehended by immigration authorities. Any whole-government civil-custody total needs an explicit inclusion decision for ORR and a custody-service subset of its account (not all refugee assistance). No ORR cost number was added here.
