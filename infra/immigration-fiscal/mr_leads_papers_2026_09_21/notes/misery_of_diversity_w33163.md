claude-opus-5[1m]

**Verdict:** A cross-sectional cross-country regression of standardized subjective well-being on Ashraf–Galor ancestry-adjusted predicted genetic heterozygosity, instrumented by prehistoric migratory distance to Addis Ababa, on ≤151 countries; mechanisms are traced through Fund-for-Peace state-fragility composites and WVS perceptions, and a first-vs-second-generation immigrant comparison (ESS/GSS) is used to argue the channel is social rather than genetic. Evidence level: **associational-with-an-instrument, not a natural experiment in any conventional sense** — the "novel natural experiment" is a 50–100kya dispersal, the estimating sample is one cross-section, there are no pre-trends, no placebos, no within-country variation, and the exclusion restriction requires migratory distance to affect happiness through *no* channel but within-ethnic interpersonal diversity, which the authors' own literature (Ashraf–Galor's programme: distance predicts GDP, institutions, conflict, division of labour, cultural fragmentation) contradicts. The single most important caveat for transferring this to the Mexican-origin resident population is in the paper's own Table 2: **the diversity measures immigration actually moves — ethnic fractionalization, ethnolinguistic polarization, religious fractionalization, number of ethnic groups — are null on all five SWB outcomes (1 of 20 coefficients significant), and the between-group component of the authors' preferred measure is also null; the effect loads entirely on *within*-ethnic deep-rooted heterozygosity.** This paper is not evidence about immigration-induced diversity and the authors never claim it is.

## Question, data, sample
Does population diversity lower subjective well-being, and through what channels? Pure cross-section, country as unit.
- **Diversity:** ancestry-adjusted predicted genetic diversity (AAPD) from Ashraf & Galor (2013a), built on expected heterozygosity of 53 HGDP–CEPH indigenous populations at 780 microsatellite loci. Data Appendix p.73: "*the ancestry adjusted (i.e., accounting for post-1500 population mix) expected heterozygosity, captures the likelihood of genetic similarity between two randomly selected individuals within a country.*" **N=151, mean 0.73, SD 0.03, min 0.63, max 0.77** (Table 1, p.48).
- **Country SWB:** Life Satisfaction Index from World Database of Happiness (N=140, mean 5.84, SD 1.32); Life Ladder, Positive/Negative Affect, Affect Balance from World Happiness Report 2020 / Gallup World Poll, **country averages 2012–2020** (N=151). All standardized.
- **Individual SWB:** EVS/WVS 1981–2022 integrated trend file v3.0, 117 countries, >650,000 respondents; regression N up to 639,840.
- **Immigrants:** European Social Survey (Europe) and General Social Survey (USA). First gen N = 20,024 (ESS life satisfaction), 8,307 (ESS affect balance), 2,964 (GSS happiness); second gen N = 22,559 / 9,757 / 3,934. Years not stated in text.
- "Old World" sample = drops the Americas and Oceania (N falls to 112–125).

## Identification
Equation (1) is OLS of standardized SWB on AAPD + 11 geographic/climatic controls + continent FE. Equation (2) instruments AAPD with migratory distance to Addis Ababa through five fixed waypoints (Cairo, Istanbul, Phnom Penh, Anadyr, Prince Rupert).
- **First stage** (Table 3 Panel B, p.50): coefficient **−0.007*** (0.001)**, R²=0.771–0.774; **F = 86.10** (life satisfaction column) and **112.7** (the four Gallup columns). Individual-level F = 66.08–68.48 (Table 4A). Text p.21: "*Every thousand-mile increase in distance to Addis Ababa via migration routes leads to a 0.7 percentage-point decline in AAPD, regardless of the estimation sample.*"
- **No shift-share anywhere.** Goldsmith-Pinkham–Sorkin–Swift, Borusyak–Hull–Jaravel, Adão–Kolesár–Morales and the Jaeger–Ruist–Stuhler serial-correlation critique are all inapplicable to this design; the paper cites none of them and does not need to.
- **No pre-trends and no placebos are possible** — there is one cross-section and the regressor is fixed at the 50,000-year scale.
- SEs are heteroskedasticity-robust, **not clustered**, in every country-level table; individual-level tables cluster at country; Table 9 two-way clusters at sub-region and ancestral country.
- Post-1500 migration is handled two ways: restrict to the Old World (corr. between predicted and ancestry-adjusted diversity = 0.993 there), or instrument globally.

## Main estimates
Coefficients are **per one unit of AAPD (a 0–1 probability scale)**; outcomes are standardized (SD=1). The authors report everything per *percentage point*, i.e. coefficient × 0.01. **Interpretable magnitudes [INFERENCE, my arithmetic]:** a one-SD rise in AAPD (0.03) implies **−0.34 SD** life satisfaction and **−0.84 SD** affect balance (IV). Scaling across the full observed AAPD range (0.14) would imply −1.6 SD and −3.9 SD — larger than the outcome's own cross-country dispersion, which shows the linear extrapolation is not credible and that the effective identifying variation is within-continent and much narrower than the raw range.

Conventional diversity measures are null (Table 2 Panel D, p.49; N=139–151): Number of Ethnic Groups 0.013 (0.026), Ethnic Fractionalization −0.187 (0.368), Ethno-linguistic Polarization −0.309 (0.275), Religious Fractionalization 0.054 (0.315) on life satisfaction, and similarly across the other four outcomes. Text p.20: "*in all models that control for conventional diversity measures, only one specification (i.e., the effect of ethnic fractionalization on Negative Affect) produces a statistically significant estimate.*" Appendix Table 1 (p.62) finds no non-linearity, contrary to Ashraf–Galor's inverted U for income.

## Heterogeneity and mechanisms
- **Perceived environment** (Table 6, p.55, global IV, WVS): Freedom of Choice −3.423** (1.692), N=620,873; Perception of Democracy −13.401*** (3.668), N=290,417; **Interpersonal Trust −8.871*** (2.629)**, N=621,372.
- **Objective composites** (Table 7, p.56, global IV, N=161; higher = worse): Fragile States Index +12.585*** (3.415); Security Apparatus +12.580*** (3.855); **Factionalized Elites +16.460*** (3.895)**; **Group Grievance +16.619*** (4.078)**; State Legitimacy +14.223*** (3.858); Human Rights & Rule of Law +10.571*** (3.288); **Uneven Economic Development +10.711*** (3.450)**. These map one-to-one onto the abstract's "social cohesion / state capacity / inequality of opportunity."
- **Mediation** (Tables 8A p.57, 8B p.58) is *descriptive controlling-for*, not a formal decomposition. Life satisfaction IV falls from −11.686*** (3.337) to **−0.753 (3.029), insignificant**, once the FSI is added — 93.6% absorbed [INFERENCE: arithmetic]; the Old World OLS analogue goes −11.626*** → −1.810, 84.4%, which is the text's claim on p.32: "*our social environment quality indicators explain up to nearly 84 percent of the impact of diversity on the life satisfaction index.*" Affect balance is far less mediated: −28.504*** → −20.181*** (IV), 29.2% absorbed; text: "*absorbs up to roughly one-fourth of the impact of diversity on the Affect Balance Scale.*"
- Stratifying by education and income terciles (Tables 5A/5B, pp.53–54) reproduces the main result at every level; ordered probit (Table 4B, p.52) shifts the whole distribution, so the effect is not a small miserable tail.

## Robustness and what the authors concede
They concede that continent FE matter enormously: adding them cuts the life-satisfaction coefficient by **67%** and the life-ladder coefficient by **64.5%** (text p.19), which they read as proof that "*population diversity is not randomly distributed across different parts of the world.*" They concede post-1500 migration could bias in either direction and say so explicitly. Checks that survive: Oster (2019) δ=1, R_max=1.3R̃ (Appendix Table 4 — they claim the baseline is a lower bound); SARAR(1,1) spatial autoregression (App. T5); dropping Africa, then Africa+Middle East (App. T6A/6B — effects get *larger*); controlling for genetic distance to Denmark (App. T3, addressing Proto–Oswald's 5-HTTLPR story); log GDP, log population, legal origin, colonial history (App. T2A/2B — life satisfaction −12.617*** → −10.871**, affect balance −29.495*** → −25.497***); distance to the 1500 and contemporary technological frontiers (App. T7/T8). The Bond–Lang / Schröder–Yitzhaki cardinality critique gets four separate treatments — dichotomous indicators, the Bloem–Oswald DAM test (App. T9, p.70), Gini's Mean Difference / NLMA (App. Fig. 1), and Bloem (2022) partial identification (App. Fig. 2) — which is more than most SWB papers do and is the paper's strongest methodological feature.

## Threats the authors do not address
- [INFERENCE] **The exclusion restriction is assumed, never probed.** Migratory distance to Addis Ababa is the workhorse instrument of the entire Ashraf–Galor programme and has been used to explain income, institutions, conflict, cultural fragmentation and the division of labour. If distance reaches happiness through any of those and not through within-ethnic heterozygosity, the IV is invalid. The paper's own Table 7 treats state fragility as a *mediator*, but nothing in the design distinguishes "diversity → fragility → misery" from "distance → X → fragility → misery." There is no over-identification test, no plausibly-exogenous / Conley bounds exercise.
- [INFERENCE] **The Ashraf–Galor measure's own critique literature is absent.** No citation of the 2013 *Current Anthropology* critiques (Guedes et al.; Rosenberg & Kang) of predicted heterozygosity — its 53-population base, the imputation from a handful of loci to 151 modern states, or the ancestry-adjustment's dependence on the Putterman–Weil migration matrix.
- [INFERENCE] **151 robust-SE observations overstate precision** given strong spatial correlation in both AAPD and SWB; the SARAR appendix is a fix for the error term, not for the effective degrees of freedom. Table 8A's Old World life-satisfaction sample is N=111 with ~14 covariates.
- [INFERENCE] **The generational "fade" is never formally tested.** Comparing Table 9 panels as independent estimates: ESS life satisfaction z=1.93, ESS happiness **z=1.24**, ESS affect balance z=1.85, GSS happiness z=4.74. Three of the four generational differences are not statistically distinguishable; the paper infers dissipation purely from second-generation coefficients losing significance — the "difference between significant and not significant is not significant" error. Only the GSS result carries the claim, and it rests on N=2,964 / 3,934 with a coefficient (−10.682) roughly five times the ESS analogues on a conceptually identical scale, a discrepancy the paper never explains.
- [INFERENCE] **Affect balance is barely mediated (29%) yet is the largest effect**, so the headline mechanism story explains the cognitive measure and largely fails on the hedonic one; the abstract does not carry this asymmetry.
- [INFERENCE] **No intergroup-contact estimate exists in the paper.** Allport, Bazzi et al., Lowe and Rao are cited in the introduction, but no contact treatment, exposure measure or dosage is ever estimated. The abstract's contact framing is theoretical scaffolding, not a result.

## Answers to the repo's questions
1. **Natural experiment and diversity measure.** Not a country-or-period event: the "experiment" is the **Out-of-Africa dispersal ~50–100kya**, operationalized as migratory distance from Addis Ababa through five waypoints, which produces a serial-founder gradient in within-ethnic expected heterozygosity. The estimating sample is a **single cross-section of ≤151 countries**, with SWB measured 2012–2020 (Gallup/WHR) and 1981–2022 (WVS/EVS). Diversity is **genetic** — predicted expected heterozygosity, ancestry-adjusted for post-1500 migration (Table 1 p.48; Data Appendix p.73). It is neither ethnic, linguistic, nor birthplace diversity; those four conventional indices appear only as controls and are null (Table 2 Panel D, p.49).
2. **Main SWB estimates.** Preferred global-IV (Table 3 Panel B, p.50): life satisfaction **−11.280 (SE 3.353)** SD per unit AAPD, N=140; life ladder −9.435 (4.110), N=151; affect balance −28.157 (6.345), N=151; positive affect −29.651 (5.560); negative affect +17.266 (7.016). First-stage F 86.1–112.7. In interpretable units [INFERENCE]: **−0.34 SD life satisfaction and −0.84 SD affect balance per one cross-country SD (0.03) of diversity**; the authors' own phrasing is per percentage point, i.e. −0.11 and −0.28 SD. Individual WVS IV: life satisfaction −9.695 (2.848), N=639,840; P(dissatisfied) +4.5 pp per unit AAPD.
3. **Mechanisms.** Trust/cohesion: interpersonal trust −8.871*** (2.629), N=621,372 (T6 p.55); Group Grievance +16.619*** (4.078) and Factionalized Elites +16.460*** (3.895) (T7 p.56). State capacity: State Legitimacy +14.223*** (3.858), Human Rights & Rule of Law +10.571*** (3.288), perceived democracy −13.401*** (3.668). Inequality of opportunity: Uneven Economic Development +10.711*** (3.450). Jointly these absorb 84–94% of the life-satisfaction effect but only ~25–29% of affect balance (T8A p.57 / T8B p.58).
4. **Adaptation / intergroup contact.** Two distinct exercises, **neither of which estimates intergroup contact**. (a) *Generational horizon = one generation* (Table 9, p.59): home-country diversity still depresses first-generation immigrants (ESS life satisfaction −1.255*, happiness −1.950***, affect balance −2.977***; GSS happiness −10.682***), while second-generation coefficients are "smaller and imprecisely estimated" (+0.523, −0.849, −1.173*, +0.590) — text p.34: "*the misery of parental home country population diversity dissipates among second-generation immigrants as they are nurtured in a social ecology different from their parents.*" The stated condition is being raised in a better social environment, not contact. (b) *Cultural coping traits* (Table 10, p.60, global IV): diversity raises Competition Good +5.749** (2.193), Family Ties Index +8.619*** (2.210), Friends Important +3.707 **ns** (2.320); text p.35: "*if there were no counterbalancing effects of these variables, diversity-induced misery would further increase by almost 12.8% in the Old World sample and 11.5% in the global sample.*" So adaptation offsets roughly an eighth of the effect and the net effect stays negative.
5. **Transfer to contemporary immigration into rich countries.** **The authors never make this claim.** The word "immigration" appears in the paper only in a literature footnote; the immigrant analysis is about *home-country ancestral* diversity carried by migrants, not diversity created by immigration in the destination. The sole transfer passage is the closing policy paragraph, p.36, quoted in full: "*What policy implications do these results offer, considering the unprecedented diversity levels of today's world, where billions of people live in social environments with high levels of anonymous human interaction and complexity? In line with the argument that resistance to multiculturalism is a byproduct of the evolution of the human brain, our findings recommend that simplistic approaches to curbing the never-ending diversity aversion, such as the expectation that living side-by-side is sufficient to induce favorable intergroup contact, may fall short of the projections, in the absence of policies designed to focus on the evolutionary foundations of resistance to diversity, such as the problem of prejudice (Crisp and Meleady, 2012). However, as our results among second-generation immigrants in Europe and the USA imply, social environment improvements can attenuate the misery of diversity in the long run (Helliwell, 2020b).*" That is the whole of it — a hedged, two-sided claim with no quantitative bridge. **[INFERENCE] For this repository the paper should be treated as evidence against, not for, an immigration-diversity-misery channel**: the components immigration moves (between-group diversity, ethnic/linguistic/religious fractionalization and polarization) are estimated to be null in Table 2 Panels D and E, and AAPD for the United States is pinned by the post-1500 ancestry adjustment, essentially unmoved by post-1965 Mexican arrivals. Any citation of this paper in support of a claim about Mexican-origin residents would invert its own within-versus-between finding.
