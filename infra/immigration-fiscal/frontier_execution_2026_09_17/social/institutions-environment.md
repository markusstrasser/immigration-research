**Verdict:** Two primary causal studies support specific institutional and contact mechanisms while limiting broad cultural extrapolation. They do not estimate the effect of admitting immigrants on institutional quality. Environmental claims need a global counterfactual: destination emissions and migrants' attributed footprints are insufficient. [INFERENCE]

Bounded follow-up, 2026-09-17. This supplements [RESULT.md](RESULT.md); the CPS/GSS execution package is unchanged. Source results below were checked against primary articles. No new causal estimates are claimed here.

## Citizenship: does changing legal membership change political engagement?

**Primary evidence.** Hainmueller, Hangartner and Pietrantuono (2015), [PNAS full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4611668/), sections “Identification Strategies,” “Sample,” “Findings,” “Mechanisms.” Swiss naturalization referenda supply a fuzzy discontinuity: narrowly winning predicts eventual citizenship, including later reapplications. The study interviewed768 applicants; naturalized respondents had obtained citizenship13years earlier on average. Response was34.5% overall and45.9% within ±15percentage points of the threshold. The paper's covariate-adjusted IV estimate for its whole estimation sample increases political knowledge by0.28 on a0–1 scale and efficacy by0.25; informal participation is imprecise (+0.12, reported p<.36). Those figures are distinct from the less precise local fuzzy-RD estimates. General and institutional trust estimates are near zero and nonsignificant. Covariate balance and interview-rate continuity support the close-vote design. [SOURCE]

**What survives.** Political engagement is responsive to an institution; a static group difference cannot establish immutable political culture. But citizenship mechanically creates voting eligibility, so the composite including turnout overstates the breadth of the evidence if read as general cultural change. The result is local to applicants whose eventual citizenship changes with the close vote. Rejection could directly affect engagement, complicating the IV exclusion restriction; null trust outcomes do not prove that restriction. It is not an admission-policy effect or an estimate of government performance. [INFERENCE]

## Contact: does cooperation transfer beyond its setting?

**Primary evidence.** Mousa (2020), [Science article PDF](https://poverty-action.org/sites/default/files/publications/Building-social-cohesion-between-Christians-and-Muslims-through-soccer-in-post-ISIS-Iraq.pdf), pp.1–4, especially Fig.1. Forty-two Christian amateur soccer teams in post-ISIS Iraq were randomized to receive Muslim or Christian teammates for a two-month league. Mixed teams increased stated willingness to join another mixed team by13percentage points (p=.044), voting for a Muslim player from another team for a sportsmanship award by26points (p=.003), and reported weekly training with Muslims six months later by49points (p<.001). Restaurant-voucher and mixed-social-event outcomes outside soccer did not show detectable improvements. A national-unity attitude improved; comfort with Muslim neighbors and blame of Muslims did not. [SOURCE]

**What survives.** Cooperation can change consequential outgroup choices even after severe conflict; generalization requires separate measurement. The award vote, stated future-team willingness and reported training have different evidentiary strengths. Nonsignificance does not establish equivalence. Young male volunteers, a shared goal and supportive organizers limit transport to ordinary residential mixing or immigrant admission. Conditioning on reaching the final is post-treatment selection, so exploratory successful-team comparisons cannot identify success as the causal ingredient. [INFERENCE]

## Environment: emissions moved, emissions attributed, or emissions added?

**Primary accounting evidence.** Davis and Caldeira (2010), [PNAS article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2851800/), “Discussion” and “Materials and Methods”: consumption-based accounts allocate supply-chain emissions to final demand rather than production location. Their 2004 input-output accounting finds substantial net embodied-emission imports in affluent countries. It is an accounting demonstration, not a migration experiment; dated sector relationships and uncertain input-output adjustments limit precision. The useful lesson is the boundary, not a contemporary emissions number. [SOURCE]

**Migration-specific warning.** Liang et al. (2020), [author manuscript, p. 25](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10119108/3/Guan_1_manuscript-22.pdf), explicitly assume immigrants have the destination natives' consumption structure and use their population share. Their [primary repository abstract](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10119108/) reports a 2.9 Gt immigrant footprint in 2015. This is not evidence that removing migration would eliminate 2.9 Gt. The relevant counterfactual includes consumption and production without that migration. [SOURCE / INFERENCE]

**A discriminating test.** Specify the same people, calendar period and admissions alternative; estimate global emissions with admission minus emissions without admission. Include destination consumption, the origin consumption displaced, remittances' induced consumption, migration-related travel and construction, and changes affecting other people. Use a consistent supply-chain account to prevent double counting. Report local air-pollution exposure, land use and congestion separately: global carbon accounting cannot answer those questions. Do not control away income gains when estimating the total effect; holding income fixed estimates a different, consumption-composition channel. [ANALYTICAL DESIGN]

## What would materially improve certainty next?

1. **Institutions:** measure an institutional outcome—administrative service delivery, corruption exposure, compliance, dispute resolution or political violence—under a credible admissions or settlement contrast. Naturalization effects among established applicants cannot fill this missing link. Pair outcomes with integration-policy variation to distinguish selection from institutions' responsiveness. [PROPOSED]
2. **Contact:** require behavior toward unfamiliar outgroup members outside the assigned activity, sustained after the intervention, with intervals that can rule out a substantively small effect. A within-setting success plus imprecise off-setting outcomes leaves transport unresolved. [PROPOSED]
3. **Environment:** use actual migrant consumption and a defensible origin counterfactual, ideally linked to an admission lottery or other exogenous opportunity. Present destination production, destination consumption and global increment side by side. A lower household footprint than natives and a positive migration-induced global increment can coexist. [PROPOSED / INFERENCE]

These additions would discriminate among mechanisms. More attitude batteries, country-level GDP/CO2 regressions, or origin-country institutional rankings would add proxies without identifying the disputed admission-to-welfare link. No study here licenses a net “civilizational benefit/harm” score; that requires outcomes, counterfactuals and explicit welfare weights. [INFERENCE]
