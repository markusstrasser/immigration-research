# Lane: cultural output of the Mexican-origin population against SES-matched whites, and whether variety saturates in group size (2026-09-19)

## Goal
The repo prices fiscal, crime, housing and price channels and carries "cultural enrichment" as an unmeasured benefit claim on one side and "contributes nothing" as an unmeasured claim on the other. Build the first measured version, with two questions. (1) Per capita, at matched age and education, does the Mexican-origin population supply more, less or the same creative output as third-plus non-Hispanic whites, and in which genres? (2) Does the variety benefit natives actually consume (restaurants, music) scale with group size or saturate at low shares, so that most of the population adds costs that scale linearly while adding little further variety? The second is the operator's hypothesis ("with 1/100 of them we'd have the restaurants, and recipes can be copied"); treat it as a hypothesis with a disconfirmation arm, not a conclusion.

## Arms, in priority order
A. **Creative labour supply and earnings.** ACS PUMS 2024 1-year on disk at `~/research-data/immigration-fiscal/data/external/acs_pums_2024_1yr/` (read-only; dictionary in `acs_pums_dict/`), and the IPUMS panel in `~/research-data/immigration-fiscal/derived/immigration_microdata.duckdb` for earlier years. Groups: Mexico-born, US-born Mexican-origin (HISP = Mexican, NATIVITY), third-plus non-Hispanic white is not identifiable in ACS (no parental birthplace), so use US-born non-Hispanic white and note the difference; CPS ASEC 2025 (on disk under `cps/`) can give the third-plus split for a check. Outcomes: employment in arts, design, entertainment and media occupations (SOC 27-xxxx; list the OCCP codes used), split into performing arts and music, visual arts and design, writers and media, and film/TV; self-employment in those occupations; wage and self-employment income in them. Report raw shares and shares standardised to the white age × education (and age × education × sex) distribution, with replicate-weight SEs. This is the arm that must land.
B. **Canon and awards signal.** Winners and finalists, 1990–2025, of the Pulitzer (letters, drama, music), National Book Awards, Academy Awards (directing, writing, acting, picture), Grammy general fields, National Medal of Arts, MacArthur fellows in arts, Tony (play, musical, directing). Classify Hispanic/Mexican origin by public self-identification where documented, else Census surname list (`https://www2.census.gov/topics/genealogy/2010surnames/`), and flag the imputation rate. Compare to the group's share of the adult population and of the BA+ population in the same decades. This arm tests the operator's expectation that any signal is affirmative-action driven: split pre- and post-2015 and note that a step change at 2015–2020 without a change in the underlying labour supply from arm A would be consistent with selection-rule change rather than output change; state the limit plainly, this cannot separate the two.
C. **Registrations.** PatentsView inventor names for design patents (D-class) and utility patents, ethnicity by Census surname list, inventors per capita by state Mexican-origin share; and, if the Copyright Office bulk registration data is reachable, the same for copyright registrations of musical works. If neither is reachable within the budget, say so and stop the arm.
D. **Variety saturation.** Count Mexican-cuisine restaurants per CBSA from OpenStreetMap (Overpass API, `amenity=restaurant|fast_food` with `cuisine=mexican|tex-mex|taco|burrito`; state coverage limits) and, as a check in the metros it covers, the Yelp Open Dataset (free academic download, category "Mexican"). Regress log restaurants per 100,000 residents on log Mexican-origin share (ACS B03001) across CBSAs with controls for income, population and region; the elasticity below one is the saturation signal, and a concave fit (spline or quadratic in share) shows where variety stops growing. Do the same for all restaurants and for non-Mexican ethnic cuisines as placebos. From ACS, the share of cooks and chefs (SOC 35-2011/35-1011) who are Mexico-born, nationally and by CBSA, to state how much of the supply runs on the group's labour rather than its recipes. Disconfirmation: an elasticity near one or above, or Mexican restaurant density still rising at the highest shares, rejects saturation.
E. **Universality (lowest priority).** Whether Latin and regional-Mexican music is consumed outside the group: Luminate year-end and mid-year reports and RIAA Latin revenue reports give the Latin share of US streams and revenue; if any report gives listener ethnicity composition, use it; otherwise report the share and say crossover is unmeasured. Do not spend more than a small budget here.

## Outputs
`derived/`: per-capita creative-labour tables with SEs; awards table with imputation flags; registrations table or a stop note; CBSA restaurant panel and elasticity table; music share table. `RESULT.md` opening `**Verdict:**` on both questions, with the saturation hypothesis stated as supported, not supported or not identified, and a paragraph on what a fair "cultural benefit" line would look like in dollars (arts earnings as the market lower bound; consumer-surplus multiples as an [INFERENCE] range) beside the repo's fiscal gap of about −$7,100 per person-year vs whites (ladder 123/130).

## Limits to state
Occupation is supply, not quality; awards are gated by credentials and selection rules; surname imputation misclassifies; OSM tagging is incomplete and uneven by region; restaurants per capita conflate supply with local demand from the group itself; nothing here values within-group consumption differently from crossover consumption, and the memo should say which is which.
