## The Caplan / Clemens steel-man and their concessions

Caplan and Weinersmith's *Open Borders* (FirstSecond2019) argues that preventing willing workers, employers and landlords from transacting sacrifices large productive gains and violates freedom/equal opportunity based on birthplace. Their own introductory book pages, reproduced by Caplan in2019 and visually inspected here, join the economic case to rights and poverty reduction. The strongest version allows some incumbents to lose and asks whether targeted remedies can handle a cost more cheaply than exclusion. Caplan's own release-week account identifies separate chapters on native impoverishment, fiscal burden, culture and political danger, then targeted remedies; his November2019 explanation explicitly treats cultural/political costs as an empirical and valuation question. Weinersmith's own2019 text also acknowledges that sufficiently good reasons for exclusion could exist. [SOURCE: **actual book excerpts and both authors' own texts**, with links/access limits in [normalization and steel-man note](reads/normalization_and_steelman.md)]

Clemens2011's strongest claim is that place-dependent productivity and large barriers to moving create very large potential gains to migrants/world output; that claim does not require a large gain to incumbent natives. His reviewed models often yield **50–150% of world GDP**, explicitly conditional estimates. He **does concede** adverse destination externalities involving public goods, open space, pollution and valued homogeneity, and says their magnitudes at large scale were poorly known. He supplies no native-output percentage for that list in the checked2011 text. His then-current lack of a documented large collapse cannot bound a one-percent effect, and the all-world gain is a different denominator/counterfactual from this lineage's much smaller R1 gain. [SOURCE: Clemens2011, DOI10.1257/jep.25.3.83, author manuscript printedpp2,9–10/Table1p28; [source note](reads/normalization_and_steelman.md)]

Their follow-on transmission model makes the concession quantitative **conditional on its unassimilated dynastic-labor buffer**: Clemens–Pritchett IZA2016 Eq3p18/Table1p35 has `1−Atilde = δφ/(1−cφ)`, Mexico `δ=.125(.00377)`, assimilation `.0282(.00219)`. At the authors' conservative `c=.5` scenario, a1% loss relative to current output requires **φ=7.619% [THRESHOLD]**. The measured range of this lineage's φ is **not identified**. Neither13.335% lineage population nor3.621pp non-BA earners is φ; the old repo substitution of fiscal/generation gaps for assimilation is not repeated. [MODEL / CALCULATION: `derived/conditional_scenarios.json`; [social note](reads/social_institutions.md)]

**What size do they rule out?** No group-specific one-percent ceiling is verified in these inspected primary materials. This does not claim the entire2019 book lacks a number: its full substantive chapter pages were inaccessible. The recovery used authentic author-posted book pages and contemporaneous explanations, not a reviewer's paraphrase; the Clemens numeric/page citations explicitly use his author manuscript after the journal PDF returned403. The threshold verdict follows from the empirical evidence table, not an argument from authorial silence. [DEGRADED / ACCESS LIMIT]
