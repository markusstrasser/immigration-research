# Hold dollars and age standards fixed when comparing generations

## Context

The previous aggregate audit reproduced the original all-age baseline but left the extension on an adult-only universe. Generation claims also mixed source-coded and raw family histories. The user requested continued empirical progress and occasional critique of the constructs.

## Alternatives considered

1. Continue adding adult component totals to the all-age base. Rejected because the resulting figure lacks one consistent population and age rule.
2. Treat a shared-to-person dollar allocation change as a pure attribution experiment with ordinary person weights. Rejected as the sole test because unequal within-unit weights can change estimated national dollars.
3. Rebuild all components on the all-age domain; retain the continuity estimate and add fixed-budget attribution, common-age generation contrasts and strict raw family-history sensitivities. Adopted because it discriminates accounting effects from observed population differences without selecting a preferred sign.

## Decision

Use the consistent all-age account for expanded descriptive totals. Keep absolute partial balances beside reference gaps. Preserve person-weighted continuity estimates, but use paired head-dollar arms with unchanged population/direct-health weighting when attributing differences to reassignment. Compare generation profiles at a common fixed age distribution; retain observed-population totals for aggregate accounting.

For IIMMLA, retain the supplied classification and add explicit respondent/parent/grandparent rules, with unresolved and conflicting histories visible. Separate generic migration generation, confirmed Mexican-grandparent birthplace and supplied ethnicity. An exact-country G3 versus G4 lineage comparison remains unidentified without earlier-ancestor information.

## Evidence

[New findings](../research/immigration-all-age-and-lineage-findings-2026-09-17.md), [fiscal generator](../infra/immigration-fiscal/all_age_ledger_2026_09_17/README.md), [IIMMLA generator](../infra/immigration-fiscal/iimmla_sensitivity_2026_09_17/README.md), [review disposition](../notes/immigration-construct-review-2026-09-17.md). The fiscal run reproduces the independent baseline and held adult component means/SEs, then passes all-age and covariance guards. The controlled allocation test moves the white-reference gap by +$47.42bn while preserving national component budgets. Large adverse gaps persist. The strict IIMMLA sample retains lower G4+ degree attainment and lower arrest reports, while its small incarceration ordering is unstable.

## Revisit if

Reconciled administrative fiscal controls or improved donor data materially change component levels; a preferable incidence model has an explicit economic justification; representative linked descendants include nonidentifiers and symmetric country histories; or a specified admission-policy model supplies a defensible counterfactual. Sampling intervals alone cannot close these gaps.
