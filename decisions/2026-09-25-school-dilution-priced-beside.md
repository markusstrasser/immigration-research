# 2026-09-25: Price school dilution beside the account from its own school response (adopted)

## Context

The [September 20 decision](2026-09-20-school-quality-unpriced.md) withdrew the $0 classroom
harm and labeled the national school-quality channel unpriced until "a matched
arrival/resource/incumbent-outcome design identifies a transportable effect and a nonoverlapping
valuation". The [dilution lane](../infra/immigration-fiscal/school_dilution_2026_09_24/RESULT.md)
(ladder 222) supplies the resource link from the account itself: the adopted main case charges
the group's pupils 63–66% of average spending (CBO's response), so $51–59bn a year of school
spending follows no one. Either that money is spread over more pupils (dilution) or it was never
needed (economies of scale). F-33 function responses split it 58% instruction, 11% pupil and
staff support, 31% fixed costs.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Keep the channel unpriced.** Faithful to the September 20 condition, which asks for an
   arrival design. It leaves the account internally inconsistent: it charges a low school
   response and books no loss from the money that does not follow.
2. **Price the instruction shortfall beside the account at the account's own response
   (proposed).** $16.1bn a year in other residents' pupils' present-value lifetime earnings
   (JM's 90% range of contexts −$2.1bn to +$35.5bn; $9–13bn on bounded forms). The effect is
   transported (Jackson–Mackevicius's pooled US spending effect, Chetty–Friedman–Rockoff's
   earnings per SD), not estimated on immigrant arrivals.
3. **Add it to the fiscal-plus-social total.** It is a welfare cost of the same kind as victims'
   harm, but it is a present value of future earnings rather than a current-year cost, and its
   range crosses zero.
4. **Price at the 19-year within-district response (0.87).** $3.6bn, but only together with a
   school charge $33–36bn higher, since the same response then applies to the account's line.

## Decision (proposed; the operator adopts)

Option 2. Relabel the resource channel "priced beside the account, conditional on its school
response", quote $16bn (−$2bn to +$36bn) beside the fiscal figure, and keep it out of every
total until the operator decides on option 3. Peer effects stay unpriced in both directions,
since no US estimate excludes zero on the harm side. Class size (the same resource, $2.6bn), the
full-funding split and the long-run response are alternative measures and are never added.

## Adoption (2026-09-25)

The operator adopted option 2 on 2026-09-25 ("1 i guess"), adding that $16bn "seems not a lot
given the deteriation of standards". The resource channel is now priced beside the account,
conditional on its school response, and stays out of every total. Option 3, adding it to the
fiscal-plus-social total, was not decided and stays open. The operator's doubt concerns what the
price leaves out: peer effects and classroom standards. They stay unpriced in both directions
until a US estimate separates them from zero.

## Evidence

F-33 district finance FY2000–FY2024 (national current spending per pupil within 0.12% of Census
in four years), CCD enrollment, the school-cost lane's district weights, JM (2024) pp. 413, 418,
424–428, CFR (NBER w19424) pp. 12, 19 and Appendix Table 3; the lane's scripts reproduce 21 of 21
derived files byte for byte on the parent's rerun.

## Revisit if

A design with arrival variation (a refugee placement program, a sharp inflow) moves the
medium-run instruction response above about 0.85 or below 0.5; JM's per-dollar effect is shown to
fall steeply beyond $1,000 per pupil (39% of the central figure sits in majority-group districts);
or the local revenue fall with the group's share proves to be rate cuts, which would turn part of
it into a taxpayer gain.
