# 2026-09-19: Separate education, population composition and fiscal assumptions

The user authorized the remaining material empirical checks and asked whether new economics or mathematics would help. The repaired all-education Mexican account cannot establish a low-skill admission effect. Build direct education-by-origin resident accounts, preserve both attribution conventions, and compare against both same-education and all-education natives at common ages. Current educational attainment is a proxy, not education at entry or a measure of latent ability.

Propagate paired CPS replicates across ages and joint MEPS donor covariance; propagate independently sampled ACS institutional counts separately. Keep conditional sampling intervals distinct from model scenarios. Extend district-cost attribution using pupils' observed Hispanic/NH-white identity, consistent with the parameter definitions; also export a symmetric zero-differential account. Preserve the earlier Mexican-only attribution as historical evidence and a reproduction anchor.

Institutional facility type and public payer costs are not identified by the available ACS domains. Retain count evidence and report explicitly hypothetical additional-cost prices, without inventing a central estimate or claiming empirical bounds. Withhold lifetime profiles lacking age support and every recent-arrival forecast. The latter needs a transition/transport model that these cross-sections do not identify.

Rejected: extrapolating all-Mexico pooled costs to every education/origin; treating matched donor records as linked people; replacing missing ages with unmarked predictions; summing age standard errors independently; calling an accounting break-even amount an admission-policy welfare result; adding topology without a concrete identifying use.

Evidence: [results and methods](../research/immigration-education-fiscal-and-methods-2026-09-19.md), with verified source mappings, original all-education reproduction gates, education-partition identities, survey covariance tests, primary medical-calibration scope checks and full reruns. Below-HS Mexican-born adults have a better same-education native benchmark balance in the baseline, while HS-only adults have a worse one. This is a material counterexample to a blanket origin-based fiscal ranking.

Revisit with linked longitudinal admission/exit records, observed institutional payer/type costs, validated medical transport, or an identified policy shock. More elaborate regression on the same cross-section would not supply those missing facts.
