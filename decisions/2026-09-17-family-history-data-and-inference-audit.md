# 2026-09-17: Separate identifiable family history from ethnic-sample outcomes

## Context

The user supplied thirteen files and requested organization, linkage assessment and further tests of the recent conclusions. The intake uncovered duplicate/partial NLS exports, unusable Pew generation recodes and documentation-only ICPSR downloads.

## Decision

Use the correctly paired Pew2015/omnibus2015–16 samples for explicit ancestry-population sensitivity, retaining both rounded-share and published-count calibrations. Link NLS extracts only on validated same-cohort respondent IDs. Preserve unknown family branches and distinguish generic foreign ancestry from Mexican-specific birthplace. Repair the existing GSS generation bug and auxiliary unknown-generation categories.

Retain descriptive CILS/IIMMLA differences while withdrawing strict-floor, universal within-stratum parity and causal-class interpretations. The NLS diagnostic adds a distinct childhood-resident cohort and outcome window; it does not make incomparable surveys interchangeable. SAT/ACT availability is a data/selection question rather than a proven absence of variables.

Prior position: ladder83/87/104–106. New evidence: direct questionnaire/SAV labels, codebook-matched counts, same-ID overlap checks, independently rerun comparisons and official NLS achievement-test documentation. Threshold cleared: yes for the measurement corrections and qualified interpretations; no for a causal or universal generation verdict. **PARTIAL-UPDATE.**

The family-history schema remains a recommendation; this decision does not redefine the central question, causal tree or analysis protocol. No raw respondent records are published or committed.

## Alternatives considered

- Merge studies by coincident IDs, stack dataset variants as waves, or treat requested variables as exported: rejected by source inventories.
- Read all later-generation self-ID samples as complete descendant lineages: rejected by the observed Pew nonidentifier population and instrument limitations.
- Reverse all earlier conclusions: rejected because substantial descriptive findings and adjusted attitude patterns reproduce.

## Revisit if

Fresh NLS exports permit a broader validated biological-parent-roster reconstruction or restricted exact-country lineage analysis; actual LNS/NYC records become available; or an ancestry-inclusive survey measures the same ANES/GSS endpoints with an appropriate survey-design variance. A larger sample of the wrong population does not by itself resolve these issues.
