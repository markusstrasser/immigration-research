# 2026-10-14: The main case takes the group total at the personal allocation and counts the taxes on the business capital that serves the group's jobs: $339.6–423.6bn

## Context

A cross-lab review of v6's assumptions on 2026-10-14 (Gemini 4 Argon, GPT-6 Astra and Opus 5.5 on three packets;
`research/immigration-v6-assumption-review-2026-10-14.md`) found two method questions over its bar, in opposite
directions, and one recorded bound missing from beside the headline. The operator (13:27 JST): "Do whatever you think
is most common sense and smartest. DO all ... Push after."

1. **The allocation of mixed households.** v6's low end, specification 48, used the shared allocation: it splits each
   SPM unit's taxes and person-level benefits equally among everyone in the unit, members and non-members alike. In
   units that mix the two, that credits the lineage with part of its non-member partners' taxes. 8.684M members share
   units with 6.970M non-members. The headline is the change for all other residents, and a removal would leave those
   partners and their taxes in place.
2. **Taxes on the business capital that serves the group's jobs.** Corporate tax, business property tax and other
   production taxes responded at zero. The production module's capital-tax term was zero because it let the capital
   that follows the group's jobs "pay the same taxes elsewhere" (retention 1), an unmeasured rule. Under the case's own
   full-adjustment premise that capital is not in the US without the group, so its source taxes are lost. The receipt
   lane rejects the same view for housing, and the case already charges the mirror image: the return on the public
   capital that scales with the group.
3. **The Census coverage bound.** The 2020 PES Hispanic net undercount (4.99%) was recorded as an upper bound but never
   priced on a main case.

v6 also stated two measured residuals beside its headline (ladder 298) because a 0.5% move did not warrant a new case.
A new case is now built, so they go in.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Keep v6.** Rejected. The shared total is not the change for others, and the zero on business capital taxes
   contradicts a premise the case applies to homes and to public capital.
2. **Keep the shared allocation as the low end and the personal as the high end** (v6's rule). Rejected: the two
   allocations do not bound one object. Sharing inside a unit of members alone cannot change the total, so the whole
   difference is a transfer across the lineage's boundary.
3. **Sharing among members only as a third allocation.** Rejected. On a split that keeps each person's weight it gives
   exactly the personal total. The builders split a unit's unweighted sum, and person weights differ inside 44.6% of
   units, so on four large keys that split moves the net cost by +$1.44bn against the personal total: of the
   shared-minus-personal gap there (−$6.50bn), −$7.43bn is the boundary transfer and +$0.93bn the weight artifact
   (`mixed_unit_credits_2026_10_14/RESULT.md`).
4. **Business capital taxes at the module's Clemens-form term** (τK 24.6% on the capital income that follows the
   group's labour, $212.5bn at v6's low end). Rejected: it counts residence taxes on capital income, which follow the
   owners, and residential property, which items 2–3 of v4 already price.
5. **Business capital taxes at CBO's labour share of corporate tax only** (the floor, $12.47bn on the identified
   union). An arm: it reads an incidence share as a revenue loss, which answers a different question.
6. **Corporate tax in full at both ends.** Rejected: part of the base is rent that does not scale with the group's
   labour. Treasury's distribution method treats 63% of corporate taxable income as supernormal. The high end takes
   only the normal return; the low end takes the full base, the reading in which rents scale with the market.
7. **Mining like other industries.** An arm. At world prices the reserve, not the workforce, sets mining's output: the
   oil and ore would still be extracted, by other workers at higher wages, so its capital, severance, mineral property
   and profits stay. That is the land rule applied to the reserve.

## Decision

The main case is v6 plus five items: **$339.6–423.6bn a year** (339.552 / 423.649) at specifications 16 / 11, both at
the personal allocation, in both methods. That is **$7,942–9,909 per member** of the 42.75M-person lineage. Counting
benefits when paid (the cash set) it is $264.4–348.5bn (264.426 / 348.523).
