# 2026-10-07: The main case takes the 2026 Trustees on separate funds, retiree health on accrual, the added people's measured ages and college costs keyed by use: $389.1–461.5bn

## Context

The October 6–7 literature scan (ladder 282–286) and a review of attack surfaces on v5 (a brainstorm plus
doc-grounded reviews by GPT-6 Astra and Gemini 3.8; nine lanes, ladder 287–294 and a note on 250) priced each
candidate on v5 at its ends, specifications 48 / 11. On 2026-10-07 the operator asked to "adopt everything that makes
sense and is common sense and most smart people would agree with" (10:19 JST). On the plan to build one v6 from the
checks, propagate it and update the record once, he said "ok do what you think is good" (12:02 JST).

Four items pass that bar. Each replaces an assumption with a measurement, or puts a line on the rule the account
already applies to its neighbours:

1. **The pension accrual on the 2026 Trustees Reports, separate OASI and DI funds** (ladder 286). The 2025 paths were a
   year stale. The Trustees say the combined-fund reading "implicitly assumes that the law will have been changed", so
   current law is separate funds. The item takes all six 2026 inputs the lane reads (paths, wage growth, HI costs,
   interest rates, COLAs and bases, mortality). Set only, because the cash set counts benefits when paid.
2. **Retiree health on accrual** (ladder 288). BEA already books public pensions at the normal cost and keeps their
   legacy off the service lines. Retiree health was on pay-go. GASB 75 and OPM normal costs replace the pay-go
   premiums, and care bought for military retirees, pay-go for past service, goes to zero.
3. **The added people at their measured age mix** (ladder 292). v5 priced the 3.04M at the identified third-plus age
   mix and listed measuring it under Revisit-if. The later losses are younger (61.5% under 20).
4. **User fees and the education keys** (ladder 294). Public higher education was keyed by item P, non-school capital
   outlay per head by state. The item keys it by measured use, credits tuition and hospital charges by who pays them,
   keys Pell by the group's share and takes BEA's K-12 weight (0.775). It is priced on the union only.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Keep v5.** Rejected. Each item corrects one of the following: a stale input, a convention that current law
   contradicts, a rule applied to pensions but not to retiree health, an assumption v5 itself flagged, or a
   mislabelled key.
2. **The 2026 Trustees on the combined fund** (−$1.73 / 1.63bn on v5). Kept as an arm, because current law leads.
3. **Retiree health with the pay-go removed and no normal cost** (−$8.65 / 9.05bn). Rejected: it drops the accrual of
   today's service, which BEA keeps for pensions. At a normal-cost ratio of 1 instead of 1.20, the item is
   −$0.80 / 0.69bn: an arm.
4. **The age mix if the childhood report persisted** (−$4.12 / 6.44bn). Rejected as the central: within a birth cohort,
   adults report less loss than the cohort's children did.
5. **The fee item with transit keyed by commuters** (−$2.58 / 3.16bn in all). Transit rejected: it repeats v3's item 8.
   Weighted by where the deficit falls, the riders' key is 1.019 times the population key (decision 2026-09-29,
   rejected alternative 4).
6. **Held beside, each its own object:**
   - Van Hook–Bachmeier's fourth-plus step at face value: $413.3–493.0bn on v5 (ladder 287). Today's CPS measures the
     step at 0.880, and VHB's levels belong to an older era.
   - Later losses closing C3 like third-generation attriters: −$3.8 / 5.0bn on v5. v5's rule rests on the one direct
     measurement, co-resident fourth-generation adults (c −0.35 to −0.86 on BA+).
   - The closed federal budget: $354.6–425.6bn on v5 (ladder 282).
   - Post-2024 current law: net −$0.3bn in FY2028, and +$5.75bn a year after tax year 2030 (ladder 291).
   - Excess burden at 0.25 per dollar: $77–96bn on the cash set (ladder 250's note).
   - The cycle with budgets fixed: +$17–35bn, a component (ladder 290).
   - Nonresponse bias: +$0.2–1.5bn (ladder 285).
   - The US-born ACS residual: −$3.6–5.5bn (ladder 284).

## Decision

The main case is v5 plus items 1–4: **$389.1–461.5bn a year** (389.083 / 461.480) at specifications 48 / 11, or
**$9,101–10,794 per member** of the 42.75M-person lineage. Change from v5: −$1.211 / +0.237bn. The cash set is
$307.4–385.4bn (307.399 / 385.364; $7,190–9,014 per member).

Each item alone on v5 reproduces its source lane's band to 1e-9. Item 4 interacts with none of the others, and there
is no three-way term. The ends stay at specifications 48 / 11 in both methods.

- [FRAMING-SENSITIVE] Current law's separate trust funds. The combined fund is an arm.
- [ASSUMPTION] Item 4 prices the union only. At the union's per-member terms, the 3.04M added people would add −$0.02 /
  0.06bn.
- [ASSUMPTION] Consumers that split the case by generation, household or age take item 4 pro rata to the union amount
  of a basis line (`meta.user_fees.splits`). Its higher-education parts, Pell included, use the education line. Pell
  goes to students, and no college-enrollment key exists by person in the consumer lanes. The Pell part's own line,
  other federal benefits, would put it on cash-assistance recipients. The K-12 weight's two parts, which move row 6
  between the school and college lines, also use the education line: the consumers carry no generation amounts for
  those two added lines. In the household split this moves $0.1bn or less between schools and other education.
- The companion readings are rebuilt on v6 as whole cases, every item included:

  - C3 ± 1 SE: $386.3–465.4bn;

  - the low side with schools at the within-district 0.836: $361.3–434.9bn.
