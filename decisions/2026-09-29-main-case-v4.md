# 2026-09-29: The main case adds pension accrual, long-run property taxes and seven measured keys: $371.4–434.8bn

## Context

The September 27 main case ($321.82–387.37bn at specifications 48 / 11) left ten candidate changes open. Each was
built as a lane on September 28–29, and candidates v1–v3 bundled them. A cross-lab review (GPT-6 Astra at xhigh, every
finding verified) and a hostile attack on the first candidate (candidate_attack_2026_09_28) revised them. The pension
accrual was then valued at the benefits current law can pay (decision 2026-09-28, at the operator's word).

Until then each item had been run alone and the results summed by hand. Candidate v4 (f40e47e) ran the whole bundle
as one gated set, with every line that two items touch resolved in the open. Its adoption payload (856bd5a) and the
engine's three optional payload parts (db5840f) were built and checked before this decision. The operator adopted it
on 2026-09-29 at 15:12 JST ("1 ok do").

## Alternatives considered

1. **Keep the September 27 case.** It holds seven zeros set by convention (property taxes fixed in the long run,
   survey income-tax gradient, unmeasured payroll compliance, a 2024 workers'-compensation outlier, population keys for
   public housing, the published grid's production weights, national prices) and counts no pension promise that
   accrues while members work. Rejected: the operator's standard is the full long-run cost, priced symmetrically.
2. **The cash set as the headline ($294.70–361.82bn).** It counts benefits when paid, not when earned. Kept beside as
   the cash-flow reading.
3. **Scheduled benefits in the accrual.** It values benefits current law cannot pay after the trust fund runs out.
   Kept as an arm beside (decision 2026-09-28): current law leads.
4. **Items 8 (transit at the riders' key), 10 (hospitals' uninsured use at 0.7×) and 6b (row 2 through CBO's
   re-key).**
   - Item 8 changes nothing distinguishable (riders' key 1.019, SE 0.017).
   - Item 10's evidence exists only between regions (ladder 256).
   - Item 6b and 6a's within-group rule assume CBO's shares carry measured compliance. CBO takes nonfilers' income
     from the CPS, so they do not, and 6a runs under the payroll lane's proportional rule instead.

   All three stay beside.

## Decision

The main case is candidate v4's set: **$371.41–434.84bn a year** at specifications 48 / 11, the mean of the two
fill-in methods, or **$9,353–10,950 per member** of the 39,712,493 people the account prices. Change from September
27: +$49.60 / +$47.47bn. Across every correction's range it runs $296.8–487.3bn ($338.0–456.3bn in quadrature).

The items nearly add: every interaction is pairwise, and only payroll compliance × the accrual exceeds $0.1bn
(−0.56 / −0.58: the accrual follows the OASDI receipts that 6a lowers). Personal motor vehicle licences take both
state pricing and the miles key; the rule chosen applies the state price index to the miles-keyed amount, and each
alternative is reported beside it in v4's `overlaps.csv`.

Item 5 is framing-sensitive. The tax on structures, about four-fifths of it, is lost in every long-run reading with
full capital adjustment. The land part turns on framing: a consolidated fiscal reading gives −35.91 in all
(`receipt_side_long_run_2026_09_28`). Item 3 is calibrated to IRS totals by AGI bin, and the group's share inside
each bin stays the CPS's.

The cash set, $294.70–361.82bn ($7,421–9,111 per member), is reported beside as the cash-flow reading. The
September 27 case stays reproducible as `sept27`.
