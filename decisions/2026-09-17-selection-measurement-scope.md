# Limit the BA-share selection measure to what it measures

## Context

The user asked whether distance implies stronger selection and whether persistent poor outcomes identify genetic causes. The held selection output uses immigrant BA share minus origin-country BA share, with an ecological regression of descendant BA attainment.

## Decision

Retain the numerical series as a **BA-share difference**. Do not interpret its sign as overall skill selection or its fitted variance as a causal decomposition. No estimator, raw data, central question or analysis protocol is changed.

## Alternatives considered

Keeping the unrestricted selection label would confuse one educational threshold with the full distribution. Discarding the tabulations would lose valid descriptive information. Narrowing the interpretation preserves the useful result.

## Evidence

The generator defines the threshold difference directly. [Northern Triangle research, Table 2](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/653631518451652402/pdf/WPS8334.pdf) demonstrates that fewer graduates can coexist with fewer people at the bottom. Different periods prevent interpreting that paper as a numerical falsification of our later CPS tabulations. See the [transfer review](../research/immigration-latam-selection-transfer-2026-09-17.md).

## Revisit if

Comparable source/migrant distributions, actual parent cohorts and pre-migration measurements support a broader selection estimate. Genetic attribution requires additional identification beyond either education measure.

## Supersedes

Only the unrestricted interpretation of “negative selectivity” and causal readings of the ecological fit. Earlier results remain available as dated evidence.
