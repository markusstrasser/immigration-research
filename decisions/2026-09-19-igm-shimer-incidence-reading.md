# 2026-09-19: Correct the Shimer incidence reading

## Context

Rechecking the primary low-skilled-immigration poll while assessing the project's novelty
revealed that the September 17 audit mistook the recipients of adverse effects for immigrant
skill groups. This produced an unsupported criticism of Robert Shimer's reasoning.

## Alternatives considered

1. Retain the claim that Shimer reversed the fiscal ranking of low- and high-skilled immigrants.
   This requires changing the referent of his comment despite the low-skilled-entry question.
2. Read the comment as distinguishing wage and fiscal incidence among existing workers.
   This fits both the wording and the citizen-welfare question. [INFERENCE]

## Decision

Adopt the second reading. Withdraw the mistaken rating and its repeated summaries. Correct
the adjacent claim that only Oliver Hart mentioned fiscal costs: Shimer did too. Preserve
all original comments and vote data. Do not infer a panelist's complete reasoning or general
intelligence from an optional short comment. The comment still does not quantify total welfare.

Apply the same referent check to Carl Shapiro: greater legal admission does not necessarily
mean an equal increase in total entry. His proposed substitution for unauthorized entry fits
one possible policy counterfactual, so withdraw the categorical wrong-question criticism.
This does not establish that substitution occurs or that net welfare rises. [INFERENCE]

## Evidence

[Primary poll, Question A and Shimer response](https://kentclarkcenter.org/surveys/low-skilled-immigrants/),
re-read September 19, 2026; verbatim data in `notes/igm-immigration-polls-2026-09-17.csv`.
The question fixes the entrants as low-skilled foreign workers. Shimer separates wage costs
for low-skilled workers and fiscal costs for high-skilled workers. The earlier April memo,
`research/immigration-verified-findings-report-2026-04-10.md`, already used the incidence reading.

## Revisit if

A fuller statement by Shimer explicitly identifies these two groups as incoming immigrants.

## Supersedes

The Shimer interpretation, Hart-only fiscal-channel claim, and categorical Shapiro
wrong-question criticism in the September 17 IGM audit.
No poll counts, raw comments or fiscal estimates change.
