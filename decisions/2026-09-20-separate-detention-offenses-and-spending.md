# 2026-09-20: Separate detention, offenses and detention spending

## Context and decision

The operator requested fair crime comparisons that distinguish immigration
detention and immigration-only offenses, while retaining federal/local custody
costs. Use the [source-specific measurement and accounting contract](../research/immigration-detention-crime-and-fiscal-scope-2026-09-20.md).

The held ACS public microdata cannot split institutional residence by offense or
civil detention. Its rates stay labeled institutionalization. Texas named felony
categories and federal offense-coded records support narrower comparisons with
their own populations. ICE criminal-history labels do not turn civil custody
into a current criminal conviction. No aggregate ICE subtraction is authorized
by mismatched ACS and administrative populations. [MEASUREMENT]

## Evidence and affected interpretations

Prior position: ACS described as replicating incarceration comparisons · new evidence:
ACS dictionary and actual producer identify all institutions · threshold cleared:
yes for measurement scope · PARTIAL-UPDATE: retain counts, withdraw crime-only interpretation.

The ACS dictionary, Census group-quarters definitions, Texas study methods and
actual producer/consumer code are linked in the contract. The old ACS×BJS
offense-cost and institutional-cost calculations remain conditional proxy
scenarios. They cannot establish ordinary-crime costs or actual detention costs.
Federal payments and state/local receipts cancel in consolidated accounting;
the resources consumed remain. Existing ledger coverage must be checked before
any additional fiscal line is booked. [INFERENCE / ACCOUNTING]

## Revisit if

Compatible person-level custody/offense data or matched origin/age/sex/time
administrative aggregates allow a defensible split. Re-estimate a policy effect
only with an identified counterfactual. This changes classification and reuse,
not the raw counts or the current national fiscal total.
